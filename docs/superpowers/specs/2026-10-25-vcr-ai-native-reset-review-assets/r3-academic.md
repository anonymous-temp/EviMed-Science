# R3 - Academic and technical state of the art for an AI-native "virtual clinical research" module

Research date: 2026-10-25 (re-read and corrected the same day; no new searches). Labels: [PR] peer-reviewed, [PP] preprint, [VEN] vendor-authored, [REG] regulator or official text; "read" = the document itself was read; "secondary" = news, law-firm or vendor summary only. Unverified items say "could not verify". Quotes and URLs for each line are in the body.

## SUMMARY (about 2,400 words)

### Bottom line for the reset
1. Words first. "Synthetic" names three unrelated things (real external patients, generated records, Abadie's aggregate-unit method); "virtual trial" means simulation to some authors and remote/decentralised conduct to others (FDA's term is "decentralized elements"); "matching" names screening, propensity matching and MAIC. A four-noun vocabulary (patient-trial matching, virtual patient / virtual cohort, external control, trial simulation) plus a three-question placement rule is proposed in the body.
2. LLM matching is a throughput tool with human confirmation, not an eligibility oracle. Criterion-level accuracy is about 87% on synthetic or idealised notes (TrialGPT, Nat Commun 2024) but 63-68% on real oncology notes (PRISM, arXiv v2; experts 70%) and 87% across 30 real sites (Inato preprint) - different systems and labels, so indicative only. The only randomised trial I found (JAMA 2025, RECTIFIER) shows faster screening and more enrolment, with the same eligibility rate among completed screens.
3. "The LLM never does statistics" is supported by the evidence but is not sufficient. Asked for textbook sample sizes, GPT-4/4o alone were off by up to ~15% (one 26% outlier) and drifted between runs (Sebo & Wang 2025). PowerGPT (LLM plus statistical engines; preprint) reported 94.1% vs 55.4% sample-size accuracy in a randomised trial against a comparator the abstract does not specify. Yet agents still fail on method choice and assumptions even when the code runs (P-Bench, Aug 2026: 58.3 raw vs 30.5 strict on hard tasks), so the engine itself must own applicability checks, refusals and an assumption register.
4. Regulation has a usable frame for trial simulation and no frame for synthetic arms. ICH M15 (model-informed drug development) is final: ICH Step 4 on 29 Jan 2026, EMA in effect 23 Jul 2026, FDA final notice scheduled 2026-06-03 [REG, read]. FDA's external-control (Feb 2023), AI-credibility (Jan 2025) and Bayesian (Jan 2026) documents are still drafts; ICH E20 (adaptive designs) is not final (Step 4 sign-off expected Feb 2027). I found no qualification of virtual cohorts, synthetic control arms or digital-twin external controls in the sources I checked.
5. "Digital twin" credit comes from randomisation, not from the model. EMA's 2022 PROCOVA opinion qualifies a prognostic score as a covariate in randomised trials with continuous outcomes, says type I error control and unbiased estimation "are not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model", and declines to qualify model building [REG, read]. For non-randomised comparison there is no cheap applicable-model route; FDA's external-control draft is built on patient-level data for both arms.
6. R tooling is strong for design, simulation and borrowing (rpact 4.4.0, gsDesign 3.11.0, gsDesign2 1.2.0, simtrial 1.1.0, RBesT 1.12-0, all released 2026) and thin elsewhere: psborrow2 is 0.0.5.1 with an undeliverable maintainer e-mail on CRAN, IPDfromKM last released 2020, Mediana 2019, and the Roche MAIC package is not on CRAN.

### Q1. Taxonomy with authoritative sources
- Digital twin [REG]: NASEM (Dec 2023): "a set of virtual information constructs that mimics the structure, context, and behavior of a natural, engineered, or social system ..., is dynamically updated with data from its physical twin, has a predictive capability, and informs decisions that realize value"; the bidirectional virtual-physical interaction "is central". https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4
- Virtual patient / cohort [PR]: the newest taxonomy (Balmus et al., Clin Pharmacol Ther 2026;120(5):1181-1194) uses two axes - personalization (synthetic populations -> hybrid cohorts -> patient-specific twins) and abstraction (compartment -> whole-organ). A 2025 narrative review (Kleeberger, Cureus) says there is "no universally accepted method for generating virtual patient cohorts" and treats twins as one way to generate virtual patients. Avicenna (2016): in silico trials = "individualised computer simulation" in development or regulatory evaluation.
- In silico credibility [PR/REG]: ASME V&V 40 logic (question of interest -> context of use -> model risk = combination of model influence and decision consequence), reused in FDA's device CM&S guidance (Nov 2023, physics-based models only; secondary sources) and, per its footnote 6, adapted by ICH M15 for drugs ("modified from" ASME V&V 40-2018). VICTRE (2,986 simulated patients) is the showcase, and it is a device/imaging trial.
- Clinical trial simulation (pharmacometrics, Holford/Kimko) is the established discipline behind a "virtual trial" that tests designs; it evaluates designs, not drug effects.
- External control [REG, read]: FDA draft (Feb 2023, docket FDA-2022-D-2983, still draft on 2026-10-25) uses "externally controlled trial", "external control arm", "historical control" (earlier time) and "concurrent control" (same time, other setting). It never says "synthetic". Its scope is patient-level data from other trials or RWD; it excludes summary-level external comparisons and excludes augmenting a randomised control (augmentation is discussed in the Jan 2026 Bayesian draft). Thorlund 2020 [PR] uses "synthetic control" for external-data cohorts and never mentions Abadie. No paper I found flags the collision with Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller (JASA 2010); that statement is my synthesis.
- Target trial emulation [PR]: Hernan & Robins, Am J Epidemiol 2016;183:758-764; TARGET reporting guideline (BMJ 2025;390:e087179, also published in JAMA 2025 - JAMA pagination unverified; 21 items per secondary report). RCT-DUPLICATE (JAMA 2023): overall r=0.82; r=0.93 for the 16 closely emulated trials; about 0.53 for the rest (secondary report).
- "Virtual trial" = decentralised: FDA final guidance "Conducting Clinical Trials With Decentralized Elements" (Sept 2024) replaced the 2023 draft; Kleeberger explicitly sets this meaning aside.
- Most-confused pairs, in order of damage: synthetic control arm / synthetic data / Abadie method; "matching" x3; virtual patient vs digital twin; virtual trial (simulation) vs decentralised trial; external control vs target trial emulation vs in silico trial; clinical trial simulation vs in silico trial.

### Q2. LLM patient-trial matching 2024-2026
- TrialGPT [PR] (Nat Commun 2024;15:9074): 183 synthetic patients; criterion accuracy 87.3% vs experts 88.7-90.0%; retrieval recall >90% at <6% of the collection; user study 42.6% faster, accuracy 97.2% vs 91.7% (n.s.; 2 annotators); error analysis of 26 errors: incorrect reasoning 30.7%, label ambiguity 26.9%, missing medical knowledge 15.4%. Authors: experts stay in the loop. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183
- TrialGPT 2.0 [PP, 2026-09-01]: 288 retrospective cases, a clinician-recommended trial in the top 10 for ~91%, clinician screening time -55%; six-month prospective tumour-board deployment "expanding access ... by 90.9%"; no criterion-level real-record accuracy on the abstract. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01202
- PRISM / OncoLLM [PP; journal version not opened]: real oncology EHR; 720 criterion questions; GPT-4 68% (72% without N/A), OncoLLM 63%, GPT-3.5 53%, experts 70%; inter-annotator agreement 64-70%; enrolled-trial "ground truth" is flawed (9 of 10 top-1 non-enrolled trials were actually eligible). https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549
- RECTIFIER (MGB) [PR]: NEJM AI 2024 retrospective (1,894 test patients; per-criterion ranges 97.9-100% vs staff 91.7-100% in a trade summary, and test-set accuracy 92.7% vs 89.1% in Morrison 2025's table - both second-hand) and a JAMA 2025 RCT (online 2025-02-17): 4,476 patients, time to determination sHR 1.78 (1.54-2.06), enrolled 35 vs 19; eligible 20.8% vs 21.1% among completed screens; single centre, one trial. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514
- Others: Inato [PP, vendor] real-world 87% (485 patients, 30 sites, 36 trials) vs n2c2 93%; Massive Bio [PR, vendor-authored] prospective 3,804 patients, F1 0.82, sensitivity/specificity ~0.84, 120->30 min/patient, Cohen's kappa 0.92 against a dual-oncologist gold standard (secondary report; GPT-4 zero-shot F1 0.47); MatchMiner-AI [PP] top-20 MAP 0.94-0.95 but measured against LLM-judged relevance; Ferber et al. [PP] 88.0% on 51 synthetic EHRs; Microsoft/Providence (Wong, MLHC 2023) GPT-4 criteria structuring, real-record accuracy not verified.
- Failure modes: temporal reasoning 32% of errors, missing structured data 28%, ambiguity/negation 18% (Massive Bio; vendor, secondary); humans also read missing as satisfied (Ferber: 56/75 revised physician answers); rationales judged coherent for 75% of wrong decisions (Wornow); laterality (bilateral tumour) in PRISM. Quantified "within N days" windows: could not verify.
- Benchmarks: TREC CT 2021/2022 (synthetic admission-style patients; labels eligible / excluded / not relevant; 2022 best NDCG@10 0.6125, P@10 0.508), SIGIR 2016 (60 topics, 204,855 trials, irrelevant/potential/eligible), n2c2 2018 Track 1 (288 real patients, 13 fixed criteria, 47 teams, best micro-F1 0.91 from a RULE-BASED system, JAMIA 2019;26:1163). A 2025 systematic review (31 studies; 16 synthetic, 14 real) says comparability is limited and asks for real-data validation, private test sets, rationales with citations, and human-in-the-loop studies. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19327
- Recommended metrics (review + my synthesis): eligible-patient recall@k, false-exclusion rate / NPV of "ineligible", criterion accuracy by criterion class with "unknown" as its own class, explanation/citation validity, staff time, enrolment; randomise screening tasks (RECTIFIER design).

### Q3. LLM agents for trial design, statistics and simulation
- Trial-level agents mostly do text tasks: TrialMind [PR] (npj Digit Med 2025; search recall 0.711-0.834 vs 0.138-0.232 human baseline; extraction +16-32% over GPT-4), TrialPanorama [PP] (1.6M records, 8 tasks), ClinicalAgent [PR] (outcome prediction), Biomni [PP] (general discovery agent, 150 tools). None simulates trials with a statistical engine. I could not verify any peer-reviewed evaluation of an LLM agent driving rpact/gsDesign-type simulations; the closest are PowerGPT and N-Power AI (sample size only) and an unreviewed gsDesign MCP wrapper.
- Design with some evidence: LLM extracts parameters, a statistical engine computes (PowerGPT, preprint: in a randomised trial, sample-size accuracy 94.1% vs 55.4%, task completion 99.3% vs 77.8%, time 9.3 -> 4.0 min - each against a comparator the abstract does not specify). The direct LLM-alone evidence is Sebo & Wang (Fam Pract 2025): textbook sample sizes off by up to ~15% (one 26% outlier), run-to-run drift, a tendency to under-size.
- Where agents still fail: P-Bench (425 open-ended hypothesis tests; Aug 2026) - strict scoring nearly halves GPT-5.4's success and the example failure is a linear regression applied despite noticed outliers; StatQA (NeurIPS 2024) best 64.83%, mostly applicability errors; BixBench ~17% open-answer (bioinformatics).
- Agentic target-trial emulation exists only as 2026 preprints/early journal work: THESEUS [PR, JAMIA Open 2026] LLM -> JSON spec -> deterministic OHDSI R scripts (standardisation accuracy 0.93-0.97 OHDSI studies, 0.82-0.95 others); Biomni on Mount Sinai OMOP (5 trials x 3 runs; log-HR error 0.567 -> 0.224 only after calibration; run variability not reported); TrialCalibre1 (no RCT agreement reported).
- Silicon samples: LLM personas keep the mean and lose the spread, and drift over months (Bisbee, Political Analysis 2024); LLMs "misportray and flatten" groups (Wang et al., arXiv); commercial LLMs keep realistic distributions only for small feature subsets (Lin, CHIL 2025); a Survival-CTGAN control arm showed leakage risk and KM curves diverging after ~25 months (Elvatun, PLOS Digit Health 2025). Medical variance-shrinkage measurement for LLM cohorts: could not verify.
- What guidance says to do: ICH M15 - assumptions "identified, described, and justified, and alternatives considered", sensitivity analysis, plausible simulation scenarios with parameter uncertainty, predefined analysis plan and report; FDA-EMA Jan 2026 principles - context of use, documentation, risk-based performance assessment.

### Q4. Forecasting, twins, PROCOVA, "applicable models"
- Unlearn [VEN/REG]: "digital twin" = a disease-specific generative model's prediction of each participant's control outcome, used as a prognostic score. EMA CHMP adopted the PROCOVA qualification opinion on 15 Sep 2022: randomised Phase 2/3, continuous outcomes, ANCOVA special case, model-building step not qualified, out of scope: non-linear analysis and treatment-by-covariate interactions, sample-size reduction only with uncertainty and optimism accounted for. FDA CDER told Unlearn (blog, 2024-01-02) PROCOVA "does not appear to deviate from our guidance" on covariate adjustment (final May 2023) and did not take the ISTAND letter of intent. Company-reported gains: control arms -33% (ADAS-Cog11) / -19% (CDR-SB) in retrospective Alzheimer's analyses. No extension to binary/time-to-event found. https://www.ema.europa.eu/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/qualification-opinion-prognostic-covariate-adjustment-procovatm_en.pdf
- DT-GPT [PR] (npj Digit Med 2025): scaled-MAE gains over the next-best baseline of only 3.4% (NSCLC), 1.3% (ICU), 1.8% (AD); no calibration; weak on rare critical events (Hb<7.5 AUC 0.506). Delphi-2M [PR] (Nature 2025): AUC ~0.76 internal, 0.69 longitudinal UK Biobank, 0.67 in Denmark without retraining; calibration shown graphically; "caution against interpreting these as causal"; no trial or counterfactual use. Epic CoMET/"Curiosity" [PP, vendor]: 118M patients, no counterfactual evaluation. None is a validated counterfactual.
- Operationalising published models: appraise with TRIPOD+AI (BMJ 2024;385:e078378) and PROBAST+AI (BMJ 2025;388:e082505); calibration is the usual weak point at external validation (Van Calster, BMC Med 2019; ~200 events and 200 non-events for a precise calibration curve). Missing intercepts/baseline survival in published models is documented only qualitatively; Evidencio's regulatory status could not be verified.

## Q1. Taxonomy

### Digital twin (NASEM)
- [REG/PR-equivalent consensus report] NASEM, "Foundational Research Gaps and Future Directions for Digital Twins" (consensus study report, released 2023-12-15, NAP citation year 2024). Definition read on the NAP page: "A digital twin is a set of virtual information constructs that mimics the structure, context, and behavior of a natural, engineered, or social system (or system-of-systems), is dynamically updated with data from its physical twin, has a predictive capability, and informs decisions that realize value." The report also says "The bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical is central to the digital twin" and that a digital twin "goes beyond simulation to include tighter integration between models, observational data, and decisions" / "is more than just simulation and modeling."
  URL: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4 (fetched 2026-10-25)
  => Consequence for product vocabulary: a "digital twin" in the NASEM sense needs (a) a specific physical counterpart, (b) a live data feed that updates it, (c) predictive capability, (d) decision loop. A population-level model that is not individual-tracked and not updated per person is NOT a digital twin in this sense. Unlearn's usage (see Q4) is the clinical-trials "twin = prognostic generative model conditioned on baseline covariates" and does not satisfy (b)/(d) in the strict NASEM sense.

### Target trial emulation (TTE)
- [PR] Hernán MA, Robins JM. Using Big Data to Emulate a Target Trial When a Randomized Trial Is Not Available. Am J Epidemiol 2016;183(8):758-764. doi:10.1093/aje/kwv254. PMID 26994063. PMC4832051.
  URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26994063/  (verified existence via search 2026-10-25)
- [PR] TARGET statement: Cashin AG, Hansford HJ, Hernán MA, et al. Transparent reporting of observational studies emulating a target trial: the TARGET statement. BMJ 2025;390:e087179; parallel publication JAMA 2025;334(12):1084-1093, doi:10.1001/jama.2025.13350. 21-item checklist, six sections (item count and JAMA pagination come from search-result summaries, not from the full text I read; EQUATOR page confirms BMJ citation and simultaneous publication, record updated 2025-09-10).
  URLs: https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/22462/ ; https://www.rti.org/publication/transparent-reporting-of-observational-studies-emulating-a-target
  Scope per EQUATOR: "analyses of observational data that explicitly aim to estimate causal effects by emulating a target trial". Applies to parallel-group, individually randomised target trials (per search summary).

### Synthetic control METHOD (econometrics) — naming collision
- [PR] Abadie A, Diamond A, Hainmueller J. Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California's Tobacco Control Program. J Am Stat Assoc 2010;105(490):493-505 (extends Abadie & Gardeazabal 2003). It builds a weighted combination of *aggregate units* (states/countries) to approximate ONE treated aggregate unit's pre-intervention trajectory. This is unrelated to a "synthetic control arm" of patients in drug development, except by name.
  URL: https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/synthetic-control-methods-comparative-case-studies-estimating-effect-californias-0

### External control (FDA)
- [REG] FDA draft guidance "Considerations for the Design and Conduct of Externally Controlled Trials for Drug and Biological Products", February 2023, docket FDA-2022-D-2983. FDA page (fetched 2026-10-25) still shows status "Draft ... Not for implementation. Contains non-binding recommendations" and no final-version notice => STILL DRAFT as of 2026-10-25 per FDA page.
  Terminology on the FDA page: "externally controlled trial" = outcomes of participants receiving the test treatment compared with outcomes of people outside the trial who did not receive it (treated or untreated); "external control arm"; "historical control" = comparison group from an earlier time; "concurrent control" = comparison group from the same time period but in a different setting. (The page does not use "synthetic control".)
  URL: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-design-and-conduct-externally-controlled-trials-drug-and-biological-products
  Search summary (secondary): the draft says the likelihood of credibly demonstrating effectiveness with an external control is low and sponsors should choose a more suitable design when feasible; flags design-stage bias (estimands, aligning eligibility criteria, covariate selection, immortal-time bias, index-date selection). Source: https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2023/04/fdas-draft-guidance-on-externally-controlled-trials-answers-some-questions-leaves-others-unanswered/ (WEB, not read in full).


### In silico trials / virtual patient / virtual cohort (reviews)
- [PR] Viceconti M, Pappalardo F, Rodriguez B, Horner M, Bischoff J, Musuamba Tshinanu F. In silico trials: Verification, validation and uncertainty quantification of predictive models used in the regulatory evaluation of biomedical products. Methods 2021;185:120-127. doi:10.1016/j.ymeth.2020.01.011 (PMC7883933). Read 2026-10-25: "in silico trials" = "the use of modelling and simulation in both the preclinical and clinical evaluation" of a new medical product; the paper uses "digital trials" for hypothetical individuals "sampled from the joint distribution of a representative population" and "digital twin" for a model that "can represent each physical individual" (it does NOT use the words "virtual cohort"/"virtual patient"). Credibility follows ASME V&V 40: question of interest -> context of use ("the specific role and scope of the model in addressing the question of interest") -> model risk = combination of model influence and decision consequence.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7883933
- [PR] Balmus M, Wang S-Y, Ashton EWG, Barrows RK, Monks M, Ge Y, Lefebvre A, Niederer SA. In Silico Clinical Trials in Drug Development: Virtual Patients, Applications, and Regulatory Convergence. doi:10.1002/cpt.70492 (ISSN 0009-9236 = Clin Pharmacol Ther; the CiteDrive page gave no year - the corrected citation is Clin Pharmacol Ther 2026;120(5):1181-1194, see Supplementary). Abstract (CiteDrive copy): proposes a taxonomy of virtual patients and in silico trials "spanning levels of personalization and levels of abstraction", "from fully synthetic populations through hybrid cohorts to patient-specific digital twins"; maturity "varies widely" by organ system (cardiac, hepatic most established per a second summary); concludes personalised modelling and regulatory pathways "often do not converge within a unified framework"; wider adoption needs validation against experimental and clinical evidence. Could not open the full text; volume, issue and pages were verified later through Europe PMC (see Supplementary).
  URL: https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/in-silico-clinical-trials-in-drug-development-virtual-patients-applications-and-regulatory-convergence/
- [PR] Kleeberger JA. Virtual Patients in Clinical Trials for Drug Development: A Narrative Review. Cureus 2025;17(6):e85380. doi:10.7759/cureus.85380. Single-author, single-reviewer narrative review (weak evidence grade). Virtual patient = "computer-generated models that simulate the clinical characteristics of real patients"; digital twin = "virtual replicas of real patients, created using data from actual clinical records", continuously updated; digital twins treated as ONE of three ways to generate virtual patients (with statistical inference and random parameter sampling). Says explicitly: "there is a lack of standardized protocols for generating and utilizing virtual patient cohorts" and "no universally accepted method for generating virtual patient cohorts". Explicitly sets aside decentralised/remote "virtual clinical trials" as a different meaning of the term. Does NOT cite ASME V&V 40.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228009/
- [REG] FDA VICTRE (Badano A et al. Evaluation of Digital Breast Tomosynthesis as Replacement of Full-Field Digital Mammography Using an In Silico Imaging Trial. JAMA Netw Open 2018;1(7):e185474). 2,986 in silico patients (simulated breast models), computational reader, delta-AUC endpoint sized to SE 0.01 (half the uncertainty of the human comparative trial); direction agreed with the human comparative trial; no IRB because no human data. This is a *device/imaging* in silico trial with physics-based simulation - not a drug-trial analogue, and the generalisability question was left open by the authors. I did not open the JAMA article; figures are from FDA / NIBIB / TCIA pages in the search result.
  URL: https://www.fda.gov/science-research/about-science-research-fda/victre-trial-silico-replica-clinical-trial-evaluating-digital-breast-tomosynthesis-replacement-full
- [REG] FDA final guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" (Nov 2023): applies to physics-based/mechanistic models, NOT stand-alone ML/AI models; builds on ASME V&V 40-2018 (question of interest -> context of use -> model risk). Secondary sources only (FDA page not opened): https://medtechintelligence.com/news_article/fda-publishes-final-guidance-on-assessing-credibility-of-cms-in-device-submissions/amp
  => Important for the reset: the regulatory credibility ritual for "virtual patients" is a DEVICE + mechanistic-model tradition; for drugs the analogue is model-informed drug development (MIDD) / ICH M15 (see Q6).

### Synthetic / external control arms - authoritative wording
- [PR] Thorlund K, Dron L, Park JJH, Mills EJ. Synthetic and external controls in clinical trials - a primer for researchers. Clin Epidemiol 2020;12:457-467. doi:10.2147/CLEP.S242097 (PMC7218288). Read 2026-10-25: "Synthetic controls are defined as cohorts of patients from external data" adjusted with statistical methods; also the stand-alone use "to create a stand-alone control group solely from external data" and the augmentation use "external data can be used to augment the precision of a concurrent control group" (i.e. the hybrid/borrowing design). The paper does NOT mention Abadie's synthetic control method or the naming collision.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7218288
- [PR] Elvatun S, Knoors D, Brant S, Jonasson C, Nygård JF. Synthetic data as external control arms in scarce single-arm clinical trials. PLOS Digit Health 2025;4(1):e0000581 (Jan 23 2025). Retrospective numeric study on 645 Norwegian NSCLC patients (control chemo N=397, pembrolizumab N=248; OS). Generators CTGAN, Survival-CTGAN, DP-GAN, PATE-GAN, PrivBayes; hazard-ratio conclusions "directionally consistent" in an illustrative hybrid analysis, but KM curves diverged after ~25 months; Survival-CTGAN had a distance-to-closest-record below the hold-out (leakage risk); "Synthetic data may reduce privacy risks but does not guarantee protection". Shows the THIRD meaning of "synthetic": generated records from a generative model.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11756779/
- [VEN] Medidata "Synthetic Control Arm" is a product label for an external control arm built from de-identified patient-level data of prior clinical trials, contrasted by the vendor with RWD-based controls. - Naming collision (my synthesis; no source I found explicitly writes it down - "could not verify" that anyone has published a note on it): three unrelated things share "synthetic": (1) Abadie-Diamond-Hainmueller synthetic control METHOD = weighted donor *aggregate units* approximating one treated unit's pre-trend (econometrics; R package Synth/augsynth); (2) "synthetic control ARM" = a patient-level external control assembled from real patients (trial or RWD) and analysed with weighting/matching/borrowing; (3) "synthetic data"/"synthetic patients" = records sampled from a fitted generative model (GAN/CTGAN/Bayesian network). ### TrialGPT (NIH/NLM; Jin Q, ... Lu Z)

- [PP] Morrison BA, Sushil M, Young JS (UCSF). ### Failure modes (what the evidence supports and what it does not)
- Temporal reasoning: 32% of errors in the only prospective real-record error analysis I found (Massive Bio, vendor, secondary report). TrialGPT's own error analysis did not examine time at all because its notes are short synthetic summaries.
- Missing information: (a) Inato: real-world accuracy 87% vs 93% on n2c2 because records lack information; (b) Ferber: 56/75 revised human answers were "judged eligible though record lacks the information"; (c) PRISM: removing questions the experts marked N/A moved GPT-4 68%->72% and OncoLLM 63%->66%, and weakened the small open models. => "missing-as-satisfied" is a documented *human and model* behaviour; the TREC 3-class and the 'unknown' label exist for this reason.
- Negation/ambiguity: 18% of errors in the Massive Bio mix; surveys state LLMs still struggle with negation (Cohort-Discovery survey, arXiv:2506.15301, secondary). Quantified "within N days" windows: I found NO source that quantifies them separately -> "could not verify".
- Laterality/applicability: PRISM's single ineligible case among 10 re-checked "top-1" trials was bilateral breast cancer (left-eligible, right-not) - applicability scope inside a patient.
- Rationale fluency: Wornow: rationales judged coherent for 75% of incorrect decisions - do not use rationale quality as a proxy for correctness.
- Ground-truth softness: PRISM inter-annotator agreement 64-70% on real records; TrialGPT 3-physician consensus; RECTIFIER used a clinician gold standard. A "98% accuracy" claim is only as good as the label.

### Human-in-the-loop evidence (strongest first)
1. JAMA 2025 RCT (RECTIFIER): LLM draft + staff confirmation finished the screening faster (sHR 1.78), covered more patients, more enrolments (35 vs 19, sHR 1.79, 95% CI 1.02-3.15) - single centre, one trial. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514
2. Massive Bio prospective (vendor): ~120 -> ~30 min/patient with ~25% of cases triaged to human review (secondary). 
3. TrialGPT user study: 42.6% time saving, accuracy 97.2% vs 91.7% (n.s., 6 trials x 6 vignettes x 2 annotators). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183
4. Inato (vendor preprint): <9 min per patient review.

## Q4. Patient-level forecasting, digital twins, PROCOVA, "applicable models"

### PROCOVA / Unlearn - primary regulatory text read
- [REG, primary] EMA/CHMP Qualification opinion for Prognostic Covariate Adjustment (PROCOVA(TM)), adopted by CHMP 15 September 2022 (document dated 20 September 2022; draft 10 Feb 2022, public consultation 22 Mar-3 May 2022). I downloaded and read the CHMP statement:
  * Qualified use: "prognostic score adjustment" in "controlled randomised clinical trials with continuous outcomes" (Phase 2 and 3): Step 1 train/evaluate a prognostic model of the control outcome on HISTORICAL data independent of the trial; Step 2 use it to plan sample size; Step 3 analyse with a linear model (ANCOVA) adjusting for the predicted control outcome.
  * "the method can be regarded a special case of ANCOVA sharing the properties of type I error control and asymptotically unbiased estimates" and "Type I error control, unbiased effect estimation and confidence interval coverage are NOT dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model" - validity lives in the randomisation + pre-specification; only the efficiency gain depends on how good the model is.
  * Sample-size reduction is allowed "in principle ... if it can be ensured that the calculation is considering uncertainties in the assumptions made" and the final n still serves other trial purposes; the gain "should be evaluated at the stage of planning a trial, also taking the optimism due to prognostic model building into account"; a deflation factor (lambda) and sensitivity analyses are in the applicant's handbook.
  * "CHMP cannot qualify a formalised procedure for prognostic model development in Step 1" - model building is explicitly OUT of scope; "Establishing external validity of historical data is of paramount importance"; "Outcomes from historical data may not allow prediction of control arm outcomes of future trials in case of changes in the therapeutic landscape"; CHMP "cannot issue a statement about the precision of prognostic models in general".
  * Out of scope: non-linear analysis models and treatment-by-covariate interactions. Multicollinearity with extra covariates/stratification "not fully understood".
  * The applicant states the score "can utilize a prognostic score generated by any prognostic model, including mechanistic models, linear statistical models, as well as machine-learning-based methods".
  URL: https://www.ema.europa.eu/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/qualification-opinion-prognostic-covariate-adjustment-procovatm_en.pdf  (33 pages; read 2026-10-25)
- [VEN] Unlearn blog (posted 2024-01-02): CDER replied to Unlearn's letter of intent to the CDER/CBER ISTAND pilot that "PROCOVA does not appear to deviate from our guidance" - the cited guidance is "Adjusting for Covariates in Randomized Clinical Trials for Drugs and Biological Products"; FDA agreed PROCOVA is a special case of ANCOVA and did NOT take the LOI into ISTAND because existing guidance already covers it. (Company account of the FDA letter; I did not find the FDA letter itself.)  URL: https://www.unlearn.ai/blog/us-fda-comments-on-unlearns-procova-methodology
  => FDA has not issued a qualification of "digital twins" as a category (a second, non-vendor overview said so; secondary). Extension to binary / time-to-event: "could not verify" any EMA/FDA qualification or submission for 2025-26. (The arXiv 2212.09903 "PCA for binary outcomes using stratification" is a methods paper, not a regulatory step.)
- [VEN/PR] Unlearn Alzheimer's digital twin results are company-reported retrospective analyses: three phase 3 trials with J&J - control arm could be cut by 33% (ADAS-Cog11 at 18 months) or 19% (CDR-SB at 12 months); power +up to 10 points; AbbVie phase 2: 10-15% sample-size reduction (CDR-SB wk 96). A peer-reviewed simulation of TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 with digital twins (Dec 2025, PMC12740155) exists - I did not open it.  URLs: https://neurologylive.com/view/utility-digital-twin-ai-generated-models-improve-patient-selection-alzheimer-trials ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12740155/
- Clean reading for product vocabulary: Unlearn's "digital twin" = "a prediction of that patient's likely outcome on control/placebo" produced by a disease-specific generative model (CRBM-family) and used as a COVARIATE in a RANDOMISED trial. It is not a NASEM-style digital twin (no live physical counterpart, no decision loop) and it is not a replacement control arm. Its regulatory strength comes from randomisation, not from the model.

### LLM / foundation-model trajectory forecasters
- [PR] DT-GPT: Makarov N, et al. Large language models forecast patient health trajectories enabling digital twins. npj Digit Med 2025;8:588 (doi 10.1038/s41746-025-02004-3; 1 Oct 2025; PMC12488890). Authors list hallucination, inherited bias, context-length limit; propose trial design/interim use but NOT synthetic control arms; a patent covers LLM forecasting in trials (competing interest). ## Proposed product taxonomy (my synthesis from the sources above; the three-question rule is mine)

Place any object with three questions: (1) Are the people real or generated? (2) Is the outcome observed or modelled? (3) Is the claim about one person, a population, or a design?

Most-confused pairs, in order of damage to a product:
1. "Synthetic control arm" vs "synthetic data" vs Abadie "synthetic control method" - one phrase, three unrelated objects (real external patients / generated records / aggregate units). FDA avoids "synthetic" and says "external control". Vendors use "synthetic control arm" for real-patient external controls (Medidata).
2. "Matching" x3 - patient-to-trial matching (screening), propensity matching (MatchIt) and MAIC. All three appear in this module.
3. Virtual patient vs digital twin - NASEM needs a live bidirectional link to one real counterpart; Kleeberger treats twins as one way to make virtual patients; Balmus puts twins at the end of a personalization spectrum; Unlearn's "twin" is a prediction used as a covariate.
4. Virtual trial (simulation) vs virtual/decentralised trial (conduct). Kleeberger excludes the latter explicitly.
5. External control vs target trial emulation vs in silico trial - real vs generated controls and observational vs simulated outcomes.
6. Clinical trial simulation vs in silico trial - the first evaluates designs (operating characteristics), the second claims drug effects through a mechanistic model.

### FDA draft on externally controlled trials - primary text (PDF downloaded and read 2026-10-25, February 2023, CDER/CBER/OCE)
- Scope sentence: it "focuses on the use of patient-level data from other clinical trials or from real-world data (RWD) sources, such as registries as well as electronic health records (EHRs) and medical claims"; it "does not address other types of external controls, such as using summary-level estimates instead of patient-level data", and "does not discuss considerations for using external control data to supplement a control arm in a traditional randomized controlled clinical trial" - i.e. hybrid/augmented-RCT designs are OUTSIDE this draft (the January 2026 Bayesian draft is where augmentation is discussed).
- "In many situations, however, the likelihood of credibly demonstrating the effectiveness of a drug of interest with an external control is low, and sponsors should choose a more suitable design."
- "Sponsors should finalize a study protocol before initiating the externally controlled trial, including selection of the external control arm and analytic approach, rather than selecting an external control arm after the completion of a single-arm trial." Elements to prespecify: data sources, eligibility criteria, exposure definitions and windows, endpoints, analysis plans, missing-data and bias handling.
- "Although unmeasured confounding, lack of blinding, and other sources of bias cannot be eliminated in externally controlled trials, an assessment of the extent of confounding and bias ... [is] critically important"; the external population "should be as comparable as possible to the treatment arm population"; the index date and follow-up must be consistent between arms.
- "Sponsors must include in their marketing applications relevant patient-level data ... for both the treatment and external control arms", and ensure FDA access to source documents/data for the external arm.

## Implications for the EviMed reset
Tags: [E] = the factual premise of the line is supported by a source above; [I] = the premise itself is my inference (check before adopting); [E/I] = both. The "what to do" part of every line is my recommendation, not a finding.

### 1. Concept boundaries
- [E] Fix four nouns in the UI and in the agent's instructions: patient-trial matching, virtual patient / virtual cohort, external control, trial simulation. Everything else is a subtype or an excluded sense (table "Proposed product taxonomy").
- [E] Retire "synthetic control" as a product word: it names three unrelated things (real external patients, generated records, Abadie's aggregate-unit method). FDA's own guidance says "external control" and never "synthetic" (0 occurrences in the Feb 2023 draft's PDF text). If a user types it, the agent should ask which one they mean and map it.
- [E] Keep "digital twin" for an object bound to one real person with a data feed and a decision loop (NASEM). Unlearn-style objects are prognostic scores used as covariates in randomised trials (EMA PROCOVA); call them that.
- [I] "Virtual trial" collides with decentralised trials (Kleeberger; FDA DCT guidance Sept 2024). If the module name 虚拟临床研究 stays, the first screen should say in one line that it simulates designs and cohorts and is not remote trial conduct; inside the product say "trial simulation".
- [E] Virtual patient = unit; virtual cohort = population object with a declared target population, covariate joint distribution (and how the correlations were obtained: published, assumed independent, or fitted to IPD), size, seed, generator id and a fidelity check against a reference (Balmus 2026 personalization axis; Kleeberger: no standard method exists).
- [E] "Matching" has three senses in this module (screening, propensity matching, MAIC) - name them differently so the agent does not slide from one to the other.
- [E] Target trial emulation and external control use real patients and observed outcomes; they are not simulations and must not live under a "simulate" verb.
- [E] Patient matching ends at candidates with per-criterion met / not met / unknown plus the quoted sentence from the record; "eligible" is a clinician's judgement (TrialGPT authors: experts stay in the loop; RECTIFIER RCT kept staff review).

### 2. What the AI must not do (each rule has a measured reason)
1. [E] Compute any statistic, sample size, power or probability. GPT-4/4o alone gave textbook sample sizes off by up to ~15% (one 26% outlier) with run-to-run drift and a tendency to under-size (Sebo & Wang 2025) - the direct LLM-alone evidence. PowerGPT (LLM plus statistical engines, preprint) reported 94.1% vs 55.4% sample-size accuracy in a randomised trial against a comparator the abstract does not specify: it supports "engine-backed tools help users", not any claim about an LLM alone. Numbers come from the R engine only.
2. [E] Choose method, estimand or assumptions silently. Agents fail on method choice even when code runs: P-Bench raw 58.3 vs strict 30.5 on hard tasks (Fisher-R1, Aug 2026); StatQA best 64.83%, errors mostly applicability. Engine must (a) run applicability checks and refuse with a reason, (b) require an explicit assumption register, (c) echo every assumption and default in each result. ICH M15 wording supports this: assumptions "identified, described, and justified, and alternatives considered"; predefined analysis plan and report.
3. [E] Invent or "imagine" patients. LLM personas reproduce means but not spread (Bisbee 2024), drift over months, and break correlations as dimensionality rises (Lin 2025); a Survival-CTGAN control arm showed leakage risk and KM curves diverging after ~25 months (Elvatun 2025). Virtual patients must be drawn by engine samplers from declared distributions; any LLM-written vignette is UI illustration only.
4. [E] Treat missing information as satisfied, or hide it. Humans do it too (Ferber: 56/75 revised physician answers), real-record accuracy drops when records are thin (Inato 87% vs 93%), and TREC's three-class scheme exists for this. "Unknown" is a counted, visible output.
5. [E] Offer a fluent rationale as evidence. Wornow 2024: clinicians rated the LLM justification coherent for 97% of correct and 75% of incorrect decisions. Every cited sentence must be verified by code as present in the record (substring), and the criterion label must not be upgraded by prose.
6. [E] Select or swap the external control after seeing outcomes. FDA draft (Feb 2023): finalise protocol, control source and analysis "before initiating" the trial, "rather than selecting an external control arm after the completion of a single-arm trial". The agent may propose candidate sources; the freeze (with timestamp) happens before any outcome contact.
7. [E] Use a published prognostic model as the counterfactual of a NON-randomised comparison. EMA qualified PROCOVA only for randomised trials (continuous outcomes) and declined to qualify model building; historical outcomes may not predict future controls after the therapeutic landscape changes; discrimination fell only slightly when Delphi-2M moved to Denmark (longitudinal AUC 0.69 -> 0.67; calibration shown only as plots), but an absolute counterfactual needs calibration, which Van Calster 2019 calls the Achilles heel of prediction models (that calibration transports worse than discrimination is general literature, not shown by Delphi-2M).
8. [E] Present forecasts as counterfactual trajectories. DT-GPT reports no calibration and tiny gains (1.3-3.4% scaled MAE); Delphi-2M authors "caution against interpreting these as causal".
9. [E] Imply regulatory acceptance. FDA externally-controlled-trials (Feb 2023), AI credibility (Jan 2025) and Bayesian methodology (Jan 2026) are all still drafts; ICH E20 is not final; EMA's PROCOVA opinion (2022) is the only digital-twin-adjacent qualification found; nothing qualifies synthetic control arms or virtual cohorts. Copy and replies should say "draft guidance" and "context of use".
10. [E] Claim an LLM-agent target-trial emulation matches an RCT. Agentic TTE exists only as 2026 preprints plus one 2026 journal paper that compares nothing with an RCT (Biomni on Mount Sinai: error vs RCT log-HR 0.567 before and 0.224 after Bayesian calibration; run-to-run variability not reported).

### 3. Which tasks need which data
- Patient-trial matching [E/I]: needs (a) the trial's registry text (public), (b) a real patient record (structured + notes) for the patient-to-trials direction or a population of records for trial-to-patients, (c) separate deterministic filters for recruitment status and geography (TrialGPT excluded them). Validation needs real records: synthetic benchmarks (TREC, SIGIR, NIH-TrialBench) test retrieval only, and benchmark numbers should not be carried over (Inato's pipeline fell from 93% on n2c2 to 87% on 485 real records; PRISM's real-note accuracy was 63-68% against TrialGPT's 87.3% on idealised notes - different systems and labels, so indicative only).
- Virtual patients / virtual cohort [E/I]: tier 1 published aggregates only -> parametric cohort whose correlations are assumed (must be labelled low-fidelity); tier 2 IPD (trial repositories such as Project Data Sphere, Vivli, YODA; or customer EHR) -> fitted joint distribution, fidelity-checkable; tier 3 mechanistic model with parameter distributions (PK/PD, QSP) -> credibility under context of use (V&V 40 / M15). No tier justifies LLM-generated rows.
- External control [E]: FDA's draft is built on PATIENT-LEVEL data for both arms; summary-level external comparisons are outside it. With only published curves the product can offer a different, weaker object: a threshold/benchmark comparison with a conservative bound (EMA single-arm paper: threshold from external information, CI limit not point estimate) or reconstructed-IPD descriptive comparison (HR reliable only when numbers at risk or events are reported). MAIC needs IPD for the index trial plus aggregate data for the comparator [standard method knowledge; not re-verified here].
- Trial simulation (virtual trial) [E/I]: no patient-level data needed; needs sourced inputs for control-arm outcome (rate, hazard, variance), effect scenarios, dropout and accrual, each traceable to a source and varied over a grid. ICH M15 and the FDA Bayesian draft expect assumptions and scenarios to be described and uncertainty propagated (ICH E20 is still a draft; I did not read its text).
- Target trial emulation [E]: needs a real database where both arms are observed; success depends on how closely the target design can be emulated (RCT-DUPLICATE r 0.93 for closely emulated trials vs ~0.53 otherwise, the latter from a secondary report).

### 4. Matching evaluation design (what to build before claiming accuracy)
- [E] Unit and labels: patient x trial x criterion with {met, not met, unknown}; pair level {eligible, excluded, insufficient information} as in TREC. Keep unknown as its own class in every metric.
- [E] Gold standard on REAL records: at least two clinicians independently, then adjudication; report agreement. Real-note agreement was only 64-70% in PRISM, so expect a noisy ceiling and say so. Keep a private test set (public sets can be memorised - Morrison 2025) and use public synthetic sets (TREC, SIGIR, NIH-TrialBench) only for regression of retrieval.
- [E/I] Metrics to publish: (1) eligible-patient recall@k (trial->patients) and eligible-trial recall@k (patient->trials); (2) false-exclusion rate = FN/(FN+TP) at pair level and NPV of "ineligible" (Morrison 2025's tabulation of the RECTIFIER paper lists NPV 78.6% for the system vs 73.0% for study staff, so the negative call is the weak one for both; second-hand figures); (3) criterion accuracy by criterion class - temporal windows, labs/measurements, medication history, negation/exclusion, laterality/stage - because temporal reasoning alone was 32% of errors in the only prospective real-record error analysis (vendor-authored); (4) unknown rate and unknown-correctness; (5) evidence validity = quote-in-record check (explanations 87.8% correct in TrialGPT, citations 86.7% correct in PRISM); (6) calibration of any score; (7) staff minutes per patient and time to decision; (8) downstream screened/enrolled counts.
- [E] Human-in-the-loop evidence standard: randomise at the screening-task level (RECTIFIER RCT design, JAMA 2025) and measure time, throughput and enrolment, not only accuracy; run a small audit sample of the AI's "ineligible" pool because staff only ever see the candidates the system surfaces.
- [I] Operating point: favour recall for eligible patients (staff review absorbs false positives), report false exclusions explicitly, and never let "unknown" fall out of the candidate list silently.
- [E] Subgroup checks (race, language, documentation density): Massive Bio reported the largest F1 gap ~7 points; the systematic review asks for bias assessment.

### 5. Cheapest credible route to "applicable models" without customer data
- [E] For trial simulation: do not wait for a disease model. Parameterise the control arm from published results - reconstruct IPD from Kaplan-Meier curves (Guyot-type; check by regenerating the published curve; HR reliable only with numbers at risk/events), fit parametric or piecewise models, derive a meta-analytic-predictive prior and its effective sample size from historical controls (RBesT), and run scenario grids for effect size, dropout and accrual. Credibility rests on the assumption register and the sensitivity grid (ICH M15, FDA Bayesian draft), not on realism of a patient-level model.
- [E/I] For variance reduction in randomised designs: a published prognostic equation can serve as the PROCOVA-style covariate if it was built on data independent of the trial and all its inputs are measured at baseline, because type I error control and unbiased estimation "are not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model" (EMA); only the efficiency gain depends on it, and EMA wants the gain planned with deflation for optimism and transport. [I] Standard ANCOVA algebra then gives the planning gain from the model's expected correlation with outcome; report it with a deflation factor and a range, not a point.
- [E/I] Operationalising a published Cox/logistic model: accept only models with full specification (all coefficients, predictor coding, intercept or baseline survival at stated times, intended population); appraise with PROBAST+AI/TRIPOD+AI; treat absolute risks as scenario inputs with wide recalibration ranges unless local data can estimate calibration-in-the-large and slope (Van Calster: ~200 events and 200 non-events for a precise flexible calibration curve; intercept and slope alone can be defensible in smaller data). Reject models that give only a risk score table without baseline. I could not quantify how many published Cox models omit baseline survival.
- [E] For external control there is no cheap route: it needs patient-level data on both arms. Cheapest credible: control-arm IPD from controlled-access repositories (Project Data Sphere, Vivli, YODA; request-based) for feasibility and balance diagnostics (not for confirmatory claims), or the customer's data. Everything else is a benchmark comparison and must be labelled as such.
- [E] Do NOT use LLM trajectory models, GAN/CTGAN synthetic arms or EHR foundation models as the "applicable model": their calibration, transport and leakage evidence is weak, and no regulator has qualified them.

### 6. Simulation and analysis methods that should be standard tools (maturity as of 2026-10-25)
- [E] Design operating characteristics: rpact 4.4.0 (2026-03), gsDesign 3.11.0 (2026-08) / gsDesign2 1.2.0 (2026-07, non-proportional hazards), simtrial 1.1.0 (2026-09) - active, Merck/RPACT-maintained; wrap the engine runs in an ADEMP structure (aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods, performance measures; Morris 2019; SimDesign can organise the Monte Carlo loop, its own ADEMP support not verified) and report Monte-Carlo standard errors.
- [E] Bayesian borrowing for hybrid designs: RBesT 1.12-0 (Novartis; MAP and robust mixture priors, ESS) as the core; psborrow2 0.0.5.1 only pinned and vendored (pre-1.0, CRAN lists the maintainer e-mail undeliverable); always report prior ESS, prior-data-conflict scenarios and type I error calibration by simulation (FDA Jan 2026 draft). Static power prior vs dynamic commensurate/mixture/elastic as scenario options.
- [E] Randomised-design planning with prognostic covariates: PROCOVA-style with deflation (EMA 2022; FDA covariate-adjustment guidance 2023 already covers it).
- [E/I] Accrual: Poisson-Gamma conjugate forecasting is closed-form (background knowledge, not sourced here); the CRAN `accrual` package (last release 2023-11) drags in a GUI stack (tcltk2, fgui) - reimplement the few lines in the engine instead.
- [E] External-control analysis: weighting/matching (WeightIt 2.1.0, MatchIt 4.8.1) with balance diagnostics (cobalt 5.0.0; one maintainer for all three), AIPW, RMST (survRM2, stable since 2022), adjusted survival curves (adjustedCurves 0.12.0), MAIC (maicplus 0.1.2 pre-1.0; Roche MAIC is GitHub-only) always with effective sample size reported (MAIC sandwich variance is unreliable at small ESS), plus sensitivity: E-value, negative-control outcomes, tipping-point. Pair with TARGET-style reporting items for any emulation.
- [E] KM reconstruction: keep Guyot-type with an explicit accuracy check; IPDfromKM has not been released since 2020 (reconstructKM 0.4.0, 2025-12, is newer). Mediana (2019) is legacy.
- [E] Reporting artefacts to produce by default: a model analysis plan (frozen before running) and a model analysis report (ICH M15), a context-of-use statement, an assumption register, scenario grid, seeds and package versions - cheap for an engine to emit, hard for an LLM to do reliably.
- [E] Label experimental, not standard: LLM- or GAN-generated virtual patients, foundation-model counterfactuals, synthetic data as an external control, agentic target-trial emulation.
