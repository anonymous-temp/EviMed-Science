Tags: [vendor claim] = a company's own statement; [independent] = regulator text, peer-reviewed paper, preprint by non-vendor authors, or trade press; "could not verify" = not established. Section marks (§) point to the detail below, where each claim carries its URL and date. About 55 searches and 60 page fetches were used (budget was about 40; late finds changed the picture). Primary documents were read for the EMA PROCOVA opinion, Anthropic's 2026-01-11 launch, Medidata Plus and NEXT New York releases, Medidata's SCA fact sheet, Nova's jinkō site and GitHub repo, the Paradigm/Ochsner release, the TrialGPT paper, the UT Southwestern preprint, and Unlearn's press hub and 2026 arXiv paper.

## 1. Bottom line

1. The direction is right and already visible at vendors. In 2026 the vendor closest to the proposed product (Nova In Silico, jinkō) runs its virtual-population and trial-simulation engine "headless" for agents and ships 24 MIT-licensed skills that install into Claude, ChatGPT and Codex (§5b). Veristat (2026-05), Cytel (2025-02) and Certara (2026-07) use the same shape: LLM agent in front, validated deterministic engine behind; Keiji AI delivers agent outputs "with sources, methods, and code" (§6, 7, 16, 23). No source replaces the engine with the model.
2. The four tasks are four different markets with different evidence burdens. Matching has the best independent evidence and the lightest regulatory scrutiny; synthetic control and trial design draw the most (§22, 25.1). One conversation can host all four only if each answer is labelled by its evidence class.
3. "Control arm" is three different methods, and regulators treat them differently: prognostic covariate adjustment inside an RCT (EMA-qualified 2022-09-15; validity comes from randomisation), external control from real historical patients (FDA 2023 draft guidance, still a draft as far as I could find), and model-predicted control for single-arm trials (Unlearn's own 2026 preprint rests on "no unmeasured confounders") (§1.2, 19).
4. Concept boundaries are supported by independent sources but vendors use the words incompatibly. NASEM reserves "digital twin" for a model of one real counterpart with a data loop; a peer-reviewed review orders the field "fully synthetic populations -> hybrid cohorts -> patient-specific digital twins"; InSilicoTrials defines a virtual patient as one model instance and a virtual cohort as a set (§20).
5. Removing recruitment operations matches where the AI part of the field stops (identify, explain per criterion, flag for human review), but the AI-native matching vendors that sell to sites bundle referral, navigation and follow-up, so this is a choice of buyer, not a market consensus (§17, 24).
6. "虚拟试验" has a real English-language collision: "virtual clinical trial" means decentralised/remote trials in operations writing and, as reported by trade press for the Danish Medicines Agency (2023), simulation without participants (§21).
7. Almost nobody publishes prices or required customer data. The few that do disclose inputs are the models to copy (§25.4, Implications).

## 2. Answers to the research questions

### (a) How the unit is named
- Digital twin = ML forecast of a real participant's outcome (Unlearn: "AI-generated forecast of one participant's clinical outcomes"; Phesi: simulated control patients assembled from comparable real cohorts) [vendor claim] (§1.1, 4).
- Digital twin = mechanistic virtual patient (Nova: "Each virtual patient is a mechanistic Digital Twin"; Certara: "virtual patient model" on a QSP platform) [vendor claim] (§5, 6).
- Virtual patient / virtual cohort = model instance / set of instances (InSilicoTrials paper; Balmus et al. taxonomy) [InSilicoTrials paper is company-authored; Balmus is independent] (§9, 20).
- Synthetic control arm = Medidata's registered name for an external control arm built from real historical patients with propensity matching or weighting; "synthetic data" (Simulants) is a separate product that generates patient-level data from the historical repository (§2.1). FDA's own term is "externally controlled trial" (§19.2).
- In silico trial / trial simulation = repeated simulation of a design: Berry FACTS, Cytel East Horizon, QuantHealth Katina, TrialPioneer SimLab (statistical or operational), Nova/Certara/InSilicoTrials (mechanistic) (§1.3, 3, 5-9).

### (b) Product unit and workflow, (c) data the customer supplies
- Unlearn: services-led; TrialPioneer relaunched 2026-01-28 as a workspace with Scout (precedent review), Hindsight (harmonised historical data), SimLab (design scenarios). Inputs named: sample size, eligibility, endpoints, comparators; no data requirements published [vendor claim] (§1.3-1.4).
- Medidata: SCA built by Medidata's research team from a database of >38,000 trials and 12 million patients, with 100+ standardised covariates; customer supplies the target trial's screening criteria and baseline characteristics (inferred from the matching description) [vendor claim] (§2.1).
- QuantHealth, Phesi: sales-led platforms; inputs not disclosed [vendor claim] (§3, 4).
- Nova: model + virtual population + protocol are versioned resources a user or agent configures (§5b). InSilicoTrials: users upload input data to model suites (§9).
- Matching vendors: patient records (EHR, notes) or questionnaires against criteria; Carebox uses questionnaires, UTSW and Massive Bio use EHR plus notes (§17, 22, 24).

### (d) Interaction: form, workbench, service, chat
- Wizard/workbench with no AI: Berry FACTS 8.0 (2025-10-23) (§8).
- Service plus workspace: Unlearn, Medidata SCA, Phesi (§1, 2, 4). No chat interface is documented for any of them.
- AI assistant inside a workbench: Cytel East Horizon R-code assistant, 2025-02-27 (prompt -> R code -> run in simulation to validate) (§7); Nova in-platform assistant (§5b).
- Agents and skills over an engine: Nova headless jinkō (§5b); Certara + NVIDIA agents, 2026-07-07 (§6); Veristat InStat, 2026-05-13 (§23); Keiji TrialMind (§16); Anthropic connectors and Agent Skills, including a sample "clinical trial protocol draft" skill, 2026-01-11 (§13).
- Chat-first general assistants: Microsoft healthcare agent orchestrator (2025-05-19; Teams chat; trials agent matches profiles to ClinicalTrials.gov; R&D-only disclaimer) (§14); Claude Science beta (2026-06-30/07-01, drug-discovery focus, no trial features named) (§26.1).
- Medidata's agentic features (Dot; Medidata Plus 2026-07-23) target study build, data transformation, de-risking and audit exploration; I found no chat interface for SCA or Simulants (§2.3).
- TriNetX launched a natural-language interface in beta on 2026-01-22: researchers ask feasibility and study-design questions, an ontology turns them into structured queries, and the answer is patient counts and site intelligence (population level, not candidate lists) (§12).
- Nothing found for OpenAI (ChatGPT Health and ChatGPT for Healthcare, January 2026, have no trial-matching feature in the sources I saw; the OpenAI link is a Massive Bio partnership, 2026-04-28) or Google (no trial-specific product) (§15, 23). Owkin's January 2026 agents and Claude connector are pathology-focused (§11).

### (e) Regulatory milestones
- EMA qualification opinion, PROCOVA: CHMP adopted 2022-09-15, dated 2022-09-20; covers Phase 2/3 RCTs with continuous outcomes; "Type I error control, unbiased effect estimation and confidence interval coverage are not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model"; CHMP "cannot qualify a formalised procedure for prognostic model development" [independent] (§19.1).
- FDA draft guidance on externally controlled trials, 2023-02, not found finalised (§19.2). FDA draft AI credibility guidance 2025-01 (context of use, risk-based). FDA-EMA joint ten "good AI practice" principles, 2026-01-14, not formal guidance (§19.3).
- FDA "supported" a hybrid Medidata SCA design for Medicenna's Phase 3 in 2020-10 [vendor claim] (§26.2); no source shows a regulator accepting a model-based single-arm digital-twin control as primary evidence (absence of evidence only).
- Certara: Simcyp "first and only EMA Qualified PBPK platform for three contexts of use" [vendor claim; contexts not named] (§6).
- Matching: Hogan Lovells (2025-01-13) expects FDA not to regulate patient matching closely because it does not affect trial design or conduct, with a device risk if software screens or diagnoses individuals for eligibility; digital-twin use in analysis draws "significant oversight" [independent legal commentary] (§25.1).

### (f) Pricing
- No public prices for any product. Models mentioned: InSilicoTrials pay-per-use SaaS (2022 paper), Medidata Plus as a paid tier without a figure, the 2024 TrialPioneer web app free, Massive Bio pre-screening free to patients, UTSW's LLM prescreening about $0.12 per patient in model cost (§25.4).

### Questions with thin or no direct evidence
- Individual-level vs population-level views: no user-research evidence found. Inference from structure: matching users act on one patient's per-criterion evidence; design and control users act on population results (power, effect, operating characteristics), and EMA's opinion makes validity independent of any individual twin's accuracy. Even Nova, which advertises per-patient mechanistic twins, shows population-level outputs in its own example (§5b, 19.1, 25.5).
- Decentralised-trial collision: confirmed for English; Chinese usage not checked (§21).

## 1. Unlearn (unlearn.ai)

### 1.1 Unit definition and naming
- Unit = "digital twin" = "AI-generated forecast of one participant's clinical outcomes" (homepage wording). Hypothetical participants are called "simulated digital twins". In a trial each enrolled participant is paired with a digital twin forecast; twins can serve as the comparator. [vendor claim] https://www.unlearn.ai/ (fetched 2026-10-25; page lists news through 2026-07-09).
- The generator is a "Digital Twin Generator" (DTG), a disease-specific ML model trained on historical patient-level data; produces a twin from baseline data only (search summary of unlearn.ai/digital-twins, CASRAI guide). [vendor claim] https://www.unlearn.ai/digital-twins (the direct fetch returned 404 on 2026-10-25; the page was reached through search results only - treat details as secondary).
- PROCOVA = "prognostic covariate adjustment": the twin's forecast of the control outcome is used as a prognostic covariate in the primary analysis of an RCT; the randomised control arm still corrects for any twin error. [vendor claim; EMA qualification is independent regulator fact, see 1.5].

### 1.2 Two distinct uses, now explicitly separated by Unlearn
This is directly relevant to EviMed's "virtual cohort" vs "synthetic control" boundary.
- Use A (RCT, smaller control arm): twin forecast as covariate in randomised analysis (PROCOVA). The randomisation protects validity. Homepage claim: re-analysis of completed Phase 3 trials -> "33% smaller control arm and 4+ months of enrollment time saved" [vendor claim]. https://www.unlearn.ai/
- Use B (single-arm trial, model-based synthetic control): arXiv 2605.12832, "Digital Twins as Synthetic Controls in Single-Arm Trials", Bertolini, Fuller, Smith, Walsh, Zhuang (Unlearn authors), submitted 2026-05-12, stat.AP. [vendor-authored preprint, not peer reviewed]. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832
  - Says single-arm trials "do not provide the gold-standard evidence of randomized controlled trials" and that outcome-model-based synthetic controls (doubly robust / AIPW estimators) are an important tool; gives power and sample-size formulas, guidance on choosing historical data, deployment under FDA draft AI guidance, and re-analyses of ALS and Huntington's data.
  - Identifiability assumptions named: consistency (SUTVA), positivity ("each treated participant has a non-zero probability of receiving control, conditional on baseline covariates"), ignorability ("There are no unmeasured confounders" - called likely the strongest assumption in single-arm settings). Historical control data must measure all confounders needed by ignorability and have sufficient covariate overlap with the trial population. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12832 (both halves of the HTML read, in two passes; second half summarised in 25.2).
  - The paper itself notes that, when covariate distributions match, AIPW reduces to the variance of "a randomized controlled trial with outcome model adjustment, as in PROCOVA" - i.e. PROCOVA is positioned as the special case inside an RCT; single-arm use is a different identification problem (observational, needs ignorability). No explicit contrast sentence between PROCOVA and single-arm use was found in either half (the text was read through a summarising fetch tool, not line by line).
  - Homepage claim for Use B: single-arm Phase 3 re-analysis "closer to the randomized result than propensity score matching (PSM)"; power on Total Functional Capacity raised from 70% to 86%. [vendor claim, not independently verified] https://www.unlearn.ai/

## 15. Patient-to-trial matching with LLMs: independent evidence

- TrialGPT (NIH/NLM; Jin, Wang, Floudas, Lu), Nature Communications 15:9074, published 2024-11-18 [independent, peer-reviewed]: three modules - Retrieval (shortlist), Matching (per criterion: explanation + relevant sentence locations + label), Ranking (aggregate criterion-level predictions to trial-level score). Criterion labels: inclusion = Included / Not included / Not enough information / Not applicable; exclusion = Excluded / Not excluded / Not enough information / Not applicable. Results: criterion-level accuracy 87.3% vs expert 88.7-90.0%; relevant-sentence location precision 90.1% / recall 87.9% / F1 88.6%; explanations judged correct 87.8%; retrieval recalls >90% of relevant trials with <6% of the collection; ranking 43.8% above the best baseline; pilot user study screening time -42.6%, accuracy 97.2% with vs 91.7% without (not statistically significant). Limits: GPT closed models; structured data, labs, imaging, longer EHR contexts not evaluated; small user study; "experts should always be in the loop". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183
  - Direct support for EviMed's design: three-state-plus labels with an explicit "not enough information", evidence sentences per criterion, and trial-level aggregation. The data caveat (synthetic patients and notes, not structured EHR) suggests the hard part is data supply, not the LLM (inference).

## 19. Regulatory milestones (primary regulator documents where possible)

### 19.1 EMA qualification opinion for PROCOVA (RCT prognostic covariate adjustment) - PRIMARY
- CHMP adopted 15 September 2022; document dated 20 September 2022 (draft agreed by SAWP 10 Feb 2022; consultation 22 Mar - 3 May 2022). [independent regulator document; I extracted the text of the PDF] https://www.ema.europa.eu/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/qualification-opinion-prognostic-covariate-adjustment-procovatm_en.pdf
- What was qualified: "a statistical methodology intended to improve the efficiency of Phase 2 and 3 clinical trials, by using trial subjects' predicted outcomes on placebo (prognostic scores) in linear covariate adjustment", for "randomized controlled clinical trials measuring continuous outcomes"; three steps: (1) train/evaluate a prognostic model on an independent historical dataset, (2) account for the score in sample-size estimation, (3) estimate the treatment effect with a linear model (ANCOVA) adjusting for the predicted control outcome.
- Key limits in the opinion (this is the regulator's own boundary between RCT adjustment and model-based controls):
  - "the method can be regarded a special case of ANCOVA sharing the properties of type I error control and asymptotically unbiased estimates"; "Type I error control, unbiased effect estimation and confidence interval coverage are not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model" - i.e. validity comes from randomisation, the model only buys precision.
  - "CHMP does not intend to single out any specific method for statistical modelling using adjustment for covariates as 'the' method"; "CHMP cannot qualify a formalised procedure for prognostic model development in Step 1"; "Establishing external validity of historical data is of paramount importance"; sample-size reductions must consider "uncertainties in the assumptions made"; an advantage over ANCOVA with a single covariate "should be justified".
  - Nothing in the opinion qualifies a single-arm or externally controlled use.
- FDA side (company account only): Unlearn reports its ISTAND letter of intent was not accepted because covariate adjustment is already covered by FDA's covariate-adjustment guidance. [vendor claim, secondary] https://www.unlearn.ai/blog/us-fda-comments-on-unlearns-procova-methodology

## 20. Independent definitions of the unit (digital twin / virtual patient / virtual cohort / in silico trial)

- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM), "Foundational Research Gaps and Future Directions for Digital Twins" (release date 2023-12-15 per a secondary source; the National Academies web edition is dated 2024): a digital twin is "a set of virtual information constructs that mimics the structure, context, and behavior of a natural, engineered, or social system", "is dynamically updated with data from its physical twin, has a predictive capability, and informs decisions". "The bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical is central to the digital twin." "A digital twin is more than just simulation and modeling." The twin is "tailored to a particular physical counterpart". "Digital twins are not yet in practical use for decision-making in the biomedical space." "A digital twin without serious considerations of VVUQ [verification, validation, uncertainty quantification] is not trustworthy." [independent] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4 (first 100,000 of 128,575 characters read; the text read did not mention virtual populations).
  - Implication: a "digital twin" strictly requires a specific real counterpart and a data loop. A virtual patient drawn from a population model is NOT a digital twin by this definition. Unlearn's "digital twin" (a forecast for each real enrolled participant, updated from baseline data, no feedback loop) is a looser use.
- Balmus, Wang, Ashton, Barrows, Monks, Ge, Lefebvre, Niederer, "In Silico Clinical Trials in Drug Development: Virtual Patients, Applications, and Regulatory Convergence", Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics (DOI 10.1002/cpt.70492; year not given on the page I read, 2025 per search result) [independent, peer-reviewed review]: proposes a taxonomy by *level of personalization* - "fully synthetic populations" -> "hybrid cohorts" -> "patient-specific digital twins" - and by level of abstraction ("compartment-based" -> "whole-organ anatomically accurate"); in silico trials = "computational models of patient physiology to simulate the effect of interventions across a virtual cohort"; concludes personalised modelling and regulatory pathways "often do not converge within a unified framework"; regulation moving "towards risk-informed credibility assessment". https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/in-silico-clinical-trials-in-drug-development-virtual-patients-applications-and-regulatory-convergence/
- Kleeberger, "Virtual Patients in Clinical Trials for Drug Development: A Narrative Review", Cureus 17(6):e85380, 2025-06-04 [independent but single-author, low-tier journal]: virtual patient = "computer-generated simulations that mimic the clinical characteristics of real patients"; digital twin = "virtual replicas of real patients, created using data from actual clinical records" continuously updated; cohort = "groups of virtual patients"; three generation routes: (1) digital twins of specific real individuals, (2) statistical inference from existing datasets (resampling, ODEs), (3) random parameter sampling checked for plausibility (Monte Carlo); regulators "are still developing guidelines"; limitations: data quality, "lack of standardized protocols", black-box, privacy. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228009/
- Pappalardo/Viceconti (Briefings in Bioinformatics 2019, via search summary only): in silico clinical trials = patient-specific models forming *virtual cohorts*. Badano et al. (arXiv 2023): classification of "digital human models" and "digital cohorts" for in silico imaging trials. [not opened; secondary summary]
- InSilicoTrials (company-authored, Ann Biomed Eng 2022): virtual patient = one model instance; virtual cohort = set of such instances with different parameter values (see section 9).
- Nova In Silico (vendor): "Each virtual patient is a mechanistic Digital Twin" - the opposite pole from NASEM (no real counterpart needed).

### Reading across the sources
- The field has no single usage. A defensible, source-backed split for EviMed's "one workspace, two concepts":
  - *virtual cohort / virtual population* = a set of parameterised or sampled units with a stated generation route (resampling, Monte Carlo, mechanistic model) - a population-level object (Balmus: "fully synthetic populations"; Kleeberger routes 2-3; InSilicoTrials "virtual cohort").
  - *virtual patient* = one unit of that cohort, or a patient-specific instance (Balmus "hybrid cohorts" / "patient-specific digital twins"); if tied to a real person and updated with data it becomes a *digital twin* in the NASEM sense.
  - *synthetic/external control* = real historical patients (Medidata SCA, Phesi) or model-predicted outcomes (Unlearn single-arm DTG) used as comparator; the evidence status differs from RCT covariate adjustment (EMA/PROCOVA) - see section 19.
  - *virtual trial / in silico trial / trial simulation* = repeated simulation of a design over a virtual cohort (Berry FACTS, Cytel East, Katina, Unlearn SimLab, Nova).

## 22. Independent evidence on matching and on synthetic/virtual controls (quality of evidence, not vendor claims)

### 22.1 Prospective human-in-the-loop LLM prescreening (the closest real deployment to EviMed's matching task)
- Dohopolski et al. (UT Southwestern), "Implementation of Human-in-the-Loop ChatGPT-based Patient Screening Across Multiple Diverse Clinical Trials", medRxiv, posted 2026-03-27 (DOI 10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890) [independent; preprint, not peer reviewed; single academic centre]. Prospective RAG pipeline run 2024-10 to 2025-09: 39,182 patients prescreened across 26 studies (21 oncology, 5 non-oncology) and 112 distinct criteria, using structured EHR data and free-text notes. Each criterion is labelled "met, likely met, likely not met, not met, uncertain, or no documentation found" with a rationale. Coordinators receive a patient list sorted by LLM eligibility; for the 914 high-likelihood patients they reviewed every criterion (5,096) and recorded success/failure; their feedback updated 27 criterion prompts across 14 trials. Aggregated criterion-level accuracy 0.94 (95% CI 0.92-0.96), sensitivity 0.98, specificity 0.81 (CI 0.71-0.88), PPV 0.95, NPV 0.93; cost about $0.12 per patient; patients with >=80% criteria met/likely met were reviewed 93.7% of the time and 43.5% became prescreen successes (vs 19.1% at 60-80%). https://www.medrxiv.org/node/1184169
  - Read-across: (1) an explicit "uncertain" and "no documentation found" state is needed; (2) the product ends at a ranked list + per-criterion evidence that a coordinator confirms; (3) specificity (false positives) is the weak side, so show evidence, not a score; (4) the human outcome (success/failure) became a feedback signal to improve criteria prompts - a loop that does not require recruitment-operations software.
- TrialGPT (Nature Communications 2024-11-18) numbers in section 15.

### 22.2 Digital twins as controls: validity warnings
- Fernandes, Porcher, Tran, Petit, "Evaluating virtual-control-augmented trials for reproducing treatment effect from original RCTs", arXiv 2507.16048, submitted 2025-07-21 (stat.ME/stat.AP) [independent preprint]: completing a control arm with CTGAN/TVAE synthetic patients trained on the recruited controls distorted the effect estimate in IST (original risk difference -0.012, SE 0.014; completed after 10% recruitment 0.004; after 50% recruitment -0.021); authors call it the "first empirical demonstration of the risk of errors and misleading conclusions" from virtual controls generated solely from trial data. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16048
- Alsumidaie, "Digital Twins Are Rewriting Clinical Trial Math. The Validation Problem Could Erase the Gains.", The Clinical Trial Vanguard, 2026-07-13 [independent opinion]: twins have "moved faster than the epistemology required to trust it"; validating modules does not validate the integrated twin; context of use is "the load-bearing wall"; says FDA has not issued a PROCOVA-style qualification opinion; EMA's 2022 PROCOVA opinion is "the closest thing to a regulatory benchmark" with narrow scope (doubts for oncology, rare disease, survival endpoints). https://www.clinicaltrialvanguard.com/opinion/digital-twins-are-rewriting-clinical-trial-math-the-validation-problem-could-erase-the-gains/

## Implications for the EviMed reset

Evidence base: sections 1-26 above. Inference is marked "(inference)"; everything else is tied to a section.

### The four-task scope
- The four tasks are four different market categories with different evidence burdens, not one category. Matching (task 1): crowded commercial field (Tempus/Deep 6, Carebox, Massive Bio, Paradigm; TriNetX for population-level feasibility counts; sections 12, 17, 24), the best independent evidence (TrialGPT 2024; UT Southwestern prospective 2026; section 22.1) and the lightest regulatory band (Hogan Lovells 2025-01-13; section 25.1). Virtual cohort / virtual patient (task 2): a modelling primitive that vendors sell as the input to tasks 3 and 4 (Nova, InSilicoTrials, Certara; sections 5, 5b, 6, 9). Synthetic/external control (task 3): the most scrutinised (sections 1, 19, 22.2). Virtual trial (task 4): a mature category with three different meanings (section 8 and below).
- Task 3 should not be one method. Three evidence classes are visibly different in the sources: (a) prognostic covariate adjustment inside an RCT - EMA-qualified (CHMP 2022-09-15), validity from randomisation, "not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model" (section 19.1); (b) external control from real historical patients with matching/weighting - Medidata SCA; secondary summaries of the FDA 2023 draft guidance say the likelihood of credibly demonstrating effectiveness with an external control is low (FDA text not opened) (sections 2.1, 19.2); (c) model-predicted control for single-arm trials - Unlearn's 2026 preprint, which rests on "no unmeasured confounders" (section 1.2). A conversational answer should name which class it is producing; the same word "digital twin" or "control" covers all three in vendor copy.
- Task 4 has three sub-kinds in the market: statistical operating characteristics of a design (Berry FACTS, Cytel East Horizon, TrialPioneer SimLab; sections 1.3, 7, 8), operational simulation of burden/sites/cost (Medidata Protocol Optimization; section 2.2) and mechanistic outcome simulation on virtual populations (Nova, Certara, InSilicoTrials; sections 5b, 6, 9). Saying which one a "virtual trial" is should be part of the tool contract (inference).
- The two-concept split in task 2 is supported by independent sources: Balmus et al. order the field as fully synthetic populations -> hybrid cohorts -> patient-specific digital twins; InSilicoTrials' paper defines a virtual patient as one model instance and a virtual cohort as a set of instances with different parameters; NASEM reserves "digital twin" for a model tied to a particular real counterpart with a data loop (section 20). Vendors use the words incompatibly (Nova: every virtual patient is a "mechanistic Digital Twin"; Unlearn: a forecast for a real enrolled participant). Recommendation (inference): virtual cohort = population-level object with a recorded generation route (resampled, sampled, mechanistic, real-derived); virtual patient = one member; avoid "digital twin" or define it narrowly.
- Independent warnings concentrate on tasks 3 and 4: control arms completed with CTGAN/TVAE patients distorted effect estimates (arXiv 2025-07-21), "validation problem" commentary (2026-07-13), NASEM "not yet in practical use for decision-making" in biomedicine (section 22.2, 20). A product that can say "cannot conclude, and why" matches these warnings (this also fits the owner's rule to decline a specific calculation with its reason).
- Breadth versus depth (inference): the sources show each incumbent wins on one data asset (Medidata's 38,000-trial database, Unlearn's disease-specific generators, Tempus/Deep 6 EMR connections, TriNetX's network). I found no source in which a vendor combines all four tasks in one conversational workspace; the closest, Nova, is mechanistic-only. That is an opening, but also means the customer-supplied data is the product's binding constraint.

### Removing recruitment operations
- Consistent with how the independent evidence and several vendors draw the line: Paradigm's platform "continuously evaluate[s] a patient's medical record against ... eligibility criteria", flags not-yet-eligible patients and lets staff review the most likely candidates; coordinators keep conversations and enrolment (section 17.3). TrialGPT and the UTSW deployment end at per-criterion evidence plus coordinator review (22.1). Microsoft's trials agent ends at "eligible trials" with an R&D-only disclaimer (14). Anthropic's January 2026 healthcare/life-sciences release has no matching or recruitment function (13). Hogan Lovells: matching is lightly regulated because it "does not affect the design or conduct" of the trial, while software that screens individuals for eligibility can approach device territory (25.1) - contact and referral add consent/privacy exposure that a research-evidence product avoids (inference).
- Counter-evidence to state plainly: the AI-native matching vendors that sell to sites and health systems bundle the funnel - Massive Bio ("matching is only the beginning"; referral orchestration, site activation, navigation, follow-up; section 24), Carebox (referral management, CRM add-on, navigators; 17.2), Formation Bio's Muse (recruitment materials; 18). So removal is a decision not to serve that buyer, not a market consensus. It is defensible if the product is for researchers who need the evidence (inference).
- Keep one boundary object: the candidate list with per-criterion status, evidence quotes, and explicit "uncertain / no documentation found" states (UTSW uses six labels; TrialGPT four). The UTSW loop in which coordinator verdicts tune criterion prompts is a quality feedback loop, not a CRM; nothing in the sources says contact status, referral state or enrolment funnel counts are needed to get that benefit (inference).

### AI-native interaction patterns worth copying
- Agent + skills + deterministic engine is where the category is going in 2025-26: Nova's headless jinkō with 24 MIT-licensed skills installable into Claude, ChatGPT and Codex (5b); Veristat InStat (intent -> specification -> validated engines; avoids "regulatory exposure associated with LLM-generated analysis code"; 23); Cytel's prompt -> R code -> simulation validation (7); Certara's agents over biosimulation (6); ClinAgent "Thin MCP, Thick Skills" (23); Anthropic connectors and Agent Skills (13). None of the sources describes the new conversational layer as replacing the validated engine.
- Router skill + narrow task skills (Nova's `jinko` router, `jinko-vpop`, `jinko-protocol`, `jinko-trial`, `jinko-trial-viz`, `jinko-task-trial-data-scoping`) and small verb-named tools over a cohort object (Owkin's connector: list_cohorts, filter_slides, survival_analysis; Anthropic's ClinicalTrials.gov connector: search_trials, get_trial_details, search_by_eligibility, analyze_endpoints).
- Versioned immutable resources, pre-run diagnostics (units, missing variables, circular references), reproducibility "from referenced inputs" (Nova), outputs "with sources, methods, and code" (Keiji), figures with code and environment (Claude Science, 2026-06-30/07-01; secondary coverage).
- Humans own assumptions: Nova ("experts control assumptions"), Certara ("scientists at the centre"), Veristat (expert review of every output). Measured reason: ClinAgent's LLM-generated specifications were only 72.1% accurate overall (0% on one dataset), so "generated specifications need expert review" (23).
- Per-criterion status with evidence sentences, an aggregated rank, and a list ordered for human review (TrialGPT; UTSW: patients with >=80% of criteria met/likely met were reviewed 93.7% of the time). "LLM extracts, code decides" (Massive Bio study: extracted features must pass a knowledge-graph check before any recommendation; vendor-affiliated).
- Conversation on operations and counts, not yet on control-arm or design statistics: TriNetX has a natural-language interface (beta, 2026-01-22) that returns patient counts and site intelligence (12); Medidata's agentic features (Medidata Plus, 2026-07-23) target study build, data transformation, de-risking and audit exploration, and the 2026-03-10 release pairs the companion Dot with 'virtual workspaces' to simulate trial performance, site strategy and cost; I found no description of a chat interface for its SCA, Simulants or Protocol Optimization (2.3). Unlearn's 2026 workspace is described without any chat interface (1.3). So a chat-first control-arm and design-simulation experience would be unusual among incumbents, with Nova's agent-operated simulation the nearest exception (inference), which cuts both ways: opportunity, and no vendor precedent for how regulators view chat-driven control-arm analysis.
- Natural language in, ontology-mapped structured query out, aggregate numbers back (TriNetX's beta conversational interface over a federated network, 2026-01-22; section 12) is a pattern for the trial-to-patients direction at cohort level; the releases do not spell out the LLM/engine split (inference).
- Not evidenced: that users prefer chat to forms for these tasks. The pattern is vendor direction, not measured user outcomes; no head-to-head study was found.

### Naming risks
- "虚拟试验 / 虚拟临床研究" vs English "virtual (clinical) trial": the English term is used for decentralised/remote trials (Trials@Home, Drug Discovery Today 2023; vendor glossaries) and, in regulatory-science writing, for simulation without participants (Danish Medicines Agency, reported 2023-03-13) (section 21). Both readings are live; the decentralised reading dominates operations vocabulary. More precise phrases in the sources: "in silico clinical trial" (Balmus, Nova, InSilicoTrials) and "trial simulation" (TrialPioneer SimLab, Katina). I did not check Chinese-language usage.
- "Digital twin": contested (NASEM strict, vendors loose; a BioPhorum survey cited by the Vanguard found 27% of surveyed biomanufacturers had no company-level definition - biomanufacturing context, not clinical). Use only with a definition, or not as a umbrella.
- "Synthetic control arm": Medidata carries it as a registered mark (SCA with the registered sign on its fact sheet); the FDA's own term is "externally controlled trial"; the academic phrase "virtual control" also appears (arXiv 2025-07). Prefer the regulator's generic term and name the method class (section 25.3).
- "Synthetic data" (Medidata Simulants, generated from a historical repository, non-regulatory design use) versus "synthetic control" (real external patients) are kept apart by Medidata; EviMed's "virtual patients" could be misread as either unless generation route is stated.
- Competitor names to avoid colliding with: TrialPioneer, TwinRCT, Simulants, Katina, jinkō, TrialMind, Dassault "Virtual Twins".

### Data-requirement disclosure patterns
- Most vendors do not publish what the customer must supply (Unlearn, Medidata, QuantHealth, Phesi: none found; TrialPioneer launch text lists design choices only). The better disclosures are worth copying: Unlearn's paper (historical control data must measure all confounders and overlap with the trial population; training data ideally cover the same populations; section 1.2); Keiji ("Deployed inside your cloud, on the data you already license"; "Raw records do not leave your environment"; section 16); InSilicoTrials (users upload their own input data; no direct solver access; section 9); Carebox (condition-based questionnaires; section 17.2); Massive Bio's form (consent "only to release your medical records" and not to trial participation; section 15).
- Disclose what was not evaluated: TrialGPT lists structured data, lab values, imaging and longer EHR contexts as untested; the UTSW preprint lists a single centre and unreviewed low-ranked patients (22.1, 15).
- Show missing-data behaviour as a first-class output: UTSW's "uncertain" and "no documentation found" labels; Massive Bio's "flagged rather than inferred". Same for tasks 3-4: name the assumption that cannot be checked from the data (ignorability, transportability, covariate overlap) and the step that was therefore declined (inference, aligned with the owner rule not to fabricate missing data).
- Separate three input classes per task: patient-level records (matching), aggregate design parameters and priors (trial simulation), historical patient-level control data (external control). They carry different privacy and provenance obligations; the sources show vendors handling them differently (in-customer-cloud for Keiji, service-built from a proprietary database for Medidata SCA).
- Pricing disclosure is absent across the field (section 25.4); no precedent to copy except pay-per-use (InSilicoTrials) and per-patient model cost (UTSW, $0.12).

### What would change these conclusions
- If the product's buyer is sites/health systems rather than researchers, the Massive Bio and Carebox evidence says the funnel is where the money is.
- If FDA finalises its external-controls guidance or FDA/EMA publish digital-twin-specific guidance, the task-3 evidence classes above should be re-read; as of 2026-10-25 I found the 2023 FDA draft still a draft and only joint "principles" (2026-01-14), not guidance (section 19).
- If a user-research study of individual-level versus population-level views exists, it did not surface: the individual-versus-population question is open (25.5).
