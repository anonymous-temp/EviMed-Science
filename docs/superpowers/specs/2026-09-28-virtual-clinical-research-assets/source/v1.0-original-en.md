# EviMed Virtual Clinical Research Platform

Product proposal v1.0 | 2026-09-28 | Status: proposed, for product review

This document is a product and capability specification, not a claim that the
features have been implemented, clinically validated, or accepted by a regulator.
It incorporates the user's decisions from the current discussion. All numerical
delivery estimates below are planning assumptions, not measured project results.

## 1. Product decision and scope

Build a general-purpose virtual clinical research platform within the hosted
EviMed SaaS. Give it a first-level navigation entry above GEO. The primary
product consists of four equally prominent research workspaces:

1. Virtual Cohorts.
2. Virtual Patients.
3. Synthetic Controls.
4. Virtual Trials.

Patient matching, recruitment collaboration, follow-up, evidence research, and
expert review connect these workspaces to real clinical research operations.
They support the research product rather than displacing its four core modules.

The platform is not limited to lung cancer or another indication. Generality
comes from reusable workflows, endpoint abstractions, statistical engines,
data contracts, and compatible disease knowledge/model packages. It does not
come from claiming that a single model predicts every disease or treatment.

Working product name: **EviMed Virtual Clinical Research**.
Proposed Chinese navigation label: **虚拟临研**.
Proposed Chinese full name: **EviMed 虚拟临床研究平台**.

### 1.1 User decisions carried forward

- The platform is a complete first-level product, placed above GEO.
- Virtual cohorts, virtual patients, synthetic controls, and trial simulation
  are the primary value proposition and remain functional in the initial release.
- Patient-to-trial and trial-to-patient matching are included.
- Recruitment partners have patient data that the user says is authorized for
  use. The exact covered purposes are recorded during data onboarding.
- One existing partner provides pre-enrollment records, study/recruitment
  protocols, exit dates/reasons, and post-exit routine-care/follow-up records.
  Its in-trial treatment assignment, actual treatment, and outcomes remain
  unavailable. This is a source-specific visibility policy, not a permanent
  limitation applied to every future customer or dataset.
- There is no authorization in this request to implement product code, deploy,
  contact patients, or access additional private datasets.

### 1.2 Product promises

- Turn a study question into a reproducible population, model, comparator,
  simulation, and reviewable evidence package.
- Show which conclusions come from observations, which from fitted models,
  and which depend on analyst-specified assumptions.
- Let teams compare scenarios and revisit decisions without losing provenance.
- Connect an approved operational study version to eligible candidate review
  and authorized recruitment workflows.
- Support multiple indications through explicit compatibility and validation
  information for each model and knowledge package.

Clinical validity is assessed for a context of use: population, intervention or
comparator, endpoint, horizon, data setting, and intended decision. Product
availability is not a blanket clinical or regulatory qualification. This
approach is informed by [ICH M15 as adopted by FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/m15-general-principles-model-informed-drug-development).

### 1.3 Product options considered

| Option | Benefit | Limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared research platform with versioned model and knowledge packages | Reuses workflows across indications while making model scope explicit | Requires a model registry and clear compatibility contracts | Recommended |
| Independently built platform for each disease | Deep local specialization | Repeats data, workflow, permission, and simulation infrastructure | Use disease packages inside the shared platform instead |
| General LLM generating complete patient histories and outcomes | Fast demonstration | Does not establish outcome distributions, causal identification, or clinical validity | LLMs assist interpretation; numerical results come from explicit engines |

## 3. Product vocabulary and scientific boundaries

| Object | Product definition | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Observed cohort | Real people selected from authorized source data using a versioned definition | Cohort membership does not establish an unbiased causal comparison |
| Synthetic cohort | Generated records sampled from an explicit distribution or model | Generated record count is not additional independent clinical evidence |
| Virtual patient | A structured simulated research object with a baseline, scenario, model, and possible trajectories | A narrative profile alone is not a validated disease model |
| Patient-derived virtual representation | A virtual object conditioned on authorized baseline data for an observed person | Predicted counterfactuals are not observed individual outcomes |
| External control | Observed people outside the target trial used for a defined comparison | Treatment, time zero, outcomes, and confounding require assessment |
| Model-based comparator | Predictions under a specified comparator scenario | Model and transport assumptions are part of the result |
| Hybrid control | Current observed control information combined with external information | Borrowing and conflict behavior must be specified and validated |
| Prognostic adjustment | A pre-specified prognostic score used in a randomized trial analysis | It is an efficiency method and is not automatically a replacement control arm |
| Virtual trial | A simulation of the sampling, treatment assumptions, observations, and analysis of a proposed study | A simulated trial is not a conducted human trial |
| Study readout | Availability of the actual study results required for its analysis | A participant leaving a study is not study readout |

### 3.1 Provenance is represented at the value level

Use an origin vocabulary that can describe mixed records without erasing their
differences:

- observed: recorded in an accessible source;
- extracted: transcribed or normalized from a source, with evidence location;
- calculated: deterministically derived from other values;
- imputed: statistically filled under a declared missing-data method;
- predicted: generated by a named fitted model;
- assumed: supplied as a scenario parameter;
- synthetic: sampled as a simulated record or trajectory.

Preserve the lineage between these categories. An extracted observation can
remain a real observation, but an imputed outcome cannot become observed merely
because it is displayed in the same table.

Report at least four separate quantities where relevant: observed patient
count, observed event count, weighted effective sample size, and generated
record/trajectory count. Prior effective sample size is a different quantity
again and must be named separately.

### 3.2 Intended use and review state are separate

Intended-use labels:

- exploration;
- study design support;
- specified study analysis;
- regulatory submission preparation.

Review dimensions:

- software and numerical verification;
- data fitness for the question;
- model validation for the declared scope;
- study-specific clinical/statistical review;
- any project-specific regulatory correspondence.

Do not combine these into a universal “regulatory-grade” or “approved model”
badge. A correct mathematical simulator can support scenario analysis without
being an empirically validated disease model.

## 4. Information architecture

The first-level entry opens a full platform, with the current EviMed project
selection retained as its scope. Proposed internal navigation:

    Overview
    Research
      Virtual Cohorts
      Virtual Patients
      Synthetic Controls
      Virtual Trials
    Operations
      Patient Matching
      Recruitment and Sites
      Follow-up
    Shared resources
      Data and Evidence
      Protocols and Trial Precedents

      Reports and Project Decisions

Use grouping within a secondary navigation surface, rather than putting every
submodule into EviMed's global sidebar. The four research entries are always
prominent; their status is not hidden behind recruitment metrics.

### 4.1 Overview

The top of the page presents four creation actions:

- Create a cohort.
- Create virtual patients.
- Build a comparator.
- Simulate a trial.

Below them:

- current project question and intended use;
- recent cohorts, comparator designs, and simulation scenarios;
- active jobs with progress and resumable failures;
- assumptions awaiting clinical or statistical review;
- data coverage and model compatibility;
- recent decisions and changes affecting existing outputs.

Operational tasks such as pending referrals and follow-ups appear in a
separate panel with role-sensitive visibility.

### 4.2 Common workspace pattern

Each core workspace has:

1. A setup area for the question, inputs, and method.
2. A central result or visual comparison area.
3. An evidence/assumptions panel.
4. A run and version history.
5. Actions to save a snapshot, compare variants, request review, and export.

The AI assistant can propose edits and explain results. Committed changes are
visible in structured fields. Editing a prompt does not silently change the
active study plan.

Represent loading, empty, error-with-retry, and success states. Also show
partial results, incompatible model/data, and stale results explicitly. A failed
calculation is not rendered as zero, no effect, no candidates, or completion.

## 5. Shared data and protocol foundation

### 5.1 Data onboarding

First-release input formats: CSV, Parquet, XLSX, a data dictionary, and supported
protocol/document uploads. Additional adapters can support FHIR, OMOP, trial
analysis datasets, registries, and customer APIs after mapping and validation.
Do not require customers to migrate an entire institution to a common model
before using a small authorized dataset.

The onboarding flow:

1. Register source owner, permitted purposes, recipients, and retention rules.
2. Map patient, encounter, treatment, observation, and outcome fields.
3. Establish units, codes, event dates, availability dates, and source evidence.
4. Identify accessible observation intervals and restricted fields.
5. Profile completeness, consistency, plausibility, duplicates, and join quality.
6. Review the mapping and save an immutable dataset snapshot.

Keep directly identifying/contact data in an appropriately controlled identity
service or at the partner. Research workspaces use scoped pseudonymous IDs.
Pseudonymized data is not automatically anonymous.

### 5.2 Three clocks and explicit missingness

Clinical facts can have distinct event, recording, and platform-availability
times. Historical matching or forecasting uses only information available at
the relevant decision time.

Missingness reasons include not measured, not recorded, not shared,
trial-restricted, outside observation, pending, and not applicable. Conflicting
evidence is separately represented. Unknown treatment is not no treatment.

### 5.3 Protocols and reusable definitions

Save the source protocol, amendment, effective dates, applicable sites/cohorts,
and reviewed structured representation. Represent criterion polarity,
conjunctions, alternatives, thresholds, units, temporal conditions, and
applicability.

Reuse a reviewed definition across cohort selection, matching, scenario
analysis, pre-screening questionnaires, and recruitment materials. A formal
amendment produces a new version and an impact list; it does not rewrite past
assessments.

Map the internal protocol representation to relevant
[CDISC USDM concepts](https://www.cdisc.org/ddf). This is an interoperability
direction, not a claim of full standards conformance in the first release.

### 5.4 Trial precedent library

Combine public trial registrations, primary publications, regulatory documents,
and authorized sponsor documents. Extract comparable study components with
source locations: population, intervention, comparator, endpoint, horizon,
eligibility, analysis, and published results.

Similarity retrieval identifies candidates for review. It does not make
different endpoint definitions or patient populations statistically equivalent.
Preserve original wording when normalizing.

## 6. Virtual Cohorts workspace

### 6.1 Entry paths

- Select an observed cohort from accessible patient-level records.
- Generate a synthetic baseline cohort from an observed distribution.
- Generate a parametric scenario cohort from declared distributions and
  dependency assumptions.
- Configure a target population from a protocol and explore data coverage
  before any patient-level source is available.

### 6.2 Core workflow

Define question and target population → choose sources → map variables →
apply reviewed criteria and time zero → inspect flow and missingness →
choose observed or synthetic output → validate → save a cohort snapshot.

### 6.3 Required functions

- Visual criteria builder with an AI drafting assistant.
- Patient-level lineage and deduplication within authorized scopes.
- Inclusion/exclusion flow with reasons and unknown counts.
- Baseline distributions, correlations, subgroups, observation coverage, and
  source/site/calendar-time composition.
- A/B comparisons of cohort definitions and protocol variants.
- Synthetic sampling with explicit seeds, generator version, distributions,
  dependencies, parameter uncertainty, and reproducible generation.
- Optional strata and weights, with separate observed and effective counts.
- Export of permitted records plus definitions, data dictionary, and validation.

For joint distributions, assess conditional relationships and uncommon
combinations, not just column means. A plausible demographic distribution does
not establish a plausible disease trajectory.

### 6.4 Validation and outputs

Outputs: cohort definition, membership or synthetic records, baseline profile,
selection flow, data coverage, lineage, validation results, and limitations.

Synthetic-data validation covers fidelity, declared constraints, utility for
the specified use, and disclosure/memorization risk where generated data derives
from protected records. Do not promise anonymity solely because data is synthetic.

A cohort with inadequate coverage can still produce a useful feasibility
report. It must not silently proceed to unsupported outcome estimation.

## 7. Virtual Patients workspace

### 7.1 Creation modes

1. **Archetype:** a user specifies an illustrative baseline and scenario.
2. **Cohort sample:** a generator creates virtual people from a saved population.
3. **Baseline-conditioned representation:** an applicable model conditions
   predictions on an authorized observed person's baseline.

The first two modes can operate with mathematical reference simulators.
The third requires model compatibility and appropriate data rights. The label
“digital twin” is reserved for a declared patient-conditioned modeling use;
it is not awarded merely for attaching a real patient ID to generated text.

### 7.2 Virtual patient contents

- stable virtual ID and origin;
- baseline features, provenance, and missingness;
- source cohort and optional permitted parent-baseline link;
- model/version and context of use;
- intervention/comparator scenario and parameter assumptions;
- future observations or outcome distributions supported by the model;
- prediction intervals and other correctly named uncertainty measures;
- sensitivity results and model limitations.

### 7.3 Interaction

The user can inspect one virtual patient, select representative cohort members,
compare scenarios, and inspect which changes altered a forecast. Graphs show
baseline, observed values where available, prediction horizons, and uncertainty.
Restricted or unobserved periods are visually distinct.

AI can answer “which assumptions explain this difference?” using saved results.
It cannot invent numerical trajectories or silently infer hidden study treatment.
Do not expose dose-response controls for a model that does not support and
validate those inputs.

Individual potential outcomes cannot both be directly observed. Scenario
differences remain model-based quantities under stated assumptions; validation
uses appropriate observed outcomes and population-level designs.

### 7.4 What ships without a clinical disease model

A reference simulator can generate continuous, binary, or event-time outcomes
under a declared mathematical scenario. It must be labeled as such in the UI
and exports. It demonstrates the complete research workflow and supports
assumption-driven design, but it is not advertised as a clinically validated
universal patient.

The absence of a compatible disease model leads to a clear choice: use a
declared reference scenario, select another qualified model, or create a model
development/data request. It does not lead to an LLM-generated substitute.

## 8. Synthetic Controls workspace

### 8.1 Four explicitly named routes

| Route | Inputs | What is produced |
|---|---|---|
| Observed external control | Target-study data plus accessible comparator treatment/outcome data | Matched/weighted cohort and study-specific comparison |
| Model-based comparator | Target baseline, scoped outcome model, comparator scenario | Predicted comparator distributions and uncertainty |
| Hybrid control | Current observed control plus external information and borrowing plan | A combined analysis with conflict and borrowing diagnostics |
| Randomized-trial prognostic adjustment | Actual randomized trial outcomes and pre-specified prognostic score | Adjusted randomized estimate; not a fabricated control arm |

The commercial page may be titled Synthetic Controls. The selected method and
origin of each control contribution must remain visible throughout the analysis.

### 8.2 Study setup

Record population, intervention, comparator, endpoint, time zero, follow-up,
intercurrent-event strategy, effect measure, and target estimand. ATT, ATE,
overlap-population effects, conditional hazard ratios, and marginal effects
must not be interchanged without explanation.

Distinguish protocol-specified analyses from exploratory variants. Freeze the
analysis plan before confirmatory use; retain amendments and prior data contact.

### 8.3 Comparator construction and diagnostics

- Evaluate treatment, outcome, visit, censoring, source, geography, and
  calendar-time comparability.
- Select confounders using the study question and clinical/causal reasoning.
  Do not automatically adjust for every available field or post-treatment fact.
- Support a validated subset of matching, weighting, outcome regression, and
  endpoint-specific doubly robust approaches.
- Display overlap, balance, weight distributions, exclusions, effective
  sample size, and remaining missingness.
- Evaluate missing outcomes, censoring, unmeasured confounding, source
  heterogeneity, and alternative plausible assumptions.
- For borrowing, report prior/information contribution, current-data conflict,
  borrowing limits, and operating characteristics under relevant scenarios.

Diagnostics do not prove absence of bias. Generated trajectories must never be
treated as independent additional observations that automatically narrow
confidence intervals. Shared model uncertainty and estimation uncertainty
must be handled by the validated method.

The [FDA external-control guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-design-and-conduct-externally-controlled-trials-drug-and-biological-products)
remains a draft as checked for this proposal. Its existence is not a blanket
authorization for model-only controls.

### 8.4 Result states and deliverables

Scientific results can be estimated, estimated with limitations, or not
estimable for the requested comparison. A completed feasibility assessment
can legitimately return not estimable with specific missing inputs.

Outputs: estimand and analysis plan, comparator definition, source lineage,
diagnostics, estimates with uncertainty, sensitivity analyses, execution code,
environment/model versions, expert comments, and decision memo.

The comparison workspace remains operational in the first release through
observed-data methods and explicit synthetic benchmark scenarios. Clinical
model-based comparisons and hybrid borrowing are separately enabled methods,
not implied by the availability of the page.

## 9. Virtual Trials workspace

### 9.1 Trial construction

Select saved cohort and optional patient/comparator models. Specify:

- population and eligibility;
- arms and allocation;
- endpoint and horizon;
- treatment-effect assumptions and uncertainty;
- recruitment, visit, dropout, missingness, and censoring assumptions;
- planned analysis;
- number of simulation replicates and reproducibility settings;
- cost and site-capacity inputs where relevant.

No new-drug treatment response is inferred solely from a standard-care
prognostic model. A treatment effect is either a declared scenario assumption
or an output of a separately appropriate intervention model.

### 9.2 Simulation families

| Family | Main outputs | Data basis |
|---|---|---|
| Population scenarios | Eligibility yield, composition, missing-information burden | Observed or synthetic baseline population |
| Recruitment operations | Enrollment/time distributions, queue delays, site capacity | Observed funnel history or labeled assumptions |
| Statistical study design | Power, type-I error, bias, coverage, event/sample-size tradeoffs | Explicit data-generating and analysis models |
| Disease/intervention trajectories | Scoped predictions under supported scenarios | Validated disease/intervention model or labeled research model |

Separate analytical calculations from Monte Carlo estimates. Show Monte Carlo
error or interval information for simulation-derived rates. More replicates
reduce simulation noise, not bias in the underlying disease model.

### 9.3 Scenario comparison

Save and compare A/B/C scenarios using explicit differences. Display the
tradeoffs between population representation, candidate availability, statistical
precision, operational duration, and modeled cost.

The platform does not auto-select the scenario with the most favorable
drug effect or smallest p-value. Selection objectives and expert rationale
are recorded.

Validate the null case as well as alternative effects. Support sensitivity
to model misspecification, case-mix shifts, and plausible missingness/censoring.
Complex adaptive designs, multiplicity, noninferiority, and dynamic borrowing
require their own validated analysis configurations.

### 9.4 Outputs

Protocol synopsis, scenario comparison, assumptions register, numerical
results, charts, simulation configuration, seed/replicate information,
analysis code, validation receipt, limitations, and expert decision record.

## 10. Generality through knowledge, model, and method packages

Three independently versioned resources provide generality:

| Resource | Contains | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Disease knowledge package | Terminology, phenotype definitions, endpoints, source mappings, clinical context | Predictive validity |
| Prediction/generation model package | Required inputs, supported outputs, executable model, parameters, scope, validation | Causal identification outside its declared design |
| Statistical method package | Estimator or simulator, endpoint compatibility, assumptions, numerical tests | Fitness of the customer's data |

A knowledge package can support parsing and cohort construction without an
outcome model. A statistical method can be generic across diseases while its
clinical input assumptions remain study-specific.

### 10.1 Required model card

- identity, owner/provider, immutable version, and execution interface;
- mathematical simulator, fitted predictor, generator, or mechanistic model;
- permitted purposes and data/deployment restrictions;
- population, treatment/comparator, endpoints, horizons, and input ranges;
- required features, units, missing-input behavior, and output semantics;
- training, calibration, and validation data described separately;
- independent validation, subgroup performance, calibration, and drift;
- uncertainty method and out-of-distribution behavior;
- software dependencies, resource needs, and reproducibility;
- known limitations, review history, and retirement policy.

Training a model can require longitudinal data even when inference only needs
baseline inputs. External model services may therefore support design-stage
prediction for a customer without its own complete trial outcomes. This does
not create the missing outcomes needed to validate that customer's actual trial.

### 10.2 Initial mathematical reference packages

Support three endpoint families without binding them to a disease:

| Reference package | Example configurable process | Analysis benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous | Baseline-outcome association, group difference, residual variance | Difference in means and specified regression/ANCOVA |
| Binary | Baseline risk, scenario treatment effect, covariate association | Risk difference and specified binary regression |
| Time to event | Event distribution, entry, follow-up and censoring | Survival estimates and a specified validated test/contrast |

Exact method support is explicit. First-release event-time settings are
restricted to their implemented assumptions; proportional-hazards, competing
risks, recurrent-event, longitudinal and non-proportional-hazards extensions
are not inferred from a generic survival toggle.

These packages make all four primary modules testable and usable for
assumption-driven research. Clinical prediction packages follow separate
evidence-based release decisions.

## 11. Patient matching, recruitment, and follow-up

### 11.1 Matching

Provide both patient-to-trial and trial-to-patient entry points. Save:
patient/evidence snapshot, protocol version, site/cohort status, decision time,
rule/model version, criterion-level evidence, and human adjudication.

Normalize each criterion into an eligibility requirement while preserving
original wording and inclusion/exclusion polarity. Use separate applicability
logic and states of met, not met, unknown, and deferred.

Missing or inaccessible treatment history does not satisfy a washout rule.
A known future reassessment time can create a task; it does not guarantee
future eligibility.

Separate clinical eligibility, evidence sufficiency, patient preference,
site availability, and operational progress. Overall ranking is not a treatment
benefit probability. Final eligibility is determined through the study team's
formal process, consistent with the assistive positioning described in the
[NIH TrialGPT FAQ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/trialgpt/faq/).

### 11.2 Recruitment and site collaboration

Track assignment, evidence requests, permitted contact, patient interest,
referral, site response, formal screening, enrollment, and closure reasons.
Keep screening consent, study enrollment, randomization, and first dose distinct
where those events are available.

Site records contain capabilities, active cohorts, capacity, competing studies,
required referral information, response ownership, and last verified status.
Start with partner-provided updates; an EHR integration is a separate adapter
and authorization project.

Generate recruitment strategies, questionnaires, patient explanations, and
coordinator materials from reviewed study definitions. Materials are drafts
until the applicable study review/publishing process is complete.

### 11.3 Follow-up

Support authorized routine-care observations and study-specific follow-up
where rights and integrations permit. Save observation windows, actual exposure,
outcome definitions, missingness, loss to follow-up, and source verification.

For the current restricted partner:

- preserve pre-enrollment history;
- retain only permitted in-study operational status;
- mark in-trial treatment/outcomes as inaccessible;
- record exit date and reason without converting them to progression or
  last-dose dates;
- build a new post-exit observation episode where authorized.

A post-exit cohort can answer a question about that defined population.
It does not automatically describe untreated natural history, original-trial
efficacy, or all originally enrolled people. Do not infer hidden assignments
from later notes or outcomes.

## 12. Evidence, expert review, and project memory

Represent an assumption as a versioned object with value/distribution, unit,
source, population, endpoint, date, owner, uncertainty, and affected results.

Each project preserves:

- reviewed evidence and data snapshots;
- protocol and scenario changes;
- clinical/statistical questions and their resolution;
- human corrections and reasons;
- failed or unsupported analyses;
- recruitment and follow-up feedback;
- forecast-versus-observed comparisons where legitimate outcomes exist.

Memory is scoped by organization, project, and purpose. Reuse of an approved
template is different from reuse of patient data or model training. Expert
review does not expand underlying data permissions.

Review roles include clinical, statistical, data quality, and operational
owners. The system records disagreements and resolutions; it does not silently
discard inconvenient results.

Report generation reads saved numeric results. It does not let prose generation
type or alter statistical numbers independently of those results.

## 13. Data contracts and persistent business objects

Use persistent application records for business state. Agent transcripts are
supporting provenance, not the database of record for patients, permissions,
cohorts, study plans, or referrals.

| Object | Essential content | Key relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Research project | Question, purpose, team, organization, data permissions, review owners | Contains study plans and research outputs |
| Source asset and snapshot | Owner, schema, source version, hash, rights, accessible windows, quality | Referenced by facts, cohorts, and runs |
| Clinical fact and evidence | Value, unit, origin, three clocks, missingness, source location, review | Belongs to a scoped patient record |
| Study plan version | Population, eligibility, arms, estimand, endpoints, analysis, effective scope | Consumed by matching and simulations |
| Assumption version | Value/distribution, basis, uncertainty, owner, effective context | Referenced by scenario versions |
| Cohort definition/snapshot | Selection logic, sources, membership/generation, weights, seed, coverage | Consumed by patients, controls, and trials |
| Virtual patient/trajectory | Origin, baseline, model, scenario, output distribution, lineage | Belongs to a saved cohort/scenario |
| Comparator design | Method, estimand, input snapshots, adjustment/borrowing specification | Produces diagnostics and study results |
| Scenario version | Cohort, model/method pins, parameters, analysis and compute settings | Produces one or more immutable executions |
| Model/method release | Scope, executable identity, input/output contract, validation and license | Used by named executions |
| Execution and result package | Inputs, execution state, progress, cost, errors, output hashes | Links to platform run ledger and artifacts |
| Matching assessment | Patient snapshot, criterion evidence, protocol/site state, review | Feeds a distinct referral record |
| Referral/follow-up episode | Owner, task/status events, permissions, observations, reasons | Does not overwrite clinical evidence |
| Decision/review record | Author, role, alternatives, rationale, evidence, resolution, timestamp | Refers to immutable versions |
| Data-use grant | Principal, purpose, organization/project/source/field/time scope, expiry | Evaluated on every relevant access |

These are logical objects, not a requirement to create one microservice or one
database table per row.

### 13.1 Lifecycle requirements

- Draft definitions are editable.
- A saved execution snapshot is immutable.
- A change creates a new version, with downstream impact and stale-result flags.
- Numerical completion and scientific review are independent.
- A scientific review is tied to exact inputs, models, methods, and outputs.
- Revoked access prevents new use even if a cached artifact exists; retained
  records follow the applicable retention policy.
- Participant exit, source follow-up availability, and study readout are
  different events.
- Job failures, unsupported analyses, and legitimate negative findings have
  distinct representations.

Existing EviMed RunEvent and run-state vocabularies remain authoritative for
execution. New business lifecycles map to those vocabularies where appropriate;
they do not silently replace them.

### 13.2 Logical service operations

| Operation | Input contract | Output contract |
|---|---|---|
| Register/profile data | Scoped asset, dictionary, mapping and purpose | Versioned profile, coverage and issues |
| Draft/review protocol | Source protocol and effective scope | Criteria, endpoint/estimand definitions and unresolved questions |
| Build cohort | Reviewed definition and source snapshots | Membership or generated records, diagnostics and lineage |
| Generate virtual patients | Baselines, model, scenario and seed | Distributions/trajectories, uncertainty and applicability result |
| Assess/build comparator | Study plan, source snapshots and method | Feasibility, diagnostics and estimates or not-estimable result |
| Simulate trial | Frozen scenario and execution budget | Replicate summaries, operating characteristics and Monte Carlo precision |
| Match candidates | Evidence/protocol/site snapshots and as-of time | Criterion decisions, evidence and review tasks |
| Assemble deliverable | Reviewed version IDs and permitted output scope | Human-readable report and machine-readable reproducibility package |

Operations are authenticated, scoped, idempotent where applicable, and return
structured, actionable errors. Interfaces are proposals; this document does not
claim that these API operations already exist.

## 14. Roles, privacy, and source-specific visibility

Access is the intersection of organization, project, role, permitted purpose,
source, field, and observation interval. Administrative access to infrastructure
does not automatically confer access to clinical records.

| Role | Default scope | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Project owner | Project configuration and permitted summaries | Define objectives, assign owners, accept project deliverables |
| Clinical reviewer | Authorized records and medical evidence | Review definitions, conflicting clinical facts, interpretation |
| Statistician | Approved analysis data and model/method settings | Estimands, analysis plans, simulation and statistical review |
| Data steward | Rights, source mappings, data quality and permitted records | Data onboarding, linkage, corrections and retention |
| Recruitment coordinator | Assigned candidates and necessary contact information | Contact, evidence requests, referral and status updates |
| Site team | Cases specifically authorized to that site | Formal screening, eligibility confirmation and feedback |
| Model developer | Explicitly approved development/evaluation datasets | Model development and release documentation |
| Sponsor/CRO viewer | Contracted project summaries and permitted cases | Review progress and evidence |
| Auditor | Time-limited, recorded access to relevant records | Inspect traceability and process compliance |

Existing legitimate authorizations remain valid within their scope. Record
whether each grant covers matching, contact, cross-study referral, research,
model improvement, cross-customer reuse, and external-model processing.
Do not assume a new module always requires re-consent, or that one authorization
automatically covers every future purpose.

The legal and ethical basis is assessed for the deployment and study.
For China, the [Personal Information Protection Law](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm)
and applicable [research ethics rules](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/qjjys/c100016/202302/6b6e447b3edc4338856c9a652a85f44b.shtml)
inform the design. This product document is not a legal determination.

GEO and virtual research may share platform infrastructure and authorized
public evidence. Patient access and research purposes do not flow into GEO
marketing/content use simply because the entries are adjacent.

the archived Java services for this product.

### 15.2 Responsibility boundaries

| Layer | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Frontend | Structured configuration, visual results, evidence inspection, scenario comparison and review |
| Server/business services | Authorization, persistent records, snapshotting, job admission, artifact access and audit |
| AI capabilities | Protocol interpretation, variable-mapping proposals, evidence research, explanation and draft materials |
| Statistical/model workers | Reproducible estimation, generation, simulation and validation |
| Domain contracts | Declared artifact shape, lineage and deterministic output checks |

Extracted clinical facts are proposed and traceable. LLMs do not calculate final
statistics. Deterministic code evaluates reviewed structured logic and closed
schemas, not open-ended clinical prose with ever-growing regex rules.

Each new research capability follows the repository convention: manifest,
skill, contract validator, and at least three real research briefs in evals.
The exact decomposition into packages is an implementation design decision;
candidate capabilities are cohort construction, virtual-patient generation,
comparator analysis, trial simulation, and patient matching.

### 15.3 Execution contract

Before numerical execution, save the authorized input snapshot, scenario,
method/model release, environment identity, and random settings. Record outputs
only when they exist and pass the corresponding integrity checks.

Jobs support cancellation, bounded resource use, visible progress, retries and
checkpoint recovery. Resume from compatible completed work rather than
silently restarting with different data or parameters.

Caches include relevant input, method, model, prompt/schema version and access
scope. Do not return stale results because the patient ID or project title
matches. Permission changes are re-evaluated independently of cache identity.

Model worker data stays within the authorized compute environment. Agents receive
the minimum necessary inputs and summaries; provider keys and identifying
records do not enter general-purpose logs or exported research artifacts.

### 15.4 Deliverable contracts and existing gate budget

Use the existing shared delivery contract path. New deterministic checks return
specific issues the run can repair in place. Scientific infeasibility can be a
valid, complete deliverable.

Do not introduce a separate set of global blocking gates or prose keyword
walls. Repository rules require new checks to begin as notices/metrics unless
they fit an already authorized invariant and delivery boundary. Business access
control, method compatibility, and scientific result status are distinct from
the platform's delivery-blocking-point budget.

## 16. Deliverables and export policy

Every completed research execution produces an inspectable package:

| Artifact | Content |
|---|---|
| Study/analysis synopsis | Question, population, estimand, purpose and method |
| Input manifest | Dataset snapshots, visibility, model/method versions, hashes |
| Assumptions register | Values/distributions, sources, uncertainty and review |
| Cohort/control definitions | Selection/generation, time zero, weights, exclusions |
| Results | Machine-readable estimates and simulation summaries |
| Visuals | Distributions, trajectories, balance, uncertainty and scenario tradeoffs |
| Execution record | Code/configuration, environment, seeds, timestamps and compute use |
| Validation and limitations | Numerical checks, model/data fit, unresolved issues |
| Decision record | Human review, alternatives considered and intended next use |

Human-readable reports may be exported as HTML/PDF or office formats using
the existing artifact infrastructure. Machine-readable exports use suitable
CSV/Parquet/JSON structures. Actual output formats are implemented and tested
before being advertised.

The export policy evaluates audience and purpose. Patient contact identifiers
and private linkage mappings are not included in ordinary research packages.
Synthetic records preserve their synthetic origin and do not inherit a claim
of anonymization.

Reports distinguish prediction intervals, confidence intervals, credible
intervals and Monte Carlo intervals where applicable. A model-generated
distribution is not represented as an observed Kaplan-Meier curve.

## 17. Complete user journeys

### Journey A: Design a study before patient-level data is available

1. Create a general study project and choose its endpoint family.
2. Upload a draft protocol or enter a structured research question.
3. Review trial precedents and parameter sources.
4. Specify the target population, distributions, dependencies and uncertainty.
5. Generate a synthetic cohort and inspect virtual patients.
6. Define comparator and intervention scenarios.
7. Run alternative trial designs and inspect operating characteristics.
8. Export a reviewed, assumption-driven design package.

The journey is complete without asserting that a fitted disease model exists.

### Journey B: Construct an external comparison from suitable observed data

1. Register target and comparator datasets and their rights.
2. Confirm treatment, outcome, covariate and time-zero compatibility.
3. Define the estimand and pre-specify analysis.
4. Construct the control and review overlap/balance/coverage.
5. Run the validated endpoint-specific estimator and sensitivity analyses.
6. Have a clinical/statistical reviewer inspect the result and limitations.
7. Export a reproducible analysis package, including a not-estimable conclusion
   if the data do not support the requested comparison.

### Journey C: Use a scoped disease model

1. Select a model compatible with population, comparator, endpoint and horizon.
2. Review required inputs, validation and permitted uses.
3. Condition on a cohort or authorized baselines.
4. Generate trajectories with uncertainty.
5. Use them in an explicitly model-based comparator or design simulation.
6. Validate using accessible independent outcomes where possible; clearly
   separate that validation from unavailable customer outcomes.

### Journey D: Connect research design to recruitment

1. Select the approved operational protocol version.
2. Match authorized patient snapshots in both directions.
3. Resolve missing evidence and clinical interpretation.
4. Let authorized personnel contact and refer interested patients.
5. Obtain actual site feedback and enrollment events.
6. Compare recruitment predictions with observed operations.
7. Maintain permitted post-exit follow-up separately from trial readout.

## 18. Initial release and subsequent expansion

### 18.1 V1 must contain a real research workflow

All four core pages ship with executable, tested functions. “Coming soon” panels,
static demonstration numbers, and a recruitment-only release do not satisfy V1.

| Area | V1 minimum | Later expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Cohorts | Observed selection, parametric/empirical synthetic baselines, snapshots, quality and comparisons | More generators, federated queries, advanced longitudinal cohorts |
| Virtual patients | Baseline-conditioned mathematical reference scenarios; trajectories and uncertainty for supported endpoints | Independently validated disease and intervention models |
| Controls | Observed external-control construction and a documented estimator per supported endpoint; explicit model-comparator reference scenarios | Validated disease-model comparisons, hybrid borrowing and specialized randomized adjustment |
| Trials | Continuous, binary and event-time reference scenarios; fixed-design comparisons and sensitivity | Adaptive/platform designs, complex intercurrent events and advanced pharmacometric models |
| Matching | Both directions, criterion evidence, unknown/deferred states and human review | Broader terminology packages and site-system integrations |
| Operations | Referral tasks, site status, actual feedback, authorized post-exit observations | Patient interface, richer integrations and workflow automation |
| Governance | Purpose scope, snapshot lineage, reviews, budgets and exports | Additional validated deployment and submission arrangements |

For V1 control analyses, specify one validated implementation in each endpoint
family: continuous mean contrast, binary risk contrast, and an event-time
contrast such as RMST at a pre-specified horizon under its stated censoring
assumptions. Add more methods only after numerical and scientific review.

Reference scenarios are overtly labeled mathematical simulations. They are
never used as evidence that any disease-specific clinical forecast is valid.
Observed-data methods remain subject to the suitability of each study.

## 19. Validation and acceptance

### 19.1 Three distinct validation questions

1. Does the software execute the declared method correctly?
2. Is the data/model appropriate for the stated population and use?
3. Does the particular study support the intended inference?

Passing the first question does not imply the other two. No universal
“95% accurate” threshold applies across all tasks.

### 19.2 Required acceptance scenarios

| ID | Scenario | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| AC-01 | First use | Four research actions lead to real configurable workflows |
| AC-02 | No patient-level data | A declared parametric cohort can complete a reference virtual trial |
| AC-03 | Cohort provenance | Observed, synthetic, imputed and predicted values remain distinguishable |
| AC-04 | Reproduction | Saved inputs/configuration/environment reproduce within the declared tolerance |
| AC-05 | New protocol version | New assessments reference the new version; old assessments remain inspectable |
| AC-06 | Restricted in-trial period | No hidden treatment/outcome is fetched, inferred as fact, or filled by an LLM |
| AC-07 | No overlap | External-control analysis reports the limitation rather than an unsupported effect |
| AC-08 | Weighting | Raw counts, weights, event counts and effective sample size agree with computation |
| AC-09 | Generated sample expansion | More generated trajectories do not count as new observed patients or erase model uncertainty |
| AC-10 | Null simulation | Type-I error evaluation is reported with Monte Carlo uncertainty and pre-specified acceptance criteria |
| AC-11 | Alternative simulation | Power, bias and coverage match the reference process within declared precision |
| AC-12 | Event-time analysis | Time zero, events, censoring and analysis horizon follow the frozen definition |
| AC-13 | Model incompatibility | Unsupported population/endpoint/input yields an applicability issue, not fabricated forecasts |
| AC-14 | Unknown eligibility | Missing evidence does not become satisfied or definitely ineligible |
| AC-15 | Matching time travel | Information first available after the assessment date is excluded from historical replay |
| AC-16 | Source correction | A correction marks affected results stale and identifies necessary reevaluation |
| AC-17 | Cross-tenant access | Reads, jobs, caches, downloads and exports respect scoped permissions |
| AC-18 | Contact authorization | Generated messages do not contact patients without the required authorized workflow |
| AC-19 | Job failure/cancel | State, partial artifacts, costs and retry options are accurate |
| AC-20 | Report numbers | Every quantitative statement maps to stored numerical results or a cited input |
| AC-21 | Review integrity | A review is invalidated or marked superseded when its relevant version changes |
| AC-22 | Exit/readout distinction | Participant exit does not enable original-trial outcome analysis |
| AC-23 | Forecast validation | Predictions are timestamped before outcomes; changes create new versions |
| AC-24 | Qualified real-data workflow | At least three real research briefs are evaluated separately from synthetic benchmarks |

Acceptance fixtures include continuous, binary, and event-time ground-truth
simulations; missing/conflicting data; shifted populations; unavailable
observation windows; and operational failure cases. Fictional fixtures are
labeled and cannot be presented as customer evidence.

Clinical-model evaluation uses suitable independent data, with patient/study/
site/time separation according to the question. Calibration data is not reused
as independent validation. Missingness, subgroup performance and uncertainty
coverage are evaluated, not just discrimination.

Matching evaluation distinguishes:

- correctness given the information available at the time;
- eventual eligibility after formal assessment and new evidence;
- actual enrollment, which also depends on preferences and site availability.

Treat expert-corrected cases and incidents as evaluation cases. General fixes
are verified against the class of failure, not one phrase.

## 20. Metrics, pricing structure, and operational ownership

### 20.1 Product metrics

- time from question to a reviewed scenario comparison;
- share of completed research packages that another reviewer can reproduce;
- analyst/clinical review effort, including corrections and rework;
- reuse of reviewed definitions and assumptions;
- scientific limitations discovered before an unsupported analysis is delivered.

### 20.2 Scientific and operational metrics

- model calibration, bias and interval coverage by validated scope;
- comparator balance, overlap, effective information and sensitivity;
- simulation Monte Carlo precision and null/alternative behavior;
- matching recall, false exclusion, false recommendation and evidence accuracy;
- time to evidence resolution, site response, screening and actual enrollment;
- accessible follow-up coverage, outcome verification and loss to follow-up;
- resource consumption, failed-job recovery and access-control incidents.

Specify denominators, time periods, cohort versions and clustering units.
Count people and patient-study pairs separately. Generation speed, candidate
count and actual enrollment are different results.

### 20.3 Commercial structure

Offer an organization subscription with project/workspace features, a transparent
compute allowance, optional statistical/model-validation services, and separately
priced licensed data or external models where appropriate.

Use project-level service statements for complex comparator or regulatory
support work. Do not charge in a way that treats generated patient count as
new clinical evidence. No competitor deal value is used as a software price.

The product owner defines scope and adoption. A clinical lead owns medical
definitions, a statistical lead owns methods and inference, a data owner owns
source suitability, and engineering/QA own reliable implementation.
