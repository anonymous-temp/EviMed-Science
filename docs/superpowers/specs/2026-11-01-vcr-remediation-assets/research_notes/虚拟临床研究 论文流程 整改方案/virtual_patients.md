## 1. In silico trial pipelines: QSP/mechanistic virtual populations and the credibility frameworks around them

### Takeaway
The QSP virtual-population pipeline is: fix a mechanistic model, bound every parameter and output to a plausible range, over-generate "plausible patients" (PPs), then select an equally weighted virtual population (Vpop) whose output distribution matches a target clinical distribution. That step is calibration, not validation; the regulators' frameworks (FDA 2023 device CM&S guidance, ICH M15, FDA 2025 AI draft, ASME V&V 40 logic) all require the question of interest, context of use (COU) and model risk to be stated first and make the evidence required scale with that risk. None of the frameworks gives a recipe for building the virtual cohort itself.

### Cited Findings

**A. Allen, Rieger, Musante 2016 - the reference Vpop algorithm**
- Bibliographic: "Efficient Generation and Selection of Virtual Populations in Quantitative Systems Pharmacology Models", CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology 2016;5(3):140-146, DOI 10.1002/psp4.12063 (journal details from a search result, not opened) [PR]. What I READ is the 2015 bioRxiv preprint PDF (text extracted with pdftotext) [PP, FT], not the journal version: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/028548v1 . Author affiliations were not captured in what I read.
- Step 1, inputs: an ODE model (the example is the van de Pas 2012 cholesterol/lipoprotein model; the preprint adds a ratio parameter k22 so it can be compared with NHANES); lower/upper bounds for every parameter AND for model outputs (states, steady states, "dynamic behavior"); a target clinical distribution (NHANES 2011-2012 LDLc, HDLc, total cholesterol approximated as multivariate log-normal). "If bounds cannot be defined empirically, feasible ranges of parameter values can be asserted from physiological knowledge or theoretical considerations" - https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/028548.full.pdf
- Step 2, generate: start from an initial guess inside the parameter bounds and optimise a modified cost g(p) that is zero whenever an output lies inside its plausible interval [l_i, u_i] (so the optimiser is "agnostic" about where in the range it stops). A "plausible patient" is a parameter set for which every component (parameters, computed concentrations, measurable combinations) is inside its plausible range. The authors describe the simulated-annealing step as "essentially an MCMC method". They generated "~300,000 plausible patients" - same preprint.
- Step 3, select (no weights): compute an inclusion probability for each PP from the ratio of the empirical density to the density of the plausible population; draw the Vpop probabilistically so that all virtual patients are equally weighted. "Only ~2% of the plausible population was selected" (about 6,000 virtual patients - this multiplication is mine, the preprint gives only the 2%) - same preprint.
- Step 4, validation as reported: 1-D histograms of the Vpop normalised to the NHANES density "indistinguishable" from the data; means, variances and covariances match the multivariate NHANES distribution (visual predictive checks, 2-D 95% surfaces); no further goodness-of-fit gain above ~300,000 PPs (Supp. Fig. 2); parameter distributions/correlations changed little between plausible population and Vpop. Validation here is a fit to the same data that defines the target, i.e. calibration in FDA's sense (see C below).
- Limits the authors state: the method "relies on the ability to generate large 'plausible populations'", so slow models are hard; a good fit "is only possible if the plausible patients densely cover the range of the observables". Because selection is probabilistic, "any number of VPops can be re-selected to bootstrap the sensitivity of the model predictions to the choice of VPop" (the built-in way to carry Vpop uncertainty). Not reported: iterations, tolerances, exact bounds, rejected candidates, any formal GOF test.

**B. Rieger et al. 2018 - generation algorithms compared**
- "Improving the Generation and Selection of Virtual Populations in Quantitative Systems Pharmacology Models", Prog Biophys Mol Biol 2018;139:15-22 (journal data from search result). I read the bioRxiv preprint (10.1101/196089, posted 2018-01-18) with pdftotext [PP, FT]: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/196089.full.pdf
- Their stated six-step workflow: (1) implement the ODE model; (2) define lower/upper limits for each state so a steady state is "plausible"; (3) define plausible limits for every parameter; (4) optimise with one of four algorithms to find PPs; (5) collect PPs until a preset number is reached; (6) acceptance/rejection sampling on the plausible population to select VPs that match the target population statistics.
- Compared simulated annealing (SA), nested SA, Metropolis-Hastings-inspired importance sampling (MH) and a genetic algorithm (GA) on the same 9-state cholesterol model. Targets of 100-7,500 PPs per algorithm; "as the number of PPs -> 5,000+, all of the algorithms generated essentially indistinguishable GoFs"; the new algorithms were more efficient below 1,000 PPs ("Vpops generated with as few as 100 PPs could have similar fits ... as the SA method with 500+ PPs"); MH and GA were fastest by clock time; GA gave "a 10-fold improvement in computational speed ... at the cost of some lost heterogeneity". Final acceptance/rejection step still improved the MH Vpop. They added a diversity metric (normalised dot product of parameter vectors) because speed was bought with imposed correlations.
- Important scope limit visible in the text: they "only used the ODE model to simulate physiologically reasonable patients at steady state and were not concerned with the transient changes" - i.e. the benchmark is a baseline (untreated) population, not treatment response.

**C. Newer QSP evidence on what actually drives a virtual population**
- Gevertz & Wares, "Assessing the Role of Patient Generation Techniques in Virtual Clinical Trial Outcomes", medRxiv 2024 [PP, SNIP]: the prior distribution, rather than the inclusion/exclusion criteria, has the larger effect on heterogeneity of the plausible population, while the percent of treatment responders is more sensitive to the inclusion/exclusion criteria - https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.11.24308775.full.pdf . Follow-up "Assessing the Role of Model Complexity in Virtual Clinical Trial Outcomes", medRxiv Dec 2025 [PP, SNIP]: the simplest model gives a plausible population that "inadequately spans the feasible trajectory space"; accept-or-perturb inclusion was more robust to priors - https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.22.25342808v1 . (Both known only from search-engine summaries; not opened.)
- Paul et al. (Sanofi), "Building virtual patients using simulation-based inference", Frontiers in Systems Biology 2024;4, doi 10.3389/fsysb.2024.1444912 [PR, VEN (four authors Sanofi R&D), FT]: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-biology/articles/10.3389/fsysb.2024.1444912/full . A virtual patient is "an individual parameter set for the QSP model" (25 parameters, chosen by expert priority and global sensitivity analysis); each of 133 anti-TNF patients (MONARCH, n=155 in arm) was fitted individually by sequential neural posterior estimation (50 rounds x 100 simulations, lognormal prior scale 0.25), about 4 h per patient; a "nearest patient fit" variant that reuses already-fitted similar patients as priors improved on plain SBI for 82% of patients (about 34% of plain SBI posterior samples beat the reference fit vs roughly 80% for the nearest-patient variant). Validation is against the SAME clinical data used for fitting (no held-out set, no external trial), and the authors concede "a QSP model may require very different parametrizations to produce similar outputs" (non-identifiability) and "the quantitative choice of 25 parameters seems arbitrary". This is the patient-by-patient alternative to prevalence-weighted Vpop selection.

**D. Vendor pipelines (read as vendor claims)**
- InSilicoTrials platform paper, Nicolo et al., Ann Biomed Eng 2023;51(1):125-136, PMC9831955 [PR, VEN: two authors founders, five employees, FT without figure images]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9831955/ . Four unrelated use cases, no common pipeline; calibration/validation uneven:
  - QT/TdP and STrhiPS: inputs are IC50 (+Hill) per ion current and test concentrations; virtual population of 1,774 in-silico hiPSC-cardiomyocytes calibrated by a multi-objective genetic algorithm against optical action-potential/Ca2+ data (example trial used 110 cells); QT/TdP classifier built on 109 CredibleMeds drugs, cross-validation accuracy 94.5% / sensitivity 94% / specificity 95%; ranolazine misclassified as unsafe. Intended use: complement early in-vitro screening.
  - Valve radial force virtual bench test: single device model, no virtual population, no calibration, validation only qualitative against literature.
  - MS TreatSim recreation of AFFIRM (natalizumab): "Due to lack of raw trial data availability, the recreation focused on recreating the global characteristics." Control-arm relapse-free at 104 weeks 40% (95% CI 25-55) vs 41% historical (good), but natalizumab effect was overestimated: >90% relapse-free in simulation vs 67% in the historical trial. n = 80 vs 40.
  - Prostate GnRH-agonist simulator: 1,000 virtual patients per schedule, published semi-mechanistic testosterone model, no separate calibration; percent castrated simulated vs real: 99.8% vs 96.8% (3M), 99.6% vs 94.0% (4M), 98.3% vs 93.4% (6M) - systematic over-prediction.
- Nova In Silico / jinko, FLAURA2 prospective prediction [CONF, VEN, AB]: PAGE 2025 abstract (Nova Insilico authors with French clinical co-authors): extension of a published mechanistic EGFR-mutated adenocarcinoma model (Darre et al., Biomedicines 2024, 12:704) with PBPK models for cisplatin and pemetrexed; calibration data are in-vitro survivability assays and xenograft tumour-growth inhibition; "virtual twin cohorts" replicate the trial protocol using publicly available patient data (number not reported in the abstract); predictions were shared publicly before the sponsor's release (an X post dated 2023-09-08). Reported agreement: HR FLAURA2 0.62 [0.49, 0.79] vs simulated 0.602 [0.49, 0.736]; median PFS combination arm 25.5 months [24.7, NC] observed vs 25.9 [25.1, 27.1] simulated; 94-98% of bootstrapped weighted log-rank tests non-significant - https://www.page-meeting.org/Abstracts/prospective-validation-of-an-egfr-mutated-nsclc-mechanistic-computational-disease-model-predicting-the-combination-treatment-efficacy-of-phase-iii-clinical-trial/ . Company/press material (secondary, via search): 5,000 virtual patients built from the demographic profile of the 30 patients in the FLAURA2 safety run-in, model with 300+ biological variables and 600+ parameters, and an HR of 0.60 (0.50-0.70) quoted in the press release, which differs from the abstract's 0.602 [0.49, 0.736] - https://outsourcing-pharma.com/Article/2023/09/18/Novadiscovery-trial-simulation-predicts-close-phase-3-results-for-AstraZeneca . I could not find or open the "npj Systems Biology & Applications" model paper the company cites; the peer-reviewed model paper I can name is Biomedicines 2024, 12:704 (title via search only).

**E. FDA 2023 "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" [REG, FT]** (final, issued 2023-11-17; PDF read with pdftotext): https://www.fda.gov/media/154985/download
- Scope statement that matters for any "virtual cohort" product: "while the general framework is expected to be applicable to in silico clinical trials, this is an emerging methodology for which best practices are still being developed, and this guidance does not provide specific recommendations for generating virtual cohorts or executing an in silico clinical trial."
- Definition of an in silico clinical trial there: device safety/effectiveness "evaluated using a 'virtual cohort' of simulated patients with anatomical and physiological variability representing the indicated patient population"; possible uses include augmenting or reducing a real trial, improving inclusion-exclusion criteria, or investigating a safety concern where a real trial would be unethical.
- Framework (following ASME V&V 40): question of interest -> context of use -> model risk -> identify and categorise credibility evidence -> set credibility factors/goals -> adequacy assessment. Eight evidence categories: (1) code verification, (2) model calibration, (3) bench-test validation, (4) in-vivo validation (subject-level), (5) population-based validation, (6) emergent behaviour, (7) model plausibility, (8) calculation verification/UQ using COU simulations.
- Calibration is explicitly not validation: "This is not validation evidence because it is not testing of the final model against data independent of model development ... Calibration evidence is weak in comparison to validation evidence"; "ensure that data for calibration is separate or not inclusive of data used for validation"; evidence is weaker "if many parameters were calibrated".
- Category 5 (the one that applies to a Vpop): compare "the mean and standard deviation of a model output across the virtual population/cohort with the mean and standard deviation from a clinical dataset"; recommend quantifying closeness of means, variances, full distributions, and comparing demographics/anatomy/pathologies/co-morbidities of "(i) the patient data used to generate the virtual cohort; (ii) the clinical dataset used for validation; and (iii) the intended patient population". Population-level evidence is used because "none of the virtual patients correspond to any one actual patient".
- Category 8: "Direct validation of the COU simulations is not possible, because if comparator data was available for the COU there would be no need for the model" - so calculation verification and uncertainty quantification on the actual COU runs is the required substitute.
- Worked example for an in silico clinical trial: code verification (1) + bench validation of the device model (3) + in-vivo validation of the baseline patient model (4) + calculation verification (3, 4 or 8) + model plausibility of the sampled parameters (7) + population-based validation (5).

**F. ICH M15 "General principles for model-informed drug development" [REG, FT of the EMA Step 5 text]**: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/ich-m15-guideline-general-principles-model-informed-drug-development-step-5_en.pdf . ICH Step 4 on 2026-01-29 and effective dates are background in r3 (not re-verified here).
- Assessment elements: question of interest; context of use (role and scope of the model); model influence (low/medium/high, "intended weight of the model outcomes in decision-making"; "In general, when model outcomes are the sole source to support the decision, model influence should be considered as high"); consequence of wrong decision (low/medium/high); model risk = combination of the two; model risk "is key for determining the requirements for model evaluation".
- Model evaluation = verification + validation + applicability assessment. Quoted recommendations: "Key M&S assumptions should be identified, described, and justified, and alternatives considered"; "Model robustness should be assessed to characterize the dependency on data, parameters, parameterization, assumptions, and associated uncertainty (e.g., sensitivity analysis)"; "External validation with independent data is encouraged"; "Simulation methods and scenarios should be described sufficiently to enable the evaluation of their plausibility and the relevance to model applicability and should account for parameter and assumption uncertainties"; "Predefined MAPs [model analysis plans] ... are recommended"; changes "should be justified, and these should be documented in the MAR [model analysis report]". Method-specific issues named: "selection bias for model-based meta-analysis, knowledge gaps for a mechanistic model, or overfitting for an artificial intelligence/machine learning model".
- The text says the assessment framework concepts were adapted from ASME V&V 40-2018 (r3 background cites the footnote; the sentence appears in the section on COU/model risk in the file I read).

**G. FDA draft "Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products" (January 2025) [REG, draft, FT]**: https://www.fda.gov/media/184830/download
- Seven steps: (1) define the question of interest, (2) define the COU, (3) assess AI model risk (model influence x decision consequence), (4) develop a credibility assessment plan, (5) execute the plan, (6) document results and deviations, (7) determine adequacy for the COU. Life-cycle maintenance (change management, re-testing) is a special consideration; early engagement with FDA is "strongly encouraged".
- Scope: AI used "to produce information or data to support regulatory decision-making regarding safety, effectiveness, or quality for drugs"; it explicitly excludes drug discovery and operational efficiencies.
- Step 4 asks for: model description (inputs, outputs, architecture, features, parameters, rationale); development data (training and tuning, split, relevance and reliability, "fit for use"); training (metrics "provided with confidence intervals", over/under-fitting controls, "Explain any calibration of the AI model"); evaluation on test data "independent of the development data" (state how independence was achieved, e.g. "data from a different clinical trial or health care system"), applicability of test data to the COU including "data drift", agreement between prediction and observation, uncertainty of predictions, limitations/biases, code verification.
- My search of the PDF text for "simulation", "in silico" and "synthetic" found only a footnote citing the device CM&S guidance; the draft does not address virtual/synthetic patients or cohort generation by name. (Absence in a text search, not a statement by FDA.)

**H. NASEM digital-twin report [REG-equivalent consensus report]**: Foundational Research Gaps and Future Directions for Digital Twins (2023-12-15; NAP 2024), https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4 [FT of first ~100k characters]. Definition: a set of virtual information constructs that mimics a system, "dynamically updated with data from its physical twin", has predictive capability and informs decisions; "The bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical is central" (p.22). Fit for purpose (p.29). "VVUQ ... a continual process that must adapt to changes in the physical counterpart" (Conclusion 2-2, p.33); "The output of a digital twin needs to include the confidence level in its prediction" (p.31); "A digital twin without serious considerations of VVUQ is not trustworthy" (p.32); "Many researchers hold that digital twins are not yet in practical use for decision-making in the biomedical space" (p.41); medical twin "will never be completely de-identifiable" (p.36). The excerpt I read contains no population-level biomedical twin discussion.

### Inferences
- A defensible QSP/mechanistic "virtual cohort" pipeline has five separable stages with different evidence status: (i) model + bounds (knowledge, plausibility only - FDA Cat. 7); (ii) PP over-generation; (iii) Vpop selection to a target distribution (calibration - FDA Cat. 2); (iv) population-level validation on a distribution NOT used in (iii) (Cat. 5) and/or prospective or retrospective trial reproduction; (v) UQ/sensitivity on the actual COU runs (Cat. 8, ICH M15 robustness). A product that reports only (iii) is reporting calibration.
- The evidence that exists for treatment-effect prediction is thin and mostly vendor-reported: one prospective-prediction abstract (FLAURA2) and one retrospective recreation that over-predicted the effect (AFFIRM: >90% vs 67%). The honest claim supported by the literature is "model-informed projection with stated assumptions", not "predicts the trial".
- Because Vpop selection is probabilistic, the way to carry population uncertainty into the answer is to re-draw several Vpops and report the spread (Allen's own suggestion), plus a prior-sensitivity check since the prior moves heterogeneity more than the inclusion criteria (Gevertz & Wares, snippet-level).
- A "digital twin" in NASEM's sense requires a live data feed and a decision loop, so none of the clinical-trial pipelines below qualify strictly; they are patient-conditioned predictive models (see section 3).

## 2. Statistical pipelines: synthetic data from IPD, and generation from published aggregates

### Takeaway
Generative pipelines from IPD (sequential trees, GANs, CTGAN/TVAE) are evaluated by fidelity (distribution distance), utility (does an analysis give the same answer) and privacy (disclosure risk); the best-controlled trial study (Azizi/El Emam 2021) reproduced a Cox result directionally with only 61% CI overlap for one endpoint and did not evaluate privacy, and the one study that used synthetic patients to complete a control arm found large distortions of the treatment effect. Pipelines that start from published aggregates (KM reconstruction, summary-statistic generators) can reproduce marginal summaries but not covariate structure unless it is assumed, and are labelled by their authors as educational or approximate.

### Cited Findings

**A. From IPD: sequential trees (the clinical-trial specialist route)**
- Azizi Z, Zheng C, Mosquera L, Pilote L, El Emam K. "Can synthetic data be a proxy for real clinical trial data? A validation study." BMJ Open 2021;11(4):e043497 (details via search) [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8055130 . Pipeline: source trial N0147 (stage III colon cancer, NCT00079274) from Project Data Sphere; 2,686 randomised patients, 1,543 control-arm patients synthesised (source already perturbed by the sponsor, so "real" means "real and anonymised"); synthesis by sequential conditional inference trees (each variable synthesised from the previous ones), quasi-identifiers and primary/secondary endpoints included, adverse events not synthesised; utility: information-theoretic (Kullback-Leibler-type) metric for univariate distributions ("within 1%"), Goodman-Kruskal tau with CI overlap for bivariate relationships (all above 50%, one exception: race vs disease-free survival), Cox-model hazard ratios with CI overlap for multivariate. Results: obstruction HR for overall survival real 1.56 (95% CI 1.11-2.2) vs synthetic 2.03 (1.44-2.87), CI overlap 61%; disease-free survival 1.51 (1.18-1.95) vs 1.63 (1.26-2.1), overlap 86%; synthetic CIs generally narrower than real. "Identity disclosure risk was not explicitly evaluated" (they cite prior literature suggesting it is low).
- El Emam K, Mosquera L, Zheng C. "Optimizing the synthesis of clinical trial data using sequential trees." JAMIA 2021;28(1):3-13, doi 10.1093/jamia/ocaa249 [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7810457/ . Six Project Data Sphere oncology trials (367-773 patients, 59-401 variables). Variable order matters: each dataset synthesised under 1,000 random orders (50 synthetic datasets per order averaged); utility measured by median Hellinger distance (univariate), relative difference in median AUROC of boosted models predicting each variable from the others (multivariate), and propensity-score "distinguishability" d = (1/N) sum (p_i - 0.5)^2. A "curriculum" order (fewest categories first) gave distinguishability above 0.05 in 5 of 6 trials (e.g. 0.232 for trial 6) and was "not consistently better than the random median result"; particle-swarm optimisation of the order on a distinguishability hinge loss (threshold 0.05) brought it to 0.0113-0.049 across the six trials. Authors: "We did not examine the privacy risks from optimizing sequential synthesis." Both papers share the El Emam group (the Azizi study is listed on the CHEO Research Institute site per a search result); I did not see a conflict-of-interest statement in the text I read, so I make no vendor-status claim for either.

**B. From IPD: GAN with differential privacy**
- Beaulieu-Jones BK et al., "Privacy-preserving generative deep neural networks support clinical data sharing", Circ Cardiovasc Qual Outcomes 2019 (PMID 31284738 via search) [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7041894 . SPRINT (9,361 participants; 6,502 with all 12 visits, split 6,000 train / 502 test); only three variables per visit (systolic BP, diastolic BP, medication count) at 12 time points; auxiliary-classifier GAN conditioned on treatment arm; a private AC-GAN trained with differential privacy (epsilon = 3.5, delta < 1e-5 for the model; text also states an overall budget of (4, 1e-5)) generated 5,000 synthetic participants. Measured: rate of adding a medication when above goal 15.14% real vs 15.51% synthetic; Spearman correlation of feature-pair structure with real 0.9645 (non-private) and 0.9185 (private); three clinicians' blinded realism score 5.18 synthetic vs 5.26 real (P = 0.333) and treatment-arm identification 76.7% real vs 82.7% synthetic; classifier transfer (train on synthetic, test on the 502 real) reported only in figures that were not in the text I read. Authors call it "early stage work", say deep models "require substantial sample sizes", and tested only "relatively low-dimensional time series". Internal inconsistency to note: MIMIC cohort 8,260 in methods vs 7,222 in results.

**C. Synthetic patients used to complete a control arm**
- Fernandes, Porcher, Tran, Petit, "Evaluating virtual-control-augmented trials for reproducing treatment effect from original RCTs", arXiv 2507.16048 (2025-07-21) [PP, AB; independent of any vendor]: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16048 . Simulated RCTs from IST/IST3 (stroke); control recruitment stopped at 10% or 50% of the planned size and the arm completed with CTGAN/TVAE patients generated from the recruited controls. Absolute risk difference for death/dependency at 14 days: original IST -0.012 (SE 0.014); CTGAN at 10% recruited 0.004 (SE 0.014), relative difference 133%; CTGAN at 50% recruited -0.021 (SE 0.014), relative difference 76%. The authors call it the first empirical demonstration of "the risk of errors and misleading conclusions" when virtual controls are generated solely from trial data. Type I error not in the abstract; full text not read.
- Background (r3): Elvatun 2025 Survival-CTGAN control arm flagged leakage risk and KM divergence after ~25 months; Delphi-2M (section 3) gives a counter-example where a model trained only on synthetic data reached AUC 0.74.

**D. Differential privacy vs fidelity**
- medRxiv 2024.12.06.24317239 [PP, SNIP only; the fetch returned 403]: described by a search-engine summary as finding that differential privacy strongly degrades fidelity, especially correlation structure, across five generators and three patient datasets - https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.06.24317239 . Treat as an unverified lead.

**E. From published aggregates**
- Guyot P et al. 2012, "Enhanced secondary analysis of survival data: reconstructing the data from published Kaplan-Meier survival curves", BMC Med Res Methodol 12:9, PMC3313891 [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3313891/ . Inputs: digitised (time, survival) coordinates, published numbers at risk by interval, total events. Algorithm: iterate per interval - estimate censoring from the gap between survival-implied and published numbers at risk, spread censoring evenly within the interval, back out events by inverting the KM equation, reconcile with the published at-risk count, then adjust the last interval to match total events. Validation: six KM pairs from four publications, three observers x two repeats x four information levels. Survival-probability error small (mean absolute error 0.272% with all information; 0.328% with neither numbers-at-risk nor total events), but hazard-ratio error rises sharply: log-HR MAE 0.017 (all information), 0.036 (no numbers at risk), 0.198 (neither; 95% CI 0.021-1.556), and log-HR SE underestimated (mean error -0.143) with neither. Example (Bonner 2006): published HR 0.68 (0.52-0.89), reconstructed 0.73 (0.57-0.94). Stated limits: constant censoring within intervals; "the algorithm may produce poor results" without at-risk numbers and totals; cannot recover subgroups or covariates ("aggregation bias"); "not equivalent to true IPD"; validated on only six curve pairs.
- Khorchani et al., SASC "A simple approach to synthetic cohorts for generating longitudinal observational patient cohorts from COVID-19 clinical data", Patterns 2022;3(4):100453, PMC8825316 [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8825316 . Inputs: group means/SDs (outcome x gender) for numeric variables and group percentages for categorical ones from a 375-participant COVID-19 CPRD cohort's summary tables; correlation with outcome/gender (|r| > 0.6) used as a constraint only above that threshold; draws are normal. Validation is mostly qualitative (KM patterns "retained"); some group summaries match (dead males, age 68.5 +/- 11.4 real vs 67.3 +/- 10.7 synthetic) and some do not (dead females, age 72.5 +/- 10.2 vs 55.5 +/- 14.6; surviving males neutrophil % 67.6 +/- 14.7 vs 39.2 +/- 10.1) although the text calls them "close". Synthea values were "more compressed" than real or SASC data (boxplots only; no fidelity metric). Authors: assumes normality; "intended for educational use or validating imputation techniques, not for causal or clinical inference"; correlations not strictly preserved across runs.
- SynthIPD (arXiv 2509.16466, v1 2025-09-19, v2 2026-07-24) [PP, AB]: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16466 . Uses published trial articles, KM curves with at-risk/censoring information and subgroup summary statistics for survival endpoints; digitises the KM curve from SVG, then generates synthetic covariates from subgroup statistics; claims "high-fidelity" with case studies and simulations but the abstract gives no numbers. Via a search summary (not read): continuous covariates would need richer summaries than trials report.
- Search-engine leads not opened [SNIP]: a Cholesky-decomposition cohort generator (MDPI Algorithms 18(8):475) noting that copula methods struggle to preserve both marginals and correlations from summary statistics alone; R package copulaSim takes empirical patient-level data, not a baseline table; a 2023 EMBC comparison of five virtual-population methods found a Gaussian copula best on distributional/correlation fidelity (https://dare.uva.nl/id/b1a086ef-17c1-4942-9bfd-68c108f176ef). I did not verify any of these.

**F. Rule-based synthetic patients (Synthea)**
- Walonoski J et al., JAMIA 2018;25(3):230-238, doi 10.1093/jamia/ocx079 [PR, SNIP/AB via search]: Synthea follows the PADARSER framework (publicly available data, clinician-authored care maps and guidelines, no real EHR), models the 10 most frequent reasons for primary-care encounters and the 10 highest-morbidity chronic conditions, simulates disease progression and care, outputs FHIR/C-CDA - https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/25/3/230/4098271 . I did not find its validation methods; the SASC paper above reports Synthea summary values as more compressed than real data.

**G. "Yale"** - I could not identify a specific Yale paper that fits the "fidelity/utility/privacy evaluation" brief; a search for Yale/YODA synthetic trial data returned the items above and nothing Yale-specific. Please send the exact title if one was meant.

### Inferences
- Common evaluation triad in the credible IPD-based work: fidelity (Hellinger/KL on marginals, correlation structure), utility (re-run the published analysis: same HR direction and CI overlap), privacy (membership/attribute disclosure, or differential-privacy epsilon). Azizi and El Emam 2021 measured the first two and explicitly not the third; Beaulieu-Jones measured privacy by construction (epsilon) but gave only correlation-level and classifier-transfer utility for a 3-variable time series.
- Utility results are single-trial demonstrations; the only multi-trial study (El Emam 2021, six trials) shows fidelity depends on variable order and on the optimisation target, so a pipeline needs an order search or an equivalent control rather than a default.
- Synthetic data are valid for method development, data sharing and design exploration; they are not a source of a new treatment effect. The one study that tested substitution (virtual controls) found relative differences of 76-133% in the risk difference.
- From aggregates only, what is recoverable is limited to what was published: marginals, and for time-to-event data a KM-shaped dataset whose HR fidelity degrades without numbers-at-risk (log-HR MAE 0.017 -> 0.198). Everything else (joint distribution, covariate effects) is an assumption that has to be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- No directly relevant study measuring fidelity/utility/privacy of a copula or Gaussian generator built only from a published baseline table was found and read; the aggregate-only items are SASC (COVID, educational) and the SynthIPD abstract.
- Beaulieu-Jones classifier-AUC values are in figures I could not read; the supplement was not available.
- DP-vs-fidelity preprint and the copula comparison: snippet only. Synthea validation: not found.

## 3. Individual prediction pipelines: digital-twin generators, LLM forecasters, prognostic models and what it takes to apply them to a new population

### Takeaway
Unlearn's "digital twin" is a conditional generative model of an individual's control-arm outcome used as a covariate in a randomised analysis; DT-GPT, TwinWeaver and Delphi-2M are patient-conditioned forecasters trained on EHR or biobank data. None of them reports calibration in the strong sense for an unseen population; in-distribution test sets give small gains (1.3-3.4% scaled MAE over the best baseline for DT-GPT), rare critical events are not predicted (AUC 0.506), and the authors caution against reading outputs as causal or counterfactual. TRIPOD+AI, PROBAST+AI and the STRATOS calibration guidance define what must be shown before a published model is applied to a different population.

### Cited Findings

**A. Unlearn (vendor) - the digital twin generator**
- Bertolini D, Loukianov AD, Smith AM, Li-Bland D, Pouliot Y, Walsh JR, Fisher CK (all Unlearn.AI), "Modeling Disease Progression in Mild Cognitive Impairment and Alzheimer's Disease with Digital Twins", arXiv 2012.13455 (2020-12-24) [PP, VEN, FT via pdftotext]: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13455 (PDF text read from https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.13455).

Previous version used 1,909 subjects/44 variables.
  - Model: two conditional restricted Boltzmann machines, one on a 3-month and one on a 6-month time scale, chained (3-month CRBM first; shared variables conditioned upon). A "Digital Subject" is an unconditional simulated record; a "Digital Twin" is a set of samples conditioned on one real subject's baseline data, "a set of Digital Twins predicts the distribution of possible outcomes" for that subject.

**F. What applying a published model to a new population requires**
- TRIPOD+AI (Collins et al., BMJ 2024) [PR, FT ~100k of 169k characters]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019967/ . 27 items, 52 subitems; uses "evaluation" instead of "external validation". Relevant items: 3b "Describe the target population and the intended purpose of the prediction model in the context of the care pathway"; 8a "Clearly define the outcome that is being predicted and the time horizon"; 9b predictors defined "including how and when they were measured"; 11 missing-data handling; 12e performance measures with rationale (discrimination, calibration, clinical utility are examples; calibration is not a standalone item in what I read); 12f "Describe any model updating (eg, recalibration) arising from the model evaluation"; 14 fairness approaches; 16 differences between development and evaluation data; 23a performance "with confidence intervals, including for any key subgroups"; 24 report the updated model and its performance.
- PROBAST+AI (Moons et al., BMJ 2025;388:e082505, doi 10.1136/bmj-2024-082505) [PR, SNIP/AB via search listings]: replaces PROBAST 2019; two parts (development, evaluation) each with four domains (participants and data sources, predictors, outcome, analysis); applicability rated for three; 16 signalling questions for development and 18 for evaluation (as listed in an Oxford publication record) - https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8b15c087-459b-43fa-ad03-1f66ed6ba370 . No independent empirical validation of the tool was found.
- STRATOS calibration, Van Calster B et al., "Calibration: the Achilles heel of predictive analytics", BMC Med 2019;17:230, PMC6912996 [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6912996 . Four levels: mean (calibration-in-the-large), weak (intercept 0, slope 1), moderate (flexible calibration curve close to the diagonal), strong (predicted = observed for every predictor combination, "utopic"). "A minimum of 200 patients with and 200 patients without the event has been suggested" for calibration curves; with small samples evaluate only weak calibration. For external validation "calibration-in-the-large is highly relevant" and the recommended view is the curve plus intercept and slope; "we recommend against using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test"; poor calibration should trigger updating (re-estimate the intercept; refit coefficients if the validation sample is large enough).
- Regulator equivalents: FDA 2025 AI draft - test data "independent of the development data", assess "applicability of the test data to the COU" and data drift (section 1G above); ICH M15 - validation plus "applicability assessment" for each intended use (section 1F).

### Inferences
- The unit that carries a claim in these pipelines is the pair (training distribution, deployment population). Every pipeline that reports external or out-of-distribution performance reports a drop (Delphi 0.69 -> 0.67; Epic 0.76-0.83 -> 0.63) or a non-significant/untested comparison (TwinWeaver trials), and Unlearn's own held-out set is stratified within the same studies. So a numeric result is only attributable to a population that is demonstrably like the training one.
- A prognostic model used as a covariate in a randomised comparison can be wrong without invalidating the test (EMA wording), but the same model used as the counterfactual for a non-randomised comparison has no such protection; only the former has a qualified regulatory route.
- The three LLM/transformer forecasters either report no calibration (DT-GPT) or report calibration only graphically (Delphi-2M) or defer it (TwinWeaver), which fails STRATOS's moderate-calibration expectation for individual risk statements; their gains are small relative to LightGBM/TFT baselines.
- A product that runs a published prognostic model on a user's patients should require (i) the model's target population and outcome horizon, (ii) predictor definitions, (iii) the local calibration check (intercept and slope at minimum, >= 200 events and non-events for a curve), and (iv) a stated update rule if calibration fails (TRIPOD+AI 12f/16/24; STRATOS).

### Gaps
- Unlearn's current generator (neural Boltzmann machine) and its newer validation (transport across studies) were not read; the CRBM paper is a 2020 preprint. I found no peer-reviewed external-trial validation of a digital twin generator in what I read.
- DT-GPT authorship/conflict statement not captured. No transportability study of DT-GPT, TwinWeaver or Delphi-2M to a new health system beyond Delphi's Danish test was found.
- PROBAST+AI details beyond question counts, and the second 69k characters of TRIPOD+AI, not read.

## 4. LLM silicon sampling failures (Bisbee 2024) and why LLMs cannot be data generators

### Takeaway
In the best-controlled test, persona-prompted ChatGPT reproduced survey means but compressed variance, gave regression coefficients that differed significantly from the human survey in 48% of 22 regressions (sign flipped in 32% of those), and drifted across months. Everything an inferential product needs (variance, correlations, reproducibility) was the part that failed.

### Cited Findings
- Bisbee J, Clinton JD, Dorff C, Kenkel B, Larson JM, "Synthetic Replacements for Human Survey Data? The Perils of Large Language Models", Political Analysis 2024;32:401-416, doi 10.1017/pan.2024.5 [PR, FT of the main text; SI and figure images not seen]: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/synthetic-replacements-for-human-survey-data-the-perils-of-large-language-models/B92267DC26195C7F36E63EA04A47D2FE
  - Design: ChatGPT 3.5 Turbo (primary; robustness with ChatGPT 4.0, 3.5 before/after its 2023-06-25 update, and Falcon-40B-Instruct), temperature 1; 7,530 ANES 2016/2020 respondents each paired with 30 synthetic personas = 3,614,400 responses (minimum 10 per human); persona attributes copied from the matched ANES respondent (age, marital status, race/ethnicity, gender, education, income, ideology, registration, party ID, political interest); outcome: 0-100 feeling thermometers for 11 groups x 2 years = 22 regressions.
  - Means: every synthetic mean falls within one ANES standard deviation of the ANES mean.
  - Variance: "the distribution of synthetic responses for some questions exhibits far less variation than human responses" (racial and religious groups). Power analysis (Table 1): ANES benchmark "size" 7.8, SD 31.4; ChatGPT "size" 12.5, SD 16.1 (the column meaning was not visible in what I read); the synthetic estimates would need just 33 partisans at 99% power, "almost an order of magnitude less" than ANES.
  - Regression coefficients: 48% of synthetic coefficients differ significantly from the ANES counterparts; in 32% of those the sign flips; partisanship is exaggerated.
  - Prompt sensitivity: dropping political information inflates error for several groups; a first-person prompt reduced polarisation but worsened mean absolute error.
  - Drift: runs in April, June and July 2023; June vs April slope 0.93 (not distinguishable from a placebo), July shows "substantial mean reversion"; one target group lost data after a formatting change; OpenAI's later seed option "does not make outputs fully identical".
- Supporting measure from the forecasting literature: untuned LLMs were far worse than a fine-tuned LLM on the same patient forecasting task (DT-GPT paper, section 3B), and no study that directly compared GPT-generated clinical cohorts with real trial IPD was found by me or by r3.

### Inferences
- Failure modes that map directly onto a virtual-cohort product: (1) variance compression -> too-narrow CIs and inflated power; (2) wrong joint structure -> wrong adjusted effects, even sign flips; (3) no calibration target and no provenance -> cannot be audited against FDA-style credibility evidence (a text generator has no parameter set to calibrate or sample from); (4) non-stationary and non-seed-reproducible -> breaks ICH M15 reproducibility/traceability and the FDA 2025 draft's requirement to track model versions.
- The evidence is from political survey data, not clinical cohorts; the transfer to clinical cohorts is an inference, as r3 also notes.
- LLMs trained or fine-tuned on real patient records (DT-GPT, TwinWeaver, Delphi-style) are a different object: they are generative models of real data, with held-out evaluation, not prompt-conjured populations.

### Gaps
- Bisbee SI and figures (exact MAE values) not available to me; the Cambridge landing page was read as text via a fetch tool, so the numbers above are as that tool extracted them from the main text.
- No direct clinical-cohort test of LLM-generated virtual patients found.

## 5. Minimum inputs each pipeline needs from a user, and what each does when no patient data exists

### Takeaway
Every pipeline needs a statement of the decision/COU and a target population definition; beyond that the data requirement splits cleanly into three tiers: knowledge-only (mechanistic model + published distributions), aggregate-only (published tables and KM curves), and patient-level (IPD or individual baseline records). Only the first two work with no patient data, and both then carry an explicit "assumed" label on whatever the aggregates do not give.

### Inferences
- Regulators ask for the same inputs as a precondition regardless of method: question of interest, COU, risk rating (FDA 2023, ICH M15, FDA 2025), the target population's demographic and clinical description (FDA Cat. 5 three-way comparison), and a pre-specified analysis plan (ICH M15 MAP).
- With no patient data, the credible options are exactly two: mechanistic over-generation to published targets (labelled "plausible population, calibrated to published summaries, validated only at population level") and aggregate reconstruction (labelled "reconstructed from published curves/tables, dependence structure assumed"). Both end in design-stage or scenario claims. Neither can be presented as a cohort "like your patients" unless the user supplies patient data for the calibration/validation step.
- When a pipeline needs input the user cannot give (correlations, baseline table row, numbers at risk), the published pipelines either degrade explicitly (Guyot's four information levels), or assume (SASC's normality and independence below the 0.6 threshold); none silently fills it in.

## 6. One patient vs the population, scenarios, and pairing between scenarios (common random numbers)

### Takeaway
In the mechanistic pipelines a virtual patient is a parameter vector and the population is a set of vectors with equal weights; in the statistical/ML pipelines the population is a distribution and a "twin" is a set of samples conditioned on one real person's baseline. Pairing scenarios on the same virtual individuals is standard variance-reduction practice in health-economic microsimulation (common random numbers), with published variance reductions of 74-94% for full CRN and almost none for partial CRN, but the pairing must be maintained by event-level synchronisation and can fail for treatment-specific mechanisms. I found no QSP primary source that documents the same-VP-in-all-arms design explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Individual vs population representations:
  - Mechanistic: virtual patient = "an individual parameter set for the QSP model" (Paul 2024, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-biology/articles/10.3389/fsysb.2024.1444912/full); the Vpop is a probabilistic draw from the plausible population with equal weights (Allen 2016 preprint), re-drawable to bootstrap the influence of the Vpop choice (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/028548.full.pdf). FDA device guidance: a virtual cohort generated "by sampling parameter values across the patient population" cannot be validated patient-by-patient because "none of the virtual patients correspond to any one actual patient" (https://www.fda.gov/media/154985/download).
  - Unlearn: Digital Subject = unconditional simulated record; Digital Twin = set of conditional samples for one baseline, "predicts the distribution of possible outcomes" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13455). DT-GPT averages 30 sampled trajectories per patient for its point forecast (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488890/). Delphi-2M samples next-event and time and iterates (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589094/).
  - NASEM: a digital twin is tied to a specific physical counterpart (https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4).
- Common random numbers: Murphy DR, Klein RW, Smolen LJ, Klein TM, Roberts SD, "Using Common Random Numbers in Health Care Cost-Effectiveness Simulation Modeling", Health Serv Res 2013;48(4):1508-1525, doi 10.1111/1475-6773.12044 [PR, FT]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3725537 .
  - Purpose: "identical patient populations across treatment arms" and removal of between-arm differences not caused by the comparators. Scheme: per-patient, per-process arrays of random numbers (each source of variability its own stream with its own seed); identical initial cohorts per replication run through both arms; random numbers for events that cannot occur (e.g. after death) are skipped but kept in the sequence so later draws stay aligned.
  - Osteoporosis teriparatide vs no treatment: variance of mean incremental cost reduced 93.58% with full CRN vs 5.55% with partial CRN (initial characteristics only); 95% CI half-width reduced 74.67% (full) vs 2.81% (partial); "1,000 patients matched the CI that required 16,000 patients without CRNs"; SD of incremental cost $23,189 (full), $88,956 (partial), $91,530 (none).
  - Pitfalls stated: identical random numbers do not guarantee common patients because of "lack of event synchronization"; CRN may be inappropriate for treatment-related variables when treatments act by different mechanisms (use separate streams); CRN can increase variance when a model has interacting variables; test CRN in your own model by comparing variances with and without.
- Other CRN leads (search snippets only, not opened [SNIP]): SMDM 2009 abstract - cervical cancer model, SD of percent reduction in cancer deaths from 10.0% to 0.33% at the largest cohort size (https://smdm.confex.com/smdm/2009ca/webprogram/Paper4962.html); ISPOR 2026 poster - partitioned survival models, variance reduction factor 3.49 for incremental QALYs, convergence in about 8% of the iterations needed without CRN (https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-3-3/aligning-patients-across-arms-why-common-random-numbers-improve-efficiency-in-partitioned-survival-models); SMDM 2014 - structural bias from CRN with non-ordered outcomes (https://smdm.confex.com/smdm/2014fl/webprogram/Paper8441.html); arXiv 2409.02086 - agent-based work noting the covariance gain can be small once individuals diverge (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02086v1).
- A search tool's summary claimed that Paul 2024 shows running one parameter set under several treatments; I read that paper and it does not: it fits one anti-TNF arm and lists multi-treatment validation as future work.

### Inferences
- "Same virtual individuals in every scenario" is the correct design for comparing scenarios inside a simulation; the CRN literature quantifies the gain (74-94% for full pairing in one model) and the failure modes (desynchronisation; partial pairing nearly useless in that model). A product that only seeds a global RNG once per run does not get this property if an event consumes draws in one arm and not the other.
- A pair of simulated outcomes for the same virtual patient is a model-internal counterfactual; it carries no causal claim about real patients beyond what the mechanistic model asserts, and for statistical/ML twins the authors themselves decline the causal reading (Delphi-2M) or do not test it (DT-GPT, TwinWeaver).
- One-patient outputs (twin) and population outputs (Vpop summary) have different validation requirements: the FDA text allows only population-level evidence for sampled cohorts (Cat. 5), whereas a patient-specific prediction needs patient-level validation (Cat. 4; TRIPOD+AI individual-risk calibration).

### Gaps
- No primary QSP or trial-simulation paper that describes running the same virtual patient under placebo and active arms was found (only a PLOS ONE figure snippet, PMC7657525, not opened). The Frontiers/search claim to the contrary is unsupported.
- The CRN tutorial PMC2761656 was not opened.

## 7. Cross-pipeline comparison: steps every credible pipeline shares, and the claims each is allowed to make

### Takeaway
Seven steps recur in every pipeline that a regulator or reporting guideline accepts: (1) decision/COU and risk, (2) source data or knowledge inventory with relevance, (3) generation, (4) calibration/training kept separate from (5) validation on independent data, (6) applicability to the target population plus uncertainty/sensitivity, (7) a pre-specified plan and a report. The claim ceiling is set by the data tier and the validation level, not by the model sophistication.

### Cited Findings (shared steps with their sources)
| Step | QSP/mechanistic | IPD-based synthetic | Prediction/twin | Regulatory/reporting source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. State decision, COU, risk | question of interest and COU first (FDA CM&S; ICH M15) | utility defined by the analysis to be repeated (Azizi) | outcome, horizon, target population, care pathway (TRIPOD+AI 3b, 8a) | FDA 2023, ICH M15, FDA 2025 step 1-3 |
| 2. Inputs with provenance and relevance | bounds, target distribution, published model | trial IPD, variable list | training cohort, 80/10/10 or similar split | FDA 2025: data "relevant" and "reliable"; ICH M15 data relevance |
| 3. Generate | PP over-generation, Vpop selection | sequential trees, GAN, CTGAN/TVAE | CRBM, fine-tuned LLM, GPT-style transformer | model description in each |
| 4. Calibrate (kept apart) | Vpop fit to NHANES is calibration (FDA Cat. 2) | tuning of variable order/privacy epsilon | training/validation split; tuning data | FDA CM&S Cat. 2; FDA 2025 tuning vs test |
| 5. Validate on independent data | population-level mean/SD/distribution (Cat. 5), trial reproduction | analysis replication (HR, CI overlap), distinguishability | held-out subjects or trials; external registry (Delphi: Danish) | "test data ... independent of the development data" (FDA 2025); ICH M15 external validation "encouraged" |
| 6. Applicability + UQ | prior/inclusion sensitivity, re-drawn Vpops, Cat. 8 | privacy risk, order variability | calibration, subgroup performance, data drift | ICH M15 applicability and robustness; NASEM VVUQ; STRATOS |
| 7. Plan and report | MAP/MAR | pre-specification | TRIPOD+AI reporting; PROBAST+AI appraisal | ICH M15 MAP/MAR; FDA 2025 credibility assessment plan |

### Claims each pipeline is allowed to make (as evidenced above)
- Mechanistic Vpop / in silico trial: a model-informed projection at a stated COU and risk level, with parameter and assumption uncertainty; FDA expects the final simulation results plus credibility evidence; for treatment effect, validation against independent trial outcomes is the credible bar and the available examples are vendor-reported (FLAURA2 HR 0.62 vs 0.602; AFFIRM over-prediction >90% vs 67%). Not allowed: "this is what your patients will do" from a calibration fit alone (FDA: calibration evidence is weak).
- Synthetic data from IPD: a privacy-preserving analogue for method development and data sharing, with fidelity and utility metrics; one trial replication gives HR CI overlap 61%-86%. Not allowed: a new treatment effect or a replacement control arm (virtual-control completion distorted ARD by 76-133% in simulated RCTs).
- From published aggregates: pseudo-IPD or example cohorts for teaching, imputation tests or design simulation; survival marginals reproduce well (MAE ~0.3% with full information) but HR reliability depends on numbers at risk. Not allowed: covariate-effect or subgroup inference, or claims of IPD-equivalence.
- Unlearn-type digital twin generator: efficiency gain in a randomised trial with a continuous outcome via covariate adjustment (EMA-qualified); type I error and unbiasedness do not depend on model quality; reported sample-size savings are 5-10% (peer-reviewed AWARE abstract) to 10-30% "attainable" (vendor-authored method paper). Not allowed: use as an external control or as the treatment-effect estimate (background: r1 19.1, r3 Q4).
- DT-GPT / TwinWeaver / Delphi-2M: research-grade forecasting of lab trajectories or disease risk with small gains over strong baselines, no individual calibration shown for DT-GPT, and explicit authors' cautions against causal reading. Not allowed: counterfactual "what-if treatment" claims, rare-event alerting (AUC 0.506 for critical hemoglobin), or deployment on a new population without local calibration (Epic sepsis AUC 0.63).
- LLM persona simulation: no allowed inferential claim; evidence shows mean-matching with variance compression and sign flips.

### Inferences
- A comparison grid for a product team: the discriminating columns are (a) what is the claim tier (design projection / data-sharing analogue / RCT precision / individual forecast), (b) what data tier backs it (knowledge-only / aggregate / IPD / individual records), (c) is calibration separated from validation, (d) is the validation population independent and described, (e) is uncertainty carried to the output, (f) is the causal/counterfactual reading disclaimed. No published pipeline I read satisfies all six without vendor-authored qualification.
- The statistical-dependence structure is the weakest link in the aggregate-only tier and the least reported across all tiers (SASC correlation threshold; Guyot's censoring assumption; copula claims unverified), so any product claim about "joint distribution" from aggregates should be shown as an assumption.

### Gaps
- No published pipeline combines all of: a virtual cohort from published aggregates, prospective external validation, and a paired-scenario design with CRN. Each element is documented separately; the combination is not evidenced in what I found.
