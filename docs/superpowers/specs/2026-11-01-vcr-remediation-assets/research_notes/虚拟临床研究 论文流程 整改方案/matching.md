## Q1. What is each system's actual pipeline (input, retrieval, criterion parsing, evidence step, per-criterion label set, aggregation/ranking, human role, evaluation)?

### Takeaway
Almost every system has the same skeleton: (a) narrow the trial set (structured filter and/or lexical + dense retrieval), (b) pull evidence from the patient record per criterion (chunk + embed + top-k, or a patient summary), (c) an LLM answers each criterion with a rationale and an evidence pointer, (d) criterion answers are aggregated into a patient-trial score, (e) a human reviews the top-ranked or flagged pairs. They differ in whether eligibility text is parsed first (PRISM, Massive Bio, Ferber, Wong, UTSW's tiered prompts) or handed to the LLM raw (TrialGPT 1, Wornow, Beattie), in the label set, in whether aggregation is code or LLM, and, since TrialGPT 2.0, in whether the unit of output is eligibility at all (it now outputs a one-shot "fit score" for recommendation, with no per-criterion labels).

### Cited Findings

#### TrialGPT (Jin et al., Nat Commun 2024) [PR, ACAD (NIH/NLM)] — read
- Input: free-text patient summary (the cohorts supply it; the paper does not say how summaries are produced) plus the registry record of each trial (title, conditions, interventions, summary, inclusion list, exclusion list). — [PMC11574183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183) (DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-53081-z)
- Retrieval: LLM writes up to 32 ranked keywords from the patient note; each keyword goes to BM25 and to MedCPT; per-keyword fusion by reciprocal rank (constant C = 20) and a 1/i weight on keyword rank; top 500 trials kept. Recall@500 on the three cohorts: 50.0% with the raw note, 83.4% with GPT-3.5 keywords, 86.2% with GPT-4 keywords; 90% recall needed 5.5% of the collection with GPT-4 keywords. Collections: SIGIR 3,621 trials; TREC 2021 26,149; TREC 2022 26,581. — same source
- Criterion parsing: none described in the text I read; the model receives the inclusion list and the exclusion list as given. Two LLM calls per patient-trial pair (one for all inclusion criteria, one for all exclusion criteria), chain-of-thought, JSON output, GPT-4 (0613) at temperature 0. — same source
- Evidence step and output per criterion: (1) a relevance explanation, (2) IDs of the numbered patient-note sentences that are relevant, (3) a label. — same source
- Label sets: inclusion = "included / not included / not enough information / not applicable"; exclusion = "excluded / not excluded / not enough information / not applicable" (a separate exclusion vocabulary was chosen instead of NLI entailment labels because exclusion criteria are ambiguous). — same source
- Aggregation/ranking: with M' and N' = number of inclusion/exclusion criteria minus the "not applicable" ones, six linear scores (for inclusion and for exclusion: % met, % unmet, % not-enough-information), plus two LLM-aggregated scores (relevance R in 0–100, eligibility S in [-R, R]). Best ranker is a hand-set combination: %met_incl − 1[%unmet_incl > 0] − 1[%met_excl > 0] + LLM_relevance + LLM_eligibility (their Eq. 14). GPT-4 combination: NDCG@10 0.7275, P@10 0.6688, AUROC 0.7979 vs best baseline (BioLinkBERT cross-encoder) 0.4797 / 0.4281 / 0.6176. — same source
- Human role and evaluation: 183 patients, all synthetic or "idealized" (SIGIR 2016: 58 patients, 3,621 trials; TREC 2021 CT: 75 patients; TREC 2022 CT: 50 patients; >75,000 annotations). Criterion-level check: 1,015 patient-criterion pairs from 105 patient-trial pairs of 53 SIGIR patients, three physician annotators, consensus label (at least two agree; a discussion round if all three differ). Accuracy 0.873 overall (inclusion 0.899, exclusion 0.859) vs expert 0.887–0.900; explanations 87.8% correct / 9.66% partially / 2.56% incorrect; relevant-sentence F1 88.6%. Error analysis of 26 wrong predictions: incorrect reasoning 30.7%, ambiguous label definitions 26.9%, lack of medical knowledge 15.4%. User study: 2 physicians, 6 trials, 6 semi-synthetic vignettes, 42.6% time saved, accuracy 97.2% vs 91.7% (not significant). Authors: experts must remain in the loop. — same source

#### TrialGPT 2.0 (Fang, Jin, ... Lu; arXiv 2609.01202 v2, 28 Oct 2026) [PP, ACAD (NIH NLM with NCI, UIC, UPMC, Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation; no commercial affiliation is listed on the title page)] — read
- Input: patient context (raw records or a clinician-written summary), a predefined local trial list (an institutional portfolio, a disease-focused or geographic ClinicalTrials.gov query; 9 to 1,871 trials per setting, more than 25,000 US trials in the web deployment), and a local "matching policy" (which trial-fit factors to weigh: diagnosis/histology, disease status, biomarker, line of therapy, prior treatment, cohort/arm applicability; therapeutic over diagnostic/registry trials). Policies guide prioritisation but "do not override explicit eligibility criteria". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202), [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01202)
- Retrieval: pluggable; used only for lists of more than 1,500 trials (the same BM25 + MedCPT reciprocal-rank-fusion design as TrialGPT 1); smaller lists go straight to the backend. — same source
- Criterion parsing and per-criterion labels: none. The backend makes one trial-level assessment per candidate trial (if a trial has several arms, "whether the patient fits any clinically meaningful option"). Output is strict structured text: eligible reasons, missing information, ineligible reasons, rationale, fit score (integer 0–100), confidence (0–1). — same source
- Aggregation/ranking is by the LLM itself, then thresholded in code: fit score >90 = Highly Recommended, 80–90 = Possible Match, <80 = Low Fit; rank by fit score, ties broken by confidence. Backbone GPT-4.1 on Azure, with GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3.1-pro-preview as alternatives in the supplement. — same source
- Missing information: "missing information that could typically be obtained during screening lowers confidence rather than automatically indicating ineligibility"; clear violations lower the fit score and are listed as ineligible reasons. — same source
- Human role: recommendation-support only; clinicians keep responsibility for recommendation, referral, screening and enrolment. Output is a ranked card list with filters; PDF export. — same source
- Evaluation, retrospective (288 de-identified cases, 1,340 patient-trial pairs, five recruitment pathways): for OPR, CCF, UIC, UPMC (188 cases, 440 reviewed pairs) clinicians labelled up to 10 top-ranked "Highly Recommended" trials as Recommended / Eligible but Not Recommended / Ineligible: hit rate 0.88 at K=1, 0.91 at K=3 and K=10; eligible precision 0.89 (K=1) to 0.87 (K=10). Clinicians judged only the system's own top list, so hit rate "should not be interpreted as recall over the full candidate trial corpus" (authors' words). — same source
- NCI exhaustive review (100 cases x 9 trials = 900 pairs): three annotations per pair (one blinded independent, two from a counterbalanced experiment with and without the tool), a fourth clinician for disagreements; labels mapped to the three output categories. Exact agreement with the consensus: TrialGPT 2.0 alone 89.0%; clinicians with the tool 94.0%; without 94.2%. Class recall: Highly Recommended 103/118 (87.3%), Low Fit 671/723 (92.8%), Possible Match only 27/59. Severe Low-Fit to Highly-Recommended error: 1 pair assisted, 14 unassisted, 8 tool alone. Screening time per patient 129.8 s to 58.4 s (-55.0%; trial-level decision time summed over 9 trials, excluding reading the patient context and report generation). — same source
- Prospective: UIC Precision Oncology Tumor Board, February–July 2026, 27 cases, 339 TrialGPT-generated candidate pairs, run in parallel with the routine human trial search; the board selected 54 final recommendations. Cases with at least one final recommendation rose from 11 to 21 (+90.9%); 37 of 54 recommendations (abstract: 69%) came only from the tool, 8 were shared, 9 were human-only. Of 339 candidates, clinicians labelled 45 (13.3%) Recommended, 282 (83.2%) Eligible but Not Recommended, 12 (3.5%) Ineligible. In the 3 cases where all final picks were human-only, the tool applied protocol criteria "literally" (prior chemotherapy given for a misclassified primary; interim therapy before a required prior line; poor performance status). — same source
- Benchmarks: NIH-TrialBench, 126 synthetic vignettes by 24 NIH investigators from 11 institutes, 1,373 candidate trials, 990 reviewed pairs; hit rate 0.63 (K=1) to 0.95 (K=10); target-trial Recall@10 70% (macro) vs 54% for TrialGPT 1.0, 43% for ChatGPT-5.5 Thinking and 21% for Gemini-3.5 Flash Extended Thinking. Public TREC/SIGIR sets: P@10 +4.5%, nDCG@10 +5.7%, MRR +9.8%, MAP +10.2% over TrialGPT 1.0; 3.20x faster; input tokens -58%, output tokens -73%. Runtime 21.7 s per case (25 cases). Reviewer survey: 85 of 104 item ratings 4 or 5; perceived efficiency 3.85 and decision confidence 3.92 (of 5) were the lowest items. — same source

#### PRISM / OncoLLM (Gupta, Basu, ... Singh; arXiv 2404.15549 v2, Apr 2024) [PP; [VEN]: Triomics Research authors, with Medical College of Wisconsin and Mount Sinai; a journal version is reported by secondary sources but I did not open it] — read
- Input: all unstructured notes of a patient from a single cancer centre's research warehouse (hundreds per patient) and the trial's free-text criteria from ClinicalTrials.gov. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549)
- Criterion parsing (once per trial, LLM): GPT-3.5 turns each criterion into one or more yes/no questions connected by Boolean logic in disjunctive normal form. Manual check of 50 trials: 1% of questions missed, 2% incorrectly formed, about 89% of criterion-level Boolean logic correct. — same source
- Evidence step: spaCy sentence chunks with one-sentence overlap; Azure Ada embeddings, cosine similarity to the question; retrieved chunks are ordered chronologically (not by score), starting with age and enrolment date, each tagged with note date and type, to help temporal questions. — same source
- Per-question output (zero-shot CoT, temperature 0, JSON): question explanation, answer explanation, answer Yes / No / N/A ("N/A" = not applicable or insufficient information, one label), confidence 1–5. — same source
- Aggregation (deterministic): answers are composed through the DNF tree; where some answers are N/A the code marginalises over their possible values (each of the 2^N combinations weighted 1/2^N) and sets Met if P > 0.66, Not met if P < 0.34, else N/A. A patient-trial score then comes from one of three rules: simple counting; "iterative tier" (walk 4 clinical-importance tiers until the first violation); "weighted tier". — same source
- Evaluation: 720 criterion-derived questions from 50 patients (about 10,000 notes), five medical doctors (every question read by at least one, about 12 by all five; GPT-3.5 pre-selected the relevant segments for the annotators: about 800,000 segments reduced to about 8,000 at 98% precision / 94% recall). Question-level accuracy: GPT-4 68% (72% after dropping questions whose expert answer was N/A), OncoLLM (14B fine-tune) 63% (66%), GPT-3.5 53%, Mixtral-8x7B 49%, Qwen14B 43%, Mistral-7B 41%; experts 70% on the 109 questions annotated by two experts; inter-annotator agreement 64% (all five) and 70% (two best) on those 109. — same source
- Ranking evaluation: 98 patients, each with the one trial they enrolled in plus 10 same-cancer, actively recruiting trials; fraction with the enrolled trial in the top 3: OncoLLM weighted-tier 65.3% vs GPT-3.5 iterative-tier 61.2% (GPT-4 not run for cost). Checking 10 top-1 non-enrolled trials, a data expert found the patient eligible for 9, so "top-3 accuracy" ≈ 95%; the one ineligible case was a left- versus right-breast laterality error. — same source

#### Wornow et al. (Stanford; arXiv 2402.05125 v3, Apr 2024) [PP, ACAD] — read
- Input: a patient's notes (n2c2 2018: 288 diabetic patients, 2–5 notes each, 202 train / 86 test) and 13 fixed free-text inclusion criteria (no exclusion criteria). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125)
- Evidence step: either the notes are fed whole, or a two-stage design pre-filters 512-token chunks by embedding similarity (BGE or MiniLM) to the criterion and keeps top-k before the "assessment" LLM. Four prompt layouts tested: All-Criteria/All-Notes, All-Criteria/Individual-Notes (ACIN), Individual-Criteria/All-Notes, Individual-Criteria/Individual-Notes. — same source
- Output per criterion: criterion, medications, rationale, decision MET / NOT MET (NOT MET explicitly includes "impossible to assess given the provided information"), confidence Low/Medium/High. — same source
- Aggregation across notes is deterministic and per criterion (Appendix C): "max" (met if any note says met) for 9 criteria, "min" for ENGLISH, "most recent note within the window" for the three time-bound ones (dietary supplement 2 months, ketoacidosis 12 months, MI 6 months). — same source
- Results (ACIN, GPT-4): overall Micro-F1 0.93, Macro-F1 0.81 vs prior rule-based SOTA 0.91 / 0.75. Criterion definitions had to be hand-refined on 3 met + 3 not-met training patients per criterion (GPT-4 micro-F1 0.85 to 0.93). Cost for the test set: ACIN GPT-4 $133.08 (377 calls); individual-criterion/individual-note $1,021.65 (4,901 calls). Rationale audit: 486 GPT-4 rationales (20 correct + 20 incorrect predictions per criterion) rated by two clinicians; for correct decisions 89% correct / 8% partially / 3% incorrect; for incorrect decisions 67% / 8% / 25%. The abstract's "97% of correct and 75% of incorrect" is correct + partially correct. Annotators of n2c2 itself: Cohen's kappa 0.54. — same source

#### n2c2 2018 Track 1 cohort selection (Stubbs et al., JAMIA 2019) [PR] — read
- 288 patients with 2–5 longitudinal records (about 781,006 tokens), 13 fixed criteria (e.g. MI-6MOS, HBA1C 6.5–9.5%, ADVANCED-CAD, MAKES-DECISIONS), labels "met" / "not met" (annotators could mark "possibly met", resolved at adjudication), two medically trained annotators, average Cohen's kappa 0.54 (KETO-1YR -0.1, ADVANCED-CAD 0.37); 202 train / 86 test; 47 teams, 109 system outputs; best micro-F1 0.91 (rule-based, Graz); top 10 = 4 rule-based, 6 hybrid, none pure ML; top six not significantly different. No retrieval step; no free-text trial criteria. — [PMC6798568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6798568)

#### TREC Clinical Trials track (2021–2022) [benchmark] — TREC 2022 overview read
- Topic = a synthetic 5–10 sentence admission-style patient description; collection = ClinicalTrials.gov. Assessors (physician graduate students at OHSU, NLM indexers, other experts) judge pooled results on a 3-point scale: Not Relevant, Excluded ("has the condition but the exclusion criteria make the patient ineligible"), Eligible. Rationale for three labels: separate "not enough information to qualify" (Not Relevant) from "explicitly Excluded". 2022: 50 topics, 41 runs from 11 teams, pooled to depth 40, 35,394 judgements: 3,949 Eligible (11%), 3,047 Excluded (9%), 28,481 Not Relevant. NDCG scores Eligible 2 / Excluded 1 / Not Relevant 0; other metrics treat only Eligible as relevant. Recruitment status and location were not reasons for ineligibility. — [NIST TREC 2022 overview](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec31/papers/Overview_trials.pdf)

#### RECTIFIER (Unlu et al., MGB) [PR; ACAD] — NEJM AI 2024 not read; JAMA RCT page partly read
- Design of the RCT's screening process (from the JAMA page): eligibility criteria were split into those "reliably determined by structured data queries" and those that were not; patients meeting the structured criteria entered the trial (193,616 excluded by the exclusion criteria); 4,476 randomised 1:1 (2,242 AI-assisted; 2,234 manual), randomisation blocked and applied after each structured query; RECTIFIER parses unstructured notes, PDFs and reports against the remaining inclusion/exclusion criteria; staff time split equally and staff crossed over between arms; CONSORT-AI reporting. The fetched page text did not describe the AI-arm output or who confirmed eligibility. — [JAMA 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514)
- NEJM AI 2024 (secondary only): GPT-4 retrieval-augmented over about 120 notes per patient, 13 eligibility prompts for COPILOT-HF, test set of 1,894 patients; single-question and combined inclusion+exclusion question strategies and a GPT-3.5 variant compared; gold standard by a blinded expert clinician; accuracy 97.9–100% (RECTIFIER) vs 91.7–100% (study staff) per criterion; authors recommended keeping a clinician's final check of AI-eligible patients; about $0.11 per patient (single question) or $0.02 (combined). — [MGB newsroom](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/ai-screens-heart-failure-patients-for-clinical-trial-eligibility), [2 Minute Medicine summary](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/a-large-language-model-demonstrates-superior-cost-effectiveness-in-screening-heart-failure-candidates-for-clinical-trials)

#### UT Southwestern prospective deployment (Dohopolski, Esselink, Desai, Grones, Patel, Jiang, Peterson, Navar; medRxiv 10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890, posted 2026-03-27, "Implementation of Human-in-the-Loop ChatGPT-based Patient Screening Across Multiple Diverse Clinical Trials") [PP, ACAD] — read
- Input: Epic data from Caboodle/Clarity refreshed nightly, prescreen lists several times a week with a 2–4 day look-ahead; notes, imaging, pathology reports and labs (some signals such as active medications and the latest lab values are better taken from structured tables); scanned external PDFs excluded; look-back typically 0–180 days, set per trial. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- Evidence step: per-patient vector store; hierarchical nodes (document, paragraph, sentence); top 30 nodes per criterion sent to the LLM; GPT-4o (Oct 2024–Mar 2025), GPT-4o-mini (Mar–Jun 2025), GPT-4.1-mini (Jun 2025 onwards); text-embedding-3-large; Azure, HIPAA environment. — same source
- Criterion parsing (by humans, once per trial): study teams pick 2–7 criteria at onboarding and rank them into Tier 1 and Tier 2; each criterion becomes a prompt with its name, verbatim protocol text and a "tips" block of decision rules (later enriched with PubMed context by a companion agent). Tier 2 is evaluated only if Tier 1 is "met" or "likely met". 27 criteria were revised at least once after coordinator feedback from April 2025. — same source
- Per-criterion labels: met, likely met, likely not met, not met, uncertain, no documentation found (plus a 5-point certainty score and a rationale). Patient score = percentage of the trial's criteria labelled met or likely met; coordinators asked to review patients at 50% or higher (later 75% for some teams). — same source
- Human checkpoint: coordinators review all AI-flagged candidates before any provider outreach, recording per criterion met / not met / insufficient data; discordance needs a written rationale and is resolved by junior-to-senior re-assessment or single-reviewer adjudication; a reminder fires after 4 days unreviewed; prompt changes are human-approved and versioned. — same source
- Numbers: 39,182 patients prescreened (Oct 2024–Sep 2025) for 26 studies (21 oncology, 5 non-oncology) with 112 distinct criteria; 914 patients sent to coordinator review (Table 2 denominators sum to 916). Criterion-level agreement of the LLM labels with coordinators over 5,096 criteria (TP 3,880, TN 942, FP 220, FN 74 sum to 5,116; uncertain/no-documentation labels excluded): accuracy 0.94 (0.92–0.96), sensitivity 0.98, specificity 0.81 (0.71–0.88), PPV 0.95, NPV 0.93, F1 0.97 in the Results; kappa not reported. Review rate 55.1% (544/987) for scores 60–80% vs 93.7% (372/397) at 80% or more; "prescreen success" 19.1% (104/544) vs 43.5% (162/372). Cost $1,283.48 for 10,596 patients = $0.12 per patient. Time per patient, enrolment and accrual were not measured. — same source

#### TrialMatchAI (Abdallah, ... Nikolski; arXiv 2505.08508 v1, May 2025; published Nat Commun 2026, 10.1038/s41467-026-70509-w, which I did not open) [PR in journal / PP version read; ACAD (Bordeaux, NKI, Oslo, VHIO, ECRIN)] — arXiv v1 read
- Input: structured patient fields plus free-text notes; trials indexed in Elasticsearch at trial level and, separately, at criterion level (each inclusion/exclusion criterion is its own document, enriched with normalised entities and synonyms). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08508v1), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08508)
- Pipeline: pre-filter (age, sex, location, recruitment status, obvious population mismatches) → hybrid retrieval, BM25 + BGE-M3 HNSW vector search, k = 500 → criterion-level re-ranking with a fine-tuned Gemma-2-2B (binary relevance of each retrieved criterion to the patient, trial score by max/mean/sqrt-sum aggregation, e.g. 70% sqrt-normalised sum + 30% max) → eligibility assessment by fine-tuned Phi-4 with medical chain-of-thought, one JSON object per criterion with original text, class and justification. — same source
- Per-criterion labels: inclusion Met / Not Met / Unclear / Irrelevant; exclusion Violated / Not Violated / Unclear / Irrelevant. The final composite score rewards satisfied inclusion and violated-exclusion avoidance and drops "Unclear" and "Irrelevant" criteria from the calculation. — same source
- Evaluation: synthetic TREC 2021/2022 patients (median nDCG@10 about 0.75; recall about 90% at 500 retrieved); expert review of 950 synthetic patient-criterion pairs (inclusion: 88.8% of "Met" and 91.1% of "Not Met" correct; exclusion: 89.7% of "Violated" and 96.9% of "Not Violated"); "real-world" arm = 52 metastatic cancer patients from the NKI WIDE study with the recorded trial assignment as ground truth: at least one relevant trial in the top 20 for 92.3% (top 5: 84.6%; MRR about 0.49). The criterion-level expert check on this arm used 100 biomarker inclusion criteria against semi-synthetic profiles rewritten from molecular tumour board reports, 91% judged correctly "Met". — same source

#### MatchMiner-AI (Altreuter, ... Kehl; Dana-Farber and Mayo; arXiv 2412.17228, now version 3, Aug 2026) [PP, ACAD] — v3 read
- Pipeline: (1) an open-weight LLM extracts a trial's core eligibility (age, sex, cancer type/histology, burden/treatment intent, biomarkers, prior treatment) into a target-population "space"; (2) an LLM summarises the longitudinal EHR text chunk by chunk into a running structured summary; (3) a fine-tuned Qwen3-0.6B embedding model ("TrialSpace") retrieves the top 20 spaces per patient or top 40 patients per trial; (4) a distilled re-ranker/regressor ("TrialChecker") predicts a 0–5 ordinal "does the patient meet the core criteria and how specifically"; (5) a "boilerplate exclusions" check (e.g. uncontrolled brain metastases, recent second primary). Trained on synthetic EHR data, teacher labels from an LLM. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- Evaluation: distillation fidelity MAP@20 0.95 / 0.94 vs 0.44 / 0.38 for a baseline embedding; in a 50-patient sample only 19 (38%) had tumour genomic data for the rules-based MatchMiner while MatchMiner-AI returned trials for all 50; on the 19: 80% (204/256) vs 53% (60/113) of suggestions rated "reasonable" by a frontier LLM judge. Five oncologists annotated 5 trial spaces for each of 20 patients (25 pairs dual-annotated): oncologist-oncologist agreement on "reasonable consideration" 84.0%, kappa 0.606 (95% CI 0.199–0.905); overall across five concepts 91.2%, kappa 0.784; oncologist vs Claude overall 84.0%, kappa 0.640; vs Gemma-4-31B 82.4%, kappa 0.610. Six-month pilot with four oncologists, then eight; no workflow outcomes reported. — same source

#### Massive Bio neuro-symbolic multi-agent system (Loaiza-Bonilla et al., ESMO Real World Data and Digital Oncology 2026) [PR; VEN (5 of 7 authors Massive Bio employees; co-founders with equity)] — PMC full text read
- Input: notes, scanned PDFs/faxes, pathology and imaging reports (157,367 pages, about 86.5 M tokens); trial criteria from ClinicalTrials.gov ingested monthly and parsed into clauses. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/)
- Pipeline: LLM agents (extraction, eligibility, matching) plus a knowledge graph (OncoGraph; NCI Thesaurus, SNOMED CT, MeSH mapping) that evaluates inclusion, exclusion and temporal rules deterministically; ranking by "clinical relevance and degree-of-fit" with confidence scores (the aggregation formula is not specified). — same source
- Labels per patient-trial pair: Eligible / Not eligible / Requires Clinician Judgment (excluded from the binary F1). An undocumented mandatory criterion is labelled Not Eligible for that constraint; undated temporal references go to Requires Clinician Judgment. About 15% of pairs (low confidence or conflicting evidence) get prioritised human review; a separate triage targets the top 25% most uncertain pairs (the paper does not reconcile the two figures). — same source
- Evaluation: 3,804 consecutive patients (ECOG 0–2), 23,912 candidate pairs, 17,912 matches confirmed by oncologists; gold standard from two board-certified oncologists reviewing each EHR (Cohen's kappa 0.92); 80/20 train/test split (the grouping unit and test size are not stated). Held-out F1 0.8246 (abstract 0.82, CI 0.81–0.83), sensitivity 0.8375, specificity 0.8359, precision 0.8121; GPT-4 zero-shot F1 0.47, GPT-4 CoT 0.67; ablations 0.79 (no graph), 0.78 (single agent). Discordance causes: complex temporal logic 32%, missing structured data 28%, ambiguity/negation 18%, extraction/OCR 12%, ontology mismatch 10%. Time: median about 120 min to about 30 min (15 automated + 15 clinician review; measurement method not described). Enrolment not measured. — same source

#### Ferber et al. (Kather lab; arXiv 2407.13463, Jul 2024) [PP, ACAD with AstraZeneca co-authors] — first 100 K characters read
- Pipeline: free-text oncology EHR → GPT-4o extracts fields → GPT-4o writes MongoDB queries over a ClinicalTrials.gov cancer snapshot of 105,600 trials (structured pre-filter on status, location, study type, conditions) → vector search (bge-large-en-v1.5, ChromaDB, up to 5 LLM-generated queries, top 50) → LLM prescreen to about 20–30 trials → per-trial criteria split by GPT-4o (up to two nesting levels, inclusion/exclusion separated) → per-criterion True / False / unknown with chain-of-thought → "scores based on the number and ratio of fulfilled criteria" (whether code or LLM is not specified; ranking quality "has not been evaluated"). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13463v1)
- Evaluation: 15 base cases expanded to 51 synthetic EHRs, one target trial per patient, 1,589 criteria, five oncologists, majority vote, no kappa reported; 14/15 target trials in the top 10; criterion accuracy 88.0% (1,398/1,589) vs the majority label, 92.7% after three annotators re-reviewed the 191 discordant criteria (75 resolved in the model's favour, 56 of those 75 because the humans had marked a criterion satisfied when the record lacked the information). — same source

#### Microsoft/Providence (Wong et al., MLHC 2023; arXiv 2308.02180) [PP/proceedings, ACAD+industry] — read
- Structure-then-match: GPT-4 structures each oncology trial's criteria into five categories (disease state, histology inclusion, biomarker inclusion, histology exclusion, biomarker exclusion) as JSON in disjunctive normal form (nested AND/OR/NOT), normalised to NCI Thesaurus; patient attributes come from the deployment's separate NLP extraction system; matching is plain code. Gold for structuring: three molecular pathologists structured 53 trials. Results (tables read in the full text): entity extraction only (Table 1) — F1 histology / biomarker: Criteria2Query 32.8 / 39.2, GPT-3.5 zero-shot 34.2 / 39.7, GPT-4 zero-shot 65.4 / 66.7, GPT-4 3-shot 64.8 / 72.5; complete DNF match logic (Table 2, "very stringent") — GPT-4 3-shot F1 48.0 (histology), 38.4 (biomarker), 29.6 (both), GPT-4 zero-shot 42.1 / 22.2 / 24.0; end-to-end matching against implicit feedback from a deployed expert system (molecular pathologists selecting from its candidates; 68,485 candidates, 84% positive) — precision / recall / F1: GPT-4 3-shot 87.6 / 67.3 / 76.1, GPT-4 zero-shot 86.7 / 46.8 / 60.8, GPT-3.5 zero-shot 88.5 / 11.6 / 20.6, the expert system itself 84.3 / 97.4 / 90.4 (the authors note the expert system is favoured because users mainly judged its own candidates). Correction to r3: 76.1 / 60.8 / 20.6 are end-to-end matching F1, not criteria-structuring F1. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02180)

#### Earlier parsing-to-query systems and LLM successors
- Criteria2Query (Yuan et al., JAMIA 2019) [PR]: sentence split → CRF named entity recognition (Condition, Drug, Measurement, Procedure, Observation; trained on 230 Alzheimer's trials) → NegEx → relations (has_temp, has_value) → AND/OR detection → OMOP concept mapping (OHDSI concept sets, UMLS synonyms, Usagi) → temporal/numeric normalisation (SUTime) → JSON for ATLAS/SQL; human edits entities and concept sets in the interface. 125 criteria from 10 trials plus 52 user-typed criteria: entity F1 0.795, relation F1 0.805, entity normalisation accuracy 0.514 (0.447 on ClinicalTrials.gov criteria), logic detection 0.864; the paper has no missing-data analysis. — [PMC6402359](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6402359)
- Criteria2Query 3.0 (Park, Fang, Weng; OHDSI brief report) [snippet-only]: three few-shot GPT prompts replace the NLP stack: extract concepts with OMOP domain tags, build a PostgreSQL query, reason over the query for inclusion/exclusion logic. — [OHDSI brief](https://ohdsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/423-Park-BriefReport.pdf)
- EC2Seq2Sql (Yang et al., PLoS One 2026;21(2):e0341827) [PR, ACAD]: researcher selects trials → BART-large-CNN converts criteria into structured patterns over seven domains (condition, procedure, observation, laboratory, drug, age, gender) → GPT-4 (temperature 0, LangChain) writes SQL → run on the hospital EHR's structured tables (no notes). Benchmark (split 80/10/10 by trial): exact-set match 0.84, execution accuracy 0.91; real world: 41 hepatocellular carcinoma patients at one hospital, execution accuracy 1.00, "clinical match accuracy" 0.88 (share of retrieved patients experts confirmed eligible). Limits stated: nested logic flattened, temporal/negation errors, seven domains omit prior treatment and genetic markers. — [PMC12900307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12900307/)
- THESEUS (Kim, Kim, Kim, You; Yonsei; medRxiv 10.64898/2026.03.13.26348306 v2; JAMIA Open 2026;9(4) ooag131) [PP read; PR version via announcement only; the corresponding author is CEO of PHI Digital Healthcare per the preprint's disclosures]: this is target-trial-emulation design, not patient-trial matching, but the same "parse once, run deterministically" split: an LLM maps a free-text study design into a constrained JSON schema; rule-based logic converts the validated spec into Strategus R scripts; a self-audit loop repairs failing code. Preprint: 15 OHDSI studies plus 5 non-OHDSI external-validation studies, eight unnamed proprietary LLMs; study-level standardisation accuracy 0.91–0.98 (primary-analysis text) falling to 0.67–0.85 on full methods sections for OHDSI studies and 0.27–0.47 for non-OHDSI full methods; first-run script executability 0.80–1.00 (OHDSI; 0.93–1.00 after self-audit) and 0.60–1.00 (non-OHDSI; 1.00 after self-audit). The JAMIA Open announcement says "up to 95-97% accuracy"; a Dryad record for the data lists 30 studies (15 + 15), so the journal version's evaluation set differs from the preprint's. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.13.26348306), [OHDSI forum](https://forums.ohdsi.org/t/new-publication-in-jamia-open-theseus-an-ai-agent-for-automating-executable-strategus-r-scripts-from-free-text-study-designs/25637), [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.qnk98sfzs)

#### Other systems seen at abstract level
- Inato (Callies, Bodinier, Ravaud, Davarpanah et al.; arXiv 2503.15374, Mar 2025) [PP, VEN] — abstract-only: integration-free multimodal pipeline over unprocessed EHR documents; 93% criterion-level accuracy on n2c2 2018 (288 patients) and 87% in a real-world set of 485 patients from 30 sites against 36 trials; reviewers under 9 minutes per patient ("80% improvement" over manual). Label set, aggregation and review protocol are not on the page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15374)
- Stanford QSU single-patient case (Weng et al.; medRxiv 10.64898/2026.07.06.26354647, 2026-07-10) [PP, ACAD] — abstract-only: zero-shot LLM process vs a manually built human benchmark for one pancreatic cancer patient: sensitivity 81.1%, specificity 89.3%, precision 86.5%; about 73% of discrepancies judged reducible by better prompting and information access. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26354647)
- "The Daily Dose" (Holmes, ... Waddle; arXiv 2605.26346, May 2026) [PP; affiliations not shown on the page] — abstract-only: daily personalised email to radiation-oncology physicians with EHR-derived summaries and automatically flagged trials; one-month anonymous survey, 55 respondents, 83.6% daily or several times a week, usefulness 3.43/5, about 27% estimated at least 10 min/day saved; no accuracy figures. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26346)
- UTSW 2024 predecessor (Beattie et al., Cureus 2024;16(5):e60044) [PR, ACAD]: retrospective on public n2c2 2018 data; LlamaIndex RAG (1,024-token chunks, 10% overlap, ada-002, top-5), per-criterion True/False with evidence; GPT-3.5 Turbo (182 held-out patients, 2,366 criterion judgements) accuracy 0.8081, sensitivity 0.7988, specificity 0.8154, micro-F1 0.7852; GPT-4 on only 40 patients (cost) accuracy 0.8692, micro-F1 0.8589; weak temporal reasoning (MI-6MOS accuracy 0.65), no inter-annotator agreement reported. — [PMC11162699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162699/)
- MCW / OncoLLM deployment [secondary/snippet]: a pilot of OncoLLM in four disease teams since 2024; the ASCO abstract snippet says over 97% of upcoming patients are reviewed each month; I could not open the abstract (HTTP 403) and found no enrolment or accuracy numbers. — [MCW story](https://cancer.mcw.edu/stories/trailblazers-in-innovation), [ASCO abstract](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP.2025.21.10_suppl.144)

### Inferences
Step-by-step comparison (my synthesis of the findings above; every cell is sourced above):

| System | Narrow trial set | Criteria handled as | Evidence from record | Label per criterion | Aggregation | Output unit | Human step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TrialGPT 1 | LLM keywords, BM25+MedCPT, RRF, top 500 | raw inclusion/exclusion lists, 2 calls per pair | note sentences cited by ID | included/not included/not enough info/NA (mirrored for exclusion) | code: 6 percentage scores; LLM: R and S; hand-set sum | ranked trials | expert stays in loop |
| TrialGPT 2.0 | pluggable retrieval only above 1,500 trials | raw criteria, no per-criterion output | not itemised | none (eligible reasons / missing info / ineligible reasons) | LLM fit 0–100 + confidence, code thresholds 90/80 | Highly Recommended / Possible Match / Low Fit | clinician decides, tool is parallel suggestion |

| TrialMatchAI | pre-filter + BM25/vector | each criterion indexed on its own | retrieved context | Met/Not Met/Unclear/Irrelevant | code: composite excluding Unclear/Irrelevant | ranked trials | not specified in v1 |

- Where the papers agree: (1) a cheap first-stage narrowing precedes any LLM reading, always including a structured filter (status, location, age/sex, cancer type; RECTIFIER's structured query; Ferber's MongoDB filter; TrialMatchAI's pre-filter); (2) the record is never put into the LLM whole except in short-note benchmarks — retrieval of top-k chunks per criterion (UTSW 30, Wornow k in {1,3,5,10}, Beattie 5) or a precomputed patient summary (MatchMiner-AI); (3) a rationale and an evidence pointer ride with each answer; (4) the human reads a short, ranked or flagged list and decides.
- Where they differ materially: criteria handling (parsed once vs raw); the label set (2 labels in Wornow vs 3 in Massive Bio vs 6 in UTSW); who aggregates (code in PRISM/Wornow/UTSW/TrialMatchAI; the LLM in TrialGPT 2.0; both in TrialGPT 1); and what is being predicted (eligibility in most; recommendation fit in TrialGPT 2.0, where 83.2% of the system's candidates were "Eligible but Not Recommended").
- The pipelines cluster into three design families: (A) per-criterion reading plus deterministic aggregation (PRISM, Wornow, UTSW, TrialMatchAI, Massive Bio); (B) pair-level holistic LLM judgement (TrialGPT 2.0, MatchMiner-AI's regressor); (C) parse-to-query on structured data (Criteria2Query, EC2Seq2Sql, Wong, THESEUS-style). Family A is the only one that yields a criterion-level audit trail and a place to hold unknown separate from not-met.

### Gaps
- RECTIFIER NEJM AI 2024 full text not read: its question format, per-criterion label set and the rule combining criterion answers into "eligible" are unverified (only secondary summaries). The JAMA RCT page text I fetched did not describe the AI-arm output or the human checkpoint.
- TrialGPT 2.0: supplementary notes (policy contents, prompts) not read; I do not know whether any criterion-level reasoning is hidden inside the single call.
- Inato, Stanford single-patient, Daily Dose, Criteria2Query 3.0, MCW/OncoLLM: abstract or snippet only.
- PRISM's peer-reviewed version and TrialMatchAI's Nature Communications version were not opened; numbers are from the arXiv versions and may differ.
- THESEUS: the JAMIA Open full text was not read; the preprint's 15 + 5 evaluation sets differ from the 30 studies in the Dryad record.
- Ferber: the final ~183 K characters (supplement) not read; how the ratio score is computed is unspecified in the part I read.

## Q2. How is missing information treated (unknown vs not-applicable vs not-met), and is aggregation deterministic or LLM-based?

### Takeaway
There is no shared convention. Three distinct treatments appear: unknown folded into "not met" (Wornow/n2c2, Massive Bio for mandatory criteria), unknown kept as its own label that is excluded from the score (TrialMatchAI, UTSW's validation, Massive Bio's "Requires Clinician Judgment") or marginalised (PRISM), and unknown turned into lower confidence rather than a rejection (TrialGPT 2.0). Papers that aggregate with code can state their rule; the one that aggregates with an LLM (TrialGPT 2.0) states only thresholds on the LLM's own number.

### Cited Findings
- TrialGPT 1 keeps "not enough information" and "not applicable" as two separate labels. "Not applicable" criteria are removed from the denominators (M', N'); "not enough information" is retained as its own percentage feature. The combined ranker penalises only a confirmed unmet inclusion or a confirmed met exclusion. The authors' own error analysis attributes 26.9% of wrong labels to ambiguous label definitions, with the text naming confusion between "not enough information" and "not applicable". — [PMC11574183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183)
- TrialGPT 2.0: missing information lowers the confidence number and is listed under "missing information"; it does not cause an automatic "ineligible" label. Final category is a threshold on the LLM's fit score. — [arXiv 2609.01202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202)
- PRISM: one label "N/A" for both "not applicable to the patient" and "insufficient information"; criteria with N/A sub-questions are resolved by assuming each possible answer equally likely (probability 1/2^N for N unanswered questions) and thresholding the resulting probability at 0.66 / 0.34; a Boolean short-circuit is used where one known answer already decides (Q1 AND Q2 with Q1 = No). Accuracy is reported both with and without questions whose expert answer was N/A (GPT-4 68% vs 72%; OncoLLM 63% vs 66%). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549) (full text read)
- Wornow: binary decision; "NOT MET" if the criterion is not met "or it is impossible to assess given the provided information". Across notes a deterministic per-criterion rule (max, min or most-recent-in-window) combines decisions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125) (full text read)
- n2c2 2018: gold labels "met" / "not met" only; annotators' "possibly met" was resolved into one of the two at adjudication; the paper does not say how absence of documentation is labelled, except that ENGLISH is presumed met unless an interpreter or another language is recorded. — [PMC6798568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6798568)
- TREC CT: three labels deliberately separate "not enough information" (Not Relevant) from "explicitly excluded" (Excluded). Recruitment status and location are never grounds for ineligibility. — [NIST overview](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec31/papers/Overview_trials.pdf)
- TrialMatchAI: "Unclear" and "Irrelevant" are separate classes (inclusion and exclusion); both are left out of the score computation; the criterion-level expert check reports class-wise correctness, e.g. "Unclear" 94.2% and "Irrelevant" 97.4% for inclusion criteria on synthetic data. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08508v1)
- UTSW 2026: "uncertain" and "no documentation found" are two of six labels; a patient's score counts met and likely met; criterion-level accuracy/specificity were computed with uncertain and no-documentation items excluded; a Tier-1 criterion that fails stops Tier-2 criteria from being evaluated at all (authors list this as a limitation because false negatives cannot be corrected). — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- Massive Bio: an undocumented mandatory criterion is "Not Eligible" for that constraint; undated temporal references go to "Requires Clinician Judgment"; those pairs are excluded from the binary F1. Missing structured data accounts for 28% of discordant pairs. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/)
- Ferber: label "unknown" counted as its own category; prompt rules tell the model an unmentioned comorbidity is assumed absent unless an exclusion requires explicit confirmation, but "unknown" if the symptoms make it plausible. Human annotators did the opposite more often: 56 of the 75 revised human answers (74.7%) were physicians who had called a criterion eligible/ineligible when the record lacked the information. The authors argue N/A outputs are less harmful than wrong calls. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13463v1)
- Criteria2Query (2019): no missing-data analysis. — [PMC6402359](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6402359)
- Rules versus LLM aggregation: code aggregation — PRISM (DNF + marginalisation + tier scores), Wornow (max/min/recent), UTSW (percentage met/likely met, tier gate), TrialMatchAI (composite score), Massive Bio (graph rules); LLM aggregation — TrialGPT 2.0 (fit score and confidence), TrialGPT 1's R and S scores (but best result uses both); Ferber says only "scores based on the number and ratio of fulfilled eligibility criteria"; Wong, THESEUS and EC2Seq2Sql let the LLM parse and leave the matching to code/SQL.

### Inferences
- Because "unknown" is the commonest real-world answer (e.g. Massive Bio reports missing structured data as 28% of discordance; Inato attributes its 87% vs 93% gap to records lacking information), any pipeline that collapses unknown into not-met (Wornow, n2c2 scoring) will under-recall eligible patients in practice, and any evaluation that drops unknown items from the metric (UTSW, Massive Bio's F1, PRISM's second column) will overstate accuracy. The metric must report the share of criteria labelled unknown next to accuracy on the decided ones.
- A defensible three-way design that combines the evidence: separate labels for met, not met, unknown (insufficient information) and not applicable (TrialGPT 1, TREC, TrialMatchAI), a rule in code that decides what unknown does to the patient score (PRISM's probabilistic rule, TrialMatchAI's omission, TrialGPT 2.0's confidence discount), and a default that unknown never excludes a patient from the human review list.
- UTSW's Tier-1 hard gate and TrialGPT 2.0's literal protocol reading (3 of 27 prospective cases) are two documented ways a deterministic or literal rule removed a patient that humans kept; both argue for showing near-misses and the reason, not hiding them.

### Gaps
- n2c2's handling of absent documentation is not explicit in the paper; RECTIFIER's treatment of unknown is unverified (NEJM AI not read).
- No paper I read compares code aggregation against LLM aggregation on the same cases except TrialGPT 1's internal comparison (LLM-only eligibility score NDCG@10 0.7252 vs combined 0.7275, nearly equal); I found no head-to-head on real records.

## Q3. How is eligibility parsed once and reused? How are structured data and free text divided?

### Takeaway
The "parse once per trial, reuse for every patient" step exists in PRISM, Wong/Microsoft, Massive Bio, Ferber, MatchMiner-AI, TrialMatchAI (criterion-level index) and the parse-to-query line (Criteria2Query, EC2Seq2Sql, THESEUS-style), and is done by humans in the UTSW deployment; TrialGPT 1 and 2, Wornow and Beattie do not parse. Structured EHR data is used as the first-pass filter or for specific fields; notes are read by retrieval + LLM. Parsed output quality is measured only in a few papers and is the weakest-measured link.

### Cited Findings
- PRISM: per-trial GPT-3.5 "trial-to-questions" module; 50 trials checked by hand: 1% questions missed, 2% malformed, about 89% Boolean logic correct at criterion level; then reused for every patient and for the reverse (trial-to-patients) search, which PRISM calls both directions with one pipeline. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549)
- Wong et al.: GPT-4 structuring into disease state, histology and biomarker inclusion/exclusion in DNF; gold = 53 trials structured by three molecular pathologists; exact reproduction of the full match logic is hard (GPT-4 3-shot F1 29.6 for histology + biomarker conjunctions) even though entity extraction is good (F1 about 65–72); Criteria2Query "delivered subpar performance on oncology trial entities" (F1 32.8 histology, 39.2 biomarker). The authors truncate criteria to the first 40 lines and focus on oncology histology/biomarkers only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02180)
- Criteria2Query (2019) entity-normalisation accuracy 0.514 and logic detection accuracy 0.864 on 177 criteria; trained on Alzheimer's trials only, which the authors say caused failures in other domains. — [PMC6402359](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6402359)
- Massive Bio: monthly ingestion of ClinicalTrials.gov into clauses mapped to NCI Thesaurus, SNOMED CT and MeSH; ontology mismatch causes 10% of discordant pairs. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/). Knowledge-graph upkeep of about 20 curator-hours plus 4 oncologist-hours per month is a figure from a secondary commentary recorded in r3 (not found in the PMC text I read) — [CancerNetwork](https://cancernetwork.com/view/closing-the-trial-access-gap-neuro-symbolic-ai-and-oncology-trial-matching)
- UTSW 2026: parsing is a human onboarding step (2–7 criteria, two tiers, verbatim text plus tips), versioned, and revised after coordinator feedback (27 criteria revised at least once). Structured tables are used where "more reliable" (active medications, latest lab values) per the paper; scanned PDFs are excluded. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- RECTIFIER RCT: eligibility criteria divided into those "reliably determined by structured data queries" (executed first, repeatedly, last run 2024-08-28) and the remainder read by the LLM from notes/PDFs/reports. — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514)
- Ferber: structured MongoDB prefilter on trial fields; criteria split once per trial by GPT-4o (up to two nesting levels) and the same split given to the human annotators. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13463v1)
- TrialMatchAI: criterion-level Elasticsearch index; second-level hybrid search retrieves the criteria most relevant to the patient before the Phi-4 assessment; trials re-indexed with normalised entities. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08508v1)
- MatchMiner-AI: extracts only six core eligibility fields per trial ("space") and checks "boilerplate" exclusions separately; ignores ECOG and measurable-disease criteria in its human annotation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- EC2Seq2Sql: structured EHR tables only; criteria compressed into seven domains, which the authors say drops prior treatment, genetic markers and comorbidities. — [PMC12900307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12900307/)
- TrialGPT 1, Wornow, Beattie and TrialGPT 2.0 hand the criteria text to the LLM at match time (Wornow notes that on n2c2 the one-line criterion definitions were too thin and had to be hand-refined, "increasing the specificity of criteria is an essential first step"; Wornow suggests real protocols could replace that step). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125)

### Inferences
- Parse-once pays off most when (a) the same trial is run against many patients (UTSW: 112 criteria, 26 studies, 39,182 patients; Massive Bio: monthly ingestion) and (b) a human can fix the parse once for everyone; UTSW's design makes the human edit the unit of reuse. Parse errors propagate to every patient, so PRISM's 1%/2%/89% manual check is the minimum audit worth copying — and only PRISM, Wong, Criteria2Query and THESEUS report it.
- The split in practice is "structured data answers numerics, status, demographics, active meds, latest labs; notes answer histology, line of therapy, temporality" (RECTIFIER, UTSW, Ferber); no paper reports a controlled comparison of structured-first against notes-only on the same patients.
- THESEUS is evidence for the general pattern "LLM → constrained JSON schema → deterministic executor", but it is for study design, and its accuracy fell from 0.91–0.98 to 0.27–0.47 (non-OHDSI) as the input moved from a short design description to full methods text; parse quality depends strongly on how much text the LLM must interpret.

### Gaps
- No paper reports how often a parsed criterion set is invalidated by a protocol amendment, or an incremental re-parse policy. Massive Bio says monthly ingestion; UTSW says prompts updated after feedback; nothing quantifies drift.
- Cost and quality of parse-once vs per-pair reading were not compared on the same trials in any paper I read.

## Q4. Real-record versus synthetic-case accuracy; agreement among human experts

### Takeaway
Criterion-level accuracy is high (87–91%) on synthetic or idealised notes (TrialGPT 87.3%, Ferber 88.0%, TrialMatchAI about 89–91% per class) and on the narrow n2c2 task (GPT-4 micro-F1 0.93). On real unstructured records it ranges from 63–68% (PRISM, 720 questions, experts 70%) to 87% (Inato, abstract) to 94% (UTSW, but on criteria with decided labels, among high-ranked patients, after prompt tuning on coordinator feedback). Human experts agree with each other only 64–91% depending on task and sample, so measured "accuracy" is bounded by the reference.

### Cited Findings
Synthetic, idealised or fixed-criterion data:
- TrialGPT: criterion accuracy 0.873 (1,015 pairs, 3 annotators) vs expert 0.887–0.900; patients are synthetic or idealised. — [PMC11574183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183)
- Ferber: 88.0% (1,398/1,589) on 51 synthetic EHRs; 92.7% after re-review of discordant items. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13463v1)
- TrialMatchAI: expert review of 950 synthetic pairs: inclusion "Met" 88.8%, "Not Met" 91.1%; exclusion "Violated" 89.7%, "Not Violated" 96.9%. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08508v1)
- n2c2 2018 (real de-identified diabetic notes, 13 fixed criteria): Wornow GPT-4 overall micro-F1 0.93 vs rule-based 0.91 (macro 0.81 vs 0.75); Beattie GPT-4 accuracy 0.8692 on 40 patients and GPT-3.5 0.8081 on 182; Inato 93% criterion accuracy. — [Wornow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125), [PMC11162699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162699/), [Inato](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15374)

Real clinical records:
- PRISM: 63% (OncoLLM) and 68% (GPT-4) question-level accuracy on 720 questions from 50 cancer patients; experts 70% on 109 double-annotated questions; five-annotator agreement 64%, best two 70%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549)
- Inato [VEN, abstract-only]: 87% on 485 real patients from 30 sites vs 36 trials; authors attribute the gap from 93% on n2c2 to "difficulty replicating human decisions when records lack sufficient information". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15374)
- UTSW 2026: criterion-level accuracy 0.94, sensitivity 0.98, specificity 0.81 (0.71–0.88), NPV 0.93 over 5,096 coordinator-reviewed criteria; the "reference" is the coordinator's label, uncertain and no-documentation items excluded; only patients the pipeline already ranked high were reviewed. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- Massive Bio [VEN]: F1 0.82 (sensitivity 0.8375, specificity 0.8359) at pair level on a held-out 20% of 23,912 pairs; kappa 0.92 between its two oncologists. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/)
- TrialGPT 2.0 NCI set (curated referral notes, 100 cases, 900 pairs, three-class): tool alone 89.0% exact agreement with the consensus; Possible Match recalled in only 27 of 59; clinicians 94.0% (with) / 94.2% (without) — but each clinician's own label was one of the three that built the consensus, so those two figures are not independent. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202)
- Stanford single patient: sensitivity 81.1%, specificity 89.3%, precision 86.5% against a two-author manual benchmark. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26354647)
- MatchMiner-AI: no real-record criterion accuracy; the evaluation uses an LLM judge and 25 dual-annotated pairs (human-human kappa 0.606 on "reasonable consideration", CI 0.199–0.905). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- RECTIFIER (secondary, retrospective, real notes of 1,894 test patients): criterion accuracy 97.9–100% vs study staff 91.7–100%; sensitivity 92.3% vs 90.1% and specificity 93.9% vs 83.6% as reported in a search summary (r3 recorded the Morrison table's staff sensitivity as 90.8%; I did not resolve the difference). — [MGB newsroom](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/ai-screens-heart-failure-patients-for-clinical-trial-eligibility)

Human expert agreement as reported:
- n2c2 2018: average Cohen's kappa 0.54 between two annotators; per-criterion from -0.1 (KETO-1YR, skewed) to 0.63 (MI-6MOS) and 0.37 (ADVANCED-CAD). — [PMC6798568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6798568)
- PRISM: 64% across five annotators, 70% for the two best (109 questions). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549)
- TrialGPT 1: three annotators, consensus rule; individual experts 88.7–90.0% against that consensus. — [PMC11574183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183)
- Ferber: five oncologists, majority vote, no agreement statistic; discordant items re-reviewed by three; "we do not call it ground truth". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13463v1)
- MatchMiner-AI: 91.2% overall (kappa 0.784) over 125 concept pairs, 84.0% (kappa 0.606) on "reasonable consideration". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- Massive Bio: kappa 0.92 (vendor). TREC: assessors' agreement not read. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/)
- Reference-standard problems, in the authors' words: PRISM says enrolled-trial "ground truth" understates performance because patients are eligible for concurrent trials (9 of 10 sampled non-enrolled top-1 trials were eligible); Beattie notes some n2c2 labels appeared mislabelled; Wornow notes 25% of GPT-4's wrong decisions came with a rationale clinicians judged fully correct, "either the ground truth label is incorrect, or there is legitimate variation in clinical judgement". — [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549), [Beattie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162699/), [Wornow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125)
- Typical error causes on real records: temporal reasoning (Beattie MI-6MOS accuracy 0.65; UTSW "timing errors"; Massive Bio 32% of discordance; Wornow "pulling outdated medication and lab values"), laterality (PRISM), literal protocol reading (TrialGPT 2.0), missing information (Massive Bio 28%).

### Inferences
- Numbers from different papers are not comparable: unit (criterion vs pair vs case), label set, whether unknown is scored, and reference (consensus, single coordinator, enrolled trial, LLM judge) all differ. A product team should compare designs, not headline accuracies.
- The higher figures sit on the easiest cuts: short/idealised text (TrialGPT, Ferber), fixed-criterion benchmark (n2c2), decided-label subset and high-score patients (UTSW), annotator reference derived with the tool in view (PRISM pre-selected segments with GPT-3.5; TrialGPT 2.0 consensus including assisted reviewers).
- Human-human agreement of roughly 64–70% (PRISM), 84% (MatchMiner-AI on "reasonable consideration", n=25, wide CI) and kappa 0.54 (n2c2) suggests criterion-level accuracy above about 90% against a single reference cannot be distinguished from the reference's noise without double annotation of at least a sample.

### Gaps
- No paper I read reports per-criterion-type accuracy on real records for a system with a stated unknown rate and a fixed denominator including unknowns.
- RECTIFIER's per-criterion sensitivity and specificity on real notes (NEJM AI) not verified from the primary text.

## Q5. What human checkpoints exist in real deployments, and what workflow outcomes were measured?

### Takeaway
Every deployed or prospective system keeps a human decision after the model; none lets the model exclude a patient silently. The only randomised evidence is RECTIFIER (faster screening, equal eligibility among completed screens, more enrolments, single centre). TrialGPT 2.0 and UTSW report screening-time and yield measures; none of the other deployments measured enrolment.

### Cited Findings
- RECTIFIER RCT (MGB, heart-failure trial, May 31–Sep 28, 2024): primary outcome time to eligibility determination, subdistribution HR 1.78 (95% CI 1.54–2.06); eligible 458/2,242 (20.4%) AI vs 284/2,234 (12.7%) manual; among completed screens eligibility equal, 20.8% (458/2,205) vs 21.1% (284/1,347), P = .86; more than 99% of completed AI screens determined within 15 days vs 50 days; 37 vs 887 still unscreened at the end; enrolment (exploratory) 35 (1.6%) vs 19 (0.9%), HR 1.79 (1.02–3.15). Single centre, one use case. Prospective accuracy was not reported. — [JAMA 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514)
- TrialGPT 2.0: counterbalanced crossover on 100 NCI cases showed trial-level decision time 129.8 to 58.4 s per patient (-55.0%) and fewer severe over-recommendations when assisted (1 vs 14); prospective tumour-board run: cases with at least one final recommendation 11 to 21 (+90.9%), the board decides after seeing both lists; reviewer questionnaire favourable but the lowest scores were perceived efficiency (3.85/5) and decision confidence (3.92/5). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202)
- UTSW 2026: coordinators review every AI-flagged patient before provider outreach; per-criterion verdicts recorded; discordance adjudicated; reminders at 4 days; reviewed 55.1% of 60–80%-score patients vs 93.7% of ≥80%-score patients; prescreen success 19.1% vs 43.5%; cost $0.12 per patient; enrolment, accrual, time per patient not measured. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- Massive Bio: about 15% of pairs prioritised for human review; median time about 120 to about 30 minutes per patient (15 automated + 15 clinician review); enrolment not measured; method of timing not described. — [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/)
- TrialGPT 1 user study: 42.6% time saved; accuracy 97.2% vs 91.7% (not significant); 2 annotators, 6 vignettes. — [PMC11574183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574183)
- Inato [VEN]: reviewers under 9 minutes per patient (abstract). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15374)
- MatchMiner-AI: six-month pilot with four oncologists, then eight; feedback logged in a supplement; no outcome numbers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- Daily Dose: 55-respondent survey after one month; satisfaction associated with perceived time saved; no matching accuracy reported. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26346)
- MCW OncoLLM pilot: over 97% of upcoming patients reviewed each month (ASCO abstract snippet); no enrolment data found. — [MCW](https://cancer.mcw.edu/stories/trailblazers-in-innovation)
- RECTIFIER NEJM AI authors recommended keeping a clinician's final check of AI-eligible patients (secondary). — [MGB newsroom](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/ai-screens-heart-failure-patients-for-clinical-trial-eligibility)

### Inferences
- Time-saved figures use different denominators: TrialGPT 2.0's 55.0% excludes reading the patient context; Massive Bio's 15 + 15 minutes has no stated method; RECTIFIER's outcome is time to a determination across a whole list (the AI arm simply finished far more screens). They are not interchangeable.
- The RECTIFIER pattern (same eligible yield among completed screens, 37 vs 887 patients left unscreened, about 24 times fewer) suggests the benefit is throughput, not a different eligibility decision; enrolment gain is exploratory (CI lower bound 1.02).
- The checkpoints that recur: human confirms every flagged candidate before outreach (UTSW, RECTIFIER authors' recommendation, TrialGPT 2.0), human resolves a model-human conflict with a written reason (UTSW), human-approved and versioned prompt/criteria changes (UTSW, TrialGPT 2.0's "new system version needs local revalidation").

### Gaps
- No deployment reports patient-level harms (wrongly screened out and later found eligible) except through proxies; TrialGPT 2.0's "Low Fit" recall (92.8%) and its 8 severe over-recommendations are the nearest.
- RECTIFIER RCT: the fetched page did not describe who confirmed eligibility in the AI arm; the prior r3 note says staff did a short chart review of AI-flagged candidates, which I could not reconfirm this session.
- Enrolment effect beyond one trial is unknown.

## Q6. Which evaluation design is credible for a small team (patient-grouped splits, metrics, sample sizes)?

### Takeaway
The credible designs in the literature are (1) a randomised or counterbalanced comparison of the human workflow with and without the tool on the same cases (RECTIFIER RCT; TrialGPT 2.0 NCI crossover), (2) criterion-level labels double-annotated on a sample with an agreement statistic and bootstrap intervals clustered by patient (MatchMiner-AI, UTSW, TrialGPT 2.0), and (3) a system fixed before inference and versioned (TrialGPT 2.0). Few papers say how patients are grouped across development and test; none of the LLM-only pipelines I read states a patient-grouped split protocol explicitly except n2c2's patient-level 70/30 split.

### Cited Findings
- n2c2 2018 splits by patient (202 train / 86 test; each patient 2–5 notes). — [PMC6798568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6798568)
- EC2Seq2Sql splits its benchmark 80/10/10 by trial; Massive Bio reports an 80/20 split without naming the unit; MatchMiner-AI uses non-overlapping random trial samples (3,000 for training, 500 for evaluation). — [PMC12900307](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12900307/), [PMC13091143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091143/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- TrialGPT 2.0: system versioned (code, prompt, backbone, decoding settings, retrieval configuration, trial corpus, matching policy fixed before inference); for retrospective cases the policy is tuned only with clinician feedback from other cases; bootstrap 95% CIs over cases (10,000 resamples), Wilcoxon signed-rank for paired timing; counterbalanced assistance (each of two clinicians reviews opposite 50-case halves); fourth clinician breaks ties; main metric chosen as hit rate@K; "substantive changes ... should be treated as a new system version and undergo local revalidation". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202)
- MatchMiner-AI: 2,000-sample cluster bootstrap over the 25 dual-annotated units, because five concepts per unit are not independent; kappa CIs very wide (reasonableness 0.606, 0.199–0.905). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17228)
- RECTIFIER RCT: 1:1 randomisation, blocked, after each structured query; staff crossed over; time-to-event with ineligibility as competing risk; CONSORT-AI. Two denominators for eligibility rate shown (all randomised vs completed screens) and they disagree in significance. — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514)
- UTSW 2026: weighted bootstrap 95% CIs; sensitivity/specificity only on reviewed (high-ranked) patients; prompts were revised after coordinator feedback while performance was being measured. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full)
- Systematic review (r3's Morrison et al., 2025): recommends criterion-level accuracy/sensitivity/specificity/F1, ranking NDCG@10 / P@10, rationale accuracy, citations, human-in-the-loop studies and private test sets to avoid LLM memorisation of public benchmarks. — [arXiv 2509.19327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19327) (not re-read)
- Metrics used by TrialGPT 2.0 beyond accuracy: hit rate@K (at least one clinician-recommended trial in top K), recommended precision@K, eligible precision@K, severe over-/under-recommendation counts (Highly Recommended vs Low Fit reversals), class-specific recall. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01202)
- Audit of rationales as its own sample: Wornow stratified 20 correct + 20 incorrect predictions per criterion (486 rationales) and PRISM rated final-answer accuracy, explanation accuracy and citation correctness. — [Wornow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05125), [PRISM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15549)

### Inferences
(All of this section is my reasoning from the findings above, not a claim by a paper.)
- Split by patient, never by criterion row, for anything tuned (prompt tips, thresholds, fine-tuned or distilled models); when criteria are parsed once per trial, also hold out trials for the parse evaluation. Papers that revised prompts after feedback (UTSW, Wornow's hand-refined n2c2 definitions) report test metrics from the same cases that informed the revisions.
- Report, per criterion class, accuracy on decided items together with the share labelled unknown, and the false-exclusion rate (eligible patient rated ineligible) of the patient-level rule on a sample that includes low-scoring patients; UTSW's reviewed set (score at least 50%) cannot estimate that.
- Sample size: for a proportion near 0.90 a 95% interval half-width of 5 points needs about 139 independent items (1.96^2 x 0.9 x 0.1 / 0.05^2) and 3 points about 385; criteria within a patient are correlated, so count patients, use patient-clustered bootstrap (MatchMiner-AI, TrialGPT 2.0), and treat 25 double-annotated pairs (kappa CI 0.199–0.905) as too few to estimate agreement. TrialGPT 1's 105 pairs and PRISM's 109 double-annotated questions are about the smallest agreement samples that still gave usable intervals.
- A small team can copy the cheap versions of the credible designs: a counterbalanced with/without-tool review of the same 50–100 cases by two reviewers (TrialGPT 2.0 NCI design; time per case recorded), a fixed system version, a third reviewer for disagreements, and a prospective shadow run in which the tool and the usual process run in parallel and the final list is attributed by source (TrialGPT 2.0 POTB: 37 of 54 final picks tool-only).
- Because the reference noise is high, add one dual-annotation stratum and report agreement with kappa and percent (n2c2, PRISM, MatchMiner-AI all show the ceiling is not 100%).

### Gaps
- No paper I read pre-registered a non-inferiority margin for false exclusion; none reports a power calculation for a matching accuracy study.
- Whether public benchmarks (TREC, SIGIR, n2c2) are contaminated for current LLMs is asserted in the systematic review but I found no measurement.
- I did not locate TREC 2023 or a post-2022 shared task with real patient records.
