## Q1. Target trial emulation: mandatory protocol components, order, and empirical agreement with RCTs

### Takeaway
Every TTE source orders the work the same way: causal question -> a written protocol of the hypothetical randomised trial (7 components) -> operationalise each component in the database -> time zero = eligibility met and strategy assigned -> confounding adjustment under stated identifying assumptions -> control analyses -> report with TARGET's 21 items. Empirical agreement with RCTs is high only when the target trial can be closely emulated: RCT-DUPLICATE r = 0.93 (16 closely emulated trials) against 0.53 (the other 16), and only about 15% of 220 high-impact trials were judged replicable with available real-world data at all.

### Cited Findings

**A. Hernán & Robins 2016 [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH]** - the protocol components, in the order of the paper's Table 1:
- 1 eligibility criteria; 2 treatment strategies; 3 assignment procedures; 4 follow-up period (starts at randomisation, ends at event/death/loss/administrative limit); 5 outcome; 6 causal contrasts (intention-to-treat and per-protocol); 7 analysis plan. Source: [Hernán & Robins, Am J Epidemiol 2016;183:758-764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/) (PubMed 26994063, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26994063/).
- Emulation sequence in the paper: (a) validate data and apply the same eligibility criteria at baseline (criteria cannot use post-baseline events); (b) assign eligible people to the strategy their baseline data match, comparing initiators because "comparisons of prevalent users with never users does not correspond to any contrast between treatment strategies" that a trial could compare; (c) adjust for all confounders needed for exchangeability (matching, PS, stratification, regression, standardisation, IPW, g-estimation, doubly robust); (d) outcome from the database, "independent outcome validation is often warranted"; (e) ITT approximated by comparing initiators, per-protocol needs g-methods. Same source.
- Time zero: "The best way to emulate time zero of the target trial is to define time zero" as the point when an eligible person initiates a strategy; "starting after randomization could result in selection bias"; with repeated eligibility use one eligible time or emulate "multiple nested trials". Grace periods: assign by cloning (two copies, censor each clone when data stop matching its strategy) with IPW for the informative censoring; with cloning an ITT effect "cannot be emulated". Same source.
- Checks named in the paper: reversed strategies (surprising estimates suggest an emulation failed), outcome controls, treatment controls. Same source.
- When emulation is not possible (the paper's own list): database lacks baseline confounders ("successful emulation of the target trial's random assignment is not possible"); blinded outcome ascertainment; placebo-controlled trial with tight adherence monitoring; people whose claims vanish must be censored not excluded; and "it is generally impossible to determine whether the emulation failed because of uncontrolled confounding". Same source.

**B. TARGET reporting guideline [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH]** - 21 items, six sections, BMJ 2025;390:e087179, simultaneous JAMA version ([BMJ page](https://www.bmj.com/content/390/bmj-2025-087179); [EQUATOR 22462](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/22462/)). The order that matters for a product:
- Methods item 5 (each data source: original purpose, type, setting, period, linkage, quality metrics) comes BEFORE item 6.
- Item 6 target trial specification: 6a eligibility; 6b treatment strategies "detailed enough to implement"; 6c assignment (eligible individuals randomly assigned, may know allocation); 6d follow-up starts at assignment, when it ends; 6e outcomes, how and when measured, main one; 6f causal contrasts, competing events, effect measures; 6g assumptions needed to identify each estimand; 6h analysis plan per estimand (missing data, confounding adjustment, models, sensitivity analyses).
- Item 7 emulation: 7a how eligibility was operationalised "and any imperfections"; 7b strategies incl. codes and adherence information; 7c baseline classification into strategies incl. cloning; 7d time zero incl. the start for no-action comparators; 7e outcomes incl. codes, algorithms, validation; 7f contrasts and competing events; 7g(i) identifying assumptions (exchangeability given baseline variables, censoring, non-adherence, competing events) and 7g(ii) how those variables were defined and ascertained; 7h(i) analysis modifications, models, software; 7h(ii) sensitivity and additional analyses "e.g., negative controls, quantitative bias analysis".
- Results 8-14: counts assessed/eligible/assigned (flow diagram, unique individuals reported separately from clones), baseline by strategy before and after adjustment, follow-up and censoring by strategy, missingness per variable, outcome frequency incl. absolute risks over time, effect estimates (absolute and relative), all sensitivity results. Discussion 15-16: interpret in light of precision and identifying assumptions; limitations = differences between target trial and emulation, data-mapping problems, plausibility of assumptions incl. unmeasured confounding. Other 17-21 (ethics, registration, data/code sharing, funding, conflicts) - listed by topic only.
- Item 1a: state the study is "an observational emulation of a target trial", avoiding the wording "emulated trial". Scope per search summary: parallel-group, individually randomised target trials with baseline-confounder adjustment ([SEC, same two links]).

**C. RCT-DUPLICATE, Wang SV et al., JAMA 2023;329(16):1376-1385 [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH]** ([PMC10130954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130954/)):
- Process in order: extract PICOT from each RCT -> build claims-based analogues of each element -> feasibility analyses (power, balance on key confounders, standardised differences < 0.1 after matching; "No treatment-stratified outcome data were examined at this stage") -> prespecify primary/secondary analyses including control outcomes -> register each protocol on ClinicalTrials.gov after feasibility and before exposure-outcome analyses -> implement -> compare. Selection required that treatment, comparator, outcome and key criteria be measurable in claims and that the database study have power at least equal to the RCT.
- Design: 32 trials (30 completed, 2 ongoing), new-user (initiator) cohorts, active comparators (a no-effect active new-use comparator as placebo proxy in 10 placebo trials), PS matching on more than 100 pre-exposure characteristics with a 1% caliper, Cox models, Optum Clinformatics, IBM MarketScan and Medicare data. Time zero was "not generally specified" in the summary; for the 3 hospital-initiated antiplatelet trials it was first dispensing after discharge.
- Results (n = 32 / closely emulated 16 / less close 16): Pearson r 0.82 (0.64-0.91) / 0.93 (0.79-0.97) / 0.53 (0.00-0.83); statistical-significance agreement 75% / 94% / 56%; estimate agreement 66% / 88% / 50%; standardised-difference agreement 75% / 88% / 69%; Cohen kappa 0.57 / 0.89 / 0.31. Mean difference in log HR 0.01 (95% CI -0.38 to 0.39). 35 negative/positive control outcomes, 29 (83%) met expectations. (The r3-academic.md note that a JAMA erratum corrected significance agreement to 72% is not reproduced on the PMC page I read.)
- "Closely emulated" was a post hoc label: none of (follow-up started in hospital; run-in selecting responders to one arm; randomisation mixed with stopping baseline maintenance therapy; delayed treatment effect), and comparator and outcome emulation at least moderate with one rated good. Authors' limits: assumes each RCT is internally valid; agreement can come from offsetting errors; limited generalisability from the multi-step trial selection; many tightly controlled trials cannot be emulated in databases.

**D. Feasibility ceiling: Bartlett et al., JAMA Netw Open 2019 [PR; SEC]** - of 220 trials in high-impact journals (2017) only 15% could feasibly be replicated with currently available real-world data; only 39.1% had an intervention that could be established from observational data; the commonest blockers were unapproved new drugs (34.1% of trials) and educational/behavioural/procedural interventions (14.1%) ([PMC6802419](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6802419) listed in search; figures from the [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/focus-of-the-week/replicating-rcts-with-realworld-data-is-unlikely-for-most-trials) and [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/researchers-find-15-of-trials-could-be-replicated.html) result snippets).

**E. BenchExCal (Benchmark, Expand, Calibrate)** - Wang SV, Russo M, Glynn RJ, Bradley MC, He J, Concato J, Schneeweiss S, Clin Pharmacol Ther 2025;117(6):1820-1828, PMID 40067205, DOI 10.1002/cpt.3621 [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY]. The paper's own title is "...Design and Process for a Planned Empirical Evaluation": it proposes the approach and describes how it will be evaluated by simulation and five planned empirical examples; it does not report agreement performance ([PubMed search page](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=BenchExCal)).
- Workflow in the R package vignette (benchexcal 0.1.0, MIT licence, hosted on an individual's r-universe, so not a CRAN-checked package; its authorship relation to the CPT paper was not verified) [third-party software documentation]: (1) Stage 1 benchmark: `agreement()` on a completed RCT and its RWE emulation with SA (significance agreement), EA (RWE HR inside the RCT 95% CI) and SD (standardised difference, agreement when |z| < 1.96); (2) Stage 1 divergence on the log-HR scale xi1 = log(HR_rwe1) - log(HR_rct1); (3) Stage 2 calibration for the new indication: xi2 = sqrt(var2*/var1*) x xi1 and theta2_cal = log(HR_rwe2) - xi2, with an inflated variance; (4) `tipping_point()` on the calibration; (5) forest plot. It needs three HR + 95% CI sets (RCT1, RWE1, RWE2). Worked example: xi1 = -0.0592, scale factor 1.301, xi2 = -0.0770; uncalibrated stage-2 HR 0.850 (0.780-0.930) becomes 0.918 (0.760-1.109); tipping point xi2 = -0.0735 against a prior SD 0.0851, "Conclusion is SENSITIVE to plausible Stage 1 divergence". The vignette frames the wider calibrated CI as "the bias-variance trade-off that BenchExCal makes explicit" ([vignette](https://xxue064.r-universe.dev/benchexcal/doc/benchexcal-intro.html), first 100,000 of 196,913 characters read; it does not define the variance terms in prose and states no full assumption list).
- Automation: TrialCalibre (arXiv 2604.25832 [PP]) says BenchExCal is "resource-intensive and difficult to scale" and proposes a multi-agent system ([arXiv PDF listed in search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25832)); a NeurIPS 2026 listing "TrialCalibre1" reports a routing dev set with 10/10 route matches and 7/7 applicable reference-trial matches (search snippet only, [listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175506)); r3-academic.md already notes no RCT agreement was reported for it.

### Inferences
- A product comparator workflow that stops at "PS + Cox" is missing, at minimum, the protocol-first items: the written 7-component target trial, a feasibility step that looks at data availability but not at treatment-stratified outcomes, registration/freeze before outcome analysis, and control outcomes. RCT-DUPLICATE and TARGET together make "freeze, then run" and "report imperfect operationalisation" part of the method, not packaging.
- The 0.93 vs 0.53 split is the strongest published number for a user-facing statement: agreement was good only for designs without the four listed problems, and the split is post hoc. A tool should not display "RCT-level agreement" as a property of the method.
- BenchExCal is a promising calibration idea whose published evidence is still a plan; its formula transports one indication's divergence to another by a variance scale factor, an assumption the vignette does not spell out. Treat it as sensitivity/benchmark output, not as a bias correction a user can rely on.

### Gaps
- TARGET items 17-21 and the exact JAMA citation were not verified from the checklist text (fetch listed them by topic only).
- RCT-DUPLICATE: the fetched summary said time zero was "not generally specified" (it gave only the hospital-initiated exception); the exact time-zero rules per trial were not extracted.
- Bartlett 2019 abstract/trade coverage only; the primary text was not opened.
- No peer-reviewed BenchExCal performance result was found in two searches on 2026-11-01 (one for the framework, one for "empirical evaluation results"); only the CPT 2025 design paper, the R package and the TrialCalibre agent papers surfaced.

## Q2. External control arms: regulator sequences, what comes first, what makes the route fail

### Takeaway
Regulators put a suitability decision before any statistics: FDA (2023 draft) asks whether an external control can credibly separate drug effect from natural history, prognostic differences, lack of blinding and concomitant therapy, and says that in many situations the answer is no and another design should be chosen; EMA (2024) and CDE (2025 No. 54, 2026 No. 27) say the same for single-arm and historical controls and require large, clear effects, well-understood natural history and prespecified conservative thresholds. The 2026 causal review (arXiv 2605.03282, FDA-affiliated co-authors, preprint) formalises the sequence as a six-step roadmap and names covariate shift and outcome drift as the two biases.

### Cited Findings

**A. FDA draft guidance, Feb 2023, [REG; READ-FULL]** ([PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download); [landing page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-design-and-conduct-externally-controlled-trials-drug-and-biological-products)). Draft status on 2026-10-25 is as reported in r3-academic.md; I did not re-check it today. The document's own order is the sequence:
1. Suitability first (Section II): "In many situations, however, the likelihood of credibly demonstrating the effectiveness of a drug of interest with an external control is low, and sponsors should choose a more suitable design, regardless of the prevalence of disease"; sponsors should decide whether evidence can distinguish the drug effect from natural history, prognostic differences, lack of blinding and differences in concomitant therapy. Footnote 14: "A non-inferiority approach is not recommended using an externally controlled trial design." Footnote 17: unsuitable when natural history is not understood sufficiently "or when the disease course is considered well-understood but is variable". Natural-history-driven endpoints (objective response in oncology) are the cited favourable case.
2. Protocol before data: "Sponsors should finalize a study protocol before initiating the externally controlled trial, including selection of the external control arm and analytic approach, rather than selecting an external control arm after the completion of a single-arm trial"; prespecify data sources, eligibility, exposure definitions and windows, endpoints, analytic plans, bias minimisation. Footnote 18: document every data source accessed and the feasibility evaluations, justify selection and exclusion, show the final analytic dataset "was not chosen to favor particular study results", keep audit trails.
3. Estimand framework (ICH E9(R1)): population, treatment and comparator, outcome, intercurrent events, summary measure.
4. Feasibility of confounder capture: before deciding the design is suitable, "sponsors should confirm that recognized, important prognostic characteristics can be assessed in the data sources"; effect size: "more likely to provide convincing results when the effect size on a well-characterized outcome of interest is anticipated to be large".
5. Populations, treatment attributes (dose, timing, adherence, concomitant therapy; if care-delivery factors cannot be accounted for "a different design approach (e.g., randomized controlled trial) should be considered"), index date/time zero (immortal time worked example; discrete clinical event such as MI or heart-failure hospitalisation makes a good shared index date; "when the event ... is not discrete and readily identifiable ... determining a suitable index date can be difficult or may not be possible"; no-treatment comparators are "especially challenging"), outcomes (blinded assessment, re-adjudication, same timing and criteria in both arms; RECIST-type endpoints hard in EHR data).
6. Data comparability table with ten rows: time periods, geographic region, diagnosis, prognosis, treatments, other treatment-related factors, follow-up periods, intercurrent events, outcome, missing data. Using a completed trial whose results are already known as the control is "especially problematic". "availability of a dataset containing patients with the disease of interest does not guarantee that there is sufficient information on relevant clinical characteristics".
7. Analysis (Section III.C): SAP prespecified and submitted before enrolment; design and SAP "blinded to any observed external control data" except planned feasibility analyses of variable availability and missingness; changes date-stamped with rationale; no recommended method, but "adding complexity ... usually requires making additional assumptions, which often cannot be substantiated"; balance judged against an a priori threshold (footnote 30: "FDA does not endorse a single approach", standardised mean difference given as an example); "Especially when the anticipated effect size is modest, an externally controlled trial may not be an appropriate study design"; a priori quantitative or qualitative bias analyses; missing-data strategy with sensitivity analyses; misclassification; a proportional-hazards sensitivity analysis; prespecified subgroup analyses.
8. Regulatory contact and data: early discussion giving (1) why the design is appropriate, (2) data sources and fitness, (3) planned analyses, (4) data-submission plan; marketing applications must contain patient-level data for both treatment and external control arms.

**B. EMA reflection paper on single-arm trials, EMA/CHMP/458061/2024, CHMP 9 Sep 2024 [REG; READ-FULL]** ([PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-establishing-efficacy-based-single-arm-trials-submitted-pivotal-evidence-marketing-authorisation-application_en.pdf)):
- Scope: does not address building an external control outside the trial protocol, but where a SAT is contrasted with one "the present paper fully applies"; prospectively designed non-randomised external arms "may not be considered SATs, but key considerations ... may still apply".
- Core logic: a SAT observes outcomes only under the investigational treatment, so "knowledge external to the SAT about what would have happened ... (i.e. the counterfactual) is necessary". "Isolation of treatment effect": a SAT isolates it only if individual outcomes are observed that "could not occur without effective treatment" within the follow-up window; then a causal interpretation is conceptually possible despite the design.
- Where the effect cannot be isolated: success is a justified, prespecified threshold that the lower/upper confidence limit must pass; external-information thresholds carry "inherent risk of erroneous conclusions"; "a conservative threshold should be chosen", e.g. the confidence-interval limit rather than the point estimate of the external information; "scenarios without unambiguous isolation of the treatment effect on the individual level represent a critical risk for the interpretation of a SAT".
- Analysis: SAP before the first patient; all initiators in the primary analysis; "the method of estimation is of utmost importance, as the distribution of covariates is by design not calibrated against a control"; one cannot "disentangle prognostic from predictive effects" in a SAT; strong prognostic factors "strengthen the need for a randomised concurrent control"; biomarker-defined populations need very strong external knowledge of the natural course.

**C. China (CDE/NMPA).**
- 2025 No. 54, single-arm trials supporting routine approval of anti-tumour drugs, signed 2025-12-29 [REG; READ-FULL, xfdyb.com mirror]: ([PDF](https://www.xfdyb.com/uploadfile/ueditor/file/20260106/1767670029501034.pdf); notice [ccfdie.org](https://www.ccfdie.org/cn/yjxx/yphzp/webinfo/2026/01/1759934909271867.htm)). A SAT may be considered as a confirmatory study only if all four hold, and meeting them "does not mean it will certainly be accepted" (encouraged to consult CDE): (1) an indication where an RCT is hard (very rare tumour, low-frequency mutation, pan-tumour); (2) "外部对照数据清晰" - "SAT 设计不设立平行对照，但是其实质是与外部数据（通常为历史数据）的对照研究. 只有在外部对照数据充分可靠的情况下，才有可能提升 SAT 结果的可靠性"; historical control "应来自高级别循证医学证据，应具备相近年代、相似疾病背景和足够的样本量" and its reliability must be analysed; (3) clear mechanism of action; (4) outstanding efficacy. Order: determine the target population first, then derive the historical control and its result. Time-to-event endpoints "很难解读，且与历史对照进行比较时容易产生偏差", so response rates are typically the SAT endpoint; confirmation needs longer follow-up for survival benefit and larger sample size for robustness, via a "composite SATs" mode or a "sole SAT" mode.
- 2026 No. 27, clinical evaluation for clinical trial applications, signed 2026-03-25 [REG; as read in r2-china.md, not re-read]: s.2.4 "多数情况下不建议使用历史对照。仅在有限情况下 ... 该疾病自然病史已公认且可被高度预期，治疗效应足够明显以至于对其它因素引入的偏差不足以引起错误判断" ([PDF](https://www.cnpharm.com/upload/resources/file/2026/04/13/187383.pdf), [notice](https://m.cnpharm.com/c/2026-04-13/1094856.shtml)).
- 2020 No. 1, RWE for R&D [REG; as read in r2-china.md, not re-read]: external control mainly for single-arm trials, "平行外部对照设计要优于历史对照"; lists limitations (care settings, technology drift, diagnostic criteria, outcome measurement, baseline differences, data quality); methods "倾向评分（PS）方法，虚拟匹配对照方法" plus "敏感性分析和偏倚的定量分析" ([mirror PDF](http://srsrmyy.cn/uploads/files/20211224/ffa4170eaa8552befcf040d5c741ea9d.pdf)).
- 2026 No. 6 (Bayesian borrowing) is in Q5.

**D. Causal review, Zhu, Izem, Yang, Yuan, Pang, van der Laan, Nie, Emir, Mishra-Kalyani, Lee, Yang; arXiv 2605.03282, 5 May 2026 [PP; READ-FULL]** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)). Affiliations on the first page: NC State, Duke, Novartis, MD Anderson, Genentech, Pfizer, and FDA CDER Office of Biostatistics (three authors); an author disclaimer says views are not necessarily the agency's, and the project was FDA-supported. Mixed academic/industry/regulator authorship, not peer reviewed.
- Six-step roadmap (Figure 1) for both single-arm+EC (SAT+EC) and hybrid controlled trials (HCT): Step 1 scientific question and estimand -> 2 observed data structure -> 3 identifiability assumptions -> 4 identification -> 5 statistical models, estimation and inference -> 6 sensitivity analysis. A first operational step before the roadmap is "define a comparable EC cohort through eligibility alignment".
- Two biases: covariate shift (baseline covariate differences between trial and EC) and outcome drift (outcome differences between trial controls and EC after conditioning on covariates).
- Estimand: often ATT when the target population is anchored at eligibility in the SAT; ATT may equal ATE if the EC emulates the target-trial control without extra criteria. Target trial emulation and the ICH estimand framework are both named as ways to sharpen the question.
- Data structure rules (Step 2): data "fit-for-purpose, with Y and X observed, reliable, and consistently defined across sources"; missingness in Y reported as a data-quality assessment; covariates chosen at design time (literature, DAGs); avoid instruments (associated with treatment, not prognostic); exclude post-baseline variables on the causal path.
- SAT+EC identification (Assumption 1): consistency/no interference; no unmeasured confounders given X; positivity ("assessed through covariate balance and overlap in estimated propensity score distributions"). "In practice, Assumptions 1(ii)-(iii) are often vulnerable when combining SAT and EC sources because between source differences in latent factors such as concurrency, measurement error, or follow-up intensity can introduce confounding." Estimation then "reduces to a standard causal inference problem targeting the ATT"; "simulations to evaluate the finite sample size performance ... is warranted when the SAT+EC is the primary basis of efficacy in small population settings".
- Sensitivity (Step 6): prespecify; (i) robustness to design/estimation specification (eligibility alignment, outcome definition, follow-up window, intercurrent events, summary measure, model specification); (ii) violations of identifiability: a causal gap parameter delta with tau(delta) = tau_hat - delta, tipping point = smallest |delta| whose shifted interval includes zero; E-values; negative control outcomes/exposures; two-level frameworks. These "illustrate quantitative assessment of impact of departure from an assumption rather than its validation".
- HCT (Section 4): RCT population anchors inference; adds Assumption 3 (conditional mean exchangeability of RCT controls and EC) and 4 (sampling positivity); because the RCT has an internal control arm, Assumption 3 "is, in principle, testable" but "in finite samples, such comparisons may be noisy". Lessons: "borrowing methods cannot compensate for the absence of randomization when unmeasured confounding is substantial"; "efficiency and robustness are inherently in tension"; sensitivity analysis is "a core inferential component". Section 4.7 of the review lists 19 R packages (Table 3).

**E. Empirical performance of real-world external controls against the RCT control (what "fails").**
- Quantitative bias analysis in aNSCLC, Gupta A, Hsu G, Kent S, ... Hernán MA, JAMA Netw Open 2025;8(3):e252152 [PR; page summary] ([record](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/quantitative-bias-analysis-for-single-arm-trials-with-external-co/)): trials after 2011, sample sizes 52-830 per group; the page says 15 comparisons in one place and 14 trials in another. Mean difference in log HR (original control vs ECA): 0.247 unadjusted (ratio of HRs 1.36); 0.139 after measured-confounder adjustment (1.22); 0.098 after additional external adjustment for unmeasured/mismeasured confounders (1.17). Conclusion: QBA informative where residual confounding is the main expected bias, with high adherence, few losses and no competing events.
- Friends of Cancer Research multi-partner pilot [POSTER, ISPOR 2026, authors from data vendors and pharma; layout partly scrambled] ([poster](https://friendsofcancerresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/ECA-Pilot-Project-Methods-Poster-ISPOR-2026.pdf)): nine partners built ECAs for the RESOLVE control arm (metastatic pancreatic cancer, first-line gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel, trial control N = 213, median OS 10.78 months) from a shared SAP; ATT weighting by logistic PS; "Hazard ratio (HR) estimates ranged from ~0.9 to 1.4 versus RESOLVE, with greater divergence in cohorts with smaller sample sizes, greater missingness, or less complete covariate balance"; individual HRs shown include 1.41, 1.09, 1.27, 1.05, 0.9. Conclusion: PS methods improved alignment "but covariate balance depended on data completeness, cohort size, and availability of key prognostic factors, underscoring the need to assess data fitness before interpreting ECA results".
- Carrigan et al., Clin Pharmacol Ther 2020 [PR; SEC] - EHR-derived control arms for NSCLC trials: HRs from ECA aligned closely with the RCT with one exception ([RTI listing](https://rti.org/publication/potential-using-external-control-arms-derived-electronic-health-records-replace-control)); a later atezolizumab replication found control-vs-external HRs closer to 1 for PFS than OS and a colorectal case showed HR 0.85 (Flatiron) vs 1.01 (RCT control) (same search result set; abstract-level).
- Incerti et al., arXiv 2110.03827 [PP; affiliations not on the abstract page I read]: meta-analytic framework estimating bias from historical trials' ECAs and adjusting a new ECA log HR; in the NSCLC example external controls had lower survival than trial controls (HR 0.907); R package ecmeta ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03827)); the abstract page gave no adjusted HR.

### Inferences
- The regulator order is: (1) is an external-control design defensible at all (natural history, effect size, endpoint, outcome blinding, direction of claim; superiority only for FDA), (2) protocol and data-source selection frozen before outcome data are seen, (3) estimand and time zero, (4) data-fitness table, (5) balance with an a priori threshold, (6) bias analysis, (7) patient-level submission. A product that offers a hazard ratio before steps 1-4 have a recorded answer inverts the order that FDA, EMA and CDE all use.
- All three agencies tie a good ECT to a large effect on an objective, natural-history-stable endpoint. A modest effect, a subjective endpoint or a non-inferiority claim should lead a tool to say "not an appropriate design", not to compute a weaker version.
- The empirical numbers (ratio of HRs 1.36 -> 1.17; HR 0.9-1.4 across nine partners) give a calibrated sense of the residual error a user should expect: roughly 17-36% on the HR scale in oncology OS even with good data, larger when sample size, missingness or covariate completeness are poor.

## Q3. Indirect comparison: MAIC, STC, anchored vs unanchored, and KM reconstruction

### Takeaway
NICE DSU TSD 18 (published as Phillippo et al. 2018) makes the anchored/unanchored choice a structural rule: anchored population-adjusted comparison when a connected network with a common comparator exists; unanchored only when there is no connected randomised evidence or single-arm studies are involved, and then only with evidence that absolute outcomes can be predicted and an estimate of residual systematic error. MAIC/STC give an estimate only in the comparator study's population. KM reconstruction (Guyot 2012) is accurate for survival probabilities and medians, and for hazard ratios only when numbers at risk and total events are available.

### Cited Findings

**A. MAIC and STC sequence and decision rules, Phillippo DM, Ades AE, Dias S, Palmer S, Abrams KR, Welton NJ, Med Decis Making 2018;38(2):200-211 (journal version of NICE DSU TSD 18) [PR/REG-guidance; READ-FULL]** ([PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/136613/1/0272989x17725740.pdf); DSU page [population-adjusted](https://sheffield.ac.uk/nice-dsu/tsds/population-adjusted)):
- Set-up: IPD for one trial (AB), only aggregate data for the other (AC). Anchored = common comparator arm used; unanchored = none. "Unanchored comparisons make much stronger assumptions, which are widely regarded as infeasible."
- MAIC mechanics: weight = odds of being in the AC trial versus the AB trial; log(w) = a0 + a1'X; because AC IPD is absent, a1 is found by the method of moments so that the weighted AB covariate means (and any included squared terms) equal the published AC means; outcomes are the weighted averages. Effective sample size ESS = (sum w)^2 / sum w^2 (an approximation, "likely to be an underestimation of the true ESS"); "Small ESS indicates that the weights are highly variable due to a lack of population overlap, and that the resulting estimate may be unstable"; inspect the weight distribution; "It is not possible to apply traditional propensity score tools for 'balance checking'" because moment matching imposes balance by construction. Entropy balancing is the same except weights are constrained to be as close to each other as possible, so it should have "equal or reduced standard error" for the same bias reduction.
- STC: outcome regression fitted on the AB IPD with prognostic and effect-modifier terms, predicted at the AC mean covariates.
- Rec 1: anchored population-adjusted comparison "may be considered" when connected evidence exists; "Unanchored indirect comparisons may only be considered in the absence of a connected network of randomized evidence, or where there are single-arm studies involved."
- Rec 2 (anchored): present evidence that variables are effect modifiers on the chosen scale (empirical or biologically plausible) and "quantitative evidence ... that population adjustment would have a material impact on relative effect estimates due to the removal of substantial bias".
- Rec 3 (unanchored): show "absolute outcomes can be predicted with sufficient accuracy in relation to the relative treatment effects, and present an estimate of the likely range of residual systematic error". The discussion adds: the accuracy of unanchored estimates "is entirely unknown, because there is no analysis of the potential magnitude of residual bias", quoting Hoaglin that without such evidence results "are not worthy of consideration".
- Rec 4: anchored with weighting adjusts for all effect modifiers but no purely prognostic variables (over-matching loses precision); anchored with outcome regression adjusts effect modifiers in imbalance plus any variable improving fit; unanchored adjusts for all effect modifiers and prognostic variables.
- Rec 5: compare on the linear-predictor scale; neither MAIC nor STC is "scale-free". Rec 6: state the target population; MAIC/STC "are unable to achieve estimates in any population other than that of the AC study", so a "fair comparison" for the decision population needs the shared-effect-modifier assumption; two competing manufacturers' MAICs gave opposing results, each favouring its own drug.
- Related simulation work (already in r3-academic.md): the robust sandwich variance underestimates variability at small ESS and MAIC cannot extrapolate beyond the observed covariate space ([Remiro-Azocar, Heath, Baio, Res Synth Methods 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14800); not re-read here).

**B. KM reconstruction, Guyot P et al., BMC Med Res Methodol 2012;12:9 [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH]** ([PMC3313891](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3313891/)):
- Sequence: digitise each curve separately (about half an hour per curve) with the first point T = 0, S = 1, include every time at which numbers at risk are reported, check survival never rises; record numbers at risk per interval and total events -> iterate: estimate censoring in interval i from the change in numbers at risk, spread censoring evenly in the interval, back-calculate events and at-risk counts from the KM equation, reconcile to the published number at risk at the next interval, repeat; last interval has no published at-risk count so censoring is proportional to elapsed time, adjusted so total events match the reported total; output is individual time/event data. Variants: no numbers at risk; no total events; neither (assumes no censoring, "a strong assumption").
- Validation: 12 curves from 4 trials; 22 survival probabilities, 7 medians, 6 HRs, 4 SEs of log HR; three observers each reconstructing every curve twice; with all information: mean error in survival probability -0.103% (MAE 0.272%, reproducibility SD 0.270%), median mean log-ratio 0.011 (about 1.1%, reproducibility 0.6%), HR mean log error 0.008 and MAE factor about 1.017 (reproducibility about 1.02). HR MAE by information level: no numbers at risk about 1.04; no total events about 1.03; neither about 1.22 with an upper 97.5% limit of about 4.7, so a true HR 1.5 could be reconstructed anywhere from 0.32 to 7.11; the SE of log HR is underestimated without censoring information (mean error -0.143).
- Limits stated: published curves aggregate subgroups so covariate effects cannot be modelled (bias toward the null); blurry figures and poor axes; non-informative, evenly spread censoring is assumed; HRs are averages over the risk period and are sensitive to non-proportional hazards. Recommended reporting: publish KM curves with numbers at risk at regular points and total events; compare reconstructed statistics against those published.
- IPDfromKM, Liu N, Zhou Y, Lee JJ, BMC Med Res Methodol 2021;21 [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY]: two-stage (digitise, then reconstruct) modified-iKM, R package and Shiny app ([PMC8168323](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8168323/)); CRAN version 0.1.10 dated 2020-11-11, a modification of Guyot's approach ([CRAN](https://cloud.r-project.org/web/packages/IPDfromKM/index.html)).

**C. Downstream use in comparator work.** Nothing in Guyot 2012 or the IPDfromKM abstract prescribes a downstream workflow; the DSU recommendations above apply once reconstructed data feed a MAIC/STC/unanchored comparison (the DSU document does not itself discuss KM reconstruction in the sections I read).

### Inferences
- Reconstructed IPD from published curves is a benchmark object: it carries no covariates, so it can feed a time-to-event shape or a threshold, but it cannot support a covariate-adjusted comparison, and a MAIC against a trial that has only reconstructed data has no covariates to weight on (aggregate means would still be needed).
- A tool offering "MAIC" should have to ask three questions the DSU makes explicit: is there a common comparator (anchored vs unanchored), which variables are effect modifiers on which scale and why, and which population the user actually wants the answer for. A MAIC output with no ESS and no weight distribution is below the DSU reporting standard.
- For unanchored comparisons the tool has no published way to quantify residual bias (TSD 18 says so); the honest output is "benchmark only", ideally with the QBA/E-value style ranges of Q4.

### Gaps
- Signorovitch 2010/2012 original papers not opened (only described through TSD 18); no direct quote from them.
- Newer (2019-2026) comparisons of MAIC vs STC vs multilevel NMR performance were not searched beyond what r3-academic.md holds.
- No source found that gives explicit ESS cut-offs below which MAIC should not be reported; TSD 18 gives none.
- No source read on validating KM-reconstructed IPD against real IPD beyond Guyot's own validation.

## Q4. Weighting and estimation order; balance and overlap thresholds; negative controls, E-value, tipping point, QBA

### Takeaway
Published sequences put balance targets and the SAP before outcomes (FDA), choose weights/matching under a stated estimand (usually ATT), check overlap and balance, then estimate with an outcome-model-assisted (AIPW/doubly robust) or weighting estimator, and finish with prespecified sensitivity (tipping-point, E-value, negative controls, QBA). No authoritative source fixes the numerical thresholds: 0.1 standardised difference is a convention; FDA and Austin both decline to endorse a single cut-off.

### Cited Findings

**A. Sequence and the order of operations**
- FDA 2023: balancing method is chosen and justified, an a priori similarity threshold (SMD given as an example) is set before analysis, extra analyses check "actual comparability" of key covariates after weighting, then sensitivity analyses and a priori bias analyses ([READ-FULL above](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download)).
- Causal review: positivity is checked through covariate balance and PS-distribution overlap; estimation targets ATT, with weighting, outcome modelling or doubly robust forms (IPW, AIPW with efficient influence function, TMLE); AIPW is "the solution to the equation obtained by setting the expectation of the efficient influence function (EIF) to zero" ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282), READ-FULL). For HCT Table 4 rates methods: PS matching/weighting and outcome modelling handle covariate shift but not outcome drift; doubly robust methods give high efficiency with medium bias risk "under no outcome drift"; Bayesian methods handle drift through discounting.
- RCT-DUPLICATE operationalisation: PS matching, 1% caliper, more than 100 covariates, standardised differences < 0.1 as the feasibility criterion ([PMC10130954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130954/), READ-VIA-FETCH).
- Unlearn preprint [PP; VEN-authored, READ-FULL]: ATT with AIPW (outcome model + propensity model), Section 3 decision flow (summarised in Q6), variance reduction relative to 1:1 PSM "typically in the range of 40-60%" and smaller absolute standardised bias for AIPW in two trial reanalyses (Q6) ([arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832)).

**B. Balance and overlap thresholds**
- Austin 2009, Stat Med [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH, first 100,000 of 113,484 characters]: report standardised differences; "a standardized difference of 0.1 (10 per cent) denotes meaningful imbalance in the baseline covariate" is quoted as a proposal by some researchers, while he calls fixed cut-offs (0.10, 0.25) arbitrary and recommends judging against the empirical sampling distribution of the standardised difference; significance tests for balance are not recommended; also compare variances, squared terms and two-way interactions, use Q-Q plots for prognostic covariates; if balance is inadequate re-specify the PS model (e.g. splines) and, if imbalance remains in small samples, use regression adjustment ([PMC3472075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3472075/)).
- FDA 2023 footnote 30: "FDA does not endorse a single approach for determining thresholds" ([FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download)).
- Entropy balancing, Hainmueller J, Political Analysis 2012;20(1):25-46 [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY via search]: weights for controls that satisfy prespecified moment constraints (first, second, possibly higher moments) exactly while staying as close as possible, in entropy, to base weights; output feeds standard estimators ([author PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/eb.pdf)). In the NICE document it is the constrained version of MAIC weights ([Phillippo 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/136613/1/0272989x17725740.pdf)).
- Overlap weights, Li F, Morgan KL, Zaslavsky AM, JASA 2018;113:390-400 [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY via search]: weights proportional to a unit's probability of being in the opposite group, bounded between 0 and 1, minimum asymptotic variance among balancing weights, exact mean balance for covariates in a logistic PS model ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jnlasa/v113y2018i521p390-400.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.07494)). Note the overlap-weight estimand is the overlap population, not ATT.
- Overlap numeric rules (trimming bounds, minimum ESS): I found no regulator-endorsed numeric rule; the causal review only says overlap is "assessed through ... overlap in estimated propensity score distributions".

**C. Negative controls**
- Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, Cohen, Epidemiology 2010;21(3):383-388 [PR; READ-VIA-FETCH] ([PMC3053408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)): a negative control outcome shares the set of common causes with exposure and outcome of interest but is not caused by the exposure; a negative control exposure shares the confounding structure with the main exposure but does not cause the outcome. A non-null A-N association "would indicate bias" if the control is comparable and not caused by A, but may reflect a confounder of N alone; a null result is not proof of no bias (controls sharing only some confounders "will appear unassociated despite the presence of uncontrolled confounding"); a failed control "does not identify what form of bias is operating", and size cannot be inferred from the association; "a sensitive, but blunt, tool".
- Empirical calibration, Schuemie MJ, Ryan PB, DuMouchel W, Suchard MA, Madigan D, Stat Med 2014;33(2):209-218 [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY via search]: re-running three drug-safety designs on negative-control drugs showed that most observational studies would declare significance when there is no effect; the negative-control estimates define an empirical null and calibrated p-values reduced spurious results to the nominal 5%; the 2018 PNAS paper calibrates confidence intervals using negative and positive controls ([search result](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/67635); R package [EmpiricalCalibration](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/EmpiricalCalibration/refman/EmpiricalCalibration.html)). I did not read the coverage numbers.
- In TTE practice: RCT-DUPLICATE evaluated 35 control outcomes, 29 (83%) as expected; Hernán 2016 names outcome and treatment controls; TARGET 7h(ii) names negative controls and QBA as examples (links above).

**D. E-value and tipping point**
- VanderWeele & Ding, Ann Intern Med 2017 [PR; PubMed abstract via fetch]: the E-value is "the minimum strength of association, on the risk ratio scale," an unmeasured confounder would need "with both the treatment and the outcome" to fully explain away the association, conditional on measured covariates; proposed to be reported alongside the estimate and for the confidence-interval limit closest to the null ([PubMed 28693043](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28693043/)).
- Tipping point in the external-control literature: tau(delta) = tau_hat - delta, tipping point = smallest |delta| making the interval cover zero (review Section 3.6). For hybrid designs the review lists: test-twice (revert to RCT-only when RCT-only and RCT+EC estimates differ significantly), Gordon-Schuler bounds on deviation between E[Y(0)|S=0,X] and E[Y(0)|S=1,X] with a G-value, a b-value, an E-value-informed HC-value/RD-value, and partial identification ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)). It warns: "a non-significant result does not necessarily imply negligible bias".
- The BenchExCal tipping point is on the divergence parameter (Q1E).

**E. Quantitative bias analysis**
- Gupta et al. 2025 (Q2E): prespecified QBA inputs from a targeted literature search, randomised trial data and clinician input; mean log-HR gap 0.139 -> 0.098 (ratio of HRs 1.22 -> 1.17) ([record](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/quantitative-bias-analysis-for-single-arm-trials-with-external-co/)).
- FDA 2023: "quantitative or qualitative bias analyses" planned a priori; EMA: conservative thresholds; CDE 2020 No. 1: "敏感性分析和偏倚的定量分析" (links in Q2).
- Protocol/fit-for-purpose templates in the RWE literature (named, not read): STaRT-RWE ([BMJ 2021, PubMed 33436424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33436424/)), HARPER, and SPIFD2 (steps per vendor summaries: aims, hypothetical target trial, emulation, fit-for-purpose data, documented validity concerns) [VEN summaries, not primary: [Datavant](https://www.datavant.com/?p=6055)].

### Inferences
- Defensible fixed order for an automated pipeline, assembled from the sources above (this ordering is my synthesis, each element is sourced): estimand and ATT -> variables fixed at design (no post-baseline, no instruments) -> overlap and ESS look before outcomes -> weights chosen -> balance table against an a priori threshold -> AIPW (or weighted Cox/RMST) -> negative controls and tipping-point/E-value/QBA, all prespecified -> report.
- A tool that prints "SMD < 0.1: balanced" as a pass/fail gate would go beyond what Austin and FDA say: both treat 0.1 as a convention and FDA wants the threshold set in advance and justified.
- Null negative-control results should be worded as "no evidence of bias detected by these controls", not "no bias" (Lipsitch).

### Gaps
- No numeric threshold for overlap or minimum weighted ESS from any regulator or guideline; none found in peer-reviewed sources either within this session.
- Hainmueller, Li et al., Schuemie, VanderWeele-Ding read at abstract level only.
- Lash/Fox QBA textbook-style guidance (bias parameters, multidimensional QBA) not searched.

## Q5. Hybrid borrowing: MAP, power/commensurate priors, CDE 2026 No. 6, hybrid sample size

### Takeaway
Hybrid designs keep an internal randomised control and borrow external control information; the published workflows (RBesT/MAP, the 2026 CDE guidance, the 2026 causal review) all require four things in order: assess and justify the external source (comparability, exchangeability, no cherry-picking), construct the prior with an explicit borrowing weight and ESS, evaluate operating characteristics by simulation (false-positive rate first, with at least 100,000 simulations in the CDE text), and prespecify sensitivity analyses against weakly informative or non-borrowing results. The preprint on hybrid sample size (arXiv 2608.26475) adds three external-comparability design parameters.

### Cited Findings

**A. MAP / robust MAP workflow, Schmidli H et al., Biometrics 2014;70(4):1023-1032 (doi 10.1111/biom.12242) [PR; ABSTRACT-ONLY]** and RBesT, Weber S, Li Y, Seaman JW, Kakizume T, Schmidli H, J Stat Softw 2021;100(19) [PR; READ-FULL] ([JSS PDF](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/download/v100i19/4188); Schmidli metadata at [BORIS](https://boris.unibe.ch/59957)):
- Sequence in RBesT: (1) compile historical control data and check clinical comparability of the trials and plausibility of the between-trial heterogeneity assumption; (2) `gMAP` random-effects meta-analysis by MCMC; (3) `mixfit`/`automixfit` parametric mixture approximation; (4) `robustify` adds a weakly informative component, and "the so-obtained robust MAP prior is intended for use in a pre-specified trial analysis"; (5) `ess` for informativeness; (6) `decision1S/2S` success criterion and `oc1S/2S`, `pos1S/2S` for frequentist operating characteristics and probability of success.
- Example in the paper: ankylosing spondylitis, eight historical placebo trials totalling 513 patients; the MAP prior has an effective sample size of 36 and the robust MAP prior 25; the actual trial (published with a Beta(11,32) control prior and 4:1 randomisation) randomised 24 active and 6 control patients (15 versus 1 responders).
- Mechanism: heavy tails make the prior "essentially discarded" under prior-data conflict; robustification speeds the reaction; the mixture weight expresses scepticism towards borrowing.

**B. Other borrowing families, as classified by the 2026 causal review (Table 4) [PP; READ-FULL]** ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)): Bayesian power priors (likelihood discount through a power parameter; assumes EC and trial control share the same treatment-effect parameter; software BayesPPD); commensurate priors (shrinkage via a commensurate parameter; psborrow2); elastic priors (inflate prior variance on prior-data conflict); rMAP/SAM priors (heavier-tailed or data-driven mixture weight; RBesT, SAMprior); hierarchical and clustered-hierarchical models for multi-source EC. Efficiency gains and bias risk are rated per class, and none of these methods handles covariate shift unless propensity scores are integrated. Limits discussed: dynamic borrowing "cannot perfectly detect all forms of incompatibility in small samples" (Freidlin & Korn critique), yet "can mitigate substantial discrepancies"; prognostic adjustment "does not require bias detection to achieve consistency"; the authors argue that power gains remain possible under strict type I error control in realistic semiparametric settings (methods of their Section 4.5.4), contrary to a result of Kopp-Schneider et al. that holds where uniformly most powerful tests exist.

**C. CDE 2026 No. 6, Bayesian external information borrowing (试行), signed 2026-01-20 [REG; READ-FULL]** ([PDF on cnpharm.com](https://www.cnpharm.com/upload/resources/file/2026/01/21/183608.pdf)) - chapter order is the sequence:
1. 适用性评估 (applicability, at design stage with regulator contact): (i) scientific rationale - why Bayesian borrowing (cannot enrol enough subjects; high-quality similar trial data exist), "评估应包含定量分析及模拟研究", covering exchangeability judged from comparability and consistency of external information, and false-positive / false-negative rates under the planned borrowing, decision criteria and sample size, "确保信息借用量不会主导试验结果"; (ii) data quality and reliability of the borrowed source - process of source selection shown in detail, real/reliable/comprehensive, strength of evidence and comparability; refers to the RWD guidances for data fitness; (iii) feasibility and trial integrity (adaptive/interim); (iv) interpretability and robustness - Bayesian analysis plan, clinically interpretable, sensitivity analyses prespecified.
2. 试验设计与统计分析: sources and appraisal (clinical background incl. eligibility, diagnosis, prior and concomitant therapy; study design, endpoints definitions, intercurrent-event handling; timeliness: population, practice and criteria drift); to avoid selection bias "不应仅选择对试验药物有利而排除对试验药物不利的数据来源" (do not keep only the favourable sources); prior construction (borrow control only, treatment only, both, or effect differences; distribution form by data type; multiple sources integrated into one informative prior; the borrowing weight - power parameter in power priors, mixture ratio in mixture priors - "需要在研究开始前与审评机构就先验分布权重值进行沟通并达成一致"; the same numeric weight means different things in different prior forms); hypotheses and decision criteria; sample size; analysis.
3. 模拟: design simulation and analysis simulation; scenarios must cover plausible parameter ranges incl. nuisance parameters (accrual rate, dropout), borrowing models (one- or two-arm borrowing, effect vs effect-difference borrowing, power vs mixture priors), amount borrowed, trial sample size vs sample size represented by borrowed information, decision criteria; operating characteristics include false-positive rate, false-negative rate, success probability with attention to variability; "对于假阳性率的模拟研究精度要求会高于对试验检验效能的模拟研究，因此一般会要求更多的模拟次数（例如至少 100000 次）"; documentation requirements and an appendix example simulation report (Appendix 2).
4. 结果的解读: decisions by prespecified posterior-probability criteria; point estimate and clinical meaning; sensitivity analyses; avoid prior dominating; if applicable prespecified evaluation of data-prior inconsistency; "可参考弱信息（无信息）先验或频率学方法的结果进行比对解读" (compare with weakly informative prior or frequentist results).
5. 沟通交流: three situations - protocol/SAP/simulation plan agreement (pre-IND, end of phase 2); protocol amendments (change of prior source, amount borrowed or method are "实质性变更" requiring simulation results); pre-NDA.

**D. Sample size for hybrid controlled trials: Zhu K, Yang S, Wang X, arXiv 2608.26475, submitted 27 Aug 2026 [PP; ABSTRACT-ONLY]** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26475)): a "5+3 design" for an IPW estimator of the ATE: five conventional design parameters plus three describing external-control comparability - the number of external controls without outcome drift, an overlap coefficient for covariate distributions, and a correlation coefficient linking the sampling mechanism to the control potential outcome; closed-form sample size formulas for continuous and binary outcomes under working models; simulations and a clinical application; R package hctdesign. The abstract page reports no numeric results and does not list the five conventional parameters. It states the core problem: the sample size depends on external comparability, "information that isn't available at the design stage".

### Inferences
- A design-time sample-size or power output for a hybrid arm that does not ask for assumptions on outcome drift and covariate overlap (the two comparability quantities in 2608.26475, equivalent to the two biases of 2605.03282) would be silently assuming perfect comparability. Both the CDE text and 2608.26475 say the benefit of borrowing cannot be known in advance, so scenario grids rather than a single number are the honest design output.
- The CDE guidance gives a product team a concrete simulation checklist (operating characteristics, nuisance ranges, number of replications for the false-positive rate, report appendix) that is more specific than the FDA and EMA texts in this area; the FDA Jan 2026 Bayesian draft is in r3-academic.md and was not re-read.
- The borrowing weight is a negotiated design parameter in the Chinese text, not a tuning knob to be optimised after seeing results.

### Gaps
- Schmidli 2014, power-prior (Ibrahim-Chen), commensurate (Hobbs) and elastic-prior papers: no full text read; descriptions come from the 2026 review and the RBesT paper.
- The 2608.26475 full text (formulas, the five conventional parameters, any numbers) not read.
- FDA Jan 2026 Bayesian draft and ICH E20 not re-read.

## Q6. Prognostic adjustment: PROCOVA, EMA 2022, FDA 2023, and Unlearn's digital twins as synthetic controls

### Takeaway
Prognostic covariate adjustment is a randomised-trial technique: an independently trained prognostic score is added as one covariate, which preserves type I error and unbiasedness because randomisation carries the identification, and only efficiency depends on model quality (EMA qualified exactly that, nothing more). Using the same kind of "digital twin" as a synthetic control in a SINGLE-arm trial is a different object: it is an outcome model inside an AIPW estimator whose validity depends on consistency, positivity and ignorability, with the model's influence on the estimate growing as the historical data become less relevant (vendor-authored preprint).

### Cited Findings

**A. PROCOVA / prognostic score in randomised trials**
- Schuler A, Walsh D, Hall D, Walsh J, Fisher C, "Increasing the efficiency of randomized trial estimates via linear adjustment for a prognostic score", arXiv 2012.09935 (v1 Dec 2020, v3 Dec 2021) [PP at that version; Unlearn association per r3-academic.md, affiliations not on the abstract page I read; ABSTRACT-ONLY]: a prognostic model is trained on historical data, then the treatment effect is estimated by linear regression adjusting for each subject's predicted outcome; "without incurring bias"; gain proportional to predictive accuracy beyond a linear relationship with the raw covariates; "Sample size reductions between 10% and 30% are attainable" when the prognostic model explains a clinically realistic share of outcome variance (the share is not stated in the abstract); simulations and an Alzheimer's reanalysis showed reductions in MSE and variance, no values in the abstract ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09935)).
- EMA qualification opinion on PROCOVA, CHMP adopted 15 Sep 2022 [REG; as summarised in r3-academic.md, not re-read]: randomised phase 2/3, continuous outcomes, ANCOVA special case; type I error and unbiasedness "are not dependent on the choice or performance of the prognostic model"; model building not qualified; non-linear analyses and treatment-by-covariate interactions out of scope; sample-size reduction only with uncertainty and optimism accounted for ([EMA PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/qualification-opinion-prognostic-covariate-adjustment-procovatm_en.pdf)).
- FDA, Adjusting for Covariates in Randomized Clinical Trials for Drugs and Biological Products, final, May 2023 [REG; READ-FULL] ([PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/148910/download)): covariate-adjusted analysis acceptable; "prospectively specify the detailed procedures ... before any unblinding of comparative data"; adjust for covariates "anticipated to be most strongly associated with the outcome", which may come from literature or "it may be useful to use previous studies (e.g., a Phase 2 trial) to select prognostic covariates or form prognostic indices"; non-prognostic covariates may give no gain or a loss of precision; linear-model adjustment is valid for the average treatment effect even if misspecified, with robust (Huber-White sandwich) standard errors recommended; sample size may be calculated on unadjusted methods "which will often lead to a more conservative sample size".
- The 2026 causal review notes that prognostic adjustment in hybrid trials "require[s] no assumptions beyond those for RCT-only analyses" ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)).

**B. Digital twins as synthetic controls in single-arm trials: Bertolini D, Fuller F, Smith AM, Walsh JR, Zhuang R, arXiv 2605.12832, 12 May 2026 [PP; VEN: all authors at Unlearn.AI per the first page; READ-FULL]** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832)):
- Estimand ATT. Identifying conditions the paper states: (1) consistency/SUTVA, "typically ensured by study design"; (2) positivity, P(A=0|X=x) > 0 on the support of treated covariates, "adequate support in the control data for the covariate profiles observed among treated participants"; (3) ignorability, "In randomized trials, this condition is guaranteed by design. In single-arm settings, it must be assumed ... In practice, this may be the strongest assumption made". Identifiability is assumed throughout; non-identifiable settings are "raised but not developed here".
- Decision flow for choosing the comparator (Section 3, Figure 1), "a sequence of considerations" rather than a strict tree: Q1 must the comparator be tailored to the enrolled population? If not, a fixed or externally defined benchmark (network meta-analysis, historical summary statistics, clinical reference value) with minimal assumptions but no adjustment for baseline differences (warning: regression to the mean for biomarkers). Q2 is there external data plausibly exchangeable with the control population? If not, direct PS methods "are unlikely to yield valid estimates" and outcome-model methods with structural assumptions (extrapolation, increased model reliance) are the path. Q3 is the external data rich and of high quality? If limited or incomplete overlap, outcome-model or doubly robust estimators; if large and aligned, "propensity-based methods may suffice" with outcome models adding efficiency. "Use data when you can, models when you must"; agreement of propensity-based and model-based estimates "can be used as a way to at least partially validate each other".
- Credibility framing: context of use is predicting standard-of-care outcomes that enter AIPW; model risk = decision consequence x model influence; "Decision consequence is invariably high in the registrational single-arm setting"; model influence is lowest when highly relevant historical data and a well-characterised propensity model make the causal assumptions credible, and rises as relevance declines (their Figure 3). They cite FDA's AI-credibility draft (Jan 2025) and externally-controlled-trials draft.
- Evidence presented: reanalyses of completed trials - Celebrex in ALS (about 300 patients, ALSFRS-R and FVC% at 12 months) and 2CARE in Huntington disease (UHDRS endpoints at 4 years). Average absolute standardised bias: ALS, AIPW about 6% vs about 11% for the other estimators; HD, AIPW about 14%, similar to the outcome-model plug-in, vs about 20-23% for the other methods; confidence intervals covered zero for most estimators and endpoints; variance reduction versus 1:1 PSM "typically in the range of 40-60%". The authors caution these are point estimates from a single sample. I did not verify how the standardised bias was defined from the paper text.
- Stated limit: the case for AIPW "is strongest when the training data are rich and diverse enough to support flexible, well-validated digital twins". Covered outcomes: continuous; time-to-event and binary "require adaptation".

**C. Boundary of regulatory acceptance.** Per r3-academic.md the only regulatory qualification is the randomised-trial PROCOVA opinion above; FDA CDER told Unlearn that PROCOVA "does not appear to deviate from our guidance" (company blog, [VEN], cited in r3-academic.md); no qualification of twin-based external controls was found there, and none was found in this session's searches.

### Inferences
- The identifying assumption set for "digital twin as synthetic control" is exactly the standard external-control set (consistency, positivity, ignorability) and has no advantage over PS methods on ignorability. What the twin buys is efficiency and robustness to poor overlap through the outcome model, not identification. A product should therefore never describe twin-based single-arm estimates as having randomisation-like guarantees; those guarantees exist only when randomisation exists (EMA/FDA/PROCOVA).
- In a randomised design the prognostic score may be built from any independent source, and the label "type I error unaffected" is legitimate; in a single-arm design it is not, because ignorability carries the validity.
- The two Unlearn reanalyses are the vendor's evidence on its own method in two neurodegenerative trials with continuous endpoints; they do not speak to binary or time-to-event single-arm oncology settings, which the paper itself lists as extensions.

### Gaps
- Schuler et al. published-journal version not found/read; EMA opinion and Unlearn FDA correspondence not re-read in this session.
- Unlearn 2605.12832: the power and sample-size formulas, the definition of "standardized bias" in the reanalyses and the exact FDA-AI-draft deployment steps (it lists seven steps; only step 7 was visible to me) were not extracted.
- No independent (non-Unlearn) peer-reviewed validation of twin-based synthetic controls in single-arm trials was found.

## Q7. Minimum data per route; when the honest output is "not estimable" or "benchmark only"; what the papers say a tool should tell the user

### Takeaway
Minimum data are route-specific and in several routes explicit in the sources: patient-level data on both arms for any covariate-adjusted external comparison; IPD on one trial plus aggregates on the other for MAIC/STC; numbers at risk and total events for credible KM reconstruction; a randomised control arm for hybrid borrowing and prognostic adjustment. The sources themselves name the conditions under which the answer is no estimate or only a benchmark; the same sources set what must be disclosed.

### Cited Findings

**A. Minimum data by route (as stated by the sources)**

| Route | Minimum data stated | Source |
|---|---|---|
| TTE on a database | Both strategies observed in the data, baseline confounders present, eligibility applicable at baseline, time zero definable, outcome ascertainable | [Hernán & Robins 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/); TARGET items 5, 7a-7e ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/390/bmj-2025-087179)) |
| External control arm (covariate-adjusted) | Patient-level data for both treated and control arms (also required in the marketing application); prognostic covariates measured similarly; consistent index date, follow-up and outcome criteria | [FDA 2023](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download); [arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282) |
| Hybrid control | Internal randomised control arm plus external control IPD (covariates, treatment, outcomes) | [arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282) |
| MAIC / STC | IPD of one trial plus published aggregate covariates and outcomes of the other; anchored additionally a common comparator; effect-modifier evidence | [Phillippo 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/136613/1/0272989x17725740.pdf) |
| KM reconstruction | Curve digitised monotonically; numbers at risk at regular points and total events; without both, HR reliability falls (MAE factor about 1.22, 97.5% limit about 4.7) | [Guyot 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3313891/) |
| Threshold / benchmark (single arm) | External summary information with uncertainty; prespecified conservative threshold; for China, historical control from high-level evidence of similar era, disease background and adequate sample | [EMA 2024](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-establishing-efficacy-based-single-arm-trials-submitted-pivotal-evidence-marketing-authorisation-application_en.pdf); [CDE 2025 No. 54](https://www.xfdyb.com/uploadfile/ueditor/file/20260106/1767670029501034.pdf) |
| Prognostic adjustment | A randomised trial; a prognostic model trained on independent historical data; baseline covariates | [FDA 2023 covariate](https://www.fda.gov/media/148910/download); [arXiv 2012.09935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09935) |
| Twin-based synthetic control | Rich historical patient-level training data plus the AIPW ingredients (propensity or outcome model good enough) | [arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832) |
| Bayesian borrowing | Historical controls with documented comparability; heterogeneity assumption; simulation plan | [RBesT paper](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/download/v100i19/4188); [CDE 2026 No. 6](https://www.cnpharm.com/upload/resources/file/2026/01/21/183608.pdf) |

**B. Where the sources say not to estimate, or to use a weaker object**
- Hernán & Robins: if baseline confounders are missing "successful emulation of the target trial's random assignment is not possible"; blinded-outcome and tightly monitored placebo designs cannot be emulated ([PMC4832051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/)).
- FDA 2023: choose a more suitable design when credible demonstration is unlikely; non-inferiority not recommended; modest anticipated effect "may not be an appropriate study design"; when care-delivery factors cannot be accounted for consider an RCT; a design cannot be rescued by choosing the external arm after the data ([FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download)).
- EMA 2024: without unambiguous isolation of the treatment effect, thresholds are hard to justify and a SAT is "a critical risk"; strong prognostic factors may call for a randomised control ([EMA PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-establishing-efficacy-based-single-arm-trials-submitted-pivotal-evidence-marketing-authorisation-application_en.pdf)).
- CDE: historical control "多数情况下不建议使用" (2026 No. 27, via r2-china.md); SAT as confirmatory evidence only if four conditions hold and still not certain (2025 No. 54, [PDF](https://www.xfdyb.com/uploadfile/ueditor/file/20260106/1767670029501034.pdf)).
- TSD 18: unanchored comparisons only without a connected network and then with predictive-accuracy and residual-bias evidence; otherwise "not worthy of consideration" (Hoaglin quote in [Phillippo 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/136613/1/0272989x17725740.pdf)).
- Unlearn flow: no plausibly exchangeable data -> structured-assumption models that "extend beyond what is directly supported by the observed patient-level data ... at the cost of increased model dependence"; non-tailored comparator -> benchmark ([arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832)).
- Causal review: when unmeasured confounding is substantial borrowing "cannot compensate" for no randomisation ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)).
- Bartlett 2019: only about 15% of 220 high-impact trials feasible to replicate with real-world data (Q1D).

**C. What the documents say must be disclosed (what a tool should state)**
- Label the study as an observational emulation of a target trial, not an "emulated trial"; give the target trial specification, identifying assumptions with how each variable was defined, imperfections of operationalisation, flow counts with clones separated from individuals, baseline tables before and after adjustment, missing data per variable, absolute and relative effects, all sensitivity results, and the differences between target trial and emulation as limitations (TARGET items 1a, 6g, 7a, 7g, 8-9, 11, 13-16; [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/390/bmj-2025-087179)).
- Record every data source accessed and the reason for selecting or excluding it, show the analytic dataset was not chosen to favour a result, keep audit trails, date-stamp any SAP change with rationale; provide patient-level data for both arms ([FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download)).
- State the threshold's origin and uncertainty, prespecified, conservative; do not treat an external threshold as a known constant ([EMA PDF](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-establishing-efficacy-based-single-arm-trials-submitted-pivotal-evidence-marketing-authorisation-application_en.pdf)).
- MAIC: ESS, weight distribution, effect-modifier evidence and the target population; for unanchored, the likely range of residual systematic error (TSD 18 Rec 2-6; [Phillippo 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/136613/1/0272989x17725740.pdf)).
- KM reconstruction: compare reconstructed statistics against the published ones; say which of numbers at risk and total events were available ([Guyot 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3313891/)).
- Bayesian borrowing: how sources were selected (including those unfavourable to the drug), borrowing amount/ESS, false-positive rate by simulation, comparison with weakly informative or frequentist analyses, the borrowing weight agreed in advance ([CDE 2026 No. 6](https://www.cnpharm.com/upload/resources/file/2026/01/21/183608.pdf)).
- Sensitivity: report E-values for the estimate and the CI limit nearest the null ([VanderWeele & Ding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28693043/)); tipping points as the magnitude of bias needed to overturn the conclusion ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)); calibrated intervals are wider by design ([BenchExCal vignette](https://xxue064.r-universe.dev/benchexcal/doc/benchexcal-intro.html)); a null negative control is not proof of no bias ([Lipsitch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).
- Risk statement for model-based comparators: model influence and decision consequence, with consequence "invariably high" in registrational single-arm studies ([arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832)).

### Inferences
- The sources support four distinct output classes for an automated tool: (1) estimate with full diagnostics (all minimum data present, assumptions stated); (2) estimate flagged as exploratory (e.g. covariate gaps, small ESS, drift evidence); (3) benchmark only (aggregates, reconstructed curves, thresholds, unanchored MAIC, no patient-level control data); (4) not estimable / not an appropriate design (missing confounders, no definable time zero, non-inferiority claim on external control, modest effect with subjective endpoint). The class boundaries above are drawn from the quoted statements; the labels are mine.
- Several triggers for class 4 are decidable by code from inputs (patient-level control data absent, no baseline confounders, non-inferiority objective, no time-zero rule), while "natural history well understood" and "effect large enough" are judgements the sources leave to the sponsor and regulator.

### Gaps
- No source gives a numeric minimum sample size or events count for an external-control analysis; the review's recommendation is simulation in the specific small-population setting (Q2D).
- No quantified guidance on how large a missingness fraction makes a covariate unusable; FoCR only reports that divergence rose with missingness.
- The FDA Jan 2025 AI-credibility draft and Jan 2026 Bayesian draft were not re-read.

## Q8. Cross-route synthesis: the actual analytic sequences side by side and the route-choice logic

### Takeaway
All published routes share one skeleton - question/estimand, then data fitness, then time zero or its equivalent, then balancing or borrowing, then diagnostics, then estimation, then sensitivity, then reporting - but they differ in what is fixed first: TTE and FDA fix a protocol before data, MAIC fixes the network structure (anchored or not), hybrid fixes the internal randomised control, prognostic adjustment fixes randomisation. Route choice in the sources is driven by four gates: is there randomisation, is there patient-level data on every arm being compared, is the natural history stable with a large effect, and can the estimand be anchored in the trial population.

### Cited Findings

**A. Step-by-step sequences, side by side (each cell sourced in Q1-Q7)**

| Step | TTE database study (Hernán 2016 / TARGET / RCT-DUPLICATE) | Single-arm + external control (FDA 2023; causal review SAT+EC) | Hybrid control (causal review HCT; CDE 2026 No. 6; RBesT) | MAIC / STC (TSD 18) | Prognostic adjustment, randomised (FDA 2023 covariate; EMA PROCOVA) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question / estimand | causal question; target-trial protocol of 7 components; contrasts ITT/PP | suitability decision first; estimand per ICH E9(R1); usually ATT | RCT population anchors; ATE or ATT of trial population | target population stated (Rec 6); linear-predictor scale (Rec 5) | prespecified primary analysis of ATE |
| Data fitness | data sources described first (TARGET 5); validation; feasibility without outcome by treatment | ten-row comparability table; prognostic variables must be assessable; protocol before data | external source appraisal: comparability, timeliness, no cherry-picking | IPD on one trial, aggregates on the other; effect modifiers identified a priori | independent historical data for prognostic score |
| Time zero | eligibility met and strategy assigned; cloning/grace for non-instant strategies | shared index date; avoid immortal time; unsuitable if no discrete trigger | follow-up from randomisation in the RCT; external patients aligned to eligibility | not applicable (trial-level) | randomisation |
| Balance / borrowing | PS matching or weighting, g-methods for PP | PS weighting/matching, outcome model or AIPW; a priori balance threshold | sampling PS, power/commensurate/MAP/elastic priors with a prespecified weight | method-of-moments weights; ESS; entropy balancing variant; or STC regression | covariate in linear model, no borrowing |
| Diagnostics | balance before/after, flow counts, control outcomes | balance vs threshold, overlap, missingness | prior-data conflict, ESS of prior, comparison RCT control vs EC | ESS, weight distribution; no PS balance tools | residual diagnostics, robust SE |
| Estimation | Cox/IPW/g-methods | ATT with AIPW/TMLE/weighted | posterior with robust mixture; or IPW/AIPW | weighted outcomes then indirect comparison on chosen scale | linear-model ATE |
| Sensitivity | negative/positive controls, reversed strategies, QBA | tipping point, E-value, QBA, PH check | simulated false-positive rate (at least 100,000 replications in CDE text), test-twice, G-/b-value, no-borrow comparison | quantitative evidence of material bias (anchored); residual-error range (unanchored) | sample size on unadjusted basis is conservative |
| Reporting | TARGET 21 items | SAP before enrolment; audit trail; patient-level data | simulation report; borrowing weight agreed with regulator | TSD 18 Appendix B reporting list (not read) | prespecified procedures before unblinding |

**B. Route-choice logic as published (quoted decision points)**
- Gate 0, is a comparative design defensible: FDA/EMA/CDE as in Q2 (natural history stable and effect large; superiority; objective endpoint).
- Gate 1, is there randomisation: if yes, use it as anchor; borrow only to gain efficiency (HCT) and prefer prognostic adjustment, which "require[s] no assumptions beyond those for RCT-only analyses" ([arXiv 2605.03282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03282)). If no, TTE/ECT/MAIC logic with ignorability assumed.
- Gate 2, patient-level data for every arm: yes -> PS/AIPW family or TTE; no (only published aggregates) -> MAIC/STC if IPD for one arm exists (anchored if common comparator; unanchored only with predictive-accuracy evidence) and otherwise threshold/benchmark comparisons (EMA conservative threshold; Unlearn Q1 "non-tailored benchmark").
- Gate 3, exchangeable external data (Unlearn Q2) and rich enough (Q3): decides weighting alone vs outcome-model-assisted vs structured-assumption models.
- Gate 4, quantified comparability when borrowing: outcome drift and covariate overlap (arXiv 2608.26475; 2605.03282).
- The Unlearn paper itself notes the flow may be revisited: "conclusions at one stage may prompt reconsideration of earlier choices" ([arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12832)).

### Inferences
- For a product team comparing its own comparator workflow: the earliest differences to check are (a) whether a protocol/estimand and suitability record exist before any weighting step, (b) whether time zero is an explicit, shared definition rather than an accident of the data extract, (c) whether sensitivity analyses are prespecified and reported with the main estimate, and (d) whether the output class can fall back to benchmark-only or not-estimable. These are the points where every cited document converges.
- Items the sources do not settle and a product must choose itself: numeric balance/overlap/ESS thresholds, minimum sample or events, how to translate a tipping point into a plain-language verdict, and how to display a calibrated (BenchExCal-style) interval.

### Gaps
- TSD 18 Appendix B/C (reporting list and flow charts) were not extracted, so the MAIC reporting cell is from the main text only.
- This synthesis table abstracts across documents of different legal status (regulator draft, final guidance, reflection paper, preprint review, vendor preprint); status labels are in Q2-Q6 and should be kept when it is reused.
