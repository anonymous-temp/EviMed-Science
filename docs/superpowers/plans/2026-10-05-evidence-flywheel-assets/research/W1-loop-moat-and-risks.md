## Q1. Precedents for a closed evidence loop

### 1a. MAGIC "digital and trustworthy evidence ecosystem"

No revenue figures published on the page. — https://magicevidence.magicapp.org/about/ (undated page, read 2026-10-05) ✓ (2018 foundation date from search snippet ~)
- Concept paper: Vandvik PO, Brandt L. "Future of Evidence Ecosystem Series: Evidence ecosystems and learning health systems: why bother?" ### 1b. Learning health system (US National Academy of Medicine)
- Definition (NAM Leadership Consortium Charter, concept introduced 2006): "A learning health system is one in which science, informatics, incentives, and culture are aligned for continuous improvement, innovation, and equity—with best practices and discovery seamlessly embedded in the delivery process, individuals and families active participants in all elements, and new knowledge generated as an integral by-product of the delivery experience."
- Self-assessment after ~20 years (McGinnis, Fineberg, Dzau, NAM Perspectives commentary "Shared Commitments for Health and Health Care: A Trust Framework from the Learning Health System", 16 Dec 2024): breakthroughs "have simply not translated into overall health system performance gains"; deficits reflect "systemwide fragmentation in the development, financing, and provision of services, and the delays in advancing the generation and application of actionable evidence. An especially key factor is the impact of profoundly misaligned incentives". Their response is a trust framework of "shared commitments", i.e. governance, not technology. — https://nam.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Learning-Health-System_postpublication_v3.pdf ✓ (PDF text extracted)
- Lesson for EviMed: the loop's binding constraint per NAM is incentives/trust, not tooling.

### 1c. Living systematic reviews / living guidelines
- Cochrane LSR guidance (2019 revision): make a review living only if (i) priority for decision-making, (ii) evidence uncertain, (iii) new evidence likely that would change conclusions; must pre-define search frequency, update frequency, and criteria for stopping living mode; retire when evidence becomes conclusive (GRADE), question loses pertinence, no new studies expected, or "when resources become unavailable to continue updating". — https://community.cochrane.org/sites/default/files/uploads/inline-files/Transform/201912_LSR_Revised_Guidance.pdf ; https://ebm.bmj.com/content/28/5/348 ~ (search summaries; PDF not re-read). I found no 2025 replacement of this guidance.
- Australian COVID-19 living guidelines process evaluation (Turner T et al., PLoS One 2022;17(1):e0261479, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0261479): "An updated version of the guidelines was successfully published every week"; 11 FTE staff, 7 panels, >200 health decision-makers, 66 recommendations on 58 topics by 24 Aug 2020; 4,091 citations screened, 189 included; 204,826 page views. Problems: "We still have people working 'til midnight"; staff seconded "for a finite period (most often 3–6 months)"; "The tech stuff [is] still really challenging"; doubts about uptake: "Are people actually using them?"; funded by Australian and Victorian governments plus philanthropy. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8741039/ ✓
- Funding cliff: "The Department of Health will axe its contract with the National Clinical Evidence Taskforce in the new year", announced "two weeks out from the end of its contract on 31 December" 2022, after "more than 200 recommendations" and "about 200 clinicians"; the mpox living guideline survived because its money came from a charity. — Medical Republic, 15 Dec 2022, https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/covid-evidence-taskforce-funding-pulled/8638 ✓
- How often is "living" actually needed? Australian Living Stroke Guidelines survey (Turner T et al., Health Res Policy Syst 2022;20:73, doi:10.1186/s12961-022-00866-7): monthly search judged necessary in only "three assessments (5%)"; 3-monthly 25%, 6-monthly 23%, yearly 30%; "Workload for reviewers was frequently mentioned as a concern"; one respondent: updating "more than yearly is not worth the resources." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9207845/ ✓
- COVID-NMA / McMaster living NMA lessons (Cochrane Colloquium 2023 abstract, Ibrahim S ... Brignardello-Petersen R): "Leverage technology to increase efficiency"; "Consider alternative dissemination strategies such as a website to streamline access to results and overcome publication delays"; "Conducting such reviews requires significant time, planning, and resources"; problems: "turnover of team members and linkage of multiple records associated with the same trial". — https://abstracts.cochrane.org/2023-london/lessons-learnt-conducting-living-systematic-review-and-network-meta-analysis-covid-19 ✓
- Epistemonikos L·OVE (launched as a platform in 2019 post; live since ~2016 per search snippets): "builds upon a collection of around 300.000 systematic reviews"; kept "living" by "our great Epistemonikos network of collaborators"; free access; AI-supported screening per secondary sources. — https://foundation.epistemonikos.org/en/posts/all-health-evidence-free-and-organized-in-one-place-epistemonikos-launched-the-new-l-ove (12 Dec 2019) ✓; AI role ~
- Producer-funding fragility at Cochrane itself: UK NIHR ended core infrastructure funding for UK Cochrane Review Groups on 31 March 2023; "In March, 19 of 52 groups that produce Cochrane's systematic reviews closed"; Cochrane UK (Oxford) to close the following March. — Nature news, Sept 2023 (via HIFA mirror https://www.hifa.org/node/96853382) ~ ; Cochrane's own farewell page now redirects (https://community.cochrane.org/news/fond-farewell-our-closing-review-groups → 301 to news index) ✗

### 1d. AI in living evidence, 2024–2026
- Position statement (Flemyng E, Noel-Storr A, Macura B, et al., JBI Evid Synth 2025;23(11):2162–2166, 6 Nov 2025, doi:10.11124/JBIES-25-00480; jointly Cochrane, Campbell, JBI, CEE): "Evidence synthesists are ultimately responsible for their evidence synthesis, including the decision to use artificial intelligence (AI) and automation"; may use AI "as long as they can demonstrate that it will not compromise the methodological rigor or integrity"; "should be used with human oversight"; "Any use of AI or automation that makes or suggests judgments should be fully and transparently reported" (eligibility, risk of bias, data extraction, synthesis, GRADE certainty, "drafting text that summarizes the overall strength of evidence"); disclosure must name system, version, dates, purpose, justification, financial interests, limitations; "AI tool developers should proactively ensure their AI systems or tools adhere to the RAISE recommendations". RAISE = RAISE 1 (practice), RAISE 2 (building/evaluating tools), RAISE 3 (selecting/using tools); updated June 2025 per search snippets. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594113/ ✓
- NICE "Use of AI in evidence generation: NICE position statement" (ECD11, August 2024): AI methods expected to be declared and justified, with human oversight; scope excludes AI-enabled technologies. — https://www.nice.org.uk/position-statements/use-of-AI-in-evidence-generation-NICE-position-statement (403 to my fetcher) ~ via search snippets/consultancy summaries.
- Wellcome Evidence Synthesis Infrastructure Collaborative (20 Sept 2024): "£45 million in funding over five years" for data and tools for "living evidence synthesis", "responsibly using AI", to make synthesis "faster, but cheaper too"; includes GALENOS (living evidence, mental health); 2026 follow-on call for AI-enabled LMIC hubs at £1.5–1.9M each, ~5 hubs (secondary). — https://wellcome.org/insights/articles/evidence-synthesis-infrastructure-collaborative ✓ (hubs call ~)
- otto-SR (Cao/Bobrovitz et al., medRxiv 13 Jun 2025, doi:10.1101/2025.06.13.25329541; preprint, not peer reviewed): LLM workflow reproduced/updated an issue of 12 Cochrane reviews (146,276 citations) "in two days", ~12 work-years equivalent; screening sensitivity 96.7% vs human 81.7%; extraction accuracy 93.1% vs 79.7%; changed significance in 3 of the meta-analyses. — https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.13.25329541v1 (403 to my fetcher) ~
- Baseline latency that living/AI approaches attack: mean 67.3 weeks (IQR 42) from start to publication of a systematic review; mean 5 authors; 195 PROSPERO reviews (Borah R et al., BMJ Open, 27 Feb 2017). — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5337708/ ✓
- RAISE versions: first posted on OSF Sept 2024 as one paper; second version summer/June 2025 as three papers (RAISE 1 practice; RAISE 2 building/evaluating tools; RAISE 3 selecting/using tools); authored by ICASR/Cochrane/Campbell leads + >30 authors. — https://osf.io/fwaud/wiki/home ; https://www.cochrane.org/events/recommendations-and-guidance-responsible-ai-evidence-synthesis ~ (search summaries)
- Takeaway: what kills living evidence is the recurring human cost per update and episodic funding (COVID taskforce, NIHR→Cochrane); AI lowers per-update cost but every serious body (Cochrane/Campbell/JBI/CEE, NICE) requires named-tool disclosure and human accountability for judgments.

## Q3. Moat realism

### Data moats: the sceptical baseline
- a16z, Martin Casado & Peter Lauten, "The Empty Promise of Data Moats", 9 May 2019: "Most discussions around data defensibility actually boil down to scale effects"; "the cost of adding unique data to your corpus may actually go up, while the value of incremental data goes down"; unique data "harder to find in the noise, is more of a hassle to secure, and takes longer to cleanly label over time"; defensibility more likely from "packaging differentiated technology; understanding the domain … verticalization … go-to-market dominance; and winning the talent war." — https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/ ✓
- NFX, "The Network Effects Bible" (Jul 2019, updated 2024): ranks 16 network-effect types; Data = 9th (examples Waze, Yelp); Expertise 14th, Tribal 15th; asymptote note: "After the dataset reaches a certain size, the algorithm no longer meaningfully improves as the dataset grows." — https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-bible ✓
- NFX, "The Truth About Data Network Effects" (Feb 2020): separates data network effects (rare: "A product's value grows as a result of more usage via the accretion of data"), data scale effects ("asymptote quickly"), and data embedding ("by holding more and more of your clients'/users' data, the harder you are to remove" = switching cost); "Most Founders overestimate the point of diminishing returns for data … perhaps thousands or even hundreds of data points are often enough to get 90% of the product experience value." Real-time data (Waze) is the exception that does not asymptote. — https://www.nfx.com/post/truth-about-data-network-effects ✓
- Greylock, Jerry Chen, "The New New Moats" (21 Jun 2023, updated 8 Sep 2026): "Workflows, integration with data and other applications, brand/trust, network effects, scale and cost efficiency all become drivers of economic value"; industry-specific data "begin[s] to look like a pretty deep moat, especially if you can replace or automate an entire enterprise workflow"; "The new moats are the old moats". — https://greylock.com/greymatter/the-new-new-moats/ ✓

### AI and the commons a loop depends on
- Model collapse (Shumailov I, Shumaylov Z, Zhao Y, Papernot N, Anderson R, Gal Y, "AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data", Nature, 24 Jul 2024): "indiscriminate use of model-generated content in training causes irreversible defects in the resulting models, in which tails of the original content distribution disappear"; "the value of data collected about genuine human interactions with systems will be increasingly valuable in the presence of LLM-generated content". — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y ✓ (metadata + abstract via curl). Implication: a loop that re-ingests its own AI-written evidence as "first-hand" input degrades; only human-verified / real-world inputs compound.
- AI substitutes for community contribution: "Within 6 months of ChatGPT's release, activity on Stack Overflow decreased by 25% relative to its Russian and Chinese counterparts … We interpret this estimate as a lower bound" (del Rio-Chanona RM, Laurentsyeva N, Wachs J, PNAS Nexus 2024;3:pgae400, 11 Sep 2024, doi:10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae400). — https://real.mtak.hu/205789/1/pgae400.pdf ✓ (PDF text extracted)
- Aggregator self-preferencing precedent: CJEU (C-48/22 P, 10 Sep 2024) upheld the €2.42bn fine on Google for favouring its own comparison-shopping results over rivals' in general search. — https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/09/google-shopping-eu-court-judgment-signals-stricter-enforcement-of-abuses-of-dominance ~ (law-firm summaries; competition law bites only with dominance, but the credibility issue for an aggregator ranking its own content is general)

### Why researchers publish on UGC research platforms: credit mechanisms
- Hugging Face Hub DOIs (7 Oct 2022, with DataCite): DOIs make models/datasets citable via "a permanent link that will never expire or change"; new version → new DOI, old one marked outdated; DOI'd objects "are intended to persist perpetually and may only be deleted upon filing a request". — https://huggingface.co/blog/introducing-doi ✓
- Zenodo: on first publish "we register two DOIs: a DOI representing the specific version of your record. a DOI representing all of the versions of your record", then "a DOI for every new version". — https://support.zenodo.org/help/en-gb/1-upload-deposit/97-what-is-doi-versioning ✓ (DOI versioning launched 30 May 2017 per Zenodo blog ~)
- protocols.io: founded 2012; acquired by Springer Nature 26 Jul 2023; each published protocol gets a DataCite DOI with versioning/forking (DOI detail ~); Springer Nature frames sharing as making research "more efficient, enables reproducibility and therefore builds trust in science". — https://group.springernature.com/de/group/media/press-releases/archive-2023/publisher-acquires-protocolsio-driving-open-research/25844774 ✓
- Kaggle: progression tiers Novice→Contributor→Expert→Master→Grandmaster across Competitions, Datasets, Notebooks, Discussions; medals (dataset/notebook medals come from community upvotes); "612" competition Grandmasters among "23.29 million accounts" — https://www.kaggle.com/progression (page body not rendered to fetcher) ~ (secondary summaries/Wikipedia)
- ResearchGate: RG Score retired ~Aug 2022 — formula never published, could be inflated "through heavy Q&A participation rather than research output", and "carried no mechanism for negative signals such as retractions"; replaced by Research Interest score (citations 0.5, recommendations 0.25, full-text reads 0.15, other reads 0.05). — https://www.casrai.org/news/researchgate-rg-score-retired-ri-score ~ (secondary)
- Citation incentive is measurable: articles linking to data in a repository get "up to 25.36% (± 1.07%) higher citation impact" (531,889 PLOS/BMC articles; Colavizza et al., PLOS ONE 2020;15(4):e0230416). — https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02565 ✓
- Pattern: platforms that won contributions gave *portable, citable, versioned credit* (DOI + ORCID-linked authorship + persistence guarantees) or a visible reputation ladder; opaque engagement scores (RG Score) lost legitimacy.

## Q4. Risks of AI-produced research content at scale

### The open-dataset paper flood
- Suchak T, ... Spick M, et al., "Explosion of formulaic research articles, including inappropriate study designs and false discoveries, based on the NHANES US national health database", PLoS Biol 23(5), May 2025: 341 NHANES single-factor papers; per the companion editorial (Byrne JA, Stender S, "More science friction for less science fiction", PLoS Biol 2025;23(5):e3003167, 9 May 2025) counts "averaged four per year" 2014–2021, then 33 (2022), 82 (2023), 190 (2024); "Large language models could then draft the resulting manuscript, allowing the research process to be as 'frictionless' as possible"; recommends desk rejection, in-house statistical review, API keys/unique application IDs for dataset users, FDR correction, pre-registration. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12064006/ ✓ (paper itself: https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pbio00/3003152.html ~)
- Five-database scientometric study (Spick M et al., medRxiv preprint posted 9 Jul 2025; Nature news 15 Jul 2025): FAERS, NHANES, UK Biobank, FinnGen, GBD — 11,554 PubMed-indexed papers in 2024 vs 4,001 in 2021 (2.8×), ~5,000 above ARIMA forecast; fold change China 9.5× vs rest of world 1.2×; rise in formulaic titles. — https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-02241-2 (paywall/login) ~ via https://bioethics.com/archives/98755 and search snippets.
- FAERS disproportionality analyses: "exponential increase" with "unjustified alarm and diluting true signals into overwhelming noise"; READUS-PV reporting guideline (2024) = 32 recommendations in 14 items + 12 for abstracts. — Frontiers in Pharmacology perspectives 2024/2025 (https://frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1488725/full) ~
- Directly relevant to EviMed: these five datasets and MR are exactly what the platform's specialist engines (MR agent, drug-safety/FAERS agent, NHANES-style dataset scoping) make cheap.

### How Cochrane handles updates and corrections
- "What's New" events shown on each version: "New search has been performed", "New citation required and conclusions have changed", "New citation required but conclusions have not changed", "New citation required and major changes", "Amended"; the event date becomes the citation date; search date reported in Abstract/Methods; events cannot be modified after publication. — https://documentation.cochrane.org/revman-kb/dates-and-events-261554285.html ✓
- Withdrawal "only … in exceptional circumstances" (policy 28 Jun 2019); serious errors include "factual errors in describing one or more included studies" (4 Jul 2019); a new citation is not required when only a feedback comment/reply is added. — https://documentation.cochrane.org/x/NR--Bg ✓

## Synthesis: does "publisher + aggregator = moat" hold?

Supported by the evidence
- Loops that compound in this market are distribution + workflow + trusted content: OpenEvidence went ~3M → 18–27M monthly consultations in ~15 months on *licensed* NEJM/JAMA/Cochrane/Wiley content; physician-network platforms (Medlive ~90% of licensed Chinese physicians, Doximity >85% of US physicians) hold position through identity + habit, not proprietary evidence.
- A first-hand source can be a moat asset only if it carries portable credit and audit trails (DOI with versioning, ORCID-linked human authors, Crossmark-style update status, Cochrane-style "conclusions changed" events, retraction checks). Contributors publish where they get citable, persistent credit (Zenodo/HF/protocols.io DOIs; +25% citations for linked data), not opaque scores (RG Score retired).
- Real-world, human-verified data become *more* valuable as AI text floods the web (model-collapse paper) — a loop that captures verified real-world signals (user corrections, outcome data, VCR data under governance) can compound.

Not supported / contradicted
- "Data network effect" from the platform's own AI-generated evidence: a16z/NFX show data value asymptotes early; recycling AI outputs as inputs risks model collapse and citation circularity; ICMJE (Jan 2026) says "Referencing AI-generated material as the primary source is not acceptable" — journals will not treat AI-written evidence cards as citable first-hand sources.
- Publishing at volume from open datasets is now a reputational liability, not an asset: Frontiers rejected 5,513 MR + 1,382 NHANES papers; PLOS ONE requires a second independent dataset; preprint servers turn away >10 formulaic manuscripts/day; arXiv suspends authors for unverified AI output. EviMed's MR/FAERS/NHANES engines sit exactly in the flagged zone.
- Producers do not need the aggregator and vice versa: Cochrane and NEJM license into OpenEvidence; MedSci already markets the same "evidence generation — dissemination — decision-making" chain and is losing research-service revenue to AI (−15.3% in 2025). Being both publisher and aggregator invites the self-preferencing/independence critique (WebMD–Lilly; UpToDate undisclosed COI; Google Shopping), and in China pharma-linked "knowledge" content must be labeled as advertising (Advertising Law Art. 14/19) and AI content labeled (CAC labeling measures, 1 Sep 2025).
- Living evidence fails on recurring maintenance cost and episodic funding (Australian COVID taskforce defunded Dec 2022; NIHR cut closed 19/52 Cochrane groups); most topics need ≤6-monthly updates, so "always-live" everywhere is cost without value.
