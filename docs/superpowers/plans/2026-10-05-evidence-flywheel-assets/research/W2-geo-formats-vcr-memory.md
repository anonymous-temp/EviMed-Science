## Q1 — What makes AI assistants cite a source (GEO mechanics)

### 1.1 Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", KDD '24 (Barcelona, 25–29 Aug 2024) ✓
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735 (v1 2023-11-16, v3 2024-06-28); PDF v3 text read in full (results tables).
- Setup caveat: "GPT-3.5 turbo was used for all experiments" (own generative engine built on search results) + a Perplexity.ai check where source text was uploaded as files (Perplexity does not accept source URLs). So effects are about *content of a page already retrieved*, not about getting retrieved.
- Table 1 (GEO-bench, Position-Adjusted Word Count "Overall"; baseline 19.3): Quotation Addition 27.2 (≈+41%), Statistics Addition 25.2 (≈+31%), Fluency Optimization 24.7, Cite Sources 24.6 (≈+27%), Technical Terms 22.7, Easy-to-Understand 22.0, Authoritative 21.3, Unique Words 20.5, Keyword Stuffing 17.7 (≈−8%).
- Exact quote: "The best methods improve upon baseline by 41% and 28% on Position-Adjusted Word Count and Subjective Impression respectively."
- Exact quote: "our top-performing methods, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, and Statistics Addition, achieved a relative improvement of 30-40% on the Position-Adjusted Word Count metric and 15-30% on the Subjective Impression metric."
- Persuasive/authoritative tone: "we find no significant improvement, demonstrating that Generative Engines are already somewhat robust to such changes."
- Keyword stuffing: "offer little to no improvement"; on Perplexity "it performs 10% worse than the baseline".
- Rank effect (Table 2, all sources optimized simultaneously): "the Cite Sources method led to a substantial 115.1% increase in visibility for websites ranked fifth in SERP, while on average, the visibility of the top-ranked website decreased by 30.3%." Quotation Addition rank-5 +99.7%, Statistics Addition rank-5 +97.9%.
- Domain (Table 3): Cite Sources best for "Statement / Facts / Law & Gov."; Statistics Addition best for "Law & Gov. / Debate / Opinion"; Quotation Addition best for "People & Society / Explanation / History"; Fluency for "Business / Science / Health".
- Combination: Fluency + Statistics best pair, ">5.5%" over any single method (200-example subset).
- Perplexity.ai (Table 5; baseline PAWC 24.1, SI 24.7): Quotation Addition PAWC 29.1 ("a 22% improvement"); Statistics Addition SI 33.9 (≈+37%); Keyword Stuffing PAWC 21.9.

### 1.2 Chen, Wang, Chen, Koudas (U. Toronto), "Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search", arXiv 2509.08919 (v1 2025-09-10) ✓ (abstract + HTML v1)
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08919v1
- Exact quote (abstract): "AI Search exhibit a systematic and overwhelming bias towards Earned media (third-party, authoritative sources) over Brand-owned and Social content, a stark contrast to Google's more balanced mix."
- Numbers (HTML tables, as extracted): Consumer electronics USA — GPT 92.1% earned / 7.9% brand / ~0 social vs Google 51.7% earned / 32.9% brand / 15.4% social. Automotive USA — GPT 81.9% earned / 18.1% brand / 0% social vs Google 45.1/39.5/15.4. Software USA — GPT 72.7% earned / 26.7% brand vs Google 45.4/43.7/10.9.
- Engines differ "in their domain diversity, freshness, cross-language stability, and sensitivity to phrasing"; GPT showed "near zero" cross-language domain overlap (switches site ecosystems by language) while Claude was much more stable across languages.
- No health/medical vertical in this study (verticals: consumer electronics, automotive, software, local services).
- Schema.org: only a *recommendation* ("schema markup (Schema.org) with extreme rigor ... to become an 'API-able' brand") — not a measured effect.
- Relevance for pharma GEO: brand-owned pages (a manufacturer's own site) are what AI search under-cites; third-party authoritative pages are what it cites → a neutral evidence platform is structurally in the "earned/third-party" slot only if it is perceived as independent.

### 1.4 schema.org structured data and llms.txt — measured effects
- **Google Search Central, "AI features and your website" (last updated 2025-12-10)** ✓ https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features
  - Exact: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." and site owners need not create "new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." Eligibility = indexed + snippet-eligible.
- **Ahrefs, "We Tracked 1,885 Pages Adding Schema. AI Citations Barely Moved." (L. Linehan, 2026-05-11)** ✓ (vendor study) https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/
  - 1,885 pages adding JSON-LD (Aug 2025–Mar 2026) vs 4,000 matched controls; diff-in-diff, ±30 days.
  - Google AI Overviews −4.6% ("statistically significant", "about 1 in 2,500" by chance); AI Mode +2.4% and ChatGPT +2.2% "Statistically indistinguishable from zero." Headline: "Adding schema didn't boost citations on any platform."
  - Caveat (theirs): only pages "already inside the consideration set"; discovery effect untested.
  - ~ (secondary only, not verified): a Feb-2026 experiment (Mark Williams-Cook) reportedly showed ChatGPT/Perplexity read JSON-LD as raw text rather than parsing it as structured data.
- **Ahrefs, "We Analyzed 137K Sites: 97% of llms.txt Files Never Get Read" (L. Linehan, 2026-06-15)** ✓ (vendor study) https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/
  - 137,210 domains, May 2026; 28% (38,360) publish llms.txt; exact: "97% of those files received zero traffic in May 2026". Of requests to the 3% that were read, AI bots collectively 19.5%, GPTBot 4.51% the largest single AI bot; "Zero requests came from AI bots for llms.txt files that don't exist. They never go looking." Coding agents (Claude-Code) fetched more than any AI retrieval bot. No evidence llms.txt improves AI citations.
  - Google (John Mueller, quoted there): llms.txt is not needed for AI search; framed as a token-saving crutch for coding tools.
  - Other log studies (not opened, ~): ezy.ai — 83 sites × 12 weeks, OpenAI read llms.txt 7 times, Anthropic 9, Perplexity 0, while fetching robots.txt thousands of times.
- Conclusion for Q1: no measured lift from schema.org or llms.txt on AI citation; both may still help *machine reuse* (APIs, agents) but not assistant citation.

## Q2 — Machine-readable formats for published evidence

### 2.1 HL7 FHIR EBM resources (Evidence, EvidenceVariable, Citation→PublicationRecord, ArtifactAssessment)
- **FHIR R5 (published 2023-03-26, status trial-use)**: Evidence = "FMM 1", "Trial Use", Clinical Decision Support WG ✓ https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/evidence.html — scope: "a machine-interpretable expression of an evidence concept including the evidence variables (e.g., population, exposures/interventions, comparators, outcomes, measured variables, confounding variables), the statistics, and the certainty of this evidence." Elements: variableDefinition (1..*), statistic (with sample size, CI, p, heterogeneity), certainty (coded rating, e.g., GRADE).
- **FHIR R6 is NOT yet published** ✓ (https://hl7.org/fhir/directory.html, read via curl 2026-10-05): 6.0.0-ballot4 = "FHIR R6 Ballot (1st Full Ballot)" 2025-12-18; 6.0.0-ballot5 = "2nd Full Ballot" 2026-07-17; 6.0.0-snapshot1 = "FHIR R6 Stable (Sept 2026)" 2026-07-17, status draft. R5 remains the current published release.
- **In the R6 build, Evidence, EvidenceVariable and ArtifactAssessment are marked "Normative"** ✓ (https://build.fhir.org/evidence.html, .../evidencevariable.html, .../artifactassessment.html; FHIR v6.0.0-snapshot1). Health Samurai (2026-03-19 ✓ https://www.health-samurai.io/blog/fhir-additional-resources) reported that at ballot 4 ~27 immature resources were moved to incubator IGs as "Additional Resources", listing "Citation, Evidence, EvidenceVariable, ArtifactAssessment" in an ebm-incubator "under review"; the current build shows three of the four back in core as Normative, and "R6 is the first fully normative FHIR release."
- **Citation is no longer in the R6 core resource list** ✓ (https://build.fhir.org/resourcelist.html; build.fhir.org/citation.html = 404). In the EBM IG build the resource is now called **PublicationRecord** and the change log says "PublicationRecord Resource will be moved to an EBM-Incubator Implementation Guide" ✓ (https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/ebm/en/changes.html, https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/ebm/en/citation.html).
- **EBM on FHIR IG (hl7.fhir.uv.ebm) has never been published as STU** ✓ (package-list.json via curl): 1.0.0-ballot 2023-12-17 (Jan 2024 ballot, FHIR 5.0.0), 1.0.0-ballot2 2025-03-28 (May 2025 ballot, FHIR 6.0.0); current CI build = 1.0.0-ballot3 on FHIR 6.0.0-ballot5. IG scope: citations, study design/eligibility (cohort definitions), results + variable definitions + certainty, assessments, syntheses, judgments, recommendations, compositions.
- **Project paper**: Soares, ..., Alper, "Making Science Computable Using Evidence-Based Medicine on FHIR: Standards Development Project", JMIR 2024-06-25, doi:10.2196/54265 ✓ (Crossref abstract): created ArtifactAssessment, Citation, Evidence, EvidenceReport, EvidenceVariable; HEvKA (ex-COKA) develops terminologies (study design, statistic type, statistical model, risk of bias) and the FEvIR Platform tools (human-readable viewers + JSON).
- **Adoption (real but niche)**: AWMF (German national guideline registry) — "Digitizing Germany's National Guideline Registry with an Interoperable FHIR-based Infrastructure", GMDS 2025 (Jena, 7–11 Sep 2025) ✓ https://conferences.publisso.de/en/conferences/gmds2025/25gmds053 — FHIR IG "grounded in EBMonFHIR", "existing registry entries converted to FHIR instances", IG planned at fhir.awmf.org. FEvIR Platform release 0.300.1 (2025-05-11) and MAGIC-file conversion into "hundreds of FHIR Resources" (~ search snippets of HL7 Confluence/Google-group pages; Confluence is CAPTCHA-blocked, not verified). No evidence of uptake by AI assistants, PubMed, or major journals.

### 2.2 GRADE Summary of Findings (SoF) table as a card format
- **Cochrane Handbook ch. 14 (updated Aug 2023)** ✓ https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14
  - Contents: "key information concerning the magnitudes of relative and absolute effects of the interventions examined, the amount of available evidence and the certainty (or quality) of available evidence"; "limited to seven or fewer outcomes"; "The numbers of participants and studies contributing to the analysis of each outcomes"; GRADE certainty per outcome.
  - Mandatory: "Cochrane reviews should incorporate 'Summary of findings' tables during planning and publication, and should have at least one key 'Summary of findings' table representing the most important comparisons." Produced with GRADEpro GDT (incl. interactive SoF).
- **Machine-readable SoF exists inside EBM-on-FHIR**: the May-2025 ballot IG defines a `SummaryOfFindings` profile of Composition ("an evidence report combining Evidence and EvidenceVariable Resources, organized around VariableDefinition") plus `CertaintyOfEvidence`, risk-of-bias (ArtifactAssessment), `Recommendation` and `Guideline` profiles, with examples (e.g., "GLP-1 RA vs. Standard care in Adults with type 2 diabetes with moderate CV risk"; "Bariatric Surgery Summary of Findings Example for GIN 2022") ✓ https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/ebm/2025May/artifacts.html (read via curl).
- Takeaway: SoF = the most widely *recognized* human card format in EBM (Cochrane, WHO/GRADE guideline ecosystem); its FHIR serialization is ballot-stage only.

### 2.5 schema.org MedicalScholarlyArticle, ClaimReview; Crossref/DataCite
- schema.org usage (Google web-index aggregation, "Google - August 2026") ✓ read from schema.org pages via curl: **MedicalScholarlyArticle 1K–10K domains**; ScholarlyArticle 10K–100K; MedicalWebPage 10K–100K; Dataset 10K–100K; **ClaimReview 1K–10K**; Claim 1K–10K. Search Console reporting removed from 2025-09-09.
- **Crossref** (API 2026-10-05 ✓ https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=0): 187,300,445 works total; type `report` 970,302; `posted-content` (preprints etc.) 4,114,468. **DataCite** API meta.total 137,700,648 DOIs (✓ https://api.datacite.org/dois). A DOI + Crossref/DataCite metadata is the one format with universal uptake by scholarly indexes (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar) and reference managers; supports relations (is-version-of, is-supplement-to, references).

### 2.6 Adoption ranking (my synthesis from the numbers above)
1. DOI + Crossref/DataCite metadata — >320M records; universal in scholarly tooling. (Adoption: very high)
2. schema.org Article/ScholarlyArticle/MedicalWebPage JSON-LD in HTML — 10K–100K domains; read by search engines; no measured AI-citation lift (Q1). MedicalScholarlyArticle/ClaimReview only 1K–10K, ClaimReview lost its Google display.
3. GRADE SoF table (human format) — mandatory in Cochrane, standard in GRADE guidelines; best-known "card" in EBM.
4. ORKG comparisons — ~69K papers, ~5.9K contributors.
5. HL7 FHIR Evidence/EvidenceVariable/ArtifactAssessment (+ SoF Composition profile) — Normative in the R6 *draft* but R6 unpublished, EBM IG still ballot; adopters: HEvKA/FEvIR, AWMF registry project.
6. Nanopublications — signed, provenance-rich; tiny live registry (~93K) and research-community use.
7. Micropublications — model only.

### 3.2 Methods: how aggregate evidence parameterizes simulations/external controls
- **MAIC** — Signorovitch et al., "Comparative Effectiveness Without Head-to-Head Trials", PharmacoEconomics 2010-10, doi:10.2165/11538370-000000000-00000 ✓ Crossref (cited-by 342). Re-weights IPD of one trial to match published aggregate covariate means of another.
- **EU HTA (JCA) position — Methodological Guideline for Quantitative Evidence Synthesis: Direct and Indirect Comparisons, "Adopted on 8 March 2024 by the HTA CG" (Reg. (EU) 2021/2282)** ✓ PDF https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/4ec8288e-6d15-49c5-a490-d8ad7748578f_en?filename=hta_methodological-guideline_direct-indirect-comparisons_en.pdf
  - "These methods require that all effect modifiers relevant for adjustment are measured. However, this is often unverifiable and unattainable."; "The model and covariate selection strategies for adjustment must be prespecified and based on transparent criteria."; "a conclusion can be drawn regarding an effect only if the confidence interval lies completely above or below a certain pre-specified threshold shifted away from the zero effect" (shifted-null testing).
  - Unanchored: "require the assumption of 'conditional constancy of absolute effects' ... which is very unlikely to be fulfilled. Anchored indirect comparisons preserve randomisation and should always be used in preference to unanchored methods."
- **KM → IPD reconstruction (Guyot 2012)** — Guyot, Ades, Ouwens, Welton, BMC Med Res Methodol 2012-02-01;12:9, doi:10.1186/1471-2288-12-9, PMID 22297116 ✓ (PubMed abstract; Crossref cited-by 2,266). Exact: "The validation exercise established there was no material systematic error and that there was a high degree of reproducibility for all statistics. Accuracy was excellent for survival probabilities and medians, for hazard ratios reasonable accuracy can only be obtained if at least numbers at risk or total number of events are reported."
  - External validation vs original IPD: Value in Health 2022-07-01 (Canadian Cancer Trials Group, 39 trials) ✓ https://www.ispor.org/vih-articles/Volume-25--Issue-7/Validating-Restricted-Mean-Survival-Time-Estimates-From-Reconstructed-Kaplan-Meier-Data-Against-Original-Trial-Individual-Patient-Data-From-Trials-Conducted-by-the-Canadian-Cancer-Trials-Group — OS RMST control arm mean deviation −0.01 months, MAE 0.19 months, mean % bias 0.82%; "Reconstructed IPD could be used to calculate RMST in lieu of original trial IPD".
  - Tooling: Liu, Zhou, Lee, "IPDfromKM", BMC Med Res Methodol 2021-06-01, doi:10.1186/s12874-021-01308-8 ✓ Crossref (cited-by 770).
- **MAP priors / dynamic borrowing** — Schmidli et al., "Robust meta-analytic-predictive priors in clinical trials with historical control information", Biometrics 2014-10-29, doi:10.1111/biom.12242 ✓ Crossref abstract: "under prior-data conflict, a too optimistic use of historical data may be inappropriate"; MAP prior derived from historical trials ≈ meta-analytic-combined analysis "if parameters are exchangeable across trials"; robustness by "adding an extra weakly-informative mixture component."
  - Limit: Kopp-Schneider, Calderazzo, Wiesenfarth, Biometrical Journal 2019/2020, doi:10.1002/bimj.201800395 ✓ Crossref: "strict control of type I error implies that no power gain is possible under any mechanism of incorporation of prior information, including dynamic borrowing" (when a UMP test exists) → borrowing trades type-I error for power; must be declared.
- **Living evidence → priors**: Konstantinidis ... Veroniki, "Characterizing updated and living meta-analyses Part 1", J Clin Epidemiol 2026-09 (online 2026-07-14) ✓ https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/243165/ — trial sequential analysis most common (46/51 studies); "While most approaches addressed type I error, fewer considered type II error or heterogeneity"; "no single method demonstrated superiority across contexts". (ALL-IN meta-analysis, F1000Research 11:549, anytime-valid living updates ~.) No regulator guidance found that specifically governs *living* priors (✗).

### 3.3 Keeping simulated / in-silico results distinct from empirical evidence (guidance language)
- **FDA CDRH final guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" — issued 2023-11-17** ✓ (FR 2023-25470; PDF https://www.fda.gov/media/154985/download)
  - In silico clinical trials: "device safety and/or effectiveness is evaluated using a 'virtual cohort' of simulated patients"; uses include "augmenting or reducing the size of a real world clinical trial".
  - "In all cases, there is a need to demonstrate that the CM&S is credible. For in silico device testing and in silico clinical trials, final simulation results should be submitted to FDA with supporting credibility evidence".
  - "while the general framework is expected to be applicable to in silico clinical trials, this is an emerging methodology for which best practices are still being developed, and this guidance does not provide specific recommendations for generating virtual cohorts or executing an in silico clinical trial."
  - Separates the "comparator" (test data used for validation: bench, animal, clinical) from simulation output; credibility evidence categories (code verification, calculation verification, bench/in-vivo validation...).
- **FDA draft "Considerations for the Use of Artificial Intelligence to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products" (FR 2025-01-07; still DRAFT — no final notice in Federal Register as of 2026-10-05)** ✓ PDF https://www.fda.gov/media/184830/download
  - "7-step process": (1) question of interest, (2) context of use, (3) model risk, (4) credibility plan, (5) execute, (6) document results/deviations, (7) adequacy for the COU. "credibility refers to trust, established through the collection of credibility evidence, in the performance of an AI model for a particular COU."
  - Model risk = "(a) model influence, which is the contribution of the evidence derived from the AI model relative to other contributing evidence ... and (b) decision consequence". Framework concepts taken from "ASME V&V40" sections. Also life-cycle maintenance for "data drift".
- **ICH M15** (above) uses the same model-influence × consequence logic and explicitly cites ASME V&V 40-2018; requires context of use to list model-building data separately from other evidence.
- **ASME V&V 40-2018** "Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling Through Verification and Validation: Application to Medical Devices" — cited by name in M15 and both FDA documents ✓ (via those documents; standard itself not read).
- No guidance found that literally says "label simulated results as non-empirical" — the common rule is structural: declare context of use, model influence (weight vs other evidence), and keep validation comparator data separate from model outputs.

- **AgentPoison** — Chen, Xiang, Xiao, Song, Li, arXiv 2407.12784 (2024-07-17; NeurIPS 2024 ~) ✓ abstract: average ASR "higher than 80%" with poison rate "less than 0.1%" and benign impact "less than 1%"; targets incl. a healthcare EHRAgent.

### 4.5 Mitigations proposed (and their measured cost)
- **A-MemGuard** — Wei ... Wang (NTU, Oxford, MPI, OSU per search), arXiv 2510.02373 (Oct 2025) ✓ (abstract via ICML 2026 virtual page / arXiv listing): consensus-based validation comparing reasoning paths from multiple related memories + dual memory of distilled "lessons"; "cuts attack success rates by over 95%" with "minimal utility cost". (ICML 2026 acceptance ~.)
- **Bhowmik, "The Price of Safety: Benign-Case Utility and Token Overhead of Memory-Poisoning Defenses in LLM Agents", arXiv 2609.22818 (2026-09-19, single author)** ✓ abstract — on benign traffic, write-time defenses (input sanitization, provenance checking, LLM anomaly detection) "show no utility cost we can resolve" (95% CIs ≈ ±4.5 points); a read-time reranker cut accuracy 4.4 points and "quarantines legitimate memories on 33.6% of adjudicated items, reaching as many as 106 false quarantines in a single conversation"; "Where a defense intercepts the pipeline, not whether it uses an LLM, appears to determine its benign-case price."
- Pattern across papers: provenance at write time (who/which tool/which source, immutable), trust = min(provenance, content) rather than content-only scoring (~ search-summary of arXiv 2606.22030 "provenance-capped poisoning defense", not opened), quarantine/rollback, consensus checks; shared memory additionally needs per-principal read views and verifiable deletion (GateMem).

### 4.6 Shared skills are a supply-chain surface (2026 measurements)

### 3.4 Addendum — what FDA's ECT draft says about *published aggregate* evidence as a control ✓
- FDA draft ECT guidance PDF https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download (Feb 2023, "Draft — Not for Implementation"): "this guidance focuses on the use of patient-level data from other clinical trials or from real-world data (RWD) sources"; "This guidance does not address other types of external controls, such as using summary-level estimates instead of patient-level data."; "Sponsors must include in their marketing applications relevant patient-level data ... for both the treatment and external control arms" and ensure FDA access to source data.
