## Q1. Scientific communication platform (SCP) in medical affairs

### 1.1 Definition and components
- **ISMPP poster (Alphabet Health + Alnylam), "An evidence-based framework for Scientific Communication Platform evolution and execution"**, ISMPP 2025 annual meeting poster (survey run 11 Nov–4 Dec 2024, n=54; refs accessed Mar 2025). URL: https://www.pathlms.com/ismpp/courses/106326/sections/116971/documents/149123/download ✓ (PDF text extracted)
  - Definition quoted: SCPs are "internal strategic documents that provide a foundation for consistent and accurate communications with external audiences around the disease state and pharmaceutical product or portfolio" (citing Blankenship et al., ISMPP 2022 poster).
  - "SCPs may include": "Summary of medical strategy & objectives"; "Scientific narrative with hierarchically-organized evidence-based statements"; "Lexicon of key terms".
  - Framework: "Determine pillar categories and corresponding primary statements of the scientific narrative, which when read as a unit can function as an 'elevator pitch' for the product/disease state"; then "Develop secondary and tertiary statements for each pillar"; workshops "to review and align on key datasets, references, and terms (for lexicon)"; "Plan regular updates to reflect evolving data".
  - Who builds it: development teams include Medical leads 98%, Pubs professionals 95%, MedComms professionals 91%; HEOR 88%, agency partners 81% (N=43); "40% of survey respondents indicated that [Commercial] should be included (n/N 17/43)" — inclusion of Commercial described as "debatable".
  - What it feeds (top 5 uses): Pubs planning & development 61%; Congress materials for HCPs 58%; Other educational materials for HCPs 53%; Strategy 42%; Symposia for HCPs 29%. (Patient materials do NOT appear in the top 5.)
  - Use gap: ">40% were not regularly using their SCP (n/N 15/37)"; "Only 5 respondents (N=27) had a system for monitoring metrics of SCP use"; most common format "a deck of >60 slides (14/38, 37%)"; started most often at "Phase II data readout/Phase III planning (16/43, 37%)"; most update annually (11/38, 29%). Top challenges: delayed stakeholder feedback 58%, lack of alignment 58%, complexity of story/data 39%.
  - It cites the MAPS guidance as: Portegies W, et al. *Scientific Communication Platforms—Best Practices for Medical Affairs*. MAPS 2022.
- **MAPS press release (PR Newswire), 3 Apr 2019** — first MAPS SCP guidance (with MedThink SciCom). Defines SCPs as "Scientific communication points about products or disease states that are supported by scientific evidence and are aligned with overall strategy." Covers "practical planning processes and tools ... best practices for training and rollout, and metrics to assess and ensure optimal use." URL: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/medical-affairs-professional-society-launches-resource-to-guide-development-of-scientific-communication-platforms-300823776.html ✓
- **MAPS Standards & Guidance page "Scientific Communication Platform"** (current page; authors Kieran Davey/AstraZeneca, Maureen Lloyd, Cerise James, Clare Jackson, Alka Shaunik/CSL; MAPS Evidence Generation + Medical Governance & Compliance domain teams). Page body is a gated download; the public page gives no definition. URL: https://medicalaffairs.org/standards-guidance-scientific-communication-platform/ ✓ (landing page only)
- **Veeva Vault Medical help (vendor documentation)** — how the leading medical-affairs content system models an SCP: "Scientific Communication Platforms define the scientific position, narrative, objectives, and core medical messages for products, therapeutic areas, indications, or a combination thereof." Structure: Pillars → Communication Objectives (typed "Medical" or "Commercial") → Statements ("Primary, Secondary, and Aspirational") → References & Assets. "Stakeholders leverage these communications platforms to source their messaging and data points used to substantiate medical content"; admins can "link these objectives and messages to content in MedComms using Scientific Statement linking for tracking and reporting." URL: https://medical.veevavault.help/en/lr/939158/ ✓ (vendor doc; undated living page)
- **IQVIA blog (vendor), J. Eichert, 28 Sep 2021**: "SCPs contain the clinically relevant diagnosis and treatment assumptions and beliefs that provide the foundation on which all medical communications are developed." URL: https://www.iqvia.com/locations/united-states/blogs/2021/09/scientific-communication-platforms-and-the-medical-affairs-professional ✓ (vendor blog)
- Design takeaway: the industry SCP is literally "pillar → primary statement → secondary/tertiary statements → references + lexicon", owned by Medical, consumed mainly by publications and HCP materials, and poorly measured (5 of 27 track use). Statement-to-content linking for traceability is a feature of the market-leading tool (Veeva).

### 1.2 Separating non-promotional scientific exchange (Medical) from promotion (Marketing)
- **MAPS, External Scientific Engagement (Field Medical) Standards & Guidance, 2025** (PDF dated "MAPS® 2025"; contributors from Roche, Amgen, Exact Sciences etc.). URL: https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/04_External-Scientific-Engagement_Standards-Guidance_2024_Final-Pwp.pdf ✓ (PDF text extracted)
  - Field Medical is "an integral sub-function of Medical Affairs, with an explicitly scientific and non-promotional focus."
  - "The role of Field Medical is strictly non-promotional; data points related to sales or prescriptions should not be used to assess impact"; impact is "increasingly ... described in terms of scientific awareness and advocacy in stakeholders ... and impact on patients."
  - Field Medical provides "expert, non-biased, fact-based information and data ... via scientific exchange to external stakeholders (clinicians, researchers, patient groups, payers, and others)" and "relays the voice of HCPs, patients ... back to the company" (insight generation).
  - "AI MSLs are an example of an experimental way to provide accessible scientific exchange through generative AI technology."
- **IFPMA Code of Practice 2019** (effective 1 Jan 2019). URL: https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IFPMA_Code_of_Practice_2019_EN.pdf ✓ (PDF text extracted)
  - "'promotion' means any activity undertaken, organized or sponsored by a member company which is directed at HCPs to promote the prescription, recommendation, supply, administration or consumption of its pharmaceutical product(s) through all methods of communications, including the internet." (Art. 1.2)
  - Art. 2.2: material "whether promotional in nature or not, which is sponsored by a company, should clearly indicate by whom it has been sponsored. Promotion should not be disguised."
  - Art. 3: the pre-approval ban "is not intended to restrict a full and proper exchange of scientific information concerning a pharmaceutical product, including appropriate dissemination of investigational findings in scientific or lay communications media and at scientific conferences."
  - Q&A 2.2: the Code "specifically does not seek to regulate ... Promotion of prescription only pharmaceutical products directly to the general public (e.g. direct to consumer advertising)" — left to local law. Q&A 3.1: "A public disease awareness campaign targeted at the public must not promote specific pharmaceutical products." Q&A 7.1: company-financed promotional material "must not resemble independent editorial matter."
- **ABPI Code of Practice 2024 (UK), Clause 26** — the clearest model of what a company may say to the public about a prescription medicine. URL: https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-26-relations-with-the-public-including-patients-and-journalists/ ✓
  - 26.1 "Prescription only medicines must not be advertised to the public."
  - 26.2 "Information about prescription only medicines which is made available to the public either directly or indirectly must be factual and presented in a balanced way"; it must not raise unfounded hopes or mislead on safety, and statements must not encourage "members of the public to ask their health professional to prescribe a specific prescription only medicine."
  - Disease awareness campaigns allowed if "in no way promoting the use of a specific medicine"; best practice is to post SmPC, patient leaflet and public assessment report on the company site.
- **EU Directive 2001/83/EC Art. 88(1)**: Member States "shall prohibit the advertising to the general public of medicinal products which are available on medical prescription only." URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ%3AL%3A2001%3A311%3A0067%3A0128%3Aen%3APDF ~ (wording via search snippets; well-known text)
- **US FDA final guidance, 7 Jan 2025**, "Communications From Firms to Health Care Providers Regarding Scientific Information on Unapproved Uses of Approved/Cleared Medical Products — Q&A" (SIUU): firm-initiated scientific information on unapproved uses to HCPs is allowed if "truthful and non-misleading" with what HCPs need to judge "strengths and weaknesses, validity, and clinical utility"; final version lets firms summarize clinical practice guidelines, not just reprints. URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/184871/download ~ (law-firm summaries: https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/01/fda-finalizes-guidance-on-communication-of-scientific-information)

### 1.3 China — the binding constraint on company-authored public content about a medicine
- **《中华人民共和国广告法》(2021修正)**, Beijing AMR official full text (posted 2022-04-01). URL: https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/cxfw/flfgcxfw/ggl/202204/t20220401_2646388.html ✓
  - Art. 2 defines advertising as a seller "通过一定媒介和形式直接或者间接地介绍自己所推销的商品或者服务的商业广告活动".
  - Art. 15(2): "处方药，只能在国务院卫生行政部门和国务院药品监督管理部门共同指定的医学、药学专业刊物上作广告。"
  - Art. 16: drug ads may not contain "表示功效、安全性的断言或者保证", "说明治愈率或者有效率", comparison "与其他药品、医疗器械的功效和安全性或者其他医疗机构比较", or "利用广告代言人作推荐、证明".
  - Art. 19: "广播电台、电视台、报刊音像出版单位、互联网信息服务提供者不得以介绍健康、养生知识等形式变相发布医疗、药品、医疗器械、保健食品广告。"
- **SAMR Order No. 21 《药品、医疗器械、保健食品、特殊医学用途配方食品广告审查管理暂行办法》** (published 2019-12-24, in force 2020-03-01). URL: https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2020/content_5488918.htm ✓
  - Art. 11(2): such ads may not "使用科研单位、学术机构、行业协会或者专家、学者、医师、药师、临床营养师、患者等的名义或者形象作推荐、证明。"
- Implication: in China a pharma company's own public-facing "科普" that names and differentiates its prescription drug is legally an advertisement (Art. 2), is barred from mass media (Art. 15), may not compare efficacy/safety with other drugs (Art. 16), may not use a doctor's name as endorsement (Order 21 Art. 11), and dressing it as health knowledge is expressly prohibited (Art. 19). Product-level differentiation therefore belongs in the HCP/academic layer; the public layer must be disease- and evidence-level, sponsor-labelled.

## Q2. Knowledge translation (KT) as the frame

- **CIHR definition** (CIHR learning module "Introduction to Evidence-Informed Decision Making", page dated 2012-05-25, quoting CIHR "About Knowledge Translation"): "Knowledge translation is a dynamic and iterative process that includes synthesis, dissemination, exchange and ethically-sound application of knowledge to improve the health of Canadians, provide more effective health services and products and strengthen the health care system." URL: https://cihr-irsc.gc.ca/e/45245.html ✓ (CIHR's own "About KT" page 29418 now 404s)
  - Same page on Graham et al.'s KTA framework: "a knowledge funnel where knowledge is created, aggregated, distilled and tailored into clear and practical products for use in the field. Central to the Knowledge to Action Process is that, at each phase of knowledge creation, producers customize knowledge products to the unique needs of potential users." "The action cycle contains 7 activity phases ... taken from a review of 60 theories of planned action (Graham et al 2006)." ✓
- **Graham ID, Logan J, Harrison MB, Straus SE, Tetroe J, Caswell W, Robinson N. "Lost in knowledge translation: time for a map?"** J Contin Educ Health Prof 2006;26(1):13–24, doi:10.1002/chp.47. URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/chp.47 ~ (bibliographic data via search; content via the CMAJ paper below)
- **Straus SE, Tetroe J, Graham I. "Defining knowledge translation." CMAJ 2009;181(3-4):165–168 (4 Aug 2009)**, doi:10.1503/cmaj.081229. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2717660/ ✓
  - KTA = knowledge-creation funnel (knowledge inquiry → synthesis → tools/products) + 7-phase action cycle (identify problem/select knowledge, adapt to local context, assess barriers/determinants, select-tailor-implement interventions, monitor use, evaluate outcomes, sustain use).
  - "Knowledge inquiry includes the completion of primary research." "Systematic reviews are the foundation of most activities related to knowledge translation." "At the stage of development of tools and products, the best-quality knowledge is further synthesized and distilled into decision-making tools such as practice guidelines, aids for patient decisions or algorithms." "As knowledge is distilled through each stage in the knowledge creation process, the resulting knowledge becomes more synthesized and potentially more useful to end-users."
  - Mapping to EviMed's 3 layers: deep evidence report = synthesis (2nd generation); evidence card for clinicians = tool/product for HCPs (3rd generation); popular-science piece = tool/product tailored to the public (3rd generation, different user). This is the established vocabulary for "先学术，再临床，再科普".

### Evidence-based health information (the standards for the public layer)
- **Gute Praxis Gesundheitsinformation (GPGI), DNEbM working group.** Z Evid Fortbild Qual Gesundhwes 2016;110-111:85–92, doi:10.1016/j.zefq.2015.11.005. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26875040/ ✓ (abstract via PubMed E-utilities)
  - "Evidence-based health information is distinguished by the provision of an unbiased and trustworthy description of the current state of medical knowledge." Must rest on "(a) a systematic literature search, (b) a justified selection of evidence, (c) unbiased reporting of relevant results, (d) appropriate factual and linguistic communication of uncertainties, (e) either avoidance of any direct recommendations or a strict division between the reporting of results and the derivation of recommendations, (f) the consideration of current evidence on the communication of figures, risks and probabilities, and (g) transparent information about the authors and publishers of the health information, including their funding sources." Lists "16 aspects to be addressed in methods papers."
- **Lühnen J, Albrecht M, Mühlhauser I, Steckelberg A. Leitlinie evidenzbasierte Gesundheitsinformation (Version 1.0, 20 Feb 2017; DNEbM + Univ. Hamburg; GRADE-based).** URL: https://www.leitlinie-gesundheitsinformation.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Leitlinie-evidenzbasierte-Gesundheitsinformation.pdf ✓ (170-page PDF text extracted). Recommendations (exact):
  - "Die alleinige verbale Darstellung von Risiken, Nutzen und Schaden soll nicht eingesetzt werden." (verbal-only risk/benefit/harm statements shall not be used; moderate-quality evidence)
  - "Nutzen und Schaden sollen durch absolute Risikomaße dargestellt werden." (benefits and harms shall be shown as absolute risk measures; vs relative risk reduction alone)
  - Percentages may replace natural frequencies "bei Wahrscheinlichkeiten >1%" (high quality); NNT/NNS/NNH "sollte nicht eingesetzt werden"; "gleiche Bezugsgrößen" (same denominators) should be used.
  - Graphics "können ergänzend" supplement numbers (pictograms or bar charts if used).
  - "Narrative können nicht empfohlen werden." (patient narratives/testimonials cannot be recommended)
- **Fact boxes (Harding Center for Risk Literacy) — RCT evidence for a card format.** Brick C, McDowell M, Freeman ALJ. R Soc Open Sci 2020;7:190876 (registered-report RCT, n=2,305 census-matched UK adults via YouGov). "fact boxes-simple tabular messages-led to more comprehension (d = 0.39) and slightly more knowledge recall after six weeks (d = 0.12) compared to the same information in text ... consistent ... across all levels of objective numeracy and education ... no differences between formats in treatment decisions, feeling informed or trust." URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32269779/ ✓ (abstract). Harding Center describes a fact box as a tabular summary of "the best available evidence about the benefits and harms" vs a comparison group: https://www.hardingcenter.de/en/transfer-and-impact/fact-boxes ~

## Q3. Lay / plain-language summaries

### 3.1 EU CTR 536/2014 lay summary + Good Lay Summary Practice (GLSP)
- **Good Lay Summary Practice, Version 1** — adopted by the Commission's Clinical Trials Expert Group (CTEG) 9 Jul 2021, published 4 Oct 2021 (EudraLex Vol. 10). URL: https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-10/glsp_en_0.pdf ✓ (PDF text extracted)
  - Restates the law: EU CTR Art. 37 "requires trial sponsors to submit a summary ('lay summary' or 'LS') that is understandable to laypersons for each clinical trial ... no later than 12 months from the protocol-defined end of the clinical trial, 6 months for paediatric studies, and up to 30 months for non-therapeutic Phase 1 trials." Content per Annex V: "The EU CTR Annex V lists 10 elements that must be included in the LS" — (1) trial identification, (2) sponsor name/contacts, (3) general information, (4) population, (5) investigational products, (6) "Description of adverse reactions and their frequency", (7) overall results, (8) comments on the outcome, (9) whether follow-up trials are foreseen, (10) where more information can be found.
  - Readability: "a well written LS should normally be accessible by young people from the age of 12 years upwards"; readability formulae (Flesch, Flesch–Kincaid) only "as a supplement", because "there is no direct correlation between an acceptable readability score and the actual readability of the content"; recommends patient/public reviewers and formal "user testing".
  - Non-promotion is built in: "The content of the LS should be presented in factual and objective language and should not be designed as promotional or favourable"; "When describing investigational products and comparators, sponsors should not provide promotional information"; to avoid "cherry-picking", "a sponsor policy or SOP that defines the sponsor's strategy on outcomes and endpoint selection ... should be implemented before interim or final trial results are available"; serious adverse reactions listed first.
  - Process: "Planning of the LS should start at the time of protocol preparation"; developed with ">60 industry, academia, patient and not for profit organisations".
- EU CTR applicable since 31 Jan 2022 (3-year transition ended 30 Jan 2025). Consolidated text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/536/2022-01-31 ~ (EUR-Lex blocked automated fetch; wording via GLSP and search snippets)

### 3.2 Plain language summaries of publications (PLSP) and evidence of effect
- **Open Pharma recommendations for PLS of peer-reviewed publications** — Rosenberg A et al. (Oxford PharmaGenesis with Galápagos, UCB, Ipsen, Roche, Pfizer), Curr Med Res Opin 2021;37(11):2015–2016, doi:10.1080/03007995.2021.1971185 (infographic update CMRO 2022;38(6):881–882). Per summaries: PLS should be abstract-style, jargon-free, "unbiased, non-promotional, peer reviewed, and easily accessed" and indexable in PubMed; audience "everybody", not just patients. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34511020/ ~ (full text 403; content via search summaries)
- **Cross-publisher PLSP definition (Taylor & Francis, Sage, Becaris), Open Pharma blog, 28 Jul 2026**: a PLSP is "a standalone summary of a peer-reviewed article written according to plain language principles and published in a peer-reviewed journal"; must reference the original, combine text and graphics, be peer reviewed with its own DOI, open access, and include at least one original author. URL: https://www.openpharma.blog/blog/news/cross-publisher-definition-plsps/ ✓
  - Example of the article type in use by a sponsor: "Plain Language Summary of Publication: Design of the Phase 3 AZALEA Trial of Nipocalimab ..." Am J Perinatol 2025 (PMID 40049596). ✓ (PubMed record)
- **Santesso N et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2015;68:182–190 (RCT, n=143 public, 5 countries)** — a PLS with absolute numbers + GRADE certainty table: "more participants understood the benefits and harms and quality of evidence (53% vs. 18%, P < 0.001) ... Better understanding was independent of education level." URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25034199/ ✓ (abstract)
- **Buljan I et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2018;97:86–94 (3 RCTs; students 171, consumers 99, doctors 64)** — "no difference in knowledge between the infographic and the text-based PLS"; infographic preferred (reading experience d=0.48). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29269021/ ✓
- **Bredbenner K, Simon SM, PLoS One 2019 (n=538 randomized)** — video abstracts and PLS beat graphical and published abstracts on "comprehension, a feeling of understanding, and enjoyment". URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31743342/ ✓ (note: first author paid creator of video abstracts)
- **Stoll M et al., "Plain language summaries: A systematic review of theory, guidelines and empirical research", PLoS One 2022 (90 articles; 33 empirical)** — "We did not find empirical evidence to support most of the criteria we identified in the PLS writing guidelines ... empirical evidence on criteria for high-quality PLSs remains scarce." URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35666746/ ✓
- Readability targets in practice: EU GLSP = understandable from age 12; US practice cites NIH grade 6–7 / AMA ≤ grade 6 for patient materials (e.g., PMC4139691) ~ (secondary; no nih.gov primary page read).
- **China public baseline (for the public layer): 全国居民健康素养水平 2025 = 33.69%** (+1.82 pp vs 2024; n=70,451, ages 15–69; 健康信息素养 50.83%, 基本医疗素养 32.22%, 慢性病防治素养 33.41%), NHC release 2026-01-07 via Xinhua. ## Q4. Patient journey mapping in pharma evidence and communication planning

- **Davies EL et al., "Reporting and conducting patient journey mapping research in healthcare: A scoping review", J Adv Nurs 2023;79(1):83–100** (81 articles; "76.5% (n = 62) of articles published since 2015"; reporting "inconsistent and largely non-adherent with relevant, established reporting guidelines"). Defines the method as "a rapidly growing approach for better understanding how people enter, experience and exit health services." URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36330555/ ✓ (abstract)
- **Nelissen J, Williams A, James C, Male G. "Empowering Medical Affairs Professionals in Their Critical Role in Integrated Evidence Generation Planning." Pharmaceutical Medicine, 20 Aug 2026;40(5):357–364** (MAPS-workshop-informed narrative piece): "patient experience mapping is a relatively new hybrid evidence generation activity that can be used to capture patient experiences and insights throughout the disease and treatment journey, identifying unmet needs". URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13545031/ ✓
- **MAPS External Scientific Engagement guidance, 2025** — lists "Patient journey mapping with external experts" as a Field Medical activity from Phase 1, alongside insight gathering "across all stages of the life cycle" (lifecycle chart Phase 1 → post-launch). URL: https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/04_External-Scientific-Engagement_Standards-Guidance_2024_Final-Pwp.pdf ✓
- **Information needs change by stage (primary study): Pal A et al., "Patient Preferences Towards Plain Language Resources During their Multiple Sclerosis Journey", Patient 2026;19(5):655–671** (n=14 interviews): needs "evolved from the pre-diagnostic phase, where they sought PLRs about their symptoms and potential diagnosis, to the post-diagnostic phase, where they sought PLRs about prognosis, treatment options, and adverse events"; "Healthcare practitioners, especially nurses, were the most common trustworthy primary information source and a way for most participants to validate information received from other sources"; patients asked that resources "cite peer-reviewed sources" and cover family/caregivers. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41851417/ ✓ (first author a Novartis employee; work declared independent)
- **Sponsor-published journey maps in peer-reviewed journals** (shows the format is citable evidence, not just marketing): AstraZeneca-funded "Patient Experience of Hepatocellular Carcinoma and Their Treatment Goals: An International Qualitative Study and Patient Journey Map", Adv Ther 2024;41:3598–3614 (50 patients + 12 HCPs): "Patients described treatment decisions as mostly HCP-led"; goals shift from survival toward QoL with advanced disease. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39052152/ ✓. Glenmark authors, "The Atopic Dermatitis Patient Journey: From Awareness to Advanced Care", Cureus 2026 (narrative review, pre-diagnosis awareness → maintenance → advanced therapy access). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41769453/ ✓
- Adherence stage anchor: WHO 2003, "Adherence to long-term therapy for chronic illnesses in developed countries averages 50%." URL: https://www.paho.org/sites/default/files/WHO-Adherence-Long-Term-Therapies-Eng-2003.pdf ~ (older, via search)
- Design takeaway: journey mapping is used (a) by Medical to find evidence gaps per stage and (b) to plan stage-specific information; each stage has a different evidence question (awareness: burden/symptoms; diagnosis: test accuracy/delay; treatment choice: comparative benefit-harm; adherence/follow-up: safety, persistence, real-world outcomes). No credible source found that prescribes a fixed stage list — stage names vary by disease.

## Q5. Who the public trusts for health information; doctor-authored content on Chinese platforms

### 5.1 Surveys (US/global)
- **Pew Research Center, "Where Do Americans Get Health Information, and What Do They Trust?"**, published 7 Apr 2026 (survey 20–26 Oct 2025, n=5,111 US adults). "85% of Americans say they get health information from health care providers at least sometimes"; "About two-thirds (65%) of those who ever get health information from providers say the information is extremely or very accurate"; 22% get health information from AI chatbots at least sometimes (accuracy views "more mixed"); 36% from social media, of whom 47% say it is "not too or not at all accurate". Pharma/drug companies were not among the seven sources asked. URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2026/04/07/where-do-americans-get-health-information-and-what-do-they-trust/ ✓
- **Pew, 25 Aug 2026** (survey 22–28 Jun 2026, n=3,488): 72% say it is "extremely or very important" that providers tell them when AI is used in their care; 46% unsure whether AI has been used. URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/25/americans-want-transparency-when-ai-is-used-in-their-healthcare/ ✓
- **KFF Tracking Poll on Health Information and Trust, 17 Jun 2026** (fieldwork 7–31 May 2026, n=2,480): 29% used AI tools/chatbots at least monthly for health information (vs 17% in Jun 2024); 31% social media; confidence in telling true from false: 80% for information from their doctor/provider vs 56% for AI; only 36% of AI users follow up with a doctor "most of the time".
