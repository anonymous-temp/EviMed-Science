# Candidate language job: authorization and execution design

Status: design under implementation; no background language job is enabled by this document. Base revision: 64e415ac3. Candidate trials are the first scope; protocol jobs remain separate work.

## Unit and frozen inputs

One unit is (study, owner, subject, candidate trial, language key, axis and complete criterion binding). Freeze the candidate record and criterion hashes, evaluator/prompt/model-policy versions, all input document identities and source/projection hashes, and the latest interactive judgment id, including an explicit absence. Preserve complete criterion text and all authorized patient context; a quote alone does not certify interpretation. A unit digest covers those values, never patient text in operational logs. A bounded work list carries only identities and hashes; real clinical material stays in the authorized data plane. Estimate calls and token ceilings before admission, account for every retry, and refuse unpriced or over-budget work.

## Shared fence and ordering

Study-scoped mutations acquire the per-study advisory and row locks first. A candidate unit then locks the job row, candidate row and sorted source advisory locks. Source/grant-only changes take the corresponding source lock, including a grant with study_id null; they do not subsequently acquire a study lock. File membership and erasure take the study lock before sorted source locks. Source identity is rechecked after locking; never acquire a study lock while holding a source lock. All source permissions, grant changes, role changes, file/projection mutation and erasure, subject withdrawal, candidate updates, ordinary language judgment writes, job cancel/reclaim, and background writes must participate. Missing a participant is a release blocker. Avoid network calls or model inference while holding this lock except the bounded request-body handoff below. Lock acquisition is bounded; lock timeout is a retryable operational failure, not a clinical judgment.

## Admission, transport and cancellation

A leased worker with genuine job identity first reopens every document via the existing authoritative access and cloud-permission reader, verifies subject binding and source/projection hashes and original bytes, and constructs the complete prompt in memory. Before egress acquire the shared fences and recheck owner, authorization, withdrawal, candidate binding, lease owner/attempt, cancellation, and all document bytes. Send through a provider transport that preserves the model gateway's allowlisted origin, TLS, redirect prohibition, timeout, accounting and credential confinement. Its explicit request writable `finish` event means the complete request body has been handed to the local transport, not that the provider received or processed it. Hold the shared fence through that event; abort before handoff on a failed check or canceled lease. On ambiguous write or cancellation after handoff retain an uncertain reservation for reconciliation. Release the fence immediately after body handoff, before waiting for headers or generation. A native `fetch` promise, stream pull, or response header is not this handoff boundary. If a transport cannot provide and test this event without weakening the existing gateway boundary, fail closed and keep the worker disabled.

## Commit and idempotence

After inference, validate the bounded model schema and exact UTF-16 evidence spans against every current authorized source; an unknown is an outcome, not an error. Reacquire the shared locks in the same order. Recheck current permissions, source/projection identities and file bytes, subject withdrawal, candidate record and criterion binding, live lease owner/attempt/cancel state, and that the latest interactive judgment is exactly the frozen id (including none). A changed value means discard the late result and report a retryable stale unit. In the same transaction insert the judgment, all input-document dependencies (even unknown with no quoted evidence), genuine job provenance and unique unit completion checkpoint. A unique study/unit identity with transactional conflict handling makes retries idempotent; no two workers or later interactive write can overwrite one another. Revocation and erasure cover all dependencies, not just returned quotes. Canceled/reclaimed work cannot commit. Do not manufacture a native tool-call proof or reuse a prior run's source grant.

## Required evidence before enabling

Use an isolated PostgreSQL database and barriers to race (1) source permission/grant/member revocation before handoff and before commit; (2) subject withdrawal, projection replacement and file erasure; (3) candidate record/criterion replacement; (4) interactive answer after dispatch; (5) cancellation, lease expiry and reclaim; (6) crash after model completion and retry; and (7) unknown judgment with unquoted context followed by erasure. Verify no unauthorized body handoff or late insert, no duplicate checkpoint, complete dependency erasure, bounded accounting and correct lock order. Use a fake provider only. Then test the real job queue, persistence and reader on both applicability and requirement. No deployed enablement or clinical performance claim follows from those tests. Full protocol support and a paired Arm B/C evaluation remain subsequent gates.

## Isolated prototype status

The isolated branch contains a default-off internal candidate admission, unit, per-resource fence, transport, metered model adapter, single/batch executor and authoritative source verifier. Selected real member, source, grant, candidate, cancellation and erasure mutations now participate in the new locks. Candidate-store prototypes can dispatch through an injected handoff callback and atomically commit a version/lease/CAS-checked judgment with input-document dependencies and idempotence receipt. They are not invoked by any API or worker. The metered adapter delegates reservation and settlement to the existing control-plane model gateway; the transport alone does not meter, and the callbacks are trusted internal seams, not an authorization boundary. These prototypes do not establish that the complete lifecycle is safe: account deletion, file mutation outside participating methods, candidate assessment invalidation, model-call metering and actual job execution still require work.

Still required, in build order:

1. Complete a proof that every role, global grant, source permission/status, projection, file, subject withdrawal/erasure, candidate and interactive judgment mutation participates in the study/source ordering. Audit nested transaction order, account/study deletion and out-of-band filesystem mutation; add deterministic cross-resource deadlock and race tests. The current sample covers selected mutations, not every entry point.
2. Complete candidate assessment dependency invalidation and preserve historical results on erasure. Unit/dependency schema and a prototype atomic lease/CAS commit exist; integrate the genuine source verifier and job lifecycle, including all input documents when an answer has no quotes, and test rollback, crash, reclaim and duplicate attempts.
3. Adapt the existing metered provider call to the handoff primitive while retaining exact reservation, uncertainty, response validation and cancellation semantics. Under the shared authorization fence, re-read the actual source/projection and reauthorize immediately before starting transport; hold the fence only until local writable finish. Verify a revoke racing handoff cannot send after its completed fence.
4. Add candidate-only leased job admission, execution, bounded estimation, source-set freeze and resumable idempotent result persistence. Unknowns are clinical outcomes only after a completed evaluation; failures remain execution dispositions. Preserve DSH as coordinator and do not forge a native proof.
5. Run the complete PostgreSQL barrier matrix above, actual queue-worker cancel/reclaim and dependency erasure tests, then run isolated product acceptance and the predeclared paired Arm B/C comparison. Protocol direction remains unavailable until separately implemented and tested.

An isolated PostgreSQL barrier test covers selected study/source mutations, permission revocation before and during a fake writable handoff, stale lease/CAS cases, and deletion of an unquoted unknown dependency. A synthetic unrelated-study update completed in 0.7–0.9 ms while another study/source was locked (one sample; not a production latency estimate). Earlier global-lock tests demonstrated cross-tenant blocking and that design was removed. These tests do not prove every mutator is covered, actual clinical egress is fenced, or candidate assessments are invalidated. No provider request was made.


## Additional enablement blockers

- A distinct internal `match_language` slow-lane kind and unit/batch executor have been exercised through the real PostgreSQL job queue using a fake provider. They are not registered in production composition or exposed through a user route. The candidate admission is internal and estimates uncached peak pricing, but no production route/composition or user-facing job result contract exists and no worker is enabled. The adapter's 30-second handoff/transport bounds are prototype bounds, not measured production defaults.
- Candidate writes now update a per-subject/trial/key current-version head under the study and candidate locks; an erased head is a tombstone and does not resurrect an older answer. Legacy data bootstraps from its prior ordering. Mixed-version concurrent deployment is not safe: an older binary can write without updating this head, so coordinated version fencing and migration/release tests remain required.
- The job scenario durably names pre-handoff input document/source ids and hashes, and a successful judgment retains both original and projection ids. There is no complete in-flight cancellation/erasure mechanism for those pending dependencies. Candidate assessments are marked invalid on source/judgment changes, and new evaluation snapshots use an epoch CAS; source mutation and assessment invalidation remain separate transactions for some paths, creating an unresolved visibility window.
- The single-unit executor's prompt is a prototype; frozen Arm B/C semantic equivalence, total budget planning, multi-unit partial failures and UI/readback acceptance have not been measured.


The candidate-only queue prototype passes a fake-provider round trip and deduplicates a completed unit on a fresh lease without another charge. It has not established the required multi-unit operational success, production budget ceilings or durable pre-handoff cancellation/dependency lineage. Protocol-direction background matching remains unavailable.

The candidate assessment epoch CAS rejects a delayed assessment when a new language judgment or tracked erasure changes its generation; old callers without the epoch remain compatible, so this does not establish mixed-version safety. A PostgreSQL barrier test covers an interactive judgment arriving between evaluator read and save. Source permission changes may commit before downstream invalidation and require atomicity before enablement.

## Handoff checkpoint (incomplete, do not enable)

The user directed work to stop at this checkpoint. The isolated branch includes internal candidate admission and an internally enqueued `match_language` slow-lane prototype. It is **not registered in the production composition, has no user route and is not ready to enable or merge**. A synthetic fake-provider test exercises admission, genuine PostgreSQL queue/lease, authorization, metering adapter, judgment/dependency commit, and idempotent resume. The protocol-to-patient background direction is not implemented.

The attempted lock ordering is actor, study advisory/row, job, candidate, then sorted source locks; source/grant-only mutators take the associated source lock, with study lock first when their study is known. Current touched paths include membership add/remove, source creation/status/cloud permission, document creation/deletion/projection, grant create/revoke, subject/document erasure, account/study/project deletion, candidate mutation, language write and job cancel. This list has not yet been independently proven exhaustive or free of lock inversion with outer transactions. The earlier single global lock was removed after a synthetic unrelated tenant waited about 52 ms during a 50 ms hold; with distinct study/source locks, one synthetic unrelated mutation completed in roughly 0.7–1.7 ms while another basis was held. Neither is a production contention benchmark.

A candidate head and language/study basis epochs were introduced. New evaluator attempts sample the study basis before reading data and compare both basis and language epochs on assessment write; synthetic PostgreSQL barriers cover permission and interactive-answer races, and historical candidate assessments are retained but invalidated. Some existing callers remain compatible without epoch values. Old binaries can still bypass the new heads and epochs; a database-enforced mixed-version fence and rollout proof are mandatory before enabling. Full invalidation of every candidate assessment and atomic source-grant lifecycle must be independently audited.

The job scenario durably contains document/source ids and hashes before dispatch, and completed judgments retain both original and projection dependencies, including unquoted context. There is no complete active-job dependency invalidation and cancellation workflow when a grant or subject is withdrawn. The evaluator rechecks on commit, but this does not prove every deletion, external file mutation or in-flight model cancellation race. Provider body handoff uses local writable `finish`, which is not proof of provider receipt. The metered adapter, job cancellation/reclaim, budget-estimation/retry semantics, invalid response handling, and full fake-provider race matrix need independent review and further tests. The prototype's 30-second bounds and prompt have not been measured or calibrated.

Outstanding release gates: database-enforced mixed-version compatibility; complete mutation/lock-order audit including account deletion and global grants; atomic in-flight dependency invalidation and cancel/reclaim; source and assessment validity across every evaluator path; complete budget and multi-unit partial-result handling; production composition and safe default-off configuration; protocol direction; independent security/clinical review; and real product acceptance plus a frozen paired evaluation. No real patient or paid provider call was made in this branch.
