# Offline campaigns (RSI lab)

Platform experiments run here, offline, on a dedicated lab host, and nowhere else: the online experiment engine was deleted on 2026-11-04 (`CNY 193.93` over 12,344 calls and no candidate verdict; design §23). Production keeps serving and a thin import/rollback layer (`apps/server/src/rsi*.mjs`, opt-in). The closed consumer registry admits seven surfaces, `frontier-selection` — the frontier feed's selection threshold, replayed by production's own `frontierMixSelect` over whole days —, `capability-skill` (below), whose scoring has a closed registry of its own, `harness-patch` (further below: a patched runtime image, with more scorers, a judged one among them), `tool-patch` (the same consumer for a candidate that holds code as well as text; its own consumer identity, and the runtime image's error budget, shared with harness-patch) and two module policies, `frontier-screen`, `frontier-edit` (further below: one component of a module's policy, measured with production's own classes; a qualified round is a pull-request proposal, never an import). Other surfaces answer `campaign_consumer_not_qualified` (`geo-supplements`: its yardstick needs a lane arm that writes from evidence-zone cards and a supply of cases nobody has); a surface the domain package does not list at all is `campaign_surface_invalid`.

**Who does what** (owner 2026-11-03; the manifest's `models` is `{solver, improvers[], judges[]}` and the domain refuses anything else). The solver of every measured arm is EviMed Science, DSH + DeepSeek Flash. Every improver (whoever proposes, edits, composes, selects, reflects, distils or writes a candidate) and every adjudicator (judge, labeller, jury) is a pinned Claude model, and Claude runs on the operator's box under the operator's subscription, with **one exception: the frontier-selection jury is Qwen 3.8 Max, which runs on the lab itself, unattended** (below, with the measurement that earned it). Everywhere else **the lab calls no model and holds no provider credential**: it runs the deterministic steps (plan, replays, scoring, statistics, receipts, signing, reports) and takes an operator input only as files in its inbox. The exception is one named identity, `RSI_FRONTIER_JURY` in the domain package (a judge of role `jury`, provider `dashscope`, model `qwen3.8-max`, on the surface `frontier-selection`): on any other surface, in any other role, as another Qwen or a DeepSeek model it is refused (`campaign_judge_refused`), and as an improver Qwen is refused everywhere.

## The monthly frontier-selection campaign

Production pushes, on the 1st at 00:30 Asia/Shanghai, a read-only export of months M-2 and M-1 (`scripts/ops/rsi-frontier-export.mjs`, run inside the serving web container by `deploy/rsi-campaign/production/rsi-frontier-push.sh`) into the lab's write-only inbox. Beside the frontier snapshot the same script pushes `signals-<M>.json.gz` (`scripts/ops/rsi-signals-export.mjs`, same container and limits): closed-code counts of what accounts asked and was not served (module, kind, capability, operation, data shape, reason) and of the gap leads filed (source, track, gap code, code, endpoint), as distinct accounts and occurrences over the two months, a combination under five accounts listed as `"<5"` with its occurrences withheld, no id and no text. A failure of it never fails the frontier push; no lab step reads it, the operator's tool-lead intake does. The export carries the frontier module's **incumbent policy identity**, `incumbent: {revisionId, threshold}`, from the serving layer's own function (`rsiIncumbent`, `apps/server/src/rsiServingAdapters.mjs`): `default:<digest of the code defaults>` or `evolution-module-policy-frontier:<revision>`. The lab binds exactly that as the campaign's `target.baselineRevision`, which is what the importer compares a bundle's target with (`campaign_baseline_changed` otherwise); an export without it (a release that predates the function, or an export script not yet reinstalled) is still measured, with `target.baselineRevision` `unbound:<releaseId>`: the report says the month is a lab reference only, and `finalize` refuses to build a bundle (`campaign_export_incumbent_missing`); a malformed incumbent, or one whose threshold is not the export's, is refused (`campaign_export_incumbent_invalid`). The export also carries production's dependency digest for the surface (`consumerDigest`), and `plan` and `build` compare it with this lab's: a month that differs, or an export without it, is measured, reported as not importable, its baseline `unbound-consumer:<release>`, and `finalize` refuses it (`campaign_export_consumer_changed`). To make months importable: release first, then install the export script (and delete a month's cached export on the serving host to make it again). At 02:00 the lab runs `deploy/rsi-campaign/rsi-lab-monthly.sh`, every step in the lab image (`deploy/rsi-campaign/Dockerfile`: Python 3.12 + the pinned statistics, Node 22), resumable through its `STATUS` file:

1. **plan** (`frontier_assemble.mjs plan`): replay every registered threshold on every day from the state before that day's mix (the export takes back what the mix picked, keeping item-time safety picks), and fix each day's labelled set from the replays alone — rows at or above the lowest registered threshold, safety alerts, and every row any registered threshold selects — split by phase (`idsByRole`: development first, confirmation only the ids development did not ask).
2. **calibrate and label**, on the lab, in two phases and with no one in the loop. The jury is **Qwen 3.8 Max** (`frontier_labels.mjs calibrate|label`, DashScope, the lab's one provider credential), and it is the one adjudicator that is not Claude: owner ruling of 2026-11-03, after a measurement on the same 124 calibration rows of the 2026-10-25 human anchor (`../frontier-selection/gold.jsonl`, exemplars from the odd half only), two passes each, disagreement abstains:

   The paired difference between Qwen and the best Claude setting is 0.217 (95% CI [0.058, 0.405]; row by row 12 against 6 with an abstention counted as not right, McNemar p 0.24); the two human labellers agree at κ 0.87. Rubric v2 was revised for Claude on the odd anchor rows only, and its gain over v1 is within noise. A Claude run without `--safe-mode` read the operator box's CLAUDE.md and memory and scored 0.83: that run leaked the box's context and is **not valid**. The qualification floor is the code's (κ ≥ 0.4, pick precision ≥ 0.5, abstention ≤ 35%, ≥ 90% of the rows labelled), and a calibration that does not meet it stops the campaign instead of lowering the bar (`campaign_labels_unqualified`, nothing of the month labelled).

   The jury reads only the source's own text; the rubric is **v1** (`frontier-jury-v1`, byte for byte, `frontierJuryVersion` is `frontier-jury-v1:7ac3c0f634c5`, so the labelling answers already in `state/jury-cache.jsonl`, keyed on item, pass, jury version and model, are still served). The key is read from its file (`/etc/rsi-lab/credentials/dashscope.key`, mounted read-only at `/run/rsi-credentials/dashscope.key` into these two steps and no other, network `rsi-lab-net`) and appears in no file, log or error (a failure is a closed code; a key that is missing or blank is `campaign_jury_credential_missing` before docker is asked to mount it). Money goes through the lab's budget ledger one batch of forty calls at a time: reserve an upper bound, begin the spend, make the calls, settle the priced actual (a model with no price refuses the run before money moves; a request the provider rejected by status is not charged; an attempt that may have billed, a timeout or a lost connection, retries included, is charged at its upper bound), so a stop (`campaign_budget_exceeded`, the month's cap, an unavailable provider: exit 5, outcome `not-run`, a report, no build) is resumed by starting the month again, and the cache serves every answer already paid for. No number of failed starts locks a month: a batch whose stage key was begun, settled or held from an earlier month takes the next free one, its holds staying against the ceilings (`campaign_budget_stage_keys_exhausted` names the end of that search, past 1,000 keys for one batch). A calibration or labelling with an item that has no answer for both passes ends the month the same way (`campaign_jury_unavailable` / `campaign_labelling_incomplete`) rather than building on it.
   **How a refusal reads.** A pass DashScope's content inspection refused (a 400 whose body's code is `data_inspection_failed` or `DataInspectionFailed`) is an abstention with `basis: jury-refused`: cached, not charged, not asked again, counted apart as `labelCounts.refused`. Every other 400 is about the account or the request, not the text, and arrears are one (DashScope answers them with a 400 `Arrearage`): `jury_http_400`, nothing cached, nothing charged, the run ends in `jury_unavailable` or incomplete (exit 5), the confirmation corpus stays unopened, and a healthy restart labels everything. The provider's message reaches no error or log.
   **The calibration is fresh each month** (owner 2026-10-26: calibrated each month, and one that does not qualify stops the campaign). It has its own cache, `state/<month>/calibration-cache.jsonl`, never the shared one: a start of the month resumes it without paying twice, the next month asks the provider again, so a moved model alias shows up here instead of in a replay. About ¥5 a month (the 124 rows, two passes each).
   **What the report says it cost.** Every record in a cache names the campaign that paid for it, and a file's `costCny` sums that campaign's records for the file's items over all its starts, the upper bound of a failed call included, so the signed figure is the ledger's; a record of another campaign or from before the stamp counts 0. The report prints pick, ok, no, abstained and refused apart, and no total that counts an abstention as a label.
   **Two phases.** The plan fixes the rows by phase (`idsByRole`); `label --phase development` runs first and writes `labels-development.json` (phase, month, id prefix, export digest, jury version, prompt digest, provider and model, the priced cost), `label --phase confirmation` only after `decide` below has found a candidate. The lab refuses labels made from another export, phase or jury, or from an unfinished run.
   **decide** (`frontier_assemble.mjs decide`): from the development labels alone, the registered development choice. Only a frozen candidate makes the lab label the confirmation month; a month whose development finds none ends there (`campaign_no_improving_candidate`), its confirmation month neither labelled nor opened, no alpha drawn, and its report says the confirmation month was not labelled (labelling both months up front wasted most of ¥106.59 on 2026-11-01). The runner reaches the same choice from the same source; `merge-labels` then joins the two phases (one jury, one export, each finished, each id inside its phase, the cost summed) for the build.
3. **build** (`frontier_assemble.mjs build`): manifest and inputs (judge: the jury, `{role: jury, provider: dashscope, model: qwen3.8-max, id: jury version}`, admitted by the domain on this surface alone; no improver: the candidates are the registered grid; baseline: the export's incumbent identity). Development = month M-2, confirmation = month M-1 (sealed: new items no campaign has opened). Claim `superiority`; the sampling unit is the **day** (`frontierMixSelect` shares one day's size, group shares, language target and event picks across sources, so sources inside a day are not independent); endpoint = a day's weighted accuracy over its labelled set, a pick weighing 3; groups = confirmation days with ≥20 labelled rows (decided from labels, never outcomes). The supply report gives days, labelled rows, abstentions, replay fidelity against production's real picks, selector drift between lab and production, and the minimum detectable effect. Fewer than 25 measured confirmation days make the month directional: the runner reserves no alpha (`directional.mjs`) and the statistics give no verdict.
4. **run** (`run.mjs run`): development freezes the registered threshold with the highest mean day difference among those losing no safety pick and selecting at most 1.1× the baseline volume (ties: nearest the baseline; no positive difference ends the campaign). Before confirmation the runner reserves the quarter's error budget and the corpus in the confirmation ledger (a corpus is opened by one campaign only; a surface's confirmations draw at most α = 0.05 per quarter, 0.05/3 per monthly campaign), then measures the frozen policy and the baseline and stops at `awaiting-evaluation`.
5. **receipt** (`evaluator.mjs receipt`, own Ed25519 key, no network): recomputes the development choice and the confirmation (twice — a deterministic consumer gives the same bytes) and signs a receipt bound to the runner's request, including the integrity facts it attests. The runner accepts it only when those facts equal its own observations; a missing or unbound receipt is retried, never persisted.
6. **resume** (`run.mjs resume`): the registered statistics (`statistics.py`, procedure `rsi-paired-group-v5`: one-sided paired t at half the claim's alpha, ≥25 groups, influence and zero-variance checks; a candidate losing a safety pick is not qualified; `superiority` requires the zero-model-call cost identity).
7. **report**: `monthly_report.mjs` writes `report.json` and a Chinese `report.md`, signed by the evaluator, into `/srv/rsi-lab/outbox/<month>/`, whatever the verdict or the stop. A qualified campaign is also finalized (`evaluator.mjs evidence` + `finalize.mjs`, publisher and evaluator file keys, network none) into a signed `bundle.json`, whose expiry is the campaign's deadline, and published under the name of its bytes into `outbox/bundles/` for production to pull ("In production", below).

`reports/statistics-v5-qualification.json` is the procedure's own qualification: two seeds of 20,000 simulations per scenario, including superiority nulls (normal and skewed) and a modest effect at 25 and 31 day groups and at α 0.05 and 0.05/3. It is mathematical evidence about the procedure, never about a real candidate.

## The capability-skill campaign and its scorer registry

A candidate is a personal skill mounted on the account its arm runs under; the arms ran on production (one lane, automated) and what reaches the lab is each run's final answer and settled cost (`skill-arms` sources, `skill_campaign.mjs`). What scores an answer is the registration's `scorer`, an id of the closed, frozen table in `skill_scorers.mjs` — the registry the file means by "Closed consumer registry" (`campaign_runner.mjs`): no executable path, no dynamic import, no function handed in by the caller; an id outside the table answers `campaign_scorer_not_qualified`, and a registration that names none is `medcalc`, what every campaign before the table was.

| scorer | source `benchmark` | row | the run's score in [0, 1] | failure slice (registration key → row field) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `medcalc` | `medcalc-bench` | `{id, calculatorId, gold: {answer, lower, upper}}` | MedCalc-Bench's `evaluate.py` over the `Final answer:` line: 0 or 1 | `failureCalculators` (integers) → `calculatorId` |
| `bixbench-deterministic` | `bixbench` | `{id, topic, gold: {questions: [{n, qid, eval_mode, ideal, range}]}}`, one row per capsule | the share of the capsule's `str_verifier` and `range_verifier` questions answered correctly under the benchmark's own graders; `llm_verifier` questions are not scored and a question a judge would have to settle counts as not correct, so this is a floor of the judged score | `failureSlice` (strings) → `topic` |
| `arb-deep` | `autoresearchbench-deep` | `{id, category, gold: {type: 'deep', titles, arxiv_ids, is_none}}` | 1 if the first candidate after `Final answer:` is the target paper (arXiv id, or title equal / truncated / 0.92 similar), or an explicit `None` for a `None` target; titles in the judge band are not matched | `failureSlice` → `category` |
| `arb-wide` | `autoresearchbench-wide` | `{id, category, gold: {type: 'wide', titles, arxiv_ids}}` | the IoU of the gold arXiv-id set and the answer's ids, a title-only line mapped to its gold id (`score_arb.py`'s `iou`) | `failureSlice` → `category` |

The BixBench port follows the benchmark's `graders.py` (Future-House/BixBench, read 2026-10-31): `str_verifier` is equal after keeping `[a-zA-Z0-9]` lower-cased, or the answer a substring of the ideal (the open-answer harness's `partial_match`, so a short answer such as `1` matches `166`); `range_verifier` is `lower <= value <= upper` on the first number of the answer. One deliberate deviation: an answer that cleans to nothing is never correct (the official `"" in target` is true). The answers are read as `score_bixbench.py` reads them (after the last `Final answers:` line, one `<n>.` line per question; ASCII digits only, where Python also reads another script's digits, so the port can miss a credit and never grants one). The AutoResearchBench ports are `score_arb.py` rule for rule, including a port of `difflib.SequenceMatcher`'s ratio. `tests/fixtures/scorer-vectors.json` holds synthetic inputs with the Python scorers' outputs; `tests/test_scorer_vectors.py` rebuilds it from them (so a change to `score_arb.py` fails there until the file is regenerated with `python3 -I tests/scorer_vectors_gen.py`) and `tests/skillScorers.test.mjs` asserts the JS ports reproduce it.

The scorer is part of the consumer identity: `skillCampaignConsumerIdentity(registration)` is the digest of `skill_campaign.mjs`, `skill_scorers.mjs` and the scorer id, pinned in the registration as `statistics.consumer_id` and attested in the evaluator's receipt. Three consequences. A registration made for one scorer cannot be evaluated under another. Any change to either file is a new identity, so a campaign is assembled and run by the same image: the lab runs its own image (`rsi-lab-install.sh lab` builds it from `git archive HEAD`), the 2026-11-01 development-only campaign is unaffected by this code, and the lab image must not be reinstalled between assembling a campaign and running it. And the medcalc numbers are unchanged by the registry — `tests/skillScorers.test.mjs` pins the development and confirmation digests, the observation and cost digests and the statistics of the pre-registry fixture — while its identity digest, being a hash of the code, is not the pre-registry one.

**Candidates come from the operator's Claude session, not from the lab** (the lab's Qwen improver, `skill_improver.mjs`, and its `improve`/`improve2` steps were deleted 2026-11-03). The session writes `{improvers: [{role, provider: "anthropic", model: "claude-…"}], candidates: [{title, description, instructions}]}`; `skill_assemble.mjs --register --candidates raw.json --out candidates.json` gives each candidate its content address (arm `cand-<12 hex of the digest>`, digest, size), the arms are run on production under those ids, and the assembly records the improvers in the manifest (`models: {solver, improvers, judges: []}`: the scorers are deterministic code, so no judge) and the registration, recomputing the address of any candidate that carries its text (`campaign_candidate_digest_mismatch`). The candidates cost the lab nothing: the report has no improver spend. `skill_assemble.mjs` takes `--scorer <id>`, `--failure-slice a,b` (strata) and `--pilot-rationale` for a scorer other than `medcalc`; `--failure-calculators` is unchanged; the lab's `rsi-lab-skill.sh <month> run` has the one command.

**Judged surfaces are not in this registry** (open-ended answers, rubrics, research reports, BixBench `llm_verifier`): a personal-skill campaign scores with code or not at all. The `harness-patch` consumer has a registry of its own that adds a judged scorer, and builds what a judged consumer needs — see the next section.

## The harness-patch campaign (2026-11-01, RSI round 1)

A candidate is a patch of the runtime image: `{guidanceSupplement, skills: [{name, description, body}]}` applied to the image the baseline ran on, measured on benchmark runs that happened on a lane stack (the baseline on the unmodified image, each candidate on a delta image). The runs, the verdict records and the pre-registration reach the lab as files; nothing here builds an image, runs a lane or imports anything into production — a qualified result is a pull-request proposal, never an activation (the surface is registered `shadow`).

- **The candidate and its digest** (`harness_campaign.mjs`). `harnessPatchDigest` is the sha256 of the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the bundle (skills sorted by name, names `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,63}$`) **and the image identity it was measured on** (`{id, digest}`): the same patch on another image is another candidate. A candidate arm `cand-<12 hex of the digest>` carries its bundle, and the lab recomputes the digest from it; a digest that is not the bundle's, an arm id that is not the digest's, or an always-on growth (guidance supplement plus each skill's name and description) over 4,000 characters refuses the source. Sizes are UTF-16 code units. `export_harness_arms.py` computes the same digest in Python; `tests/fixtures/scorer-vectors.json` (`harnessDigest`) holds vectors both sides reproduce.
- **The tool-patch surface** (2026-11-02, `tool_patch_campaign.mjs`). A candidate that holds code (a tool, a connector) beside the text that tells the model about it is the same campaign under another surface key: the registration says `surface: "tool-patch"` (`register_round.py --surface tool-patch`; a harness-patch registration writes no such field, so its digest is what it was), the manifest's `surface` and `candidateKind` are `tool-patch`, and its arm adds `harness.code = {image (the patched image the arm ran on), patchSha256 (of the code diff), files: [{path, sha256}], toolCatalogueChars (what its tools add to the first request)}`, bound into the candidate digest (`harnessPatchDigest(bundle, image, code)`; Python: `export_harness_arms.py --surface tool-patch`, `make_harness_bundle.py --tool-patch --code-path ...`) and counted in the always-on growth, so the 4,000-character cap prices the tool catalogue too. A registration for one surface refuses the other's arms (`harness_arms_candidate_invalid`) and a manifest whose surface is not the registration's (`campaign_surface_registration_mismatch`). The error budget is the consumer's (`rsi-alpha:<prefix><consumer>:<quarter>`, `alphaLedger` in `campaign_runner.mjs`), and harness-patch and tool-patch both change the runtime image (consumer `dsh-harness`), so they draw on one ledger; rows written under a surface's own key before 2026-11-03 still count and are never rewritten. The consumer identity (`tool-patch-arms-v1`) digests this surface's own files; a harness-patch campaign's identity, and every harness-patch pin written before the surface existed, is untouched.
- **Tool leads** (2026-11-04, `tool_leads.mjs`, brief `briefs/tool-lead-scout.md`). Finding tools the platform lacks in the literature was the online engine's scout (the owner's idea of 2026-10-04; deleted 2026-11-04 after about 96% of its CNY 193.93 had gone to scouting, all 37 paper-scouting runs of the live deployment ended "waiting for material" and no candidate ever got a verdict). It is now an operator-run intake and no service: an operator's Claude session (Sonnet reads sources, Opus decides) writes lead cards (`rsi-tool-lead-cards/1`: method, track, tool kind, the round it goes on to — `tool-patch` or `capability-skill` —, sources by PMID or DOI each with a verbatim quote, a Europe PMC query, a development plan naming a benchmark family and its scorer, optionally the signal codes it answers). `node tool_leads.mjs check --cards cards.json [--signals signals.json] [--pools $BENCH_ROOT/pools] [--literature-fixture f | --literature-record f] --out leads.json` calls no model and decides what code can: the schema; each quote as a substring (`quoteIsPresent`) of the title or abstract Europe PMC holds for the PMID or DOI (the URL is built here and never taken from a card; each response's sha256 is recorded); the 24-month count of papers whose title or abstract carries the method's own name or an alias, from a query held to a closed `TITLE_ABS:`-fenced syntax with every clause tied to that name (otherwise `tool_lead_card_invalid` before any request); coverage against what ships (tool graph, runtime tools, capabilities, method records, virtual-study methods) as the names matched — an exact name or a card name wholly inside a shipped one is `tool_lead_already_shipped`, a shipped name inside the card's is only shown as `related`; demand from the signals export alone, for the signal groups the operator selected: printed beside the score as the operator's claim, because no code ties a group to the method, and not part of it; and eligibility ported from `evolutionResearchEligibility` and tightened by what the online run taught: a lead with no measurable development data (no benchmark family of the lab's scorer table, a scorer that table does not list for it, with `--pools` fewer than 25 confirmation rows) is refused before any request is made (`tool_lead_no_measurable_development`), as are track X, fewer than two distinct verified sources and fewer than two papers in 24 months. Priority is `evolutionPriorityBreakdown`'s weights on measured features only (literature 3·log1p of the fenced count, verified sources up to 5, coverage 3, development 2), the breakdown beside the score; a count a card states is ignored and listed beside the measured one. The output (`rsi-tool-leads/1`) is deterministic given the cards, signals, pools, tree and recorded responses and carries every input's digest. A lead is a hypothesis and proves nothing: it goes on to a tool-patch or capability-skill round, whose registration and statistics decide.
- **A round's money** (`round_spend.mjs`, 2026-11-04). Metered in three places and printed as three lines that are never added: the lab's ledger (no campaign file carries a reading of it: 未提供), the solver runs (settled CNY per arm from the sources: `lane-usage-ledger-settled-cny` for harness-arms, the production ledger for skill-arms, the reference price list for module-arms, plus Harness2's improvement-phase rollouts; a run with no settled cost is counted apart and makes the total a lower bound) and Claude (tokens by role and model: Harness2's `rsi1-ledger.jsonl`, with cache writes; the judge from the sources' `judged` section and claude_judge.py's `judge.log`, which keeps API-equivalent USD per call and no tokens; the improvement record's roles; a role that recorded no tokens is `unmetered`, never 0; costs the exporter lists in `<export>.uncertain-costs.json` make the lane total a lower bound, a judge's several parts directories are merged by packet, and a missing count, partial usage or `usd none` reads as unknown). `node round_spend.mjs --label <id> --out <dir>/round-spend.json --source <export>... [--h2-ledger <ledger> --h2-domain <family>] [--judge-parts <dir>] [--improvement improvement.json]` runs on the operator's box, names every input by sha256 and carries the digest of its own body; it travels to the lab beside `improvement.json`, and `harness_report.mjs` / `skill_report.mjs` print it as `## 费用` (未提供 without it, or when its digest is not its body's). `monthly_report.mjs` states the jury's CNY and that the month has no lane or Claude spend.
- **Sources** (`kind: "harness-arms"`, one per role, written by `evals/public-benchmarks/tools/export_harness_arms.py` from bench-driver/3 result folders and a gold file or the item files). A run's status must be a delivery outcome — `succeeded`, `failed` or `timeout`; a solver's non-delivery scores 0. A run whose usage record still showed an uncertain call or an open reservation when the driver read it is read again for a bounded time (`bench-driver.mjs --usage-settle-s`, default 90 s, recorded as `costSettle`); one that never closes keeps the ledger's settled sum as its cost, a lower bound, and is listed in `<out>.uncertain-costs.json` beside the source — only a run with no cost record at all has a null cost. An infrastructure failure (a refused dispatch, a driver error, an interrupted or cancelled run, a runtime that died, a missing result, a delivered run whose answer was not captured) is never a run: the exporter exits 3 and writes nothing (`--allow-incomplete` writes what exists, plus `<out>.incomplete.json`), and a source that lacks a run, or carries one with another status, is refused as `harness_arms_runs_incomplete` / `harness_arms_runs_infrastructure`. Development and confirmation must name the same image.
- **The scorer table** (`harness_scorers.mjs`): the four of `skill_scorers.mjs`, the same objects, and two more, selected by name with no default. `mbc-normalized` is MedBrowseComp's `score_shortanswer.py` rule for rule; a case only a judge could settle (`needs_judge`) counts as not correct, so it is a floor of the judged score, like `bixbench-deterministic`. `judged-verdicts` takes the run's score from digested verdict records (below). The personal-skill surface keeps its four; a harness registration naming anything else answers `campaign_scorer_not_qualified`.
- **The A/A control.** Every development source carries an `aa` arm: the unmodified image run a second time. It is registered (`control`), it can never be chosen, frozen or carried into confirmation (a confirmation with a control arm is refused), and a candidate is eligible only if its failure-slice gain exceeds the absolute failure-slice difference the control shows against the baseline (the noise floor, recorded in the development result next to the control's own figures) as well as the rule of the other campaigns (positive slice gain, overall not below the baseline by more than the margin). Ties go to the smaller always-on growth. No eligible candidate ends the campaign with `campaign_no_improving_candidate`, nothing opened. K, the registered number of candidate arms, bounds the development source. The development side of the runner's validation is one function (`harnessDevelopmentIssue`) that the preview (`harness_assemble.mjs --preview`), `--development-only` and the assembly apply as the runner does: a source with no control arm, rows or repeats other than the registered ones, more candidates than K, a control that was never registered or a judge budget below the registration's own need is refused by name before anything is opened (round 1's DeepResearch Bench preview said eligible and `run` then refused `campaign_control_arm_missing`).
- **The seal.** A source says its `role`; the development choice reads a development source only and the confirmation scoring a confirmation source only (`harness_confirmation_scored_before_opening`, `campaign_source_role_mismatch`). A confirmation export holds the answers and the costs and nothing that scores them: its rows carry no gold (`<stem>.gold.json` travels apart) and its verdict records are a separate file. `harness_assemble.mjs`, in the lab, joins them — gold only for exactly these rows, records only if the file's bytes are the digest the export recorded — and refuses a confirmation that arrives with gold or scores already in it (`harness_confirmation_not_sealed`). As exported, a confirmation source is not a source the consumer can score. The runner reserves the quarter's alpha and the corpus before the confirmation stage scores anything, as for the other surfaces; the ledger is the consumer's (`dsh-harness`, shared with tool-patch), and `alpha.perCampaign × campaignsPerQuarter` may not exceed the quarter's 0.05 (the registration says how many campaigns share it).
- **Judged families** (`judged-verdicts`, benchmarks `deepresearchbench`, `researchclawbench` and, since 2026-11-02, `bixbench`; rows are `{id, category}`, no gold). BixBench is here because most of its capsules hold only `llm_verifier` questions, which `bixbench-deterministic` refuses, so a code-only floor measures almost nothing: the exporter's `bixbench-judged` benchmark (source benchmark `bixbench`, category = the capsule's topic) takes the judge's per-question lines (`--dialect questions --question-ids`, judge/bixbench.md) and writes one score per run and pass, the share of the capsule's questions judged correct, computed in code; a capsule's answer is one judge call. What a judged consumer needs, as this README had listed it, and where each is:
  - *A judge identity that is Claude, fixed and recorded per run.* The registration names it (`judge: {id, provider, model, effort, form, passes, maxCalls, qualification}`; the same model at another CLI effort is another judge); anything but provider Anthropic with a pinned `claude-*` model is refused (`campaign_judge_refused`) before any verdict is read, by the assembly and by the runner, and the manifest records it in `models.judges`. What judged is bound, never typed: `claude_judge.py judge` takes `--effort` and `--judge-id` (required), records them with the model in the parts directory's `judge.json` and stamps every verdict line with `judge`, `model` and `effort`; `remap` refuses a part without `judge.json` or a line stamped otherwise and carries the stamps; the exporter takes the judge from `--registration`, requires every `--judge-record` (`judge.json`) and every verdict line to name the same id, model and effort, and writes that identity in the source's `judged.judge`, which the lab holds field by field (effort included) to the registration's judge. Every verdict record carries the judge id it was written under, and a record under another id refuses the source.
  - *A qualification with measured agreement and the noise band of repeated judging, on the verdict's own scale.* The registration carries `qualification: {measuredOn, form, scale, agreement, noiseSd, noiseBand95}`, copied by `register_round.py --judge-noise` from the `judge-noise/2` measurement `tools/noise_sample.py band` computes per family and per form from the judge's repeated verdict files (and, for the pairwise form, drb_pairwise order files): every figure on the 0-1 scale of the verdict the lab scores (`scale: verdict-0-1`: rubric points / 100, the RACE ratio, a capsule's share of correct questions, the pairwise candidate share (3 + s) / 6), the agreement sign agreement between repeated judgings with ties excluded (pairwise: the two orders of a swapped couple; absolute: the order of two units in two passes), so a judge that never states a preference has none. Nothing of it can be typed in, and a measurement of another form (the absolute RACE band for a pairwise judge), another scale or without an agreement is refused. The thresholds are fixed in code and a registration cannot set one: agreement ≥ 0.9 and `noiseBand95 / sqrt(passes)` ≤ the registration's margin. A judge below the agreement, above the band, measured in another form or scale, or with missing figures is `campaign_judge_unqualified`.
  - *Judge calls budgeted and settled.* `maxCalls` bounds the verdict lines, and `register_round.py` computes what the registration needs from its own rows, repeats, passes and candidates (development: baseline, the A/A control and up to K candidates; confirmation: the baseline and one candidate; pairwise judging records the arms that are not the baseline) and refuses a maximum below it (`judge.callsRequired` records the figure; the assembly refuses a registration whose maximum is below it, `campaign_judge_budget_below_registration`); the source records `calls` and a cost of exactly `{settledCny: 0, note}`: the judge is Claude under the operator's subscription, so its calls are not metered in CNY and the report says 0 yuan **with the note and the call count**, never silence. The arms' own costs (the lane stack's usage ledger, identity `lane-usage-ledger-settled-cny`) are what the cost ratio (at most 1.1, the procedure's own bound; any other ratio is `campaign_cost_ratio_unsupported`) is tested on.
  - *Verdict files digested into the sources, so a stage re-runs to the same bytes and the evaluator recomputes from the same records.* The export writes the records (`{row, arm, repeat, pass, score, judge}`, score in [0, 1]) as JSONL beside the source with their digest and the digests of the raw judge files it came from; the assembly checks the bytes against that digest (`campaign_judge_verdicts_changed`) and inlines the records, so the manifest's source digest covers every one of them — a record changed afterwards is another source (`campaign_source_changed`) and the evaluator, which recomputes the choice and the confirmation twice from the same records, signs nothing that differs. A verdict is required for every pass of every delivered run (`harness_judge_verdicts_incomplete`) and for no other run; a run that did not deliver scores 0.
  - *Repeated verdicts are averaged per run*, in a fixed order. `form: absolute` scores each report on its own; `form: pairwise` (DeepResearch Bench's A/B, passes in order-swapped couples, the swap handled by `drb_pairwise.py` before export) gives each non-baseline arm its preference share in [0, 1] against the baseline, whose score is 0.5 by definition. Dialects of the raw verdict lines the exporter reads: `unit`, `percent`, `race`, `pairwise`.
  The confirmation verdicts of a judged family necessarily exist before the lab opens the corpus (the harness lab has no judge; its one model is the frontier jury's). What the framework binds is that the policy was frozen on development alone and recomputed, that the records are the digested file, and that the aggregation into scores is done only by the lab's code: the exporter never prints a score.
- **Pre-registration** (`tools/register_round.py`): names the development and confirmation rows by ascending `sha256(salt|id)` from the registered pools minus the tasks already seen by earlier steps (`--ppc`), the rows that cannot be run or built (`--exclude-ids`: a `{reason, ids}` file recorded with its sha256 and reason, for example a capsule whose files are over the upload limit) and the rows earlier campaigns opened (`--exclude-opened`, repeatable: an earlier registration's confirmation rows leave both roles and its development rows the confirmation role, recorded with each file's sha256 and re-checked by `--check`), the repeats, the scorer, the failure slice, margin, cost ratio, alpha allocation, K, the A/A control and (judged) the judge, with a digest; it decides nothing about outcomes (`groupsBelowMinimum` is an observation). The assembly refuses a pre-registration whose digest is not its content's, and a source whose rows are not the registered ones (`campaign_rows_unregistered`).
- **Fewer than 25 groups: a directional campaign** (2026-11-02; the frontier campaign too since 2026-11-03, its groups being the measured confirmation days). The procedure (`statistics.py`, unchanged) refuses a population below 25 independent groups — in the family, or in the failure slice — so such a campaign cannot reach a qualified verdict, and the runner no longer spends the quarter's error budget on it. Decided from the registered counts alone (`directional.mjs`): the confirmation stage reserves the corpus with `alpha` 0 and `directional: true` and writes nothing to the quarter's ledger (`confirmationDecision`); the corpus is spent all the same (no other campaign may open it, and a campaign that opened it directionally cannot later claim an error budget for it); the evaluation is `inconclusive` whatever the arms scored (`campaign_directional_below_minimum_groups` first among the reasons, scope `directional-reference`, never `qualified`); the reports (`harness_report.mjs`, `skill_report.mjs`) call it 方向性参考 from the group counts alone and say that no α was drawn. A quarter's `campaignsPerQuarter` therefore counts only the campaigns that can qualify.
- **A refused receipt: the signed cost closure** (2026-11-02, `harness_closure.mjs`). A run's cost is part of the confirmation source, whose digest is the corpus digest the manifest names and the checkpoint's binding covers; a cost written into it afterwards is another corpus (another opened-corpus record, `campaign_checkpoint_input_changed`). The request's result digest covers the observations and not costs, so scores can be shown unchanged, but the evaluator does not repair the source. When the receipt is refused with `campaign_cost_measurement_missing` (runs whose usage record still showed an uncertain call or an open reservation when the driver read it; MedCalc round 1 had 2 of 822), `rsi-lab-harness.sh <month> <family> close` runs the closure in the image the campaign ran in: it checks the checkpoint is the campaign's (binding, frozen policy, chronology, look 1, no verdict yet), recomputes the development choice and the confirmation observations twice and finds them equal to the checkpoint's, takes the cost of exactly the runs that have none from the ledger's own record of each (`cost-closure.json`, made by `make_cost_closure.py` from the runs' `result.json`; the settled sum, a lower bound), runs the registered statistics over the same observations with those costs, and signs a report (`rsi-campaign-closure-v1`) that states the deviation, the runs and what the ledger said of them, and that the error-budget reservation is untouched (the closure reads no ledger and writes none). A lower bound is not a complete cost: the closure attests `complete_costs: false`, the procedure answers `inconclusive` ("complete cost identity not attested"), and that is the verdict recorded — a closure never turns a campaign whose costs are not known into a qualified one. `harness_report.mjs` puts the deviation in its own section and in the conclusion. It is not the receipt path.
- **The lab.** `deploy/rsi-campaign/rsi-lab-harness.sh <YYYY-MM> <family> develop|run|close`, resumable through `STATUS` like the skill script, same container flags and keys (no provider credential is mounted: the lab has no improver for this surface; the candidates' authors are Claude and arrive as `improvement.json`, a required inbox file for `develop` and `run`: a non-empty `improvers` list of pinned Claude models, else `campaign_improvement_missing`, and the manifest's `models.improvers` is that list). `develop` recomputes the development gate from development data and signs a report, opening nothing; `run` assembles, runs, takes the evaluator's receipt, resumes into the statistics and signs the report. The receipt path the evaluator profile (`evaluator-profile-harness.json`, written by `rsi-lab-install.sh lab`) names is shared, so the receipt and resume steps run under a lock with `state/harness-current` pointing at the family being signed.

## The module-policy campaign (2026-11-04)

The yardsticks of the platform's own modules, which lived in the online experiment engine until it was deleted. `module_campaign.mjs` is the harness consumer with another kit (`MODULE_KIT`; `harness_campaign.mjs` takes a kit last and never imports it): the A/A control, K, the sealed confirmation, the choice rule, the directional rule, the Claude improvers and judge, and the one development check the preview, `--development-only`, the assembly and the runner all apply (`harnessDevelopmentIssue`) are the harness's.

**A qualified round is a pull-request proposal, like a patch of the image, and nothing is imported.** It ends at the signed report (`rsi-module-policy-report-v1`), which says so and names the component and the text's sha256; a reviewed pull request changes the default in the code, and cites the report. There is no bundle and no publish, because production has no witnesses or fuses for these surfaces. `finalize.mjs` refuses a module manifest (`campaign_frozen_candidate_changed`) and `evaluator.mjs evidence` refuses the surface (`campaign_consumer_not_qualified`).

- **The candidate.** One component of one policy: `{screenInstructions}` or `{editInstructions}`, valid by the function serving uses (`validateModuleEvolutionPolicy`), and not the incumbent's text (`module_candidate_not_one_component`, `module_candidate_invalid`, `module_candidate_unchanged`). `candidates.json` is a Claude session's, `{improvers, candidates: [{title?, policy}]}`. Digest: canonical JSON of surface, component, text and the consumer's dependency digest (`computeRsiDependencyClosure`, profiles in `RSI_DEPENDENCY_PROFILES`), so the same text over another editor is another candidate. The baseline is the code's own text, `code-default:<16 hex>`. A source's `image.digest` holds the dependency digest and `arms[id].harness.policy` the text.
- **The arms** (`module_arms.mjs run`, the operator's box; the lab holds no DeepSeek key). Production's `FrontierEditor`, on `deepseek-flash`, the key from `--key-file` and in no output. A candidate reaches them as a served policy (`createModuleEvolutionPolicies`). An edit candidate older than the answer's keys is refused before any call (`module_candidate_not_current`), and a run the class did not serve the candidate to stops the command (`module_arms_candidate_not_served`: the editor stamped the shipped version). The lab checks the same from the source: one stamp per candidate arm, not null, not the incumbent's. Baseline, `aa` and the candidates run in development; baseline and the frozen candidate in confirmation. The arms of a case and repeat run in hashed order, because the provider's cache makes the second arm cheaper. The source is `kind: module-arms`: `answer.units` (one per variant of the case, the variants one group; a rewrite's `numbers.source` says how many numbers its source holds), tokens call by call, and the cost they come to at the reference price list, which the lab recomputes (`module_arms_cost_invalid`; cost identity `operator-box-reference-price-cny`). A response with no usage or a model with no price stops the command (exit 4, `module_arms_cost_unpriced`). A call the provider did not answer makes its run infrastructure, even if the class's retry recovered: exit 3, nothing written unless `--allow-incomplete` (the lab then refuses it as incomplete). The same command again runs only what is missing (`<out>.journal.jsonl`); a torn last line is dropped with a note and that run runs again, and a broken line elsewhere stops the command (`module_arms_journal_invalid`). A solver's non-delivery (`frontier_screen_invalid`, a cut-off answer) stays as a run with status `failed` and scores 0. A confirmation source travels without gold (`<stem>.gold.json`), joined by the assembly.
- **The scorers** (`module_scorers.mjs`, a closed table; a scorer measures only its surfaces).
  - `frontier-numbers`, a FLOOR: every number and unit of the rewrite is in its source (the editor's own check), and a rewrite of a source that has numbers states at least one of them, so dropping them all fails. The closed answer is right where the case expects one (lane; evidence type, flags and industry kind for those kinds of case; the screen's own lane too on `frontier-screen`), and verification is `passed` or `repaired`. A case with `abstentionExpected` is met by a rewrite with no summary and no unsupported number. The floor asks for one number, not for the main ones: keeping one of ten passes it.
  - `frontier-source`: the floor times the fidelity verdict through the judged records path (`module_arms.mjs packets`, `claude_judge.py judge --dialect questions`, `verdicts`). The judge is Claude, `form: absolute`, on the code's thresholds, never Qwen. Its brief asks whether every claim is supported and whether the source's main results were kept. Its qualification is copied from a `judge-noise/2` measurement, family `frontier-source`, by `module_arms.mjs register --judge judge.json --judge-noise judge-noise.json`, and nothing of it can be typed in: `claude_judge.py remap --keys dir/operator/keys.json --parts p --dialect questions --bench-key frontier-source --repeats N --out d/frontier-source`, then `noise_sample.py band --verdicts d --out judge-noise.json`.
- **The bar** (`moduleDeterministicChecks`). For a scorer with a code check: a candidate whose pass share is below the incumbent's by more than the noise band of the incumbent's own runs (at least three: baseline and `aa` by repeat, so development repeats at least 2) is not eligible, and at confirmation ends the campaign (`campaign_deterministic_check_regressed`). The band is the noise of the difference of means it is compared with: twice the standard error of the runs' pairwise differences over the groups, at least 0.02. The online engine's `evolutionDeterministicChecks` used twice the standard deviation there, which on 25 groups is 5 times too wide. A failure the incumbent has too is not held against the candidate.
- **What can qualify.** Only `frontier-edit` scored by `frontier-source` on a registered export draw of at least 25 groups (`moduleDirectional`, decided from the registration alone). Every other round is directional, with no α and its corpus spent, and its report says why: `campaign_directional_confirmation_not_sealed` (a confirmation from the repository's cases, whose gold the improver can read, at any count; manifest `exposure: exposed`), `campaign_directional_export_floor_only` (`frontier-numbers` on export rows: with no gold the floor and the bar are one test, and nothing pulls against writing less), `campaign_directional_below_minimum_groups`.
- **What binds an empty-gold confirmation, stated honestly.** Export rows have no gold, so anyone holding the file can score the confirmation source, and sealing it does not hide anything. What binds instead is the registered draw (the export's digest, the salt, the rule, the counts, all in the registration before any arm runs; `run` refuses another export, `campaign_rows_unregistered`) and the candidate frozen on development before the confirmation is opened.
- **Supply.** The screen has 5 lane cases, so its campaigns are directional. `frontier-edit` has 36 labelled development cases and a confirmation supply: `register --items-from-export <file> --sample <n> --salt <s>` draws public export items by ascending sha256(salt|id), one per event, never what development saw, source text only, one item one group (`draw` prints the counts). An export row has no expected lane, so the screen refuses it (`module_arms_export_not_supported`). On a fixture export, 80 items gave 67 eligible and `--sample 30` gave 30 groups; no real export has been drawn yet. `--exclude-from <round or source>` applies to pool rounds and draws alike: what an earlier round confirmed leaves both roles, what it developed on leaves confirmation.
- **The error budget.** `frontier-screen` and `frontier-edit` are one consumer, `frontier-editor` (one class, one policy document), so they share one quarter's α. The `autopilot-planner` surface, its `autopilot-checklist` scorer and its arm were deleted on 2026-11-09 with the scheduler they measured.
- **The receipt.** `evaluator.mjs receipt` is the one command for every surface and reads which it signs from the manifest; it recomputes the module's development choice and confirmation as it does the harness's.
- **The lab.** `rsi-lab-harness.sh <YYYY-MM> <family> develop|run` runs a module round with the same inbox as a harness family (`harness-<family>-<month>/`: `registration.json` from `module_arms.mjs register`, `development.json`, `confirmation.json`, `confirmation.gold.json`, `improvement.json` required, each with its `.sha256`; `frontier-source` adds both `*.verdicts.jsonl`). The script reads no surface: the assembly, the runner and the evaluator do, from the files. No provider credential, no model on the lab: the arms ran on the operator's box. `close` is not for a module round, whose every run has a priced cost or is refused.

## In production

A qualified bundle is imported with no approval step and runs in shadow: nothing changes for readers. After 200 executions over 2 days it serves every round; after the same exposure again it is promoted: its component becomes the module's own policy (`evolution-module-policy-frontier`, what `rsiIncumbent` reads), the journal ends as `promoted`, and expiry, witnesses and the campaign no longer apply. The surface is free, and the next month's baseline is that revision. A bundle's expiry is the campaign's deadline (the month's end) and only bounds reaching promotion. An activation that fails after its intent committed is finished by the next poll, with no new approval.

A qualified month publishes its bundle and the signed report names its digest. A refused evidence or finalize is outcome `finalize-refused`, exit 8, and the next start signs the evidence anew. A month measured against no baseline or another consumer is `not-importable`, exit 0, and nothing is signed. The installed records name only `frontier-selection`; a qualified module round is a pull-request proposal and never reaches the importer.

What stops a campaign before that, each rolling back to the incumbent: the volume bound (`registered-volume-bound`: past 50 picks, the candidate's selected volume within 0.5 to 1.1 times the incumbent's, read per pool day with the mix's picks taken back; the same bound the development choice and the confirmation apply, `RSI_FRONTIER_VOLUME_BOUND`, counted the same way, safety alerts on both sides), corrupt installed bytes, a trust or registry change, a dependency or baseline change, expiry.

`frontier-selection`'s consumer is `apps/server/src/frontierSelection.mjs` and `packages/domain/src/frontierMix.mjs` (6 files with what they import), bound to the model and `FRONTIER_SCORE_VERSION`. When that digest changes, run `rsi-lab-install.sh lab` from the serving commit, then `production`; until then a month is measured, reported as not importable and never finalized, and an active campaign on the old digest stops at its next read. A promoted policy stays; if `FRONTIER_SCORE_VERSION` moved, its threshold sits on another scale and keeping it is an operator's decision.

To read what is in force, inside the web container: `node scripts/ops/rsi-policy-stop.mjs --surface frontier-selection`. Two acts, each with `--reason <closed-code>`: `--apply` stops the live campaign and never touches a promoted policy; `--restore-promoted` puts back what the promotion replaced, only on the surface that promoted it and not under a live campaign. `status` is `applied` only when something changed; a stopped journal whose rollback cannot run is named in `notes` and blocks nothing.
