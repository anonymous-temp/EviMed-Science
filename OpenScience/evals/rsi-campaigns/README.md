# Offline campaigns (RSI lab)

Platform experiments run here, offline, on a dedicated lab host; production keeps only serving and a thin import/rollback layer (`apps/server/src/rsi*.mjs`, opt-in). The closed consumer registry admits four surfaces, `frontier-selection` — the frontier feed's selection threshold, replayed by production's own `frontierMixSelect` over whole days —, `capability-skill` (below), whose scoring has a closed registry of its own, `harness-patch` (further below: a patched runtime image, with more scorers, a judged one among them) and `tool-patch` (the same consumer for a candidate that holds code as well as text; its own error-budget ledger and consumer identity). Other surfaces answer `campaign_consumer_not_qualified`; a surface the domain package does not list at all is `campaign_surface_invalid`.

## The monthly frontier-selection campaign

Production pushes, on the 1st at 00:30 Asia/Shanghai, a read-only export of months M-2 and M-1 (`scripts/ops/rsi-frontier-export.mjs`, run inside the serving web container by `deploy/rsi-campaign/production/rsi-frontier-push.sh`) into the lab's write-only inbox. At 02:00 the lab runs `deploy/rsi-campaign/rsi-lab-monthly.sh`, every step in the lab image (`deploy/rsi-campaign/Dockerfile`: Python 3.12 + the pinned statistics, Node 22), resumable through its `STATUS` file:

1. **plan** (`frontier_assemble.mjs plan`): replay every registered threshold on every day from the state before that day's mix (the export takes back what the mix picked, keeping item-time safety picks), and fix each day's labelled set from the replays alone — rows at or above the lowest registered threshold, safety alerts, and every row any registered threshold selects.
2. **calibrate** and **label** (`frontier_labels.mjs`): a two-pass Qwen jury (a model family other than the DeepSeek screener; temperature 0, exemplar order reversed between passes; disagreement abstains) reads only the source's own text. Calibrated each month on the even half of the 2026-10-25 human anchor (`../frontier-selection/gold.jsonl`, exemplars from the odd half only); unqualified calibration stops the campaign (`campaign_labels_unqualified`). First calibration, 2026-10-26: pick-vs-other κ 0.875 (the two human labellers: 0.87), pick precision 0.94, recall 0.85, abstention 2.4%, ¥5.2 for 248 calls. Every call is reserved and settled in the lab's budget ledger (`RsiCampaignBudget`: ¥1,000 per campaign, ¥30,000 per Beijing month) and cached, so a rerun never pays twice.
3. **build** (`frontier_assemble.mjs build`): manifest and inputs. Development = month M-2, confirmation = month M-1 (sealed: new items no campaign has opened). Claim `superiority`; the sampling unit is the **day** (`frontierMixSelect` shares one day's size, group shares, language target and event picks across sources, so sources inside a day are not independent); endpoint = a day's weighted accuracy over its labelled set, a pick weighing 3; groups = confirmation days with ≥20 labelled rows (decided from labels, never outcomes). The supply report gives days, labelled rows, abstentions, replay fidelity against production's real picks, selector drift between lab and production, and the minimum detectable effect.
4. **run** (`run.mjs run`): development freezes the registered threshold with the highest mean day difference among those losing no safety pick and selecting at most 1.1× the baseline volume (ties: nearest the baseline; no positive difference ends the campaign). Before confirmation the runner reserves the quarter's error budget and the corpus in the confirmation ledger (a corpus is opened by one campaign only; a surface's confirmations draw at most α = 0.05 per quarter, 0.05/3 per monthly campaign), then measures the frozen policy and the baseline and stops at `awaiting-evaluation`.
5. **receipt** (`evaluator.mjs receipt`, own Ed25519 key, no network): recomputes the development choice and the confirmation (twice — a deterministic consumer gives the same bytes) and signs a receipt bound to the runner's request, including the integrity facts it attests. The runner accepts it only when those facts equal its own observations; a missing or unbound receipt is retried, never persisted.
6. **resume** (`run.mjs resume`): the registered statistics (`statistics.py`, procedure `rsi-paired-group-v5`: one-sided paired t at half the claim's alpha, ≥25 groups, influence and zero-variance checks; a candidate losing a safety pick is not qualified; `superiority` requires the zero-model-call cost identity).
7. **report**: `monthly_report.mjs` writes `report.json` and a Chinese `report.md`, signed by the evaluator, into `/srv/rsi-lab/outbox/<month>/`, whatever the verdict. A qualified campaign is also finalized (`evaluator.mjs evidence` + `finalize.mjs`, publisher and evaluator file keys, network none) into a signed `bundle.json`. In this phase nothing is imported into production.

`reports/statistics-v5-qualification.json` is the procedure's own qualification: two seeds of 20,000 simulations per scenario, including superiority nulls (normal and skewed) and a modest effect at 25 and 31 day groups and at α 0.05 and 0.05/3. It is mathematical evidence about the procedure, never about a real candidate.

## The capability-skill campaign and its scorer registry

A candidate is a personal skill mounted on the account its arm runs under; the arms ran on production (one lane, automated) and what reaches the lab is each run's final answer and settled cost (`skill-arms` sources, `skill_campaign.mjs`). What scores an answer is the registration's `scorer`, an id of the closed, frozen table in `skill_scorers.mjs` — the registry the file means by "Closed consumer registry" (`campaign_runner.mjs`): no executable path, no dynamic import, no function handed in by the caller; an id outside the table answers `campaign_scorer_not_qualified`, and a registration that names none is `medcalc`, what every campaign before the table was.

| scorer | source `benchmark` | row | the run's score in [0, 1] | failure slice (registration key → row field) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `medcalc` | `medcalc-bench` | `{id, calculatorId, gold: {answer, lower, upper}}` | MedCalc-Bench's `evaluate.py` over the `Final answer:` line: 0 or 1 | `failureCalculators` (integers) → `calculatorId` |
| `bixbench-deterministic` | `bixbench` | `{id, topic, gold: {questions: [{n, qid, eval_mode, ideal, range}]}}`, one row per capsule | the share of the capsule's `str_verifier` and `range_verifier` questions answered correctly under the benchmark's own graders; `llm_verifier` questions are not scored and a question a judge would have to settle counts as not correct, so this is a floor of the judged score | `failureSlice` (strings) → `topic` |
| `arb-deep` | `autoresearchbench-deep` | `{id, category, gold: {type: 'deep', titles, arxiv_ids, is_none}}` | 1 if the first candidate after `Final answer:` is the target paper (arXiv id, or title equal / truncated / 0.92 similar), or an explicit `None` for a `None` target; titles in the judge band are not matched | `failureSlice` → `category` |
| `arb-wide` | `autoresearchbench-wide` | `{id, category, gold: {type: 'wide', titles, arxiv_ids}}` | the IoU of the gold arXiv-id set and the answer's ids, a title-only line mapped to its gold id (`score_arb.py`'s `iou`) | `failureSlice` → `category` |

The BixBench port follows the benchmark's `graders.py` (Future-House/BixBench, read 2026-10-31): `str_verifier` is equal after keeping `[a-zA-Z0-9]` lower-cased, or the answer a substring of the ideal (the open-answer harness's `partial_match`, so a short answer such as `1` matches `166`); `range_verifier` is `lower <= value <= upper` on the first number of the answer. One deliberate deviation: an answer that cleans to nothing is never correct (the official `"" in target` is true). The answers are read as `score_bixbench.py` reads them (after the last `Final answers:` line, one `<n>.` line per question; ASCII digits only, where Python also reads another script's digits, so the port can miss a credit and never grants one). The AutoResearchBench ports are `score_arb.py` rule for rule, including a port of `difflib.SequenceMatcher`'s ratio. `tests/fixtures/scorer-vectors.json` holds synthetic inputs with the Python scorers' outputs; `tests/test_scorer_vectors.py` rebuilds it from them (so a change to `score_arb.py` fails there until the file is regenerated with `python3 -I tests/scorer_vectors_gen.py`) and `tests/skillScorers.test.mjs` asserts the JS ports reproduce it.

The scorer is part of the consumer identity: `skillCampaignConsumerIdentity(registration)` is the digest of `skill_campaign.mjs`, `skill_scorers.mjs` and the scorer id, pinned in the registration as `statistics.consumer_id` and attested in the evaluator's receipt. Three consequences. A registration made for one scorer cannot be evaluated under another. Any change to either file is a new identity, so a campaign is assembled and run by the same image: the lab runs its own image (`rsi-lab-install.sh lab` builds it from `git archive HEAD`), the 2026-11-01 development-only campaign is unaffected by this code, and the lab image must not be reinstalled between assembling a campaign and running it. And the medcalc numbers are unchanged by the registry — `tests/skillScorers.test.mjs` pins the development and confirmation digests, the observation and cost digests and the statistics of the pre-registry fixture — while its identity digest, being a hash of the code, is not the pre-registry one.

`skill_assemble.mjs` takes `--scorer <id>`, `--failure-slice a,b` (strata) and `--pilot-rationale` for a scorer other than `medcalc`; `--failure-calculators` and the lab's `rsi-lab-skill.sh` calls are unchanged.

**Judged surfaces are not in this registry** (open-ended answers, rubrics, research reports, BixBench `llm_verifier`): a personal-skill campaign scores with code or not at all. The `harness-patch` consumer has a registry of its own that adds a judged scorer, and builds what a judged consumer needs — see the next section.

## The harness-patch campaign (2026-11-01, RSI round 1)

A candidate is a patch of the runtime image: `{guidanceSupplement, skills: [{name, description, body}]}` applied to the image the baseline ran on, measured on benchmark runs that happened on a lane stack (the baseline on the unmodified image, each candidate on a delta image). The runs, the verdict records and the pre-registration reach the lab as files; nothing here builds an image, runs a lane or imports anything into production — a qualified result is a pull-request proposal, never an activation (the surface is registered `shadow`).

- **The candidate and its digest** (`harness_campaign.mjs`). `harnessPatchDigest` is the sha256 of the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the bundle (skills sorted by name, names `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,63}$`) **and the image identity it was measured on** (`{id, digest}`): the same patch on another image is another candidate. A candidate arm `cand-<12 hex of the digest>` carries its bundle, and the lab recomputes the digest from it; a digest that is not the bundle's, an arm id that is not the digest's, or an always-on growth (guidance supplement plus each skill's name and description) over 4,000 characters refuses the source. Sizes are UTF-16 code units. `export_harness_arms.py` computes the same digest in Python; `tests/fixtures/scorer-vectors.json` (`harnessDigest`) holds vectors both sides reproduce.
- **The tool-patch surface** (2026-11-02, `tool_patch_campaign.mjs`). A candidate that holds code (a tool, a connector) beside the text that tells the model about it is the same campaign under another surface key: the registration says `surface: "tool-patch"` (`register_round.py --surface tool-patch`; a harness-patch registration writes no such field, so its digest is what it was), the manifest's `surface` and `candidateKind` are `tool-patch`, and its arm adds `harness.code = {image (the patched image the arm ran on), patchSha256 (of the code diff), files: [{path, sha256}], toolCatalogueChars (what its tools add to the first request)}`, bound into the candidate digest (`harnessPatchDigest(bundle, image, code)`; Python: `export_harness_arms.py --surface tool-patch`, `make_harness_bundle.py --tool-patch --code-path ...`) and counted in the always-on growth, so the 4,000-character cap prices the tool catalogue too. A registration for one surface refuses the other's arms (`harness_arms_candidate_invalid`) and a manifest whose surface is not the registration's (`campaign_surface_registration_mismatch`). The runner keys the error-budget ledger by the manifest's surface, so tool campaigns draw on `tool-patch:<quarter>` and never on the text surface's quarter, and the consumer identity (`tool-patch-arms-v1`) digests this surface's own files; a harness-patch campaign's identity, and every harness-patch pin written before the surface existed, is untouched.
- **Sources** (`kind: "harness-arms"`, one per role, written by `evals/public-benchmarks/tools/export_harness_arms.py` from bench-driver/3 result folders and a gold file or the item files). A run's status must be a delivery outcome — `succeeded`, `failed` or `timeout`; a solver's non-delivery scores 0. A run whose usage record still showed an uncertain call or an open reservation when the driver read it is read again for a bounded time (`bench-driver.mjs --usage-settle-s`, default 90 s, recorded as `costSettle`); one that never closes keeps the ledger's settled sum as its cost, a lower bound, and is listed in `<out>.uncertain-costs.json` beside the source — only a run with no cost record at all has a null cost. An infrastructure failure (a refused dispatch, a driver error, an interrupted or cancelled run, a runtime that died, a missing result, a delivered run whose answer was not captured) is never a run: the exporter exits 3 and writes nothing (`--allow-incomplete` writes what exists, plus `<out>.incomplete.json`), and a source that lacks a run, or carries one with another status, is refused as `harness_arms_runs_incomplete` / `harness_arms_runs_infrastructure`. Development and confirmation must name the same image.
- **The scorer table** (`harness_scorers.mjs`): the four of `skill_scorers.mjs`, the same objects, and two more, selected by name with no default. `mbc-normalized` is MedBrowseComp's `score_shortanswer.py` rule for rule; a case only a judge could settle (`needs_judge`) counts as not correct, so it is a floor of the judged score, like `bixbench-deterministic`. `judged-verdicts` takes the run's score from digested verdict records (below). The personal-skill surface keeps its four; a harness registration naming anything else answers `campaign_scorer_not_qualified`.
- **The A/A control.** Every development source carries an `aa` arm: the unmodified image run a second time. It is registered (`control`), it can never be chosen, frozen or carried into confirmation (a confirmation with a control arm is refused), and a candidate is eligible only if its failure-slice gain exceeds the absolute failure-slice difference the control shows against the baseline (the noise floor, recorded in the development result next to the control's own figures) as well as the rule of the other campaigns (positive slice gain, overall not below the baseline by more than the margin). Ties go to the smaller always-on growth. No eligible candidate ends the campaign with `campaign_no_improving_candidate`, nothing opened. K, the registered number of candidate arms, bounds the development source. The development side of the runner's validation is one function (`harnessDevelopmentIssue`) that the preview (`harness_assemble.mjs --preview`), `--development-only` and the assembly apply as the runner does: a source with no control arm, rows or repeats other than the registered ones, more candidates than K, a control that was never registered or a judge budget below the registration's own need is refused by name before anything is opened (round 1's DeepResearch Bench preview said eligible and `run` then refused `campaign_control_arm_missing`).
- **The seal.** A source says its `role`; the development choice reads a development source only and the confirmation scoring a confirmation source only (`harness_confirmation_scored_before_opening`, `campaign_source_role_mismatch`). A confirmation export holds the answers and the costs and nothing that scores them: its rows carry no gold (`<stem>.gold.json` travels apart) and its verdict records are a separate file. `harness_assemble.mjs`, in the lab, joins them — gold only for exactly these rows, records only if the file's bytes are the digest the export recorded — and refuses a confirmation that arrives with gold or scores already in it (`harness_confirmation_not_sealed`). As exported, a confirmation source is not a source the consumer can score. The runner reserves the quarter's alpha and the corpus before the confirmation stage scores anything, as for the other surfaces; the ledger is the surface's own (`harness-patch:<quarter>`), and `alpha.perCampaign × campaignsPerQuarter` may not exceed the quarter's 0.05 (the registration says how many campaigns share it).
- **Judged families** (`judged-verdicts`, benchmarks `deepresearchbench` and `researchclawbench`; rows are `{id, category}`, no gold). What a judged consumer needs, as this README had listed it, and where each is:
  - *A judge identity that is not on the solver's family, fixed and recorded per run.* The registration names it (`judge: {id, provider, form, passes, maxCalls, qualification}`); an id or a provider containing `deepseek`, or the manifest solver's provider, is refused (`campaign_judge_family_refused`) before any verdict is read, by the assembly and by the runner. Every verdict record carries the judge id it was written under, and a record under another id refuses the source.
  - *A qualification with measured agreement and the noise band of repeated judging.* The registration carries `qualification: {agreement, noiseSd, noiseBand95, measuredOn, minimumAgreement, maximumNoiseBand}`; the figures come from the judge-noise measurement of `evals/public-benchmarks` (`tools/noise_sample.py`) and the swapped-order agreement of `tools/drb_pairwise.py`; the thresholds are the owner's and are set at registration. A judge whose measured agreement is below the minimum, or whose band is above the maximum, or whose figures are missing is `campaign_judge_unqualified`.
  - *Judge calls budgeted and settled.* `maxCalls` bounds the verdict lines, and `register_round.py` computes what the registration needs from its own rows, repeats, passes and candidates (development: baseline, the A/A control and up to K candidates; confirmation: the baseline and one candidate; pairwise judging records the arms that are not the baseline) and refuses a maximum below it (`judge.callsRequired` records the figure; the assembly refuses a registration whose maximum is below it, `campaign_judge_budget_below_registration`); the source records `calls` and a cost of exactly `{settledCny: 0, note}`: the judge is Claude under the operator's subscription, so its calls are not metered in CNY and the report says 0 yuan **with the note and the call count**, never silence. The arms' own costs (the lane stack's usage ledger, identity `lane-usage-ledger-settled-cny`) are what the cost ratio (at most 1.1, the procedure's own bound; any other ratio is `campaign_cost_ratio_unsupported`) is tested on.
  - *Verdict files digested into the sources, so a stage re-runs to the same bytes and the evaluator recomputes from the same records.* The export writes the records (`{row, arm, repeat, pass, score, judge}`, score in [0, 1]) as JSONL beside the source with their digest and the digests of the raw judge files it came from; the assembly checks the bytes against that digest (`campaign_judge_verdicts_changed`) and inlines the records, so the manifest's source digest covers every one of them — a record changed afterwards is another source (`campaign_source_changed`) and the evaluator, which recomputes the choice and the confirmation twice from the same records, signs nothing that differs. A verdict is required for every pass of every delivered run (`harness_judge_verdicts_incomplete`) and for no other run; a run that did not deliver scores 0.
  - *Repeated verdicts are averaged per run*, in a fixed order. `form: absolute` scores each report on its own; `form: pairwise` (DeepResearch Bench's A/B, passes in order-swapped couples, the swap handled by `drb_pairwise.py` before export) gives each non-baseline arm its preference share in [0, 1] against the baseline, whose score is 0.5 by definition. Dialects of the raw verdict lines the exporter reads: `unit`, `percent`, `race`, `pairwise`.
  The confirmation verdicts of a judged family necessarily exist before the lab opens the corpus (the lab has no judge). What the framework binds is that the policy was frozen on development alone and recomputed, that the records are the digested file, and that the aggregation into scores is done only by the lab's code: the exporter never prints a score.
- **Pre-registration** (`tools/register_round.py`): names the development and confirmation rows by ascending `sha256(salt|id)` from the registered pools minus the tasks already seen by earlier steps (`--ppc`) and the rows earlier campaigns opened (`--exclude-opened`, repeatable: an earlier registration's confirmation rows leave both roles and its development rows the confirmation role, recorded with each file's sha256 and re-checked by `--check`), the repeats, the scorer, the failure slice, margin, cost ratio, alpha allocation, K, the A/A control and (judged) the judge, with a digest; it decides nothing about outcomes (`groupsBelowMinimum` is an observation). The assembly refuses a pre-registration whose digest is not its content's, and a source whose rows are not the registered ones (`campaign_rows_unregistered`).
- **Fewer than 25 groups: a directional campaign** (2026-11-02). The procedure (`statistics.py`, unchanged) refuses a population below 25 independent groups — in the family, or in the failure slice — so such a campaign cannot reach a qualified verdict, and the runner no longer spends the quarter's error budget on it. Decided from the registered counts alone (`directional.mjs`): the confirmation stage reserves the corpus with `alpha` 0 and `directional: true` and writes nothing to the quarter's ledger (`confirmationDecision`); the corpus is spent all the same (no other campaign may open it, and a campaign that opened it directionally cannot later claim an error budget for it); the evaluation is `inconclusive` whatever the arms scored (`campaign_directional_below_minimum_groups` first among the reasons, scope `directional-reference`, never `qualified`); the reports (`harness_report.mjs`, `skill_report.mjs`) call it 方向性参考 from the group counts alone and say that no α was drawn. A quarter's `campaignsPerQuarter` therefore counts only the campaigns that can qualify.
- **A refused receipt: the signed cost closure** (2026-11-02, `harness_closure.mjs`). A run's cost is part of the confirmation source, whose digest is the corpus digest the manifest names and the checkpoint's binding covers; a cost written into it afterwards is another corpus (another opened-corpus record, `campaign_checkpoint_input_changed`). The request's result digest covers the observations and not costs, so scores can be shown unchanged, but the evaluator does not repair the source. When the receipt is refused with `campaign_cost_measurement_missing` (runs whose usage record still showed an uncertain call or an open reservation when the driver read it; MedCalc round 1 had 2 of 822), `rsi-lab-harness.sh <month> <family> close` runs the closure in the image the campaign ran in: it checks the checkpoint is the campaign's (binding, frozen policy, chronology, look 1, no verdict yet), recomputes the development choice and the confirmation observations twice and finds them equal to the checkpoint's, takes the cost of exactly the runs that have none from the ledger's own record of each (`cost-closure.json`, made by `make_cost_closure.py` from the runs' `result.json`; the settled sum, a lower bound), runs the registered statistics over the same observations with those costs, and signs a report (`rsi-campaign-closure-v1`) that states the deviation, the runs and what the ledger said of them, and that the error-budget reservation is untouched (the closure reads no ledger and writes none). `harness_report.mjs` puts the deviation in its own section and in the conclusion. It is not the receipt path and attests no complete cost.
- **The lab.** `deploy/rsi-campaign/rsi-lab-harness.sh <YYYY-MM> <family> develop|run|close`, resumable through `STATUS` like the skill script, same container flags and keys (no provider key: the lab has no improver for this surface). `develop` recomputes the development gate from development data and signs a report, opening nothing; `run` assembles, runs, takes the evaluator's receipt, resumes into the statistics and signs the report. The receipt path the evaluator profile (`evaluator-profile-harness.json`, written by `rsi-lab-install.sh lab`) names is shared, so the receipt and resume steps run under a lock with `state/harness-current` pointing at the family being signed.

```sh
node evals/rsi-campaigns/harness_assemble.mjs --preview --development dev.json --registration registration.json      # the dev box: the development choice, nothing written

node evals/rsi-campaigns/harness_report.mjs dir 2026-11

node evals/rsi-campaigns/run.mjs run|resume|report --manifest dir/manifest.json --inputs dir/inputs.json --config /run/rsi-lab/config.json --out-dir dir/run
node evals/rsi-campaigns/evaluator.mjs receipt|evidence|report|qualification ...
pnpm test:rsi
```

A rehearsal names explicit windows and a prefix: `rsi-lab-monthly.sh 2026-10 --development-days 2026-10-01..2026-10-12 --confirmation-days 2026-10-13..2026-10-25 --id-prefix rehearsal-` (a rehearsal is never finalized).

## Lab host

`deploy/rsi-campaign/rsi-lab-install.sh lab` builds the image on the lab host from `git archive HEAD` and installs the launcher, the owner inputs under `/etc/rsi-lab/owner-inputs` (mounted read-only at `/run/rsi-lab`) and the timer; `rsi-lab-install.sh production` installs the export, the push script and its timer on the serving host. The lab database is its own PostgreSQL container on its own network (`evimed_rsi_lab`, role `rsi_lab`); keys are generated on the lab host and never leave it; the DashScope key is the only provider credential and only the jury steps mount it. The lab configuration's `transport: {kind: "none"}` is a lab without the OSS channels: public and synthetic campaigns run, an authorized-incident campaign does not. The OSS transport and Secrets Manager signer remain in the code for when those channels are provisioned.

Local verification: `python3 -m pip install --target ../.evimed-local/toolchains/rsi-python -r evals/rsi-campaigns/requirements.txt`, then `pnpm test:rsi`, and the server's `rsi*.test.mjs` serially with a disposable localhost `OPEN_SCIENCE_TEST_POSTGRES_URL`.

Not yet: paid DSH consumers and skill/tool/harness activation, the production import of a bundle (the thin layer is opt-in and not deployed), authorized-incident campaigns (they need the OSS channels), and a GEPA arm. The Harness2 arm is `evals/harness-adaptation` (round 1, 2026-11-01): it produces hypotheses for candidates, and the harness-patch consumer measures them.
