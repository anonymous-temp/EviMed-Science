# ASPREE trial

## Design and results

- ASPREE enrolled community-dwelling men and women in Australia and the United States aged 70 years or older (or ≥65 years among blacks and Hispanics in the United States) without cardiovascular disease, dementia or disability, randomly assigned to 100 mg enteric-coated aspirin or placebo. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"From 2010 through 2014, we enrolled community-dwelling men and women in Australia and the United States who were 70 years of age or older (or ≥65 years of age among blacks and Hispanics in the United States) and did not have cardiovascular disease, dementia, or disability. Participants were randomly assigned to receive 100 mg of enteric-coated aspirin or placebo."} -->
- Of the 19,114 persons enrolled, 9525 were assigned to aspirin and 9589 to placebo, with a median follow-up of 4.7 years. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"Of the 19,114 persons who were enrolled in the trial, 9525 were assigned to receive aspirin and 9589 to receive placebo. After a median of 4.7 years of follow-up"} -->
- The use of low-dose aspirin as a primary prevention strategy in older adults resulted in a significantly higher risk of major hemorrhage and did not result in a significantly lower risk of cardiovascular disease than placebo. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"The use of low-dose aspirin as a primary prevention strategy in older adults resulted in a significantly higher risk of major hemorrhage and did not result in a significantly lower risk of cardiovascular disease than placebo."} -->
- In ASPREE, the risk of death from any cause was 12.7 events per 1000 person-years with aspirin and 11.1 with placebo (HR 1.14; 95% CI 1.01-1.29), with cancer the major contributor to the higher mortality in the aspirin group. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"The risk of death from any cause was 12.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.1 events per 1000 person-years in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.14; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.01 to 1.29). Cancer was the major contributor to the higher mortality in the aspirin group, accounting for 1.6 excess deaths per 1000 person-years."} -->
- Cancer-related death occurred in 3.1% of participants in the aspirin group and 2.3% of those in the placebo group (HR 1.31; 95% CI 1.10-1.56). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"Cancer-related death occurred in 3.1% of the participants in the aspirin group and in 2.3% of those in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.10 to 1.56)."} -->
- Higher all-cause mortality was observed among apparently healthy older adults who received daily aspirin than among those who received placebo and was attributed primarily to cancer-related death, a result described as unexpected and to be interpreted with caution. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"Higher all-cause mortality was observed among apparently healthy older adults who received daily aspirin than among those who received placebo and was attributed primarily to cancer-related death. In the context of previous studies, this result was unexpected and should be interpreted with caution."} -->
