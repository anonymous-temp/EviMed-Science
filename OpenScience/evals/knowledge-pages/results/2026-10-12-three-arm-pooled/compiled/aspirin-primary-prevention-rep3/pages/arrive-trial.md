# ARRIVE trial (aspirin in moderate cardiovascular risk)

## Design and population

- ARRIVE was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre study done in seven countries, in which eligible patients were men aged 55 years and older or women aged 60 years and older with an average cardiovascular risk deemed moderate on the basis of the number of specific risk factors; patients at high risk of gastrointestinal or other bleeding, or with diabetes, were excluded. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"ARRIVE is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre study done in seven countries. Eligible patients were aged 55 years (men) or 60 years (women) and older and had an average cardiovascular risk, deemed to be moderate on the basis of the number of specific risk factors. We excluded patients at high risk of gastrointestinal bleeding or other bleeding, or diabetes."} -->
- Patients in ARRIVE were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive enteric-coated aspirin tablets (100 mg) or placebo tablets once daily, with patients, investigators and others involved in treatment or data analysis masked to treatment allocation. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) with a computer-generated randomisation code to receive enteric-coated aspirin tablets (100 mg) or placebo tablets, once daily. Patients, investigators, and others involved in treatment or data analysis were masked to treatment allocation."} -->
- The primary efficacy endpoint of ARRIVE was a composite outcome of time to first occurrence of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack, and safety endpoints were haemorrhagic events and incidence of other adverse events analysed in the intention-to-treat population. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The primary efficacy endpoint was a composite outcome of time to first occurrence of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack. Safety endpoints were haemorrhagic events and incidence of other adverse events, and were analysed in the intention-to-treat population."} -->
- Between July 5, 2007, and Nov 15, 2016, 12,546 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive aspirin (n=6270) or placebo (n=6276) at 501 study sites, with a median follow-up of 60 months. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Between July 5, 2007, and Nov 15, 2016, 12 546 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive aspirin (n=6270) or placebo (n=6276) at 501 study sites. Median follow-up was 60 months."} -->

## Results

- In ARRIVE, the overall incidence of serious adverse events was similar in both treatment groups (1266 [20.19%] with aspirin vs 1311 [20.89%] with placebo), as was the overall incidence of adverse events (5142 [82.01%] vs 5129 [81.72%]). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The overall incidence rate of serious adverse events was similar in both treatment groups (n=1266 [20·19%] in the aspirin group vs n=1311 [20·89%] in the placebo group. The overall incidence of adverse events was similar in both treatment groups (n=5142 [82·01%] vs n=5129 [81·72%] in the placebo group)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, the overall incidence of treatment-related adverse events was low but differed between groups (1050 [16.75%] with aspirin vs 850 [13.54%] with placebo; p<0.0001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The overall incidence of treatment-related adverse events was low (n=1050 [16·75%] vs n=850 [13·54%] in the placebo group; p\u003c0·0001)."} -->
