# Aspirin for primary prevention of cardiovascular events

## Benefit on vascular events

- In six primary prevention trials of about 95,000 individuals at low average risk, aspirin allocation produced a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% aspirin vs 0.57% control per year, p=0.0001), driven mainly by a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In the primary prevention trials, aspirin allocation yielded a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% aspirin vs 0.57% control per year, p=0.0001), due mainly to a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction (0.18%vs 0.23% per year, p\u003c0.0001)."} -->
- In a meta-analysis of 13 trials randomizing 164,225 participants without known cardiovascular disease, aspirin use was associated with a significant reduction in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 vs 61.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 0.89, 95% credible interval 0.84-0.95; absolute risk reduction 0.38%, number needed to treat 265). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with significant reductions in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 61.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (hazard ratio [HR], 0.89 [95% credible interval, 0.84-0.95]; absolute risk reduction, 0.38% [95% CI, 0.20%-0.55%]; number needed to treat, 265)."} -->
- In the ARRIVE trial of 12,546 patients at moderate estimated risk, the primary composite endpoint occurred in 269 (4.29%) patients on aspirin versus 281 (4.48%) on placebo, a non-significant difference (HR 0.96; 95% CI 0.81-1.13; p=0.6038). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"In the intention-to-treat analysis, the primary endpoint occurred in 269 (4·29%) patients in the aspirin group versus 281 (4·48%) patients in the placebo group (hazard ratio [HR] 0·96; 95% CI 0·81-1·13; p=0·6038)."} -->
- In the Japanese Primary Prevention Project of 14,464 patients aged 60 to 85 years with atherosclerotic risk factors, the 5-year cumulative primary outcome event rate did not differ significantly between aspirin and no aspirin (2.77% vs 2.96%; HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.77-1.15; P=.54). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8c085ce86e8550efe8d1ab710dba800f","quote":"The 5-year cumulative primary outcome event rate was not significantly different between the groups (2.77% [95% CI, 2.40%-3.20%] for aspirin vs 2.96% [95% CI, 2.58%-3.40%] for no aspirin; hazard ratio [HR], 0.94 [95% CI, 0.77-1.15]; P = .54)."} -->
- In ASPREE, among 19,114 older adults without cardiovascular disease, the rate of cardiovascular disease was 10.7 events per 1000 person-years with aspirin and 11.3 with placebo, a non-significant difference (HR 0.95; 95% CI 0.83-1.08). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"After a median of 4.7 years of follow-up, the rate of cardiovascular disease was 10.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.3 events per 1000 person-years in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.95; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.83 to 1.08)."} -->
- In the ASCEND trial of 15,480 adults with diabetes and no evident cardiovascular disease, serious vascular events occurred in a significantly lower percentage of the aspirin group than the placebo group (658 participants [8.5%] vs 743 [9.6%]; rate ratio 0.88; 95% CI 0.79-0.97; P=0.01). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"During a mean follow-up of 7.4 years, serious vascular events occurred in a significantly lower percentage of participants in the aspirin group than in the placebo group (658 participants [8.5%] vs. 743 [9.6%]; rate ratio, 0.88; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.79 to 0.97; P=0.01)."} -->
- Across the primary prevention trials, aspirin's effect on stroke was not significant (0.20% vs 0.21% per year, p=0.4) and vascular mortality did not differ significantly (0.19% vs 0.19% per year, p=0.7). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"The net effect on stroke was not significant (0.20%vs 0.21% per year, p=0.4: haemorrhagic stroke 0.04%vs 0.03%, p=0.05; other stroke 0.16%vs 0.18% per year, p=0.08). Vascular mortality did not differ significantly (0.19%vs 0.19% per year, p=0.7)."} -->

## Bleeding harm

- In the primary prevention trials, aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10% vs 0.07% per year, p<0.0001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"Aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10%vs 0.07% per year, p\u003c0.0001), and the main risk factors for coronary disease were also risk factors for bleeding."} -->
- In the meta-analysis of 13 trials, aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding compared with no aspirin (23.1 vs 16.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 1.43, 95% credible interval 1.30-1.56; absolute risk increase 0.47%; number needed to harm 210). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding events compared with no aspirin (23.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 16.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (HR, 1.43 [95% credible interval, 1.30-1.56]; absolute risk increase, 0.47% [95% CI, 0.34%-0.62%]; number needed to harm, 210)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0.97%) patients on aspirin versus 29 (0.46%) on placebo (HR 2.11; 95% CI 1.36-3.28; p=0.0007). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0·97%) patients in the aspirin group versus 29 (0·46%) in the placebo group (HR 2·11; 95% CI 1·36-3·28; p=0·0007)."} -->
- In ASCEND, major bleeding events occurred in 314 participants (4.1%) on aspirin versus 245 (3.2%) on placebo (rate ratio 1.29; 95% CI 1.09-1.52; P=0.003), with most of the excess being gastrointestinal and other extracranial bleeding. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"In contrast, major bleeding events occurred in 314 participants (4.1%) in the aspirin group, as compared with 245 (3.2%) in the placebo group (rate ratio, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.09 to 1.52; P=0.003), with most of the excess being gastrointestinal bleeding and other extracranial bleeding."} -->
- In ASPREE, the rate of major hemorrhage was 8.6 events per 1000 person-years with aspirin and 6.2 with placebo (HR 1.38; 95% CI 1.18-1.62; P<0.001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"The rate of major hemorrhage was 8.6 events per 1000 person-years and 6.2 events per 1000 person-years, respectively (hazard ratio, 1.38; 95% CI, 1.18 to 1.62; P\u003c0.001)."} -->

## Net value and guideline positions

- In primary prevention without previous disease, aspirin is of uncertain net value because the reduction in occlusive events must be weighed against any increase in major bleeds. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In primary prevention without previous disease, aspirin is of uncertain net value as the reduction in occlusive events needs to be weighed against any increase in major bleeds."} -->
- In ASCEND, aspirin prevented serious vascular events in persons with diabetes and no evident cardiovascular disease but also caused major bleeding events, and the absolute benefits were largely counterbalanced by the bleeding hazard. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"Aspirin use prevented serious vascular events in persons who had diabetes and no evident cardiovascular disease at trial entry, but it also caused major bleeding events. The absolute benefits were largely counterbalanced by the bleeding hazard."} -->
- The USPSTF concludes with moderate certainty that aspirin use for primary prevention in adults aged 40 to 59 years with a 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk has a small net benefit, and that initiating aspirin in adults 60 years or older has no net benefit. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_4634e2a7a07fd8375f49298d2fb101f7","quote":"The USPSTF concludes with moderate certainty that aspirin use for the primary prevention of CVD events in adults aged 40 to 59 years who have a 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk has a small net benefit. The USPSTF concludes with moderate certainty that initiating aspirin use for the primary prevention of CVD events in adults 60 years or older has no net benefit."} -->
- The USPSTF recommends that the decision to initiate low-dose aspirin for primary prevention in adults aged 40 to 59 years with a 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk be individual, and recommends against initiating low-dose aspirin in adults 60 years or older. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_4634e2a7a07fd8375f49298d2fb101f7","quote":"The decision to initiate low-dose aspirin use for the primary prevention of CVD in adults aged 40 to 59 years who have a 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk should be an individual one. Evidence indicates that the net benefit of aspirin use in this group is small. Persons who are not at increased risk for bleeding and are willing to take low-dose aspirin daily are more likely to benefit. (C recommendation) The USPSTF recommends against initiating low-dose aspirin use for the primary prevention of CVD in adults 60 years or older. (D recommendation)."} -->
