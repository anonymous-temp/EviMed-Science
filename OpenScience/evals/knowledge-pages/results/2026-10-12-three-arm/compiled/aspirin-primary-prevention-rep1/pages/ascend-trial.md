# ASCEND trial

## Design and results

- ASCEND randomly assigned adults with diabetes but no evident cardiovascular disease to aspirin 100 mg daily or matching placebo, with a primary efficacy outcome of first serious vascular event and a primary safety outcome of first major bleeding event. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"We randomly assigned adults who had diabetes but no evident cardiovascular disease to receive aspirin at a dose of 100 mg daily or matching placebo. The primary efficacy outcome was the first serious vascular event (i.e., myocardial infarction, stroke or transient ischemic attack, or death from any vascular cause, excluding any confirmed intracranial hemorrhage). The primary safety outcome was the first major bleeding event (i.e., intracranial hemorrhage, sight-threatening bleeding event in the eye, gastrointestinal bleeding, or other serious bleeding)."} -->
- A total of 15,480 participants underwent randomization, with a mean follow-up of 7.4 years. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"A total of 15,480 participants underwent randomization. During a mean follow-up of 7.4 years"} -->
- There was no significant difference between the aspirin and placebo groups in the incidence of gastrointestinal tract cancer (157 [2.0%] vs 158 [2.0%]) or all cancers (897 [11.6%] vs 887 [11.5%]). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"There was no significant difference between the aspirin group and the placebo group in the incidence of gastrointestinal tract cancer (157 participants [2.0%] and 158 [2.0%], respectively) or all cancers (897 [11.6%] and 887 [11.5%]); long-term follow-up for these outcomes is planned."} -->
