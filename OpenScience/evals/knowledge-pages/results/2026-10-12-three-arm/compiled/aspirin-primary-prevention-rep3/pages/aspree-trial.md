# ASPREE trial (aspirin in the healthy elderly)

## Design and population

- From 2010 through 2014, ASPREE enrolled community-dwelling men and women in Australia and the United States who were 70 years of age or older (or 65 years of age or older among blacks and Hispanics in the United States) and did not have cardiovascular disease, dementia, or disability, randomly assigned to 100 mg of enteric-coated aspirin or placebo. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"From 2010 through 2014, we enrolled community-dwelling men and women in Australia and the United States who were 70 years of age or older (or ≥65 years of age among blacks and Hispanics in the United States) and did not have cardiovascular disease, dementia, or disability. Participants were randomly assigned to receive 100 mg of enteric-coated aspirin or placebo."} -->
- The primary end point of ASPREE was a composite of death, dementia, or persistent physical disability, and secondary end points included major hemorrhage and cardiovascular disease defined as fatal coronary heart disease, nonfatal myocardial infarction, fatal or nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for heart failure. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"The primary end point was a composite of death, dementia, or persistent physical disability; results for this end point are reported in another article in the Journal. Secondary end points included major hemorrhage and cardiovascular disease (defined as fatal coronary heart disease, nonfatal myocardial infarction, fatal or nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for heart failure)."} -->
- Of the 19,114 persons enrolled in ASPREE, 9525 were assigned to receive aspirin and 9589 to receive placebo, with a median of 4.7 years of follow-up. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"Of the 19,114 persons who were enrolled in the trial, 9525 were assigned to receive aspirin and 9589 to receive placebo. After a median of 4.7 years of follow-up"} -->

## Mortality findings

- In ASPREE, a total of 1052 deaths occurred during a median of 4.7 years of follow-up, and deaths were classified according to the underlying cause by adjudicators unaware of trial-group assignments. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"A total of 1052 deaths occurred during a median of 4.7 years of follow-up. The risk of death from any cause was 12.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.1 events per 1000 person-years in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.14; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.01 to 1.29)."} -->
- ASPREE concluded that higher all-cause mortality was observed among apparently healthy older adults who received daily aspirin than among those who received placebo and was attributed primarily to cancer-related death, a result described as unexpected in the context of previous studies and to be interpreted with caution. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"Higher all-cause mortality was observed among apparently healthy older adults who received daily aspirin than among those who received placebo and was attributed primarily to cancer-related death.
