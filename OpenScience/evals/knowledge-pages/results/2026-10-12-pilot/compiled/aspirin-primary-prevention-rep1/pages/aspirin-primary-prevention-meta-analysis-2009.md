# 2009 collaborative meta-analysis of individual participant data on aspirin in vascular disease

## Scope and methods

- The 2009 collaborative meta-analysis compared long-term aspirin versus control in six primary prevention trials (95,000 individuals at low average risk, 660,000 person-years, 3554 serious vascular events) and 16 secondary prevention trials (17,000 individuals at high average risk, 43,000 person-years, 3306 serious vascular events). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"We undertook meta-analyses of serious vascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, or vascular death) and major bleeds in six primary prevention trials (95,000 individuals at low average risk, 660,000 person-years, 3554 serious vascular events) and 16 secondary prevention trials (17,000 individuals at high average risk, 43,000 person-years, 3306 serious vascular events) that compared long-term aspirin versus control."} -->

## Results

- In the primary prevention trials of the 2009 meta-analysis, aspirin allocation yielded a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% vs 0.57% per year, p=0.0001), due mainly to a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In the primary prevention trials, aspirin allocation yielded a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% aspirin vs 0.57% control per year, p=0.0001), due mainly to a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction (0.18%vs 0.23% per year, p\u003c0.0001)."} -->
- In the primary prevention trials of the 2009 meta-analysis, aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10% vs 0.07% per year, p<0.0001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"Aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10%vs 0.07% per year, p\u003c0.0001), and the main risk factors for coronary disease were also risk factors for bleeding."} -->
- In the secondary prevention trials of the 2009 meta-analysis, aspirin allocation yielded a greater absolute reduction in serious vascular events (6.7% vs 8.2% per year, p<0.0001), with reductions of about a fifth in total stroke and in coronary events. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In the secondary prevention trials, aspirin allocation yielded a greater absolute reduction in serious vascular events (6.7%vs 8.2% per year, p\u003c0.0001), with a non-significant increase in haemorrhagic stroke but reductions of about a fifth in total stroke (2.08%vs 2.54% per year, p=0.002) and in coronary events (4.3%vs 5.3% per year, p\u003c0.0001)."} -->
- In both primary and secondary prevention trials of the 2009 meta-analysis, the proportional reductions in the aggregate of all serious vascular events seemed similar for men and women. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In both primary and secondary prevention trials, the proportional reductions in the aggregate of all serious vascular events seemed similar for men and women."} -->
