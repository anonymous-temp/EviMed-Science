# 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis of aspirin for primary prevention

## Scope and methods

- The 2019 systematic review included randomized clinical trials enrolling at least 1000 participants with no known cardiovascular disease and a follow-up of at least 12 months, comparing aspirin use with no aspirin (placebo or no treatment). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Randomized clinical trials enrolling at least 1000 participants with no known cardiovascular disease and a follow-up of at least 12 months were included. - The review included 13 trials randomizing 164,225 participants with 1,050,511 participant-years of follow-up; the median age of trial participants was 62 years, 47% were men, 19% had diabetes, and the median baseline risk of the primary cardiovascular outcome was 9.2%. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"A total of 13 trials randomizing 164 225 participants with 1 050 511 participant-years of follow-up were included. - The primary cardiovascular outcome of the review was a composite of cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke, and the primary bleeding outcome was any major bleeding as defined by the individual studies. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"The primary cardiovascular outcome was a composite of cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke. ## Results

- In the 2019 meta-analysis, aspirin use was associated with a significant reduction in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 vs 61.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 0.89, 95% credible interval 0.84-0.95; absolute risk reduction 0.38%; number needed to treat 265). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with significant reductions in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 61.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (hazard ratio [HR], 0.89 [95% credible interval, 0.84-0.95]; absolute risk reduction, 0.38% [95% CI, 0.20%-0.55%]; number needed to treat, 265)."} -->
- In the 2019 meta-analysis, aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding events compared with no aspirin (23.1 vs 16.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 1.43, 95% credible interval 1.30-1.56; absolute risk increase 0.47%; number needed to harm 210). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding events compared with no aspirin (23.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 16.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (HR, 1.43 [95% credible interval, 1.30-1.56]; absolute risk increase, 0.47% [95% CI, 0.34%-0.62%]; number needed to harm, 210)."} -->
