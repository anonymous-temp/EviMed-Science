# ARRIVE trial

## Design and population

- ARRIVE is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre study done in seven countries. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"ARRIVE is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre study done in seven countries."} -->
- Eligible patients were men aged 55 years and older or women aged 60 years and older with an average cardiovascular risk deemed moderate on the basis of the number of specific risk factors; patients at high risk of gastrointestinal bleeding or other bleeding, or with diabetes, were excluded. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Eligible patients were aged 55 years (men) or 60 years (women) and older and had an average cardiovascular risk, deemed to be moderate on the basis of the number of specific risk factors. We excluded patients at high risk of gastrointestinal bleeding or other bleeding, or diabetes."} -->
- The primary efficacy endpoint of ARRIVE was a composite outcome of time to first occurrence of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The primary efficacy endpoint was a composite outcome of time to first occurrence of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack."} -->
- Between July 5, 2007, and Nov 15, 2016, 12,546 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive aspirin (n=6270) or placebo (n=6276) at 501 study sites, with a median follow-up of 60 months. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Between July 5, 2007, and Nov 15, 2016, 12 546 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive aspirin (n=6270) or placebo (n=6276) at 501 study sites. Median follow-up was 60 months."} -->

## Results

- In ARRIVE, the primary endpoint occurred in 269 (4.29%) patients in the aspirin group versus 281 (4.48%) in the placebo group (HR 0.96; 95% CI 0.81-1.13; p=0.6038). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"In the intention-to-treat analysis, the primary endpoint occurred in 269 (4·29%) patients in the aspirin group versus 281 (4·48%) patients in the placebo group (hazard ratio [HR] 0·96; 95% CI 0·81-1·13; p=0·6038)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0.97%) patients in the aspirin group versus 29 (0.46%) in the placebo group (HR 2.11; 95% CI 1.36-3.28; p=0.0007). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0·97%) patients in the aspirin group versus 29 (0·46%) in the placebo group (HR 2·11; 95% CI 1·36-3·28; p=0·0007)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, the overall incidence rate of serious adverse events was similar in both treatment groups (1266 [20.19%] with aspirin vs 1311 [20.89%] with placebo). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The overall incidence rate of serious adverse events was similar in both treatment groups (n=1266 [20·19%] in the aspirin group vs n=1311 [20·89%] in the placebo group."} -->
- In ARRIVE, the overall incidence of treatment-related adverse events was low (1050 [16.75%] with aspirin vs 850 [13.54%] with placebo; p<0.0001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"The overall incidence of treatment-related adverse events was low (n=1050 [16·75%] vs n=850 [13·54%] in the placebo group; p\u003c0·0001)."} -->
