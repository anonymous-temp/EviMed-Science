# Aspirin for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease

## Effect on cardiovascular events

- In a meta-analysis of six primary prevention trials in about 95,000 individuals at low average risk, aspirin allocation produced a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% aspirin vs 0.57% control per year, p=0.0001), due mainly to a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In the primary prevention trials, aspirin allocation yielded a 12% proportional reduction in serious vascular events (0.51% aspirin vs 0.57% control per year, p=0.0001), due mainly to a reduction of about a fifth in non-fatal myocardial infarction (0.18%vs 0.23% per year, p\u003c0.0001)."} -->
- In the same meta-analysis, the net effect of aspirin on stroke was not significant and vascular mortality did not differ significantly. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"The net effect on stroke was not significant (0.20%vs 0.21% per year, p=0.4: haemorrhagic stroke 0.04%vs 0.03%, p=0.05; other stroke 0.16%vs 0.18% per year, p=0.08). Vascular mortality did not differ significantly (0.19%vs 0.19% per year, p=0.7)."} -->
- In a meta-analysis of 13 trials randomizing 164,225 participants without known cardiovascular disease, aspirin use was associated with a significant reduction in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 vs 61.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 0.89, 95% credible interval 0.84-0.95; absolute risk reduction 0.38%). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with significant reductions in the composite cardiovascular outcome compared with no aspirin (57.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 61.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (hazard ratio [HR], 0.89 [95% credible interval, 0.84-0.95]; absolute risk reduction, 0.38% [95% CI, 0.20%-0.55%]; number needed to treat, 265)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, which enrolled patients at moderate estimated risk of a first cardiovascular event, the primary composite endpoint occurred in 269 (4.29%) patients in the aspirin group versus 281 (4.48%) in the placebo group (HR 0.96; 95% CI 0.81-1.13; p=0.6038). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"In the intention-to-treat analysis, the primary endpoint occurred in 269 (4·29%) patients in the aspirin group versus 281 (4·48%) patients in the placebo group (hazard ratio [HR] 0·96; 95% CI 0·81-1·13; p=0·6038)."} -->
- In the Japanese Primary Prevention Project of patients aged 60 to 85 years with hypertension, dyslipidemia, or diabetes, the 5-year cumulative primary outcome event rate was not significantly different between aspirin and no aspirin (2.77% vs 2.96%; HR 0.94; 95% CI 0.77-1.15; P=.54). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8c085ce86e8550efe8d1ab710dba800f","quote":"The 5-year cumulative primary outcome event rate was not significantly different between the groups (2.77% [95% CI, 2.40%-3.20%] for aspirin vs 2.96% [95% CI, 2.58%-3.40%] for no aspirin; hazard ratio [HR], 0.94 [95% CI, 0.77-1.15]; P = .54)."} -->
- In ASPREE, among adults aged 70 years or older without cardiovascular disease, dementia, or disability, the rate of cardiovascular disease was 10.7 events per 1000 person-years with aspirin and 11.3 with placebo (HR 0.95; 95% CI 0.83-1.08). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"After a median of 4.7 years of follow-up, the rate of cardiovascular disease was 10.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.3 events per 1000 person-years in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.95; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.83 to 1.08)."} -->
- In ASCEND, among adults with diabetes but no evident cardiovascular disease, serious vascular events occurred in a significantly lower percentage of participants in the aspirin group than in the placebo group (658 [8.5%] vs 743 [9.6%]; rate ratio 0.88; 95% CI 0.79-0.97; P=0.01). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"During a mean follow-up of 7.4 years, serious vascular events occurred in a significantly lower percentage of participants in the aspirin group than in the placebo group (658 participants [8.5%] vs. 743 [9.6%]; rate ratio, 0.88; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.79 to 0.97; P=0.01)."} -->

## Bleeding harm

- In the primary prevention trials of the 2009 meta-analysis, aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10% vs 0.07% per year, p<0.0001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"Aspirin allocation increased major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeds (0.10%vs 0.07% per year, p\u003c0.0001), and the main risk factors for coronary disease were also risk factors for bleeding."} -->
- In the 2019 meta-analysis of 13 trials, aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding events compared with no aspirin (23.1 vs 16.4 per 10,000 participant-years; HR 1.43, 95% credible interval 1.30-1.56; absolute risk increase 0.47%). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"Aspirin use was associated with an increased risk of major bleeding events compared with no aspirin (23.1 per 10 000 participant-years with aspirin and 16.4 per 10 000 participant-years with no aspirin) (HR, 1.43 [95% credible interval, 1.30-1.56]; absolute risk increase, 0.47% [95% CI, 0.34%-0.62%]; number needed to harm, 210)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0.97%) patients in the aspirin group versus 29 (0.46%) in the placebo group (HR 2.11; 95% CI 1.36-3.28; p=0.0007). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"Gastrointestinal bleeding events (mostly mild) occurred in 61 (0·97%) patients in the aspirin group versus 29 (0·46%) in the placebo group (HR 2·11; 95% CI 1·36-3·28; p=0·0007)."} -->
- In ASCEND, major bleeding events occurred in 314 participants (4.1%) in the aspirin group versus 245 (3.2%) in the placebo group (rate ratio 1.29; 95% CI 1.09-1.52; P=0.003), with most of the excess being gastrointestinal bleeding and other extracranial bleeding. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"In contrast, major bleeding events occurred in 314 participants (4.1%) in the aspirin group, as compared with 245 (3.2%) in the placebo group (rate ratio, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.09 to 1.52; P=0.003), with most of the excess being gastrointestinal bleeding and other extracranial bleeding."} -->
- In ASPREE, the rate of major hemorrhage was 8.6 events per 1000 person-years with aspirin and 6.2 with placebo (HR 1.38; 95% CI 1.18-1.62; P<0.001). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"The rate of major hemorrhage was 8.6 events per 1000 person-years and 6.2 events per 1000 person-years, respectively (hazard ratio, 1.38; 95% CI, 1.18 to 1.62; P\u003c0.001)."} -->

## Mortality

- In ASPREE, among apparently healthy older adults, the risk of death from any cause was 12.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.1 in the placebo group (HR 1.14; 95% CI 1.01-1.29), with cancer the major contributor to the higher mortality. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"The risk of death from any cause was 12.7 events per 1000 person-years in the aspirin group and 11.1 events per 1000 person-years in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.14; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.01 to 1.29). Cancer was the major contributor to the higher mortality in the aspirin group, accounting for 1.6 excess deaths per 1000 person-years."} -->
- In ASPREE, cancer-related death occurred in 3.1% of participants in the aspirin group and 2.3% of those in the placebo group (HR 1.31; 95% CI 1.10-1.56). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_400696c0380783b1753ade39d010ccba","quote":"Cancer-related death occurred in 3.1% of the participants in the aspirin group and in 2.3% of those in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.10 to 1.56)."} -->
- In ARRIVE, there were 321 documented deaths in the intention-to-treat population (160 [2.55%] in the aspirin group vs 161 [2.57%] in the placebo group). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_a80cd40ea6db9ff8486b4ad6e10638d2","quote":"There were 321 documented deaths in the intention-to-treat population (n=160 [2·55%] vs n=161 [2·57%] of 6276 patients in the placebo group)."} -->
- In the 2009 meta-analysis of primary prevention trials, vascular mortality did not differ significantly between aspirin and control (0.19% vs 0.19% per year, p=0.7). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"Vascular mortality did not differ significantly (0.19%vs 0.19% per year, p=0.7)."} -->

## Cancer outcomes

- In ASCEND, there was no significant difference between the aspirin and placebo groups in the incidence of gastrointestinal tract cancer (157 [2.0%] vs 158 [2.0%]) or all cancers (897 [11.6%] vs 887 [11.5%]). <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"There was no significant difference between the aspirin group and the placebo group in the incidence of gastrointestinal tract cancer (157 participants [2.0%] and 158 [2.0%], respectively) or all cancers (897 [11.6%] and 887 [11.5%]); long-term follow-up for these outcomes is planned."} -->

## Overall balance of benefit and harm

- In primary prevention without previous disease, aspirin is of uncertain net value because the reduction in occlusive events needs to be weighed against any increase in major bleeds. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_8d9ae273169bf27b347ca66421434090","quote":"In primary prevention without previous disease, aspirin is of uncertain net value as the reduction in occlusive events needs to be weighed against any increase in major bleeds."} -->
- In ASCEND, aspirin prevented serious vascular events in persons with diabetes and no evident cardiovascular disease at trial entry but also caused major bleeding events, and the absolute benefits were largely counterbalanced by the bleeding hazard. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_98e15daeb7feed7b008fd69ffce6745f","quote":"Aspirin use prevented serious vascular events in persons who had diabetes and no evident cardiovascular disease at trial entry, but it also caused major bleeding events. The absolute benefits were largely counterbalanced by the bleeding hazard."} -->
- In ASPREE, low-dose aspirin as a primary prevention strategy in older adults resulted in a significantly higher risk of major hemorrhage and did not result in a significantly lower risk of cardiovascular disease than placebo. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_e9c7fb4c1ac80a88d29e42eb4e27608e","quote":"The use of low-dose aspirin as a primary prevention strategy in older adults resulted in a significantly higher risk of major hemorrhage and did not result in a significantly lower risk of cardiovascular disease than placebo."} -->
- Across the 2019 meta-analysis of 13 trials, aspirin use in individuals without cardiovascular disease was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular events and an increased risk of major bleeding. <!-- evimed-source:{"sourceId":"src_64f23983bfe8fd167aa6725f288d7375","quote":"The use of aspirin in individuals without cardiovascular disease was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular events and an increased risk of major bleeding."} -->
