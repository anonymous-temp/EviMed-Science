## What this run found

- **pbcseq (primary, n = 205 people, each tested once):** MAE of the individualized forecast minus that of last value -0.012 [-0.046, 0.019], minus that of the person's own line -0.244 [-0.318, -0.179] (95% person-bootstrap intervals). **the protocol does not allow the claim here: individualization is not shown to beat last-value-carried-forward**.
- **aids (secondary, n = 223 people, each tested once):** MAE of the individualized forecast minus that of last value -0.279 [-0.442, -0.119], minus that of the person's own line -2.239 [-2.988, -1.617] (95% person-bootstrap intervals). the protocol allows the claim "individualization helps" here.
- **actg175 (secondary, n = 1342 people, each tested once):** MAE of the individualized forecast minus that of last value -0.345 [-0.433, -0.262], minus that of the person's own line -7.850 [-8.355, -7.360] (95% person-bootstrap intervals). the protocol allows the claim "individualization helps" here.

In one sentence: on the primary dataset (pbcseq, log bilirubin, six months ahead) the individualized forecast's error is smaller than that of the straight-line extrapolation of the person's own history and than that of the population model without history (descriptive comparisons: the protocol attaches no claim to them), but individualization is not shown to be better than simply repeating the last measured value (the interval of the difference includes zero); on the two secondary datasets (JM aids, ACTG175) it did beat both, with intervals excluding zero. The claim is made for those two datasets only, as secondary results, and is not pooled with pbcseq.

## What may and may not be said from it

- Claim allowed by the protocol: **"individualization helps" on JM aids and on ACTG175 (secondary datasets)**, against both last value and the person's own line, intervals excluding zero. It is a statement about a mixed model with a random intercept and slope fitted on the other folds, for CD4 on the square-root scale, 6 months (aids) and about 76 weeks (ACTG175, nominal weeks) ahead, in these two trial populations.
- Claim **not** allowed on the primary dataset: on pbcseq the individualized forecast's MAE is 0.012 below last value (interval -0.046 to 0.019). The honest reading is "no detectable difference from repeating the last value"; its error is also lower than the straight-line extrapolation's and the population model's, which is descriptive only (the protocol makes no claim about those comparisons). By horizon the MAE of the individualized forecast against last value (all later visits, point errors only, no interval was computed, so none of this is a finding and no claim attaches to it): 0.321 vs 0.360 within 6 months, 0.329 vs 0.325 at 6-12 months, 0.489 vs 0.504 at 12-24 months, 0.570 vs 0.676 beyond 24 months.
- Nothing is claimed for the beta_logit Alzheimer model on real people (no public individual-level data). Its individualization is validated by known-truth simulation only (N130d-e).
- This is not evidence of a "digital twin": calibration and a validation record are required beside individual conditioning, and calibration here is mixed (below).

## Calibration, as measured

- The individualized forecast's intervals are slightly conservative on pbcseq: 50% coverage 0.615 (bootstrap interval 0.546 to 0.683, excludes 0.50), 80% 0.859 (0.815 to 0.902, excludes 0.80), 95% 0.971 (0.946 to 0.990, includes 0.95). On ACTG175 the 50% and 80% levels are also slightly high (0.552 and 0.835, intervals exclude nominal) and 95% is on target (0.943). On aids all three include nominal (0.578, 0.848, 0.951).
- The population model without history is over-wide on pbcseq at 95% (coverage 0.980) and too narrow at 80% on aids (0.749, interval 0.691 to 0.803): its spread does not describe the people it is applied to there.
- The plug-in forecasts' intervals (last value, the person's own line) use the root-mean-square error of the same forecast on the training folds as their width. That fixes their scale to the training error; it does not make them calibrated: last value's 50% coverage on aids is 0.592 (bootstrap interval 0.529 to 0.655, which excludes 0.50). They are a fairer comparison for the model-based intervals than an arbitrary fixed width, and no claim is made about either.

## Limits

pbcseq and aids are randomized-trial visit schedules; the history window of pbcseq carries the target's 60-day tolerance (the protocol says why); the model is linear in time and the fitted covariance is treated as known; ACTG175 times are nominal weeks and `cd496` is missing for 37% of patients, so the 1,342 are those who stayed; ACTG175's history is two points; the aids 12-18 month horizon row has 3 people. Comparing with the literature: TADPOLE found no submitted method beating a plain mixed-effects benchmark on ADAS-Cog13; this evaluation compares a mixed model with simple baselines on different data and says nothing about TADPOLE's task.

## Invalid first run (kept, not a result)

The first run (runner at `dbcc66e2b`, output SHA-256 `c54b074035e66a40098177de3c19e9092e2c47a317b657445417caa3eefd4798`, not committed) read the folds with `strtoi`, which returns NA above 2^31 - 1, so about half of the people were neither tested nor in any training set: 93 of 205 (pbcseq), 109 of 223 (aids), 665 of 1,342 (ACTG175). It was found from those denominators. The fold rule in the protocol was unchanged; the runner computed it wrongly. Amendment 1 of `PROTOCOL.md` records the fix (`f7c2b15a8`), and the runner now stops if any eligible person is not tested. The invalid run reached the same three verdicts (not allowed on pbcseq, allowed on aids and ACTG175) with wider intervals; the verdicts above are from the corrected run only.
