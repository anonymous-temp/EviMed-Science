SYNTHETIC / TEACHING MATERIAL. Fabricated excerpts, nonclinical, not a clinical report.

S1 (https://example.org/teaching-study-1) allocated participants alternately by day and its outcome assessors knew allocation, so it carries a systematic assignment-and-detection problem rather than a randomization. S2 (https://example.org/teaching-study-2) used a computer-generated sequence but did not describe allocation concealment, lost 12/60 participants without stating a reason, and reported a mean difference of 1.2 points (95% CI -0.5 to 2.9) on a scale it never names. Neither excerpt states the population, and neither defines the improvement outcome or a minimal important difference; S1 reports no interval for its 24/40 versus 20/40 comparison.

Because the interval in S2 spans from a small worsening to a benefit several times the point estimate, and because S1's allocation and blinding problems leave its counts open to systematic error, the two excerpts do not establish either benefit or harm at 8 weeks in adults, and no practice conclusion can be drawn from them. That uncertainty is a property of the supplied material, not a finding that the interventions are ineffective. What would reduce it: the population definition and eligibility for both studies, an analysis population statement for the 12 non-completers, disclosure of how S1's allocation was concealed, an interval for S1's comparison, a named scale with its minimal important difference, and a prespecified protocol or registration record for either study.
