01 · Scenario

100 shared study seats, first-come booking

  • A booking time is not the same for everyone in this case
  • 100 shared seats, first-come reservations, no extra capacity
  • Booking opens at library opening; no-shows occupy some seats
  • One group can book at opening; the other is in compulsory class
  • No individual may be identified, so only seat- and period-level counting is possible
02 · Incentives

Booking costs nothing to hold

  • Who decides (booking at opening), who pays (those excluded), who gains (the holder) are three different people
  • Holding a seat has no cost; releasing it brings the holder nothing
  • The gain is immediate and private; the cost is delayed and lands on someone else
  • Access rule quietly turns free time into the entry ticket
  • No one has to intend harm — this is what the rule rewards
03 · Uncertainty

What is not actually known

  • No measured no-show rate exists — this case gives structural facts only
  • Not confirmed: the time clash causes it, or demand is simply lower for the second group
  • Not confirmed: no-shows are motive-driven, or cancelling is just too much friction
  • Both rival readings stay open; they are not equally weighted, and not yet excluded
  • The harmed side stops trying, so its demand disappears from the record
04 · Reversible experiment

Two switchable changes, no new seats

  • Condition A: release part of the 100 seats only in the after-class window, ranking rule untouched
  • Condition B: after booking, one tap to confirm still needed; unconfirmed seats return to the pool
  • Individuals cannot be identified, so conditions alternate by week block, not by person
  • Measure only: seat-hours actually used per period, unused seat-hours from bookings, booking requests per period
  • Any change reverts within one day; stop if identities or added capacity become necessary
05 · Evaluation

Decide in advance how the test is read

  • Success: more seat-hours actually used at peak, with no extra step for anyone
  • If both changes show little movement, the time-clash account weakens and must be redone
  • Results are read as evidence about competing explanations, not as a score for a group
  • Null results are reported, not dropped
  • Roll back at once if the confirmation step pushes new time cost onto the same group
