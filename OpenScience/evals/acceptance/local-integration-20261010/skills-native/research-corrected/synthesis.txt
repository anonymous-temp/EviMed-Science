BOUNDED SYNTHESIS - trial methodology
Question: what methodological limitation prevents treating alternate-day allocation as concealed randomization?

SOURCE
  S1. Empirical evidence of bias in treatment effect estimates in controlled trials
      with different interventions and outcomes: meta-epidemiological study. BMJ,
      2008. doi:10.1136/bmj.39465.451748.ad
      Full text preserved and read (open-access published PDF, parsed). Preserved
      byte hashes are recorded alongside the search record. It is a 2008 study and
      is used here for its definitions and its own re-analysis, not as current
      guidance. Line numbers below refer to the preserved parsed text.

1. The answer
   Alternate-day allocation is not concealed randomization, because there is no
   unpredictable assignment for the concealment to protect. Allocation concealment
   is defined as an operational property of the enrolment step - it secures strict
   implementation of a schedule of random assignments by preventing foreknowledge
   of forthcoming allocations among the people recruiting participants (S1, DIRECT).
   An alternate-day rule is arithmetic: given the pattern and one assignment, the
   next assignment is fixed by the calendar rather than by chance. Foreknowledge is
   therefore present by construction, which is the one condition the definition
   forbids (SYNTHESIZED). The point is not that the concealment mechanism was
   implemented badly; it is that there is nothing in the assignment for a mechanism
   to conceal.

2. What the source states directly
   2.1 The definition (S1, line 72, DIRECT):
       "Allocation concealment: procedures that secure strict implementation of the schedule of random assignments by preventing foreknowledge of forthcoming allocations by study participants or by those recruiting them to the trial"
       The definition has two parts. There must be a schedule of random
       assignments, and the people enrolling must not be able to foreknow an
       assignment. Concealment is about foreknowledge at enrolment, not about the
       physical opacity of a container.

   2.2 The failure mode (S1, line 76, DIRECT):
       "· Failureto concealallocation may leadto biased selectionof participants into intervention groups"
       The named consequence is selection of participants into groups.

   2.3 The classification - the load-bearing statement (S1, line 80, DIRECT):
       "Examples of procedures usually considered inadequate include using an open random allocationschedule; assignment envelopeswithout appropriatesafeguards(forexample, unsealed or non-opaque or not sequentially numbered); and alternation or rotation"
       Two things follow. First, alternation is classified as inadequate without
       reference to any envelope, seal or numbering, so it is the method itself that
       is inadequate and no handling procedure can move it. Second, alternation is
       paired with rotation: both are order- or calendar-driven sequences, which is
       the class to which alternate-day allocation belongs.

   2.4 When the bias bites (S1, line 48, DIRECT):
       "bias associated with inade-quate allocation concealment may be greater when patients’ prognosis can readily be assessed at the time of recruitment to trials, leading to the possibility of selection bias"
       The route is recruiter knowledge at the moment of recruitment - exactly the
       knowledge that a legible schedule supplies.

   2.5 Concealment is upstream of blinding (S1, line 205, DIRECT):
       "It can be argued that, unless allocation is adequately concealed, it is impossible for a trial to be adequately blinded."
       A trial can look rigorously blinded downstream and still have lost the
       protection at enrolment.

   2.6 A second, independent-in-kind observation inside the same source (S1,
       line 94, DIRECT):
       "Definitions of adequate allocation concealment and blinding were similar in all three studies. Allocation concealment was assessed as adequate, unclear, or inadequate in two studies4 7 and as adequate or inadequate in the other.6"
       The three pooled meta-epidemiological studies agreed on what concealment
       means and reported it as its own domain, separate from blinding. Note the
       grading caveat: two of them admitted an "unclear" category, which is why the
       same method can be filed as unclear in one dataset and inadequate in another.
       That is a property of the grading tool, not of the allocation rule.

3. How the limitation works (SYNTHESIZED from 2.1, 2.2, 2.4)
   Combining the definition with the failure mode gives the mechanism. Concealment
   protects the enrolment decision by removing the recruiter's ability to anticipate
   the arm. A legible schedule restores that ability, and the recruiter can act on
   it - by deferring an eligible participant, by enrolling out of stated order, or
   by steering. That yields groups that differ systematically, which is selection
   bias rather than chance imbalance. Under alternation the ability is not merely
   present but trivial: it requires no breach of any seal, only knowing the rule and
   one assignment.

4. Why the limitation is structural rather than procedural (DERIVED; not stated by
   the source in these words)
   The step from S1's word "alternation" to the question's "alternate-day
   allocation" is an inference of category membership, and it is the only place in
   this document where I move beyond the source's own wording. The source names
   alternation and rotation; it never says "alternate day". The inference is
   supported by the pairing with rotation and by the fact that alternation is
   classified on its own, next to but distinct from the mechanically porous options
   (unsealed, non-opaque, unnumbered envelopes) - the porous options are failures of
   implementation, whereas alternation is listed as a procedure. The logical
   consequence is that opacity, sealing, sequential numbering, a third-party holder
   or a sealed-envelope ceremony cannot repair it, because all of those devices
   presuppose a sequence worth hiding.
   Confidence: moderate-to-high that alternate-day allocation belongs to the class
   the source classifies as inadequate; high that the class itself is classified as
   inadequate; no confidence attached to any magnitude.

5. What is uncertain, contested, or unknown
   5.1 Magnitude is not available and the source says so itself (S1, line 266,
       DIRECT):
       "Exceptwhentheoutcomewas allcause mortality, the sizeof biasvaried between meta-analyses. Therefore, in situations when bias is likely the size of such bias is unpredictable"
       So the direction of concern can be stated and the size cannot.
   5.2 The association is outcome-dependent (S1, line 234, DIRECT):
       "Therewas little evidence of bias in trials assessing all cause mortality or other objectively assessed outcomes. In contrast, inadequate allocation concealment and lack of blinding were associated with over-optimistic estimates of inter-vention effects for subjectively assessed outcomes."
       Read this as a limit on detection, not as a licence: the sequence is legible
       to the recruiter whatever the outcome. The defect is unconditional; only the
       detectable bias is outcome-dependent.
   5.3 The literature is not unanimous, and the source says so (S1, line 48 and its
       discussion, DIRECT): several studies found that inadequately concealed trials
       exaggerate effects, and the source itself notes that others "failed to
       confirm these associations". Empirical uncertainty about the size of the
       penalty does not touch the definitional point in section 2.
   5.4 Never stated by any source inspected: that alternate-day allocation
       specifically is non-concealed. The source classifies the general procedure
       "alternation or rotation". The narrowing to a daily calendar rule is mine
       (section 4).
   5.5 No empirical estimate, baseline-imbalance figure, or percentage attributable
       to alternate-day allocation was retrieved. Not "none exists" - not retrieved
       within this run's budget.

7. Correction relative to the earlier attempt on the same question
   The earlier pass concluded that no available source names an alternation-type
   schedule and left the proposition with moderate confidence based on a mechanism
   argument (an envelope system that was locally foreseeable). The corrected
   position is stronger and differently grounded: the source read in this run
   classifies "alternation or rotation" among procedures "usually considered
   inadequate" for allocation concealment, in a sentence that separates it from
   envelope-handling failures. The earlier caveat about magnitude stands unchanged -
   the source states the size of such bias is unpredictable. The gap that remains is
   narrower than before and is stated precisely: what is missing is a source using
   the terms "alternate-day" or "calendar-based allocation" themselves, not a source
   classifying alternation in general.
